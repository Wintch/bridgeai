# Fault finding and common cases

Fault-finding guide for people on site. Every case follows the same order: **symptom → probable causes (from most
frequent to least) → how to isolate the fault safely → correct solution → what NOT to do**. Every rule has an
ID (`ELEC-DIA-n`) so the agent, a report or a message can cite it ("it's an ELEC-DIA-5 case: neutral crossed between
circuits of different RCDs").

For calculations (current of an appliance, voltage drop, cross-section, demand, protection coordination, earth
resistance) the agent uses the offline calculator `gateway electricidad ...` and does not calculate from memory. The worked
examples here help to understand the calculation and to check the result.

**Safety first.** Diagnosing is not live work. The only things the user can do without tools are:
unplug, switch the panel's switches off or on, press the RCD (*diferencial*) test button, read the meter and switch off
the main switch. Opening the panel (distribution board, *tablero*), a box or a socket outlet (*toma*), disconnecting conductors or measuring with instruments is done by a
trained person, **with the circuit switched off, locked, verified dead and signposted** (see
[site-safety.md](site-safety.md)). The few measurements that require voltage (voltage at terminals, RCD
tester, clamp meter (*pinza amperométrica*)) are done only by trained personnel, with PPE (*EPP*) and with an instrument of the category that
matches the point: **CAT IV** at the meter pillar (*pilar*), meter and incoming end of the main panel; **CAT III** as a minimum in sub-panels
(*tableros seccionales*) and fixed circuits (ELEC-SEG-13, ELEC-MED-13). Probes with finger guards and caps, without touching live parts
with your hands and without tightening anything live.

## Rules

### ELEC-DIA-1: Diagnose dead; the user only does what doesn't require opening anything
- **Rule:** any fault finding that involves opening the panel, a box, a socket outlet or a luminaire is done with the circuit under lockout (*consignado*): switched off, locked (padlock or tag on the switch), verified dead with a tester or multimeter checked before and after on a known source, and signposted. The user or the site person in charge (*encargado*), without tools, can only unplug, operate switches, press the "T" button and switch off the main switch.
- **Why:** most electrical accidents during repairs happen "because it was switched off" and it wasn't: wrong MCB, circuit fed from somewhere else, single-pole MCB that left the neutral connected, broken neutral with return voltage, charged capacitors. An interrupted neutral can carry phase voltage to earth (ELEC-DIA-14).
- **How:**
  1. Ask and write down before touching: what trips, when it started, whether it rained, whether something was added, whether it happens with everything unplugged.
  2. Switch off the main switch if there is a burning smell, smoke, sparks, water on something electrical or a person gets a shock from something.
  3. For the work, apply lockout/tagout (LOTO) as per [site-safety.md](site-safety.md). If the circuit's MCB is single-pole (or there are fuses on one line only), switching it off does not isolate the neutral: also open the two-pole main switch and verify absence of voltage on the neutral too (ELEC-DIA-27).
  4. Verify with a two-pole voltage tester (*detector bipolar*, IEC 61243-3) or a multimeter. The screwdriver voltage tester (*buscapolo*) is no good for proving absence of voltage: it depends on your body as the path to earth (it fails with insulating footwear, on a ladder or on dry floors) and it doesn't show intermediate voltages. A high-impedance multimeter can show "phantom voltage" on a disconnected cable (ELEC-DIA-26).
  5. Only then, measure dead: continuity, insulation (megohmmeter), tightness of terminals.
- **Verify:** before touching, absence of voltage between all combinations (L-N, L-PE, N-PE) at the work point, with the instrument checked before and after.
- **Don't:** "test to see if it trips" with your fingers or with a wire; work with a coworker who "holds the MCB down"; lock out with a single-pole MCB only; tighten terminals in an energized panel "because it takes a second"; send the user to open the panel over the phone.
- **Source:** Decreto 351/79 (regulation of Ley 19.587), ch. 14 and Anexo VI (dead work, lockout); details in [site-safety.md](site-safety.md) ELEC-SEG-3 to ELEC-SEG-5 and ELEC-SEG-13 (two-pole voltage tester IEC 61243-3). Prohibition of single-pole PIAs in single-phase: AEA 90364-7-770 Ed. 2017, 770.16.5.1 (see [panels.md](panels.md) ELEC-TAB-9). Limitations of the screwdriver tester: operation of the neon lamp with series resistance, and trade practice. **Not verified in a primary source:** exact item number of Anexo VI (see the safety document).

### ELEC-DIA-2: General method: isolate the fault one circuit at a time, then one section at a time
- **Rule:** every fault is narrowed down in parts, from large to small: installation → panel → circuit → section → box or appliance. Change one thing at a time and write down what happened. The fault is corrected at its cause and measured again.
- **Why:** changing several things at once (new ID, unplugging everything, tightening terminals) makes the symptom disappear without knowing what the cause was, and it comes back.
- **How:**
  1. Unplug all appliances (switching them off is not enough), switch off all light switches and, under lockout, disconnect the fixed loads: luminaires and drivers, water heater (*termotanque*), AC, extractor fans, pump. If the problem disappears, it is in an appliance or fixed load: reconnect one at a time.
  2. If it persists, switch off all MCBs, switch on the main switch and the ID, and switch on the MCBs one at a time. The one that causes the symptom marks the circuit. **This only works if the MCBs are two-pole:** with single-pole MCBs or fuses on one line only, the neutral of the circuits "switched off" is still connected, and a neutral-earth fault in circuit Y makes the ID trip when you load circuit X. In that case, with the two-pole main switch open, disconnect the neutrals from the bar and test circuit by circuit (ELEC-DIA-27).
  3. In that circuit, under lockout, measure insulation by sections: open the intermediate boxes and split the circuit in halves until you find the bad section ("by halves" search).
  4. Correct, measure again (insulation, PE continuity) and test the ID with a tester (see [testing-and-commissioning.md](testing-and-commissioning.md) ELEC-MED-4 and ELEC-MED-6).
- **Verify:** it was written down which circuit, which section and which defect was found, and the insulation values before and after.
- **Don't:** replace components "just in case" without measuring; leave appliances disconnected as a "solution" without telling the user; break walls before ruling out the fixed loads.
- **Source:** trade practice; recommendation of more circuits and more RCDs to make fault detection easier: Miravalles, "Interruptor diferencial: disparos intempestivos" (RCD: nuisance tripping), *Ingeniería Eléctrica* 336 (Oct 2018). Test criteria: see [testing-and-commissioning.md](testing-and-commissioning.md).
### ELEC-DIA-3: "Salta el disyuntor" (the RCD trips): first find out whether it is an appliance or the installation
- **Rule:** when the RCD (*interruptor diferencial*, ID, "*disyuntor*") trips, first separate whether the leakage is in an appliance or in the fixed installation, with the method of ELEC-DIA-2. The most frequent causes, in order: an appliance with leakage (water heater, washing machine, fridge, heater, oven, pump), moisture in outdoor boxes or appliances, neutral crossed or to earth, accumulated leakage from many electronic devices, overvoltages and, last, a defective ID.
- **Why:** the ID trips when the current that goes in through the phase doesn't all come back through the neutral: the difference goes to earth (through an exposed conductive part, through a person, through moisture). For a 30 mA general-type ID, tripping between 15 and 30 mA is correct (it trips between 0.5 and 1 × IΔn). The ID is almost never the culprit: it is warning you.
- **How:**
  1. Does it trip instantly with everything unplugged, the fixed loads disconnected and all MCBs (*térmicas*) off except one (two-pole MCBs)? That line has the leakage (ELEC-DIA-4) or a neutral to earth (ELEC-DIA-5).
  2. Does it trip only when plugging in or switching on an appliance at any socket outlet? The appliance has leakage: don't use it until a technician checks it.
  3. Does it trip only when switching on a load on a specific circuit, but the load works fine on another? Suspect a neutral crossed or to earth in that circuit (ELEC-DIA-5).
  4. Does it trip when it rains or when watering, even with no lightning, and hold on a dry day? Water in something outdoors: ELEC-DIA-4. Does it trip only during a thunderstorm (lightning nearby, outages and micro-outages of the network), even without anything getting wet, or now and then with no pattern? ELEC-DIA-6 and ELEC-DIA-7. When the person says "cuando llueve" (when it rains), ask whether it also trips when watering and whether it happens with rain without lightning.
  5. Does it never trip, not even with "T"? ELEC-DIA-8.
- **Verify:** after the repair, the ID holds with all circuits loaded and trips with a tester between 0.5 and 1 × IΔn, within the times for its type (general: ≤ 300 ms at IΔn; selective S: between 130 and 500 ms at IΔn; see the table above and ELEC-MED-6).
- **Don't:** bypass the ID, remove it, replace it with a 300 mA one or "run the circuit outside the RCD"; reset it again and again (more than two attempts in a row that trip immediately = look for the fault).
- **Source:** Guía práctica SRT de la Res. 900/2015 (SRT practical guide; tripping between 0.5 and 1 IΔn, ≤ 300 ms at IΔn, test with an IEC 61557-6 tester); IEC 61008-1 (type S times; see ELEC-MED-6, where they are listed as not re-verified in the current text). Order of causes: trade practice and Miravalles, *Ingeniería Eléctrica* 336 (Oct 2018).

### ELEC-DIA-4: Real leakage or moisture: find it with a megohmmeter, circuit by circuit
- **Rule:** when a circuit makes the ID trip with everything unplugged, measure the insulation resistance with a megohmmeter (megger), under lockout and with the installation separated from the network neutral, and keep splitting the circuit until you find the point. The verification criterion is at least 1 MΩ at 500 V DC per circuit; a value much lower than that of similar circuits is the clue even if it exceeds the minimum.
- **Why:** leakage from moisture (outdoor socket outlets and boxes, garden or balcony luminaires, boxes in subfloors that flood, submersible pumps, conduits with water in them) is often not visible at 220 V in dry weather, but the megohmmeter at 500 V shows it. A cable pinched while pulling it through the conduit behaves the same way. Careful: in a TT system the utility's neutral is earthed at the transformer; if the circuit's neutral is still joined to the network, the instrument measures N → network → transformer earth → ground → your earth rod (*jabalina*) → PE, and reads a few ohms or tens of ohms on a healthy installation: a false "neutral to earth".
- **How:**
  1. Lock out the circuit. Warn people: the megohmmeter applies 500 V DC.
  2. **Separate the neutral from the network:** open the two-pole main switch (or the two-pole ID that feeds the circuit) and verify it. If there are single-pole MCBs, a single-pole main switch or any doubt, also disconnect the circuit's neutral from its terminal or bar (ELEC-DIA-27).
  3. Disconnect everything electronic (LED drivers, power supplies, dimmers, sensors, doorbells, SPDs (*DPS*)); remove lamps.
  4. Measure L to PE and N to PE **separately** (not only L+N joined): if N-PE reads low and L-PE is fine, it is a neutral touching earth (ELEC-DIA-5). If N-PE reads in the order of tens of ohms, first confirm that the neutral is really separated from the network.
  5. If it reads low, open the box in the middle of the run, separate the conductors and measure towards each side. Repeat until you find the box, the section or the appliance.
  6. Outdoors: check first the socket outlets and boxes in the yard, terrace and balcony, exposed wall luminaires, the cistern pump and the electric gate.
  7. Correct: dry and replace what is damaged, use boxes, socket outlets and luminaires with the IP rating suited to the location, seal conduit entries, rewire the section if the insulation is damaged.
- **Verify:** insulation per circuit ≥ 1 MΩ at 500 V DC, of the same order as the healthy circuits (in new work they usually read tens or hundreds of MΩ); ID that holds in the rain.
- **Don't:** insulation-test with electronic appliances connected; insulation-test with the neutral still joined to the network and conclude "neutral to earth"; insulation-test upstream of the incoming device or the meter (it belongs to the utility); "dry it with a hair dryer and that's it" without changing the IP rating or the sealing; touch the conductors during the test or before the instrument discharges the circuit.
- **Source:** criterion of 1 MΩ at 500 V DC per circuit: AEA 90364-6 Ed. 2025 and IEC 60364-6:2016, table 6.1 (verification); in single-family houses, AEA 90364-7-770 Ed. 2017, Corrigendum 1 (Aug 2018), 770.19.5.2.1 (check the number in Ed. 2026). Separation of the supply for measuring: IEC 60364-6, 6.4.3.3. Procedure in [testing-and-commissioning.md](testing-and-commissioning.md) ELEC-MED-4. Moisture and insulation tests at 500 V to reveal faults not visible at 220 V: Miravalles, *Ingeniería Eléctrica* 336 (Oct 2018). **Not verified in a primary source:** the verification clause of AEA 90364-7-771 Ed. 2006 (buildings and apartments); the same 1 MΩ criterion is used because Part 6 and 770 are aligned with IEC 60364-6.

### ELEC-DIA-5: Neutral shared, crossed between circuits or touching earth
- **Rule:** each circuit has its own neutral, which leaves from the same ID (and the same two-pole MCB) as its phase and returns only to it. The neutral is not joined to the protective conductor (PE) at any point of the user's installation (TT system).
- **Why:**
  - **Crossed neutral:** if a circuit's phase goes through ID 1 and its neutral returns through ID 2, each ID sees a current that goes in and doesn't come back: both (or one) trip as soon as there is a load, even with no leakage. It happens a lot when a circuit was added "taking the neutral from the box next door" or when two circuits share a neutral.
  - **Neutral to earth:** if the neutral touches the PE or an exposed conductive part downstream of the ID (badly placed terminal, pinched cable, appliance with neutral to the casing), part of the return current comes back through earth: the ID trips under load and, worse, it may become less sensitive.
  - **Neutral of a circuit without ID:** a circuit whose phase goes through the ID but whose neutral doesn't (or the other way round) always trips under load.
- **How:**
  1. Typical symptom: the ID trips when a specific circuit is loaded, but the same appliance works fine on another circuit; or it trips when switching on the lights of a room.
  2. Lock out (two-pole main switch open) and, in the panel, verify that each neutral leaves from the terminal of the corresponding ID (follow the cable, label it).
  3. With the suspect circuit's neutral disconnected from its terminal (that way it is separated from the network, ELEC-DIA-4), measure N to PE insulation (megohmmeter) and continuity between that neutral and the neutrals of the other circuits: it must read open.
  4. Look in junction boxes (*cajas de paso*) for splices where neutrals of different circuits were "hung" together. Separate them and give each circuit its own neutral.
- **Verify:** with each circuit disconnected from the others, N-N continuity between circuits open and N-PE insulation ≥ 1 MΩ; then, each circuit loaded with its ID closed and the rest of the IDs open works without trips.
- **Don't:** "bypass" (*puentear*) the neutral from one ID to another so it stops tripping; join neutral and earth in the apartment's panel "so the earth works"; use the PE (green-yellow) as a neutral.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-771 Ed. 2006 (TT system and protective conductor separate from the neutral, clause 771.3); each circuit with its own neutral and no neutrals shared between RCDs: [panels.md](panels.md) ELEC-TAB-16. **Not verified in a primary source:** the exact AEA clause that requires a dedicated neutral per circuit (the text is paid and could not be consulted); the electrical mechanism follows directly from how the ID works (IEC 61008-1).

### ELEC-DIA-6: Accumulated leakage: many devices on a single RCD (*diferencial*)
- **Rule:** if the ID trips with no clear pattern and there is no culprit circuit or appliance, add up the standing leakage of what is connected. As a practical criterion (recommendation, not an AEA requirement), the standing leakage of what hangs from an ID should not exceed 30 % of its IΔn: ≈ 9 mA (0.3 × 30 mA) for a 30 mA ID. The solution is to spread circuits over more IDs. Where there is electronics with a DC component, the ID must be type A (type F with single-phase drives); if in addition there are trips from transients or high-frequency leakage, use enhanced-immunity versions (A-SI "superinmunizado" (super-immunized) or equivalent).
- **Why:** every electronic power supply (computers, TVs, LED drivers, chargers, washing machines, inverter AC) has filters with capacitors to earth that let a small current through to the PE. Added up, and with an ID that can trip from 15 mA (0.5 × IΔn), any transient completes the trip. Type A detects currents that AC does not see, but it is not more immune to nuisance tripping because of that: enhanced immunity is a separate feature.
- **How:**
  1. With a leakage clamp meter (*pinza de fuga*: a mA clamp meter that encloses phase and neutral together) measure the residual current of each loaded circuit (live measurement: trained personnel only, without touching live parts).
  2. If the total approaches 9 mA on a 30 mA ID, split: more IDs, each with fewer circuits (for example, one for lighting and another for socket outlets; or one per circuit). See [protection.md](protection.md) ELEC-PRO-11.
  3. ID type according to the load (ELEC-PRO-9): A where there is electronics (induction, power supplies, LED); F for inverter air conditioners and washing machines with a single-phase drive (or A if the equipment manufacturer allows it); A-SI or equivalent where the trips are from transients or high-frequency leakage.
- **Verify:** measured leakage per ID well below 0.3 × IΔn; ID tested with a tester after the change.
- **Don't:** replace the 30 mA ID with a 300 mA one or with one that is "bigger" in amperes (the ID's rated current doesn't change the sensitivity); remove the PE from devices to "cut the leakage"; choose a "superinmunizado" thinking it is type B.
- **Source:** tripping between 0.5 and 1 IΔn: IEC 61008-1 and Guía SRT Res. 900/2015. Criterion of 0.3 IΔn: IEC 60364-5-53:2019, 531.3.2, according to [protection.md](protection.md) ELEC-PRO-11 (subclause number not verified in the text). Types A, F (IEC 62423) and super-immunized A-SI as type A with enhanced immunity: Schneider Electric, "El interruptor diferencial de la A a la B y la F" (The RCD from A to B and F) (blog, Jun 2019) and FAQ; ELEC-PRO-9. More IDs and splitting of circuits: Miravalles, *Ingeniería Eléctrica* 336 (Oct 2018).

### ELEC-DIA-7: Trips during storms or overvoltages
- **Rule:** if the ID trips during thunderstorms or network switching operations, the solution is surge protection (SPD) that is well located and well connected and, if it continues, an ID with greater immunity (A-SI or equivalent on the circuits with electronics). If the design provides an ID upstream of the 30 mA ones, that ID is selective (S, up to 300 mA; ELEC-PRO-12): the circuits' 30 mA IDs remain instantaneous. The insulation must also be checked: an overvoltage can "trigger" a fault that is not visible at 220 V.
- **Why:** atmospheric transients generate currents to earth through the capacitances of the installation and the equipment. An SPD installed downstream of the ID (in a socket outlet, in a power strip with protection, inside a power supply) discharges to earth through the ID and makes it trip.
- **How:**
  1. Measure the insulation of all circuits (ELEC-DIA-4): if any reads low, that is the real problem.
  2. Locate the SPD in the panel. If it goes upstream of the IDs (TT system), with **CT2** connection (in single-phase "1+1": varistor between phase and neutral and gas discharge tube between neutral and PE) and with the backup protection the manufacturer specifies: a phase-PE varistor upstream of the ID that fails leaves a standing leakage that no RCD cuts. If it goes downstream of an ID, that ID must be type S. See [protection.md](protection.md) ELEC-PRO-18 and [panels.md](panels.md).
  3. If there are power strips with SPD or socket outlets with a built-in SPD, take them into account as a cause of trips.
  4. Evaluate A-SI IDs on the circuits with electronics, according to the design.
- **Verify:** correct insulation per circuit; SPD with indicator in good condition and CT2 connection if it is upstream of the IDs; IDs tested with a tester.
- **Don't:** leave the ID off during the storm "so it doesn't trip" (you are left without protection); use an auto-reclosing ID to cover up an insulation fault without finding it; connect an SPD with phase-PE varistors upstream of the ID in TT.
- **Source:** atmospheric overvoltages, surge arresters downstream and moisture as causes of trips: Miravalles, *Ingeniería Eléctrica* 336 (Oct 2018). Enhanced immunity of A-SI against transient overvoltages and lightning: Schneider Electric, Acti9 presentation (Voltimum, 2021). CT2 connection in TT upstream of the ID, backup and type S downstream: IEC 60364-5-53 section 534, according to [protection.md](protection.md) ELEC-PRO-18. Upstream ID up to 300 mA selective: ELEC-PRO-12.

### ELEC-DIA-8: The RCD (*diferencial*) that doesn't trip with "T", or that trips with nothing connected
- **Rule:** an ID that doesn't trip when pressing the test button, while correctly connected and energized, is replaced: it is not repaired or "tapped to unstick it". An ID that trips with its output disconnected (all MCBs off and no cables on the output) is damaged or badly wired and is also replaced. If a new ID trips just the same, the fault is in the installation.
- **Why:** the "T" button forces an internal imbalance; if it doesn't trip, the mechanism or the relay has failed and the ID protects nobody. The monthly test exists precisely to detect this.
- **How:**
  1. With normal voltage, press "T": it must trip instantly.
  2. If it doesn't trip: under lockout, verify that the ID is connected according to the manufacturer's diagram (input and output, position of the neutral). In a four-pole ID used in single-phase, the phase and the neutral must go on the poles the data sheet indicates: otherwise, the test circuit is left without voltage and the button tests nothing. Once the wiring is corrected, if it doesn't trip with "T" when energized, it is replaced.
  3. To check an ID that trips "too much": test it with a tester without loads. If it trips below 0.5 × IΔn (less than 15 mA on a 30 mA one), replace it.
  4. ID for the location: type A where there is electronics with a DC component (induction cooktops (*anafes*), power supplies); type F for inverter air conditioners and washing machines with a single-phase drive (or A if the equipment manufacturer allows it); type AC only detects sinusoidal alternating current (ELEC-PRO-9).
- **Verify:** new ID trips with "T" and with a tester: does not trip at 0.5 × IΔn and trips at ≤ 1 × IΔn. Times: general type ≤ 300 ms at IΔn and ≤ 40 ms at 5 × IΔn; type S between 130 and 500 ms at IΔn and between 50 and 150 ms at 5 × IΔn (test with sinusoidal alternating current).
- **Don't:** leave in service an ID that doesn't test; replace it with an MCB (the MCB doesn't protect people); install an ID with a sensitivity or type different from the design's without the person who signs deciding it; reject a type S ID because it takes more than 300 ms.
- **Source:** Guía práctica SRT Res. 900/2015 (monthly button test to verify mechanical operation; tests with a tester and times); IEC 61008-1 (times of the general and S types, see ELEC-MED-6). Types AC, A and F according to the current they detect: Schneider Electric, "El interruptor diferencial de la A a la B y la F" (blog, Jun 2019); IEC 62423. Test circuit between specific poles in four-pole IDs: manufacturers' data sheets. See [testing-and-commissioning.md](testing-and-commissioning.md) ELEC-MED-6 and ELEC-MED-15.
### ELEC-DIA-9: Badly wired RCD (*diferencial*) (neutral taken from the wrong place upstream or downstream)
- **Rule:** the ID is connected with the line's phase **and neutral** entering through its input terminals, and all the circuits it protects take phase and neutral from its output. The neutral entering the ID comes from the line's neutral, never from the earth terminal block; and no circuit takes its neutral from a common terminal block that doesn't go through its ID.
- **Why:** typical mistakes in panels (distribution boards, *tableros*) assembled in a hurry: neutral entering the ID taken from the PE terminal block (the ID "works" but the installation is left with neutral and earth joined), common neutral bar downstream of several IDs (crossed neutrals), ID placed after a broken or loose neutral (the ID doesn't protect and the appliances see abnormal voltage), ID connected backwards on models that have a defined input and output.
- **How:**
  1. Lock out and survey the panel: draw the real single-line diagram (*unifilar*) (where each cable comes from). See [reviewing-a-panel.md](reviewing-a-panel.md).
  2. Each ID has its own output neutral bar or terminal block (*bornera*), separate from that of the other IDs.
  3. The line's neutral enters the ID; the PE terminal block is connected only to the protective conductor and to the earthing.
  4. Respect the connection direction the manufacturer marks (input/output, position of the neutral).
- **Verify:** with the two-pole main switch open and verified dead (so that the neutral is separated from the network; if the main switch is single-pole, disconnect the ID's input neutral), continuity between the ID's input neutral terminal and the PE terminal block: open. If it reads tens of ohms with the neutral still joined to the network, it is the path through the utility's earth, not a jumper (ELEC-DIA-4). Each circuit neutral connected to the output of its own ID. ID tested with a tester.
- **Don't:** use a single neutral bar for all the IDs; bypass (jumper) neutral to earth to "fix" a high N-E voltage; leave a circuit's neutral without going through any ID.
- **Source:** trade practice and wiring indicated by ID manufacturers; separate neutral and earth terminal blocks: [panels.md](panels.md) ELEC-TAB-15 and ELEC-TAB-16; neutral-PE separation of the TT system: AEA 90364-7-771 Ed. 2006 (clause 771.3) and [earthing.md](earthing.md). **Not verified in a primary source:** specific AEA clause on neutral bars per ID.

### ELEC-DIA-10: "Salta la térmica" (the MCB trips): tell overload from short circuit
- **Rule:** the MCB (*térmica*, PIA) protects the **cable**, not the appliance. If it trips, first find out whether it is an overload (it trips after a while, with a lot of load) or a short circuit (it trips instantly, sometimes with a spark or a noise). Never increase the MCB's rating so it "holds": the rating is set by the conductor's cross-section and the type of circuit.
- **Why:** going up from 16 A to 25 A on a 2.5 mm² circuit lets the cable overheat without anything cutting it off: it is the classic cause of conduits with melted cables and fires.
- **How:**
  1. **Overload:** add up what was running. Current of a resistive appliance: I = P / U.
     *Worked example:* electric kettle 2000 W + quartz heater 1200 W + microwave 1200 W on the same 16 A socket-outlet circuit: I = (2000 + 1200 + 1200) W / 220 V = 4400 / 220 = 20 A. That is 20 / 16 = 1.25 × In: the standard does not guarantee when it trips (between 1.13 × In = 18.1 A and 1.45 × In = 23.2 A the time is not guaranteed). Depending on the manufacturer's curve it may take tens of minutes, more than an hour or not trip at all, and meanwhile the cable works near or above its current-carrying capacity. Only at 23.2 A must it trip before 1 h. That it doesn't trip doesn't mean it is fine. Solution: spread the loads or make a new circuit, not change the MCB. The cable's current-carrying capacity according to how it is installed comes from the calculator.
  2. **Short circuit:** if it trips instantly when switched on, first switch off all light switches and, under lockout, disconnect the circuit's fixed loads (luminaires and drivers, water heater, AC, extractor fans): a luminaire or a driver in short circuit is a frequent cause. If it keeps tripping with all that out → fault in the wiring (phase and neutral in contact in a box, a cover screw that pierced a cable, a nail or wall plug in the wall, ELEC-DIA-33). If it trips only when plugging something in → the appliance is short-circuited.
  3. **Starting:** AC units, pumps, compressors and transformers have a starting current several times the rated current; with a B curve they may trip on starting. The curve is chosen by the design (usually C in dwellings).
  4. If it trips with no explanation and the MCB is hot: ELEC-DIA-11 (loose terminal).
  5. Use the calculator `gateway electricidad` for current, cross-section and coordination.
- **Verify:** sum of the circuit's usual loads below the MCB's In; in case of a short circuit, the circuit's L-N insulation ≥ 1 MΩ after repairing; MCB consistent with the cross-section according to [protection.md](protection.md) and [conductors-and-conduits.md](conductors-and-conduits.md).
- **Don't:** increase the rating; fit a 32 A MCB "because the 16 one trips"; jam the lever; replace a fuse with wire; switch back on an MCB that tripped from a short circuit without finding the short; break the wall before ruling out the fixed loads.
- **Source:** IEC 60898-1 (B, C, D curves; 1.13 × In without tripping in 1 h and 1.45 × In with tripping before 1 h), according to manufacturers' catalogs (Siemens, Schneider, ABB). MCB-cross-section relationship: AEA 90364-7-771 Ed. 2006 (see [protection.md](protection.md) ELEC-PRO-4 and ELEC-PRO-6). **Not verified in a primary source:** AEA clause number on conductor-protection coordination.

### ELEC-DIA-11: Poor contact: one of the most frequent causes of electrical fires
- **Rule:** flickering lights, an MCB that trips "hot", a socket outlet or a plug that heats up, a smell of burnt plastic or a blackened terminal indicate a poor contact. Switch off the circuit, find the hot spot and remake the connection with the component in good condition, at the manufacturer's tightening torque.
- **Why:** in a loose or oxidized terminal, or one with a badly stripped conductor, the contact resistance rises and dissipates heat at a small point, even if the MCB never trips (the current is normal).
  *Worked example:* with 16 A and a contact resistance of 0.05 Ω, the power at the terminal is P = I² × R = 16² × 0.05 = 12.8 W concentrated in millimeters: enough to soften and char the plastic. With 10 A it is 5 W; the heat rises with the square of the current.
- **How:**
  1. Switch off the circuit (and the main switch if there is a strong smell or smoke).
  2. If a thermal imaging camera is available, go over the panel **under load**, with the door open and the inner cover (dead front, *contratapa*) in place (without exposing live parts) or through an IR inspection window: infrared does not go through the door or the cover. Removing the inner cover while live is for qualified personnel with PPE only. The measurement is contactless (see [testing-and-commissioning.md](testing-and-commissioning.md) ELEC-MED-23).
  3. Under lockout, check: terminals of the MCB, the ID and the bars (loose cable, insulation caught inside the terminal, cables of different cross-section in the same terminal); splices in boxes (loose, badly twisted, without suitable insulation, tape come off); socket outlets with opened-up contacts.
  4. Remake: cut the cable back to the healthy part, strip to length, lug or ferrule on stranded conductors when the terminal requires it, tighten to the torque the manufacturer specifies. Splices only in boxes and as indicated in [conductors-and-conduits.md](conductors-and-conduits.md) ELEC-CON-21 (terminal block or standardized connector where applicable). Replace any discolored or deformed component.
  5. Also check the plug and the appliance: a worn plug heats up the new socket outlet.
- **Verify:** after one hour under normal load, no noticeable heating at terminals or socket outlets (thermography if available); retightened terminals recorded.
- **Don't:** change only the MCB "because it was tripping"; put tape over a burnt terminal; leave splices loose or outside the boxes; put two cables of different cross-section in a terminal designed for one; tighten terminals live.
- **Source:** physics of heating by contact resistance (P = I²R); tightening torque and ferrules: data sheets of PIA and ID manufacturers; splices: ELEC-CON-21. **Not verified in a primary source:** Argentine statistics on fires from poor contact.

### ELEC-DIA-12: Socket outlets (*tomas*) that heat up or melt
- **Rule:** a socket outlet that heats up or deforms is replaced, and the cause is also corrected: high continuous load on a 10 A socket outlet or plug, loose or worn plug, loose socket-outlet terminal, or an adapter or power strip in between.
- **Why:** general-purpose socket outlets (10 A) cope badly with loads of several hours near their limit (heaters, fan heaters, portable AC units, oven loads, plugged-in water heaters). A plug with spread pins makes poor contact (ELEC-DIA-11).
- **How:**
  1. Identify what was plugged in and its power (rating plate). 2200 W at 220 V = 10 A: that is already at the limit of a 10 A socket outlet.
  2. Under lockout, replace the socket outlet with one of certified quality; check the terminals and conductors behind it (if the insulation is hardened or black, cut back to the healthy part or rewire the section).
  3. Replace the appliance's plug if it is burnt or loose.
  4. If the appliance has a high permanent consumption (AC, water heater, oven, fixed electric heater), give it a dedicated circuit with a 20 A socket outlet or a fixed connection, according to [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md).
- **Verify:** new socket outlet firm (the plug goes in with pressure), no heating under normal load; appliance load compatible with the socket outlet and the circuit; general socket outlets wired in 2.5 mm² as a minimum.
- **Don't:** fit a 20 A socket outlet on a general socket-outlet circuit so "the big plug fits" (if you find general socket outlets wired in 1.5 mm², that is already a defect to correct: the minimum cross-section of a TUG is 2.5 mm², see [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md)); cut or file a plug's pin so it fits; keep using a deformed socket outlet.
- **Source:** socket outlets and plugs IRAM 2071 and 2073. Res. SICyM 524/98 was repealed by Res. SIyC 16/2025 (art. 8, BO 2025-02-25). According to [regulations-and-permits.md](regulations-and-permits.md) ELEC-NOR-14, fixed socket outlets and plugs fell under the regime for materials for electrical installations (Res. SIyC 236/2024 with Anexo II of Res. SIyC 26/2025: fixed socket outlets in item 4.5.6, plugs in 4.5.8 and 4.5.9, according to [iram-standards.md](iram-standards.md) ELEC-IRA-8; Res. SIyC 237/2024; Disp. DNRT 2/2025). **Not verified in a primary source:** whether plugs and socket outlets are also listed in Anexo II of Res. 16/2025. Minimum cross-sections per circuit type (TUG 2.5 mm²): AEA 90364-7-771 and 7-770, according to [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md).

### ELEC-DIA-13: Low or high voltage: measure where it is lost before filing a complaint
- **Rule:** with low voltage (dim lights, fridge or AC that won't start) or high voltage (lamps that burn out often), measure the voltage at the incoming end of the unit's main panel (in buildings it is usually in the meter room, next to the meter) and, if there is a sub-panel (*tablero seccional*) in the apartment, also at its incoming end; with and without load, and compare with the network tolerance (± 8 % in the Edenor area: 202.4 V to 237.6 V for 220 V). If it is wrong at the incoming end, it is the utility's (*distribuidora*); if it is fine at the incoming end and wrong at the socket outlet, the voltage drop is internal.
- **Why:** the voltage drop in your own installation (long or thin main line or sub-main, loose terminal, loose neutral) gets mistaken for a network problem. A loose neutral on a unit's line lowers and alters that unit's voltage depending on the load; a loose neutral before the meters affects several units (the prelude to a broken neutral, ELEC-DIA-14).
- **How:**
  1. Measure (trained personnel; **CAT IV** multimeter at the incoming end of the main panel, CAT III at the sub-panel) L-N, L-PE and N-PE, without load and with a known load (e.g. 2000 W kettle). The no-load measurements at the incoming end and at the socket outlet are made at the same time.
  2. Calculate the drop: ΔU = U no-load − U load.
     *Worked example:* panel incoming end: 221 V without load and 219 V with 9 A (upstream drop of 2 V, normal). Kitchen socket outlet: 221 V without load (without current there is no drop) and 207 V with 9 A. Total drop at the socket outlet = 221 − 207 = 14 V; of that, 2 V is lost before the panel. Circuit drop = 14 − 2 = 12 V (5.5 % of 220 V) and go-and-return resistance ≈ 12 V / 9 A ≈ 1.33 Ω. A 20 m circuit of 2.5 mm² (40 m of copper go and return) should have 40 m × 0.00741 Ω/m ≈ 0.30 Ω (solid or stranded cable, class 1/2) or 40 m × 0.00798 Ω/m ≈ 0.32 Ω (flexible class 5), at 20 °C; hot (70 °C), about 0.36-0.38 Ω. With 9 A that would give about 3 V of drop, not 12 V: there is a poor contact along the way (ELEC-DIA-11). Use the calculator for the theoretical value.
  3. Neutral: compare N-PE without your own load and with load. In TT there can be several volts N-PE even without your load, because of the imbalance and the drop on the network neutral: that belongs to the network. What matters is how much it rises with your load; if it rises several volts, check the neutral terminals from the meter to the panel (under lockout; the meter is not touched).
  4. If the incoming voltage is out of tolerance, file a complaint with the utility and get a complaint number; if it doesn't respond, go to the regulator (in the Edenor/Edesur area, the Ente Nacional Regulador del Gas y la Electricidad (National Gas and Electricity Regulator), ENReGE, successor of ENRE: Ley 27.742, Decreto 452/2025; see ELEC-NOR-18; the provincial regulator elsewhere). The utility can install a recorder for at least one week.
- **Verify:** values written down with date and time; under normal load, voltage at the farthest socket outlet within the voltage drop margin the design set.
- **Don't:** install a stabilizer "for the whole house" without knowing where the drop comes from; tighten meter terminals (it belongs to the utility and is sealed); look for an internal fault because of a few volts N-PE that were already there without load.
- **Source:** Subanexo 4 Edenor period 2025-2030 (Res. ENRE 304/2025), item 2: permitted variations in MV and LV from −8 % to +8 %; penalty if exceeded for more than 3 % of the period, measurement of at least one week (Edesur: Res. ENRE 303/2025, subanexo not re-verified). ENReGE (formerly ENRE), FAQ: for high or low voltage problems or fluctuations, complain first to the utility. Resistances per meter: IEC 60228 (2.5 mm²: 7.41 Ω/km class 1/2 and 7.98 Ω/km class 5, max. at 20 °C), the same ones used by [testing-and-commissioning.md](testing-and-commissioning.md) ELEC-MED-3. Voltage drop limits: see [conductors-and-conduits.md](conductors-and-conduits.md). Acronym: the Boletín Oficial (e.g. Res. ENReGE 559/2026) and argentina.gob.ar write "ENReGE", the same one used by [regulations-and-permits.md](regulations-and-permits.md); the press often writes "ENRGE" (Decreto 318/2026, BO 2026-05-05). **Not verified in a primary source (in this review):** the text of item 2 of Subanexo 4 (infoleg did not respond); whether it distinguishes overhead, underground and rural supply.
### ELEC-DIA-14: Broken neutral (*corte de neutro*) in a building: appliances burned out in several apartments
- **Rule:** if appliances burn out in several units of a building at the same time, or some apartments have very high voltage and others very low, treat it as a broken or loose common neutral until proven otherwise: **switch off the incoming switches of the affected units**, request emergency service from the utility (*distribuidora*), notify the person in charge (*encargado*) or the building management, and don't restore power until the neutral is repaired and measured.
- **Why:** in a building with single-phase units spread across the three phases, the neutral shared by several units is **upstream of the meters** (*medidores*): network, service drop (*acometida*), utility service box (*caja de toma*) (NH fuses and sealed busbars), neutral busbars of the common meter cabinet and, in buildings with meters on each floor, the feeder or riser (*montante*) that supplies them. If that neutral breaks, units on different phases end up **in series** between phases (380 V) and the voltage divides according to the load: the unit with the lowest consumption gets the highest voltage.
  *Worked example:* apartment A on phase L1 with 500 W switched on (R = U² / P = 220² / 500 = 96.8 Ω) and apartment B on phase L2 with 3000 W (R = 220² / 3000 = 16.1 Ω). With the neutral broken they are in series between L1 and L2 (380 V): I = 380 / (96.8 + 16.1) = 3.37 A; voltage at A = 3.37 × 96.8 ≈ 326 V; at B = 3.37 × 16.1 ≈ 54 V. In A, power supplies, TVs and chargers burn out; in B, motors and compressors don't start and overheat. (With non-resistive loads the values change, but the phenomenon is the same.)
  After each meter, each unit has its own neutral (its main line or its sub-main, depending on where the main panel is; see [buildings.md](buildings.md), "Estructura típica" (typical structure)). If that neutral breaks, **that** unit loses supply (or gets erratic voltages if it is loose, ELEC-DIA-13), and the load-side neutral can sit at phase potential through the appliances that are switched on; but voltage is not divided between units. A broken neutral in the three-phase common services panel produces the same series effect, but only between the single-phase common services loads (landing (*palier*) lights, door intercom), not in the apartments.
- **How:**
  1. Safety: a broken neutral can carry voltage to earth. Don't touch or disconnect neutrals while the units are energized.
  2. Switch off the affected incoming switches (in buildings they are usually in the meter room; also the main switch of the apartment's panel) and unplug sensitive equipment.
  3. Locate: if there are overvoltages and undervoltages in units on different phases at the same time, the break is upstream of the meters (service drop, utility service box, meter cabinet busbars). That belongs to the utility and is sealed: **don't touch it**; request emergency service and get a complaint number. If the problem affects a single unit, it is the neutral of its line or its terminals (ELEC-DIA-13): an electrician checks it, under lockout/tagout.
  4. Responsibility boundary (Edenor/Edesur area): the utility's installation reaches up to the meter box and up to the input terminals of the first isolating device after the meter. Everything after that (main panel of each unit, its line, the apartment's panel; common services panel) is repaired by a qualified electrician hired by the user or the owners' association (*consorcio*), depending on who owns it.
  5. Aluminum joints (aerial bundled service cable) to copper at the meter pillar (*pilar*) or the utility service box: they belong to the utility. If you see a connector without a bimetallic part, or a blackened one, file a complaint; don't work on it. On the user side, every Cu-Al joint takes a suitable bimetallic connector (a direct joint corrodes and ends up as a loose connection).
  6. Damage claim: first to the utility, with a complaint number and quotes or technical reports on the appliances; if it doesn't answer or rejects the claim, to the regulator. Keep the damaged appliances.
  7. Prevention: an over/undervoltage protector in each unit acting on its incoming switch (shunt trip coil or equivalent), with timed reconnection ([buildings.md](buildings.md) ELEC-EDI-11, [protection.md](protection.md) ELEC-PRO-19). An SPD (*DPS*) does not protect against this.
- **Verify:** after the repair, with load in several units, L-N between 202.4 and 237.6 V in each one and N-PE of a few volts; neutral terminals on the user side (panels of each unit and of common services) tight and without discoloration.
- **Don't:** fit fuses or switches that break only the neutral; open the utility service box or the meter cabinet; restore the supply "to see if it came back"; connect the neutral to the building earth as a temporary fix; use the water pipe or the structure as a neutral; rely on an SPD against a broken neutral.
- **Source:** responsibility boundary: Res. ENRE 95/2021, on art. 5 inc. d) of the Reglamento de Suministro (supply regulations) Edenor/Edesur (installation up to the meter box and up to the input terminals of the first isolating device after the meter); see also ELEC-NOR-15 and [buildings.md](buildings.md) ELEC-EDI-5. Damage claim: Reglamento de Suministro art. 3 inc. e) (cited in Subanexo 4 Edenor 2025-2030); argentina.gob.ar, ENReGE (formerly ENRE), note of 2023-08-28: claim first to the service provider, which must answer within fifteen business days; official damage claim form (Jan 2025). Mechanism of voltage division with an open neutral: Miravalles, "¿Trifásica o monofásica?" (three-phase or single-phase?), *Ingeniería Eléctrica* 345; ELEC-EDI-11. Voltage protector "altamente recomendable" (highly recommended): AEA 90364-7-771 Ed. 2006 and 7-770 Ed. 2017, according to ELEC-EDI-11 and ELEC-PRO-19. **Not verified in a primary source:** whether the Reglamento de Suministro in force since the RTI 2025-2030 keeps art. 5 inc. d and the 15-business-day deadline (the official note predates the RTI and the change of regulator); the maximum time the user has to file the damage claim (press reports mention 60 calendar days; confirm with the utility or the regulator); in other provinces the local regulations apply.

### ELEC-DIA-15: Reversed phase, switches that break the neutral
- **Rule:** light switches always break the **phase**; the phase goes to the center contact of the lampholder; in socket outlets and terminals, phase and neutral go in their place. If a lamp stays live while switched off, or a plug "works backwards", correct the polarity.
- **Why:** with the switch breaking the neutral, the lampholder and the luminaire stay live even though the light is off: whoever changes the lamp gets electrocuted. Also, with phase and neutral swapped, a "neutral" that touches an exposed conductive part or the PE is actually a phase to earth: the RCD (*disyuntor* / *diferencial*, "ID") trips (and without an ID the exposed part stays live); and luminaires or appliances that break a single pole leave the internal parts live.
- **How:**
  1. Prefer dead testing: under lockout/tagout, with the phase-PE jumper in the panel, check by continuity that the switch interrupts the conductor coming from the phase terminal of the MCB (*térmica*) and that the center contact of the lampholder is on that conductor (ELEC-MED-7). If you must confirm live, do it in the switch box (not inside the lampholder), with a two-pole voltage tester or a multimeter with LoZ function to rule out phantom voltage (ELEC-DIA-26); many luminaires have no PE at the lampholder.
  2. If it is wrong, lock out and correct it in the switch box: the phase goes into the switch; the switched return goes to the lamp; the neutral goes straight to the lamp.
  3. In the panel: verify that the phase leaves from the phase terminal of each MCB and the neutral from the neutral terminal (two-pole MCBs break both, but polarity still matters downstream). If the reversal comes from the service drop, it is a complaint to the utility (ELEC-MED-21).
  4. Check appliances with a single-pole switch (heaters, water heaters): with reversed polarity, the appliance's switch leaves the heating element live.
- **Verify:** polarity test recorded (see [testing-and-commissioning.md](testing-and-commissioning.md) ELEC-MED-7).
- **Don't:** assume a light is "cut off" because the switch is off; measure live inside the lampholder; use wrong conductor colors (light blue is neutral; green-yellow is PE only).
- **Source:** regulatory practice of breaking the phase and conductor colors: AEA 90364-7-771 Ed. 2006 (see [conductors-and-conduits.md](conductors-and-conduits.md)); polarity as a dead test: IEC 60364-6:2016, 6.4.3.6 (ELEC-MED-7); phase-neutral swap and tripping: Miravalles, *Ingeniería Eléctrica* 336 (Oct 2018). Phantom voltage and LoZ function: Fluke, "Dual impedance digital multimeters".

### ELEC-DIA-16: "Me patea la canilla, la heladera o el lavarropas" (the tap, the fridge or the washing machine gives me shocks)
- **Rule:** if a person feels "kicks" (*patadas*) or tingling when touching a tap, the fridge, the washing machine, the shower or a railing, switch off the main switch and don't use it again until the leakage is found and the protective conductor and earthing are verified. This is a life-threatening situation, not a nuisance.
- **Why:** the "kick" is an exposed conductive part made live by a leakage (cracked water heater element, pinched cable, appliance with damaged insulation) that does not go to earth because the PE is missing or broken, or the earth rod (*jabalina*) is useless. If the RCD is also missing or defeated, nothing trips. In buildings it can also come from another unit through metal pipes.
- **How:**
  1. Switch off the main switch. Tell everyone not to touch.
  2. Unplug suspect appliances (water heater, washing machine, fridge, pump). If the tap is metal and there is an electric water heater or electric shower, they are the first suspects (ELEC-DIA-32).
  3. Under lockout/tagout: measure PE continuity from the panel to the socket outlet and the appliance's exposed conductive part; measure earth electrode resistance; insulation test the circuit and the appliance (ELEC-DIA-4).
  4. Verify the main equipotential bonding (metal water and gas pipes, structure) to the earth bar, according to [earthing.md](earthing.md).
  5. Verify that there is a 30 mA RCD and that it trips with a tester.
  6. If the electrician measures with an instrument (never by touching) voltage between the tap or the exposed part and earth with the unit's own main switch off, the source is external (another unit, shared pipes, the network): keep people away and notify the building management and the utility.
- **Verify:** with everything repaired and connected, exposed conductive parts without voltage to earth; PE continuity correct; Ra within table 771.3.I (max. 40 Ω with RCD ≤ 300 mA); RCD trips in time.
- **Don't:** "test" by touching with a wet hand or the back of the hand, not even to see if "it's gone"; put rubber or plastic under the appliance; connect the appliance's PE to the water pipe or the neutral; keep using the appliance "because it's just a little".
- **Source:** Guía práctica SRT Res. 900/2015 (practical guide) (table 771.3.I, 40 Ω with IΔn ≤ 300 mA; conventional limit voltage 24 V according to Decretos 351/79, 911/96, 617/97 and 249/07); AEA 90364-7-771 Ed. 2006 (clause 771.3, TT system and equipotential bonding). See [earthing.md](earthing.md) ELEC-PAT-8 and ELEC-PAT-9.

### ELEC-DIA-17: Old installations: what to prioritize when renovating
- **Rule:** in an old installation (no earth, cables with rubber insulation or with cloth/cotton braid, plug fuses or porcelain fuses, single-pole MCBs, deteriorated splices, boxes without covers, rusty iron conduit, aluminum conductors) prioritize by risk to people, in this order:
  1. **30 mA RCD** at the head of all socket outlet and lighting circuits (protects against direct contact even if there is no PE yet).
  2. **Protective conductor and earthing** with a measured earth rod and equipotential bonding.
  3. **Replacement of deteriorated conductors** (hardened rubber, cloth, insulation that cracks when bent) and of every splice that is loose, lacks proper insulation or is outside a box.
  4. **New panel** with a two-pole incoming switch and two-pole MCBs instead of fuses and single-pole devices, one per circuit, rating according to cross-section; covers on all boxes.
  5. **More circuits** to separate large loads (AC, oven, water heater) from general socket outlets.
- **Why:** old insulation cracks and leaves conductors exposed inside conduits and boxes; plug fuses get "fixed" with wire and don't protect people; single-pole MCBs leave the neutral connected (ELEC-DIA-27); without PE or RCD a leakage in an appliance can be fatal. The RCD is the upgrade with the greatest effect for cost and time; but if it trips when installed, the installation has leakages that must be found (ELEC-DIA-4), it is not "the RCD being sensitive".
- **How:**
  1. Survey: visual inspection, insulation test per circuit (under lockout/tagout and with the neutral separated from the network, ELEC-DIA-4), check whether any PE exists and whether the protective devices are two-pole.
  2. If insulation is low overall or the cables crack when moved, rewire completely: there is no point in "running new cables only where it fails".
  3. Aluminum in the internal installation: replace with copper or, if some section remains, join with connectors suitable for Cu-Al. The aluminum service drop and its joints at the meter pillar or the utility service box belong to the utility: if you see a connector without a bimetallic part, or a blackened one, file a complaint; don't work on it.
  4. Replacing the incoming switch means working on the input terminals of the first isolating device, which are always live: request a disconnection from the utility (ELEC-NOR-15).
  5. Document what was changed and what remains pending; the complete renovation is designed and signed by a professional or qualified installer ([regulations-and-permits.md](regulations-and-permits.md)).
- **Verify:** after each stage, insulation ≥ 1 MΩ at 500 V DC per circuit, RCD tested with a tester, PE continuity to each socket outlet with earth.
- **Don't:** fit three-pin socket outlets without a protective conductor behind them (they mislead the user); connect the socket outlet's earth terminal to the neutral (neutral-earth "puente" (jumper)); replace fuses with higher-rated ones or with wire; pull new 4 mm² cables through a conduit full of old cables without removing the old ones.
- **Source:** requirement of 30 mA RCD, PE and TT system for new installations: AEA 90364-7-771 Ed. 2006 (see [protection.md](protection.md) and [earthing.md](earthing.md)); two-pole PIA and prohibition of single-pole devices in single-phase: AEA 90364-7-770 Ed. 2017, 770.16.5.1 (ELEC-TAB-9); minimum insulation: AEA 90364-6 Ed. 2025 / IEC 60364-6 and AEA 770 Corrigendum 1 (2018); utility boundary: ELEC-NOR-15. **Not verified in a primary source:** an official order of priorities for renovating existing installations (no Argentine standard setting one was found: the order in this rule is technical risk-based judgment); a specific prohibition of plug fuses and aluminum conductors in indoor installations in the current AEA regulations.

### ELEC-DIA-18: Socket outlets (*tomas*) without earth, adapters, daisy-chained power strips (*zapatillas*) and extension cords
- **Rule:** every class I appliance (with a three-pin plug) is plugged into a socket outlet with the PE connected. As a technical safety criterion, don't use adapters that defeat the earth pin, or triple adapters (*triples*), or power strips (*zapatillas*) plugged into other power strips. A power strip or extension cord (*prolongación*) is sized for the sum of what is plugged in and is used fully unwound.
- **Why:** a three-to-two-pin adapter leaves the appliance's enclosure without earth (if there is a leakage, the exposed part stays live and only the RCD saves you). Daisy-chaining power strips adds loads onto a single plug and a single 10 A socket outlet, and multiplies contacts that can work loose (ELEC-DIA-11). A coiled extension reel under high load heats up because the cable cannot dissipate heat.
  *Worked example:* on a 10 A power strip (2200 W at 220 V) with a 2000 W fan heater + 900 W coffee maker: I = 2900 / 220 = 13.2 A, 32 % above its capacity: the plug, the power strip and the socket outlet heat up, even though the 16 A MCB doesn't trip.
- **How:**
  1. If a room needs more socket outlets, add outlet points (*bocas*) to the circuit or a new circuit according to [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md).
  2. If the socket outlet has no earth, the fix is to run the PE (ELEC-DIA-17), not the adapter.
  3. Power strips: certified quality, with an earth terminal, switch and marked capacity (according to ELEC-NOR-14, the current regime requires a legend of 10 A maximum simultaneous load and an automatic limiter at 10 A on multi-outlet strips); only one per socket outlet; no heating loads.
  4. On construction sites, extension cords and portable panels follow [site-safety.md](site-safety.md) (site panel with RCD, suitable rubber cables; ELEC-DIA-31).
- **Verify:** sum of loads per power strip below the marked capacity; plug and power strip cool after a while in use.
- **Don't:** cut or bend the earth pin of a plug; use triple adapters; plug AC units, heaters, ovens or water heaters into power strips or extension cords; leave extension cords under rugs or pinched by doors.
- **Source:** risk of triple adapters and adapters (multiple contacts, loss of the PE): IRAM, "Triples y adaptadores inseguros" (unsafe triple adapters and adapters), *Ingeniería Eléctrica* 347. Regulatory chain of the ban on selling triple adapters and adapters: Res. SICyM 92/98 (APSE, "Marco normativo de fichas y tomacorrientes" (regulatory framework for plugs and socket outlets), *Ingeniería Eléctrica* 305), whose regime passed to Res. 169/2018, in turn repealed by Res. SIyC 16/2025 (art. 8, BO 2025-02-25). Current regime for plugs, socket outlets and power strips: Res. SIyC 236/2024, 237/2024 and Disp. DNRT 2/2025, according to ELEC-NOR-14. **Not verified in a primary source:** whether a legal ban on selling triple adapters and adapters is still in force today (Anexo II of Res. 16/2025 could not be read and the point could not be confirmed in Disp. 2/2025); that is why this rule is presented as a technical criterion, which does not change.
### ELEC-DIA-19: Meter (*medidor*) that registers with everything off
- **Rule:** before complaining that "the meter runs by itself", check in this order: loads that are still running, leakage in circuits without an RCD (*disyuntor* / *diferencial*, "ID"), crossed meters or a connection to another unit (buildings), and only then the meter (ask the utility (*distribuidora*) to verify or calibrate it).
- **Why:** an earth leakage in a circuit protected by a 30 mA RCD cannot be large (it trips first): 30 mA × 220 V = 6.6 W. If the meter registers hundreds of watts, it is a real load: an electric water heater (*termotanque*) heating, a pump that starts because of a water leak (ELEC-DIA-29), a fridge or freezer, heating, a circuit without an RCD with an outright leakage, or a neighbor hooked onto your meter through a connection error in the meter cabinet.
- **How:**
  1. Switch off and unplug everything you can (including the water heater and the pump). Look at the meter: an electronic meter has an LED that flashes once per pulse; the constant (imp/kWh) is on the nameplate.
     *Worked example:* constant C = 1000 imp/kWh; you count n = 10 flashes in t = 60 s. P = 3600 × n / (C × t) = 3600 × 10 / (1000 × 60) = 0.6 kW = 600 W.
  2. **Crossed meters (buildings), first check:** with loads switched on in your unit, switch off the unit's incoming switch and verify that the meter that stops flashing is the one with your supply number (shown on the bill and on the meter). If a different one stops, the meters are crossed: complaint to the utility and a check with the building management. The meter room is a common area: the switching is done by the electrician or the person in charge (*encargado*).
  3. Switch off the unit's incoming switch (in buildings it is usually in the meter room, next to the meter; the main switch of the apartment's panel leaves out the section of line that runs up): if the meter keeps registering, the load is between the meter and that incoming switch, or belongs to another unit connected to your meter, or the meter is faulty. Don't touch anything there: complaint to the utility and, in buildings, inspection of the meter cabinet by an electrician together with the building management.
  4. If it stops with the incoming switch off, switch the MCBs (*térmicas*) on one at a time and see which one makes the meter register. On that circuit, look for the load or do an insulation test (ELEC-DIA-4).
  5. Additional check: with the unit unoccupied and the incoming switch off, verify that there is no voltage at any socket outlet (measured by the electrician). If there is, the unit is (also) supplied from another meter. This test alone does not detect the crossed meters of step 2.
  6. Billing complaint: first to the utility, with a complaint number; while it is being processed, pay an amount equal to the last undisputed consumption.
- **Verify:** record of the readings and tests (time, state of each switch, flashes counted, which meter stopped).
- **Don't:** open or tamper with the meter or its seals; bypass the meter "to test"; leave the water heater and pump on during the test and conclude the meter is wrong.
- **Source:** ENReGE (formerly ENRE), frequently asked questions (prior complaint to the utility; payment of the last billed and undisputed consumption; actual readings, estimation at most once a year); user-utility responsibility boundary: Res. ENRE 95/2021 (art. 5 inc. d Reglamento de Suministro (supply regulations)). Power-from-pulses formula: direct calculation with the nameplate constant. Location of the unit's main panel next to the meter: [buildings.md](buildings.md) ELEC-EDI-6. **Not verified in a primary source:** current procedure and cost of a meter calibration test at the user's request at Edenor/Edesur and at provincial utilities; validity of the frequently asked questions after the RTI 2025-2030.

### ELEC-DIA-20: Power cut and restoration: protect the fridge, AC and electronics
- **Rule:** when the power goes out, unplug or switch off sensitive and compressor equipment (fridge, freezer, air conditioner (AC), pumps) and wait a few minutes after it comes back before reconnecting them. Where cuts and fluctuations are frequent, install voltage protectors (over/undervoltage relays, with reconnection delay) for compressor equipment and an SPD (*DPS*) in the panel.
- **Why:** when power comes back there can be overvoltages, undervoltages and repeated cuts within seconds. A compressor that restarts while the gas pressure is still high starts under strain and overheats. The voltage protector disconnects outside a range and reconnects with a delay.
- **How:**
  1. A plug-in voltage protector for each fridge/freezer, or a modular one in the panel for the AC or for the whole unit (ELEC-PRO-19). Choose by the equipment's current and the cut-off range. An Argentine manufacturer publishes reconnection when the voltage is back between 176 V and 250 V for three minutes (see the data sheet of the specific model).
  2. SPD in the panel against transient overvoltages (see [protection.md](protection.md)). The voltage protector and the SPD do different things: one disconnects on sustained abnormal voltage, the other diverts microsecond surges.
  3. In buildings, if equipment burns out in several units during a cut or restoration, think of a broken neutral (ELEC-DIA-14).
  4. If something was damaged: keep the equipment, get a technical report and file a claim with the utility (damage from voltage fluctuations or abrupt restorations).
- **Verify:** the protector disconnects and reconnects according to its data sheet (indicator lights); the SPD shows good status.
- **Don't:** believe that a power strip "with protection" replaces the voltage protector; plug everything in at once as soon as the power comes back; bypass a protector that "cuts too often" without measuring the voltage (ELEC-DIA-13).
- **Source:** damage from "oscilaciones de tensión, reposiciones abruptas de suministro y/u otras deficiencias del servicio" (voltage fluctuations, abrupt supply restorations and/or other service deficiencies): argentina.gob.ar (ENReGE, formerly ENRE), note on damage claims (2023-08-28). Protectors with delayed reconnection: RBC Sitel, *Ingeniería Eléctrica* 298 (May 2015) and 314 (range 176-250 V and three minutes). **Not verified in a primary source:** range and delay of other manufacturers (they vary; see data sheet).

### ELEC-DIA-21: Adding an air conditioner (*aire acondicionado*)
- **Rule:** recommended: a fixed AC goes on its own circuit (TUE with a single unit, or single-load ACU, according to AEA 90364-7-771 and the design; ELEC-ESP-11), with MCB and cross-section calculated for the nameplate current and the starting current, protected by a 30 mA RCD (if it is an inverter, type A at least, or type F if the manufacturer asks for it; ELEC-ESP-2), with PE. At a minimum, never on a general socket outlet circuit or on a power strip. Beforehand, verify that the main line or sub-main that supplies the unit, the panel and the capacity of the service connection can take it.
- **Why:** hanging the AC off a general socket outlet circuit is the most common cause of MCBs tripping in summer, melted socket outlets and voltage drops that make the lights flicker when the compressor starts.
- **How:**
  1. Read the equipment's rated (and maximum) current on the nameplate. Approximate current: I = P / (U × cos φ); manufacturers state it.
     *Worked example:* unit with 1100 W of electrical consumption and cos φ = 0.9 (the one in ELEC-ESP-10 and ELEC-CIR-9): I = 1100 / (220 × 0.9) ≈ 5.6 A. Added to a socket outlet circuit that already carries its usual 10 A, it reaches 15.6 A: at the limit of a 16 A MCB in summer. That is why it gets its own circuit.
  2. Choose cross-section, MCB and curve with the calculator (`gateway electricidad`) and [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md) / [protection.md](protection.md) / [special-installations.md](special-installations.md) ELEC-ESP-10 and ELEC-ESP-11.
  3. Check the unit's total demand against the cross-section of the main line or sub-main that supplies it (depending on where the main panel is, see [buildings.md](buildings.md)) and against the capacity of the service connection and the tariff category (the full chain is in [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md) ELEC-CIR-25; ELEC-DIA-23 does the same calculation for a cooktop); if it is not enough, upgrade first.
  4. Outdoor unit on a balcony or facade: wiring system and box with suitable IP rating; the outdoor connection box is a classic point of leakage when it rains (ELEC-DIA-4).
- **Verify:** current measured with a clamp meter in steady state below the MCB's In; voltage at the equipment terminals during starting within what the manufacturer requires; RCD tested.
- **Don't:** plug the AC into a power strip or extension cord; fit a 20 A socket outlet on a general socket outlet circuit; leave the outdoor unit without PE.
- **Source:** special-use and single-load circuits: AEA 90364-7-771 Ed. 2006 (see [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md)); own circuit for the AC as this guide's criterion: [special-installations.md](special-installations.md) ELEC-ESP-11. Type A or F RCD for electronics with a DC component and variable-speed drives: Schneider Electric, technical blog (Jun 2019); IEC 62423; ELEC-PRO-9. **Not verified in a primary source:** an AEA clause requiring a dedicated circuit for the AC (the own circuit is a technical recommendation); a regulatory requirement for type A or F RCDs for inverter equipment in Argentina (it is a manufacturers' recommendation; check the equipment manual).

### ELEC-DIA-22: Adding an electric water heater (*termotanque*)
- **Rule:** recommended: the electric water heater goes on its own circuit or with a fixed connection (at a minimum, never on a shared general socket outlet or on a power strip), with the circuit's PE connected to the equipment's earth terminal, protected by a 30 mA RCD, and with cross-section and MCB for a resistive load that runs for hours on end. Metal pipes are connected to the earth bar through the equipotential bonding; they are not used as a protective conductor.
- **Why:** it is a continuous load (typically 1500 to 2000 W according to the nameplate, that is 7 to 9 A at 220 V) that heats socket outlets and plugs if it is plugged into a general socket outlet. A cracked heating element puts voltage on the water and the tap: without PE and without RCD it is the "kick in the shower" (ELEC-DIA-16).
- **How:**
  1. Current: I = P / U. *Example:* 2000 W / 220 V = 9.1 A, several hours a day.
  2. Circuit according to [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md) and [special-installations.md](special-installations.md) ELEC-ESP-6; equipment PE terminal connected to the circuit's protective conductor.
  3. Equipotential bonding of the metal pipes in the bathroom and kitchen according to [earthing.md](earthing.md) (equipotential bonding, not PE).
  4. If the RCD trips when the water heater heats: the element or thermostat has a leakage; replace it (ELEC-DIA-3).
- **Verify:** PE continuity up to the enclosure; equipment insulation (under lockout/tagout and with the equipment full) ≥ 1 MΩ at 500 V; RCD that does not trip in normal operation but does trip with a tester.
- **Don't:** plug it into a power strip; leave it without PE "because the RCD protects"; use the water pipe as earth; install it with the element dry (it burns out; follow the manual).
- **Source:** RCD, PE and TT system: AEA 90364-7-771 Ed. 2006 (see [protection.md](protection.md), [earthing.md](earthing.md) ELEC-PAT-8); own circuit or TUE: ELEC-ESP-6. **Not verified in a primary source:** an AEA clause requiring a dedicated circuit (it is a technical recommendation); typical market power ratings (taken as an example; use the nameplate).

### ELEC-DIA-23: Adding an induction cooker or cooktop (*anafe*)
- **Rule:** before installing an induction cooktop, check its maximum nameplate power against the unit's main line or sub-main, the panel (main switch and RCD) and the capacity of the service connection and the tariff category of a single-phase supply; give it its own circuit with suitable cross-section, MCB and RCD (type A recommended), with PE; and, if the equipment allows it, limit its maximum power.
- **Why:** 4-zone cooktops usually have maximum powers of around 7 kW: at 220 V that is more than 30 A, twice a socket outlet circuit. Added to an AC and a water heater it can exceed the capacity of an apartment's line.
  *Worked example:* cooktop 7400 W max.: I = 7400 / 220 = 33.6 A. With an AC of 5.3 A and a water heater of 9.1 A running at the same time: 33.6 + 5.3 + 9.1 = 48 A. With a 40 A main switch that is 48 / 40 = 1.2 × In: it is in the zone where the MCB can take a long time to trip or not trip at all (between 1.13 and 1.45 × In), while the line overheats. This is an unacceptable situation even if it doesn't trip. Also, 48 A × 220 V ≈ 10.6 kW: at Edenor/Edesur that already exceeds the limit of the small-demand residential category (T1, up to 10 kW) and would force a change of category and, in practice, a move to three-phase. The sensible thing is to limit the cooktop (many models let you configure the maximum power, see the manual) or to resize with a design.
- **How:**
  1. Read the nameplate and manual: maximum power, possibility of limiting the power, connection type, protection recommended by the manufacturer.
  2. Check the simultaneous demand and the cross-section of the main line or sub-main with the calculator; ask the utility about the capacity of the service connection, the tariff category and whether you must move to three-phase (in the rest of the country, whatever the utility sets).
  3. If the main switch is upsized, also check the RCD: its rated current must be ≥ that of the upstream overcurrent device (or ≥ the sum of the MCBs it supplies); a 2 × 40 A RCD is no good behind a 50 A main switch ([protection.md](protection.md) ELEC-PRO-10, [panels.md](panels.md)).
  4. Own circuit, fixed connection or a socket outlet suitable for the current (above 20 A, fixed connection: ELEC-ESP-12); 30 mA RCD, preferably type A.
  5. In buildings, upgrading one unit affects its line, the meter cabinet and the building's demand: see [buildings.md](buildings.md).
- **Verify:** current measured with all zones at maximum within the circuit's In; simultaneous demand of the unit below the main switch's In; RCD In ≥ main switch In; voltage at the cooktop terminals without drops beyond the design; RCD tested.
- **Don't:** connect it to an existing socket outlet circuit or with a 10 A plug; upsize the unit's main switch without checking the line and the RCD; use a type AC RCD if the manufacturer asks for type A; count on "if it goes over, it trips".
- **Source:** usual recommendation of type A or super-immunized type A RCDs for electronic kitchen equipment: Schneider Electric, "El interruptor diferencial de la A a la B y la F" (the RCD from A to B and F) (Jun 2019). Circuits and demand: AEA 90364-7-771 Ed. 2006 (see [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md)). Zone 1.13-1.45 × In: IEC 60898-1. RCD In ≥ upstream protection: AEA 90364-7-770 Ed. 2017, 770.15.2.2.5, according to ELEC-PRO-10. Category T1 up to 10 kW and T2 above 10 kW (three-phase) in Edenor schemes: [buildings.md](buildings.md) ELEC-EDI-3. **Not verified in a primary source:** typical maximum power of cooktops on the Argentine market (it is an example; use the nameplate); the current T1 category limit in each utility's 2025-2030 tariff schedule.

### ELEC-DIA-24: "Me quedé sin luz y los vecinos tienen" (my power is out and the neighbors have it)
- **Rule:** before calling anyone, separate in this order: (1) one of your own protective devices off (RCD, MCB or the incoming switch next to the meter); (2) the meter without voltage (display off); (3) several units without power and others with power. If it is (2) or (3), it belongs to the utility: emergency complaint with a number, and don't touch anything upstream of the first isolating device.
- **Why:** in a building the units are spread across the three phases. If the NH fuse of one phase blows in the utility service box (*caja de toma*), or one phase of the network fails, all units on that phase lose power and the rest keep their supply: "the neighbor has power". The NH fuses and the utility service box belong to the utility and are sealed; replacing them is tampering with someone else's installation and exposes you to an arc with the energy of the network.
- **How:**
  1. Look at your panel: RCD or MCBs off? In buildings, the unit's incoming switch, in the meter room? If a protective device is off, go to ELEC-DIA-3 or ELEC-DIA-10 before switching it back on.
  2. Look at the meter without opening anything: an electronic meter has a display or LED. Display off = no voltage reaching the meter.
  3. Ask which units have power. If the ones without power are a group (for example, a floor or a "column" of apartments sharing a phase), it is usually an NH fuse on that phase or the network: complaint.
  4. In a three-phase house, if part of the house works and part doesn't, or the lights in one area are dim, suspect a missing phase (ELEC-DIA-28).
  5. Complaint: through the utility's emergency channel (phone, app or web), ask for a complaint number and write it down with the date and time. Electro-dependent users (who depend on powered medical equipment): priority channel (ELEC-NOR-19).
  6. Meanwhile: unplug sensitive equipment and compressors (ELEC-DIA-20).
- **Verify:** complaint number written down; when service returns, normal voltage at the incoming supply (ELEC-DIA-13).
- **Don't:** open the utility service box or replace an NH fuse; bypass or open the meter; "borrow power from the neighbor" with an extension cord connected to your installation (it overloads their line and can backfeed voltage into your service drop); connect a generator without a changeover switch (ELEC-DIA-30).
- **Source:** the utility's NH fuses, which are not touched: [protection.md](protection.md) ELEC-PRO-20 and [buildings.md](buildings.md) ELEC-EDI-5; prohibition on tampering with the utility's installation between the connection and the first isolating device: Reglamento de Suministro ENRE, user obligations inc. e), according to ELEC-NOR-15. Distribution of units across phases: ELEC-EDI-10. **Not verified in a primary source:** current emergency channels of each utility and restoration deadlines (technical service quality, Subanexo 4).
### ELEC-DIA-25: LEDs that stay dimly lit or flicker with the switch off
- **Rule:** an LED lamp that keeps a faint glow or flashes with the switch off is diagnosed in this order: (1) a switch that breaks the neutral (dangerous: ELEC-DIA-15); (2) a switch with a pilot light; (3) a motion sensor, timer or two-wire dimmer (no neutral) that powers itself through the lamp; (4) capacitive coupling between the phase and a long switched return in the same conduit (staircase two-way circuits, long hallways). Correct the cause; don't "get used to it".
- **Why:** an LED driver turns on with very little current: with a few tenths of a milliampere it charges its capacitor and flashes. An incandescent lamp didn't show it. A switch with a pilot light lets a small current through the lamp to light its little indicator; a two-wire sensor or dimmer does the same to power itself.
  *Worked example (capacitive coupling):* phase and switched return run together for 30 m in the same conduit, with a capacitance between conductors of around 100 pF/m: C = 30 × 100 pF = 3 nF. Reactance at 50 Hz: Xc = 1 / (2π × 50 × 3 × 10⁻⁹) ≈ 1.06 MΩ. Current through the lamp with the switch open: I = 220 V / 1.06 MΩ ≈ 0.21 mA. That is enough for many drivers to glow or flicker.
- **How:**
  1. Under lockout/tagout, verify that the switch breaks the phase (ELEC-DIA-15, ELEC-MED-7). If it breaks the neutral, correct that first: it is the dangerous case.
  2. If the switch has a pilot light, try one without it.
  3. Sensors, timers and dimmers: use models with a neutral (three-wire) or LED-compatible ones, and dimmable lamps; if the manufacturer says so, fit in the luminaire the load accessory or "bypass" it supplies.
  4. Long switched returns: a switch that breaks both conductors (two-pole), a relay, or a push button with an impulse relay, or the load accessory from point 3; separate the return from other phases if it shares a conduit with other circuits.
  5. Try another lamp of another brand: some drivers are more sensitive than others.
- **Verify:** with the switch open, the lamp fully off and the switch breaking the phase.
- **Don't:** leave a switch that breaks the neutral "because the light goes off anyway"; "solve it" by connecting the switched return to the PE or the neutral; confuse this with a multimeter's phantom voltage (ELEC-DIA-26).
- **Source:** physics of capacitive coupling (Xc = 1 / 2πfC); phase breaking in switches: AEA 90364-7-771 Ed. 2006 (see ELEC-DIA-15). **Not verified in a primary source:** the capacitance per meter between conductors in a conduit (100 pF/m is a typical order of magnitude for the example; it depends on the cable and the routing) and the minimum turn-on current of each driver; load accessories are solutions from sensor and dimmer manufacturers (see the model's manual).

### ELEC-DIA-26: Phantom voltage (*tensión fantasma*): the multimeter shows voltage on a cable that has none
- **Rule:** a high-impedance digital multimeter can show tens of volts (typically between 40 and 150 V) on a disconnected conductor or an open switched return that runs next to a live conductor. Before drawing conclusions, confirm with a two-pole voltage tester (IEC 61243-3) or with the multimeter's LoZ (low impedance) function.
- **Why:** the input of an ordinary multimeter is about 10 MΩ: the small current that arrives by capacitive coupling (ELEC-DIA-25) is enough to show voltage. With the LoZ function, with an input of about 3 kΩ, that coupled voltage collapses and only a real voltage remains. The opposite mistake happens too: a broken neutral fed back through a load does have real voltage, and LoZ confirms it.
  *Worked example:* LoZ of 3 kΩ measured between phase and PE downstream of an RCD (*disyuntor* / *diferencial*, "ID"): I = 220 V / 3000 Ω ≈ 73 mA, more than the RCD's 30 mA: it can trip it. That is why, with LoZ, measure between phase and neutral; between phase and PE use the normal input or a tester that does not trip the RCD.
- **How:**
  1. If the reading on a "disconnected" conductor is odd (for example 60 V), repeat it with LoZ or with a two-pole voltage tester. If it drops to almost zero, it was phantom voltage.
  2. To verify absence of voltage before working, use a two-pole voltage tester or a multimeter checked before and after on a known source (ELEC-DIA-1).
  3. Beware of the non-contact voltage tester (*buscapolo*): it can also light up by coupling, and it does not prove absence of voltage.
- **Verify:** every reading that leads to a decision (cable live or dead, polarity) confirmed with a second method.
- **Don't:** conclude "there is a leakage" or "the neutral is live" from an unconfirmed high-impedance reading; measure with LoZ between phase and PE on circuits with an RCD; touch a conductor because the multimeter "showed little".
- **Source:** Fluke, "Dual impedance digital multimeters" (phantom voltage appears between energized circuits and nearby de-energized wiring; the LoZ function has an input impedance "on the order of three kilohms"); two-pole voltage tester IEC 61243-3: [site-safety.md](site-safety.md) ELEC-SEG-13. The LoZ current through an RCD is an Ohm's law calculation. **Not verified in a primary source:** the typical range of 40 to 150 V (it depends on the length of the run and on the instrument) and the input impedance of each model (see the manual).

### ELEC-DIA-27: Single-pole MCBs (*térmicas*), fuses in the neutral and switches that break only the neutral
- **Rule:** in existing installations with single-pole MCBs, "1P+N" devices, fuses on a single line or in the neutral, or switches that break only the neutral, assume that **the neutral of the "switched-off" circuits is still connected** (and can be live) and adapt the diagnosis and the lockout/tagout. Record them as defects to correct: in single-phase, all protective devices must be two-pole.
- **Why:** switching off a single-pole MCB leaves the neutral joined to the busbar and to the network. With a broken or shared neutral, that conductor can be at phase potential. A fuse in the neutral is worse: if it blows, everything is "off" but with phase voltage on all conductors, including the neutral. For diagnosis, the method of switching MCBs on one at a time fails: a neutral-earth fault on circuit Y makes the RCD trip when you load circuit X.
- **How:**
  1. Lock out by opening the two-pole main switch and verifying absence of voltage on the neutral as well (ELEC-DIA-1). If the main switch is also single-pole, treat the neutral as live: there is no safe way to isolate it without replacing the incoming switch, and replacing it requires a disconnection by the utility (*distribuidora*) (ELEC-NOR-15).
  2. To locate an N-PE fault: with the two-pole main switch open, disconnect the neutrals from the busbar and measure N-PE circuit by circuit (ELEC-DIA-4, ELEC-DIA-5).
  3. To locate an overload or a short circuit with single-pole MCBs, the method of ELEC-DIA-2 still works (the phase is broken), but not for leakages.
  4. If there are fuses or a switch in the neutral, take them out of service with a design: two-pole protection in their place (ELEC-DIA-17).
- **Verify:** at the end, all incoming switches and MCBs of single-phase circuits two-pole, with no fuses or devices in the neutral only.
- **Don't:** lock out with a single-pole MCB; fit or leave a fuse or a switch in the neutral only; deduce the leaking circuit by switching single-pole MCBs on one at a time.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770 Ed. 2017, 770.16.5.1 (incoming switches isolate the neutral; single-pole, 1P+N and unprotected-neutral PIA are prohibited in single-phase), according to [panels.md](panels.md) ELEC-TAB-9 and [protection.md](protection.md) ELEC-PRO-1; neutral never isolated or protected alone: ELEC-PRO-15 and ELEC-EDI-11; terminals of the first isolating device always live: ELEC-NOR-15.

### ELEC-DIA-28: Three-phase: missing phase or reversed sequence
- **Rule:** in three-phase panels or supplies (common services of a building, three-phase houses or shops), if a motor hums and doesn't start, overheats or trips its motor protection circuit breaker (*guardamotor*), or part of the installation loses power, measure the three voltages before touching the motor. If a pump or motor turns backwards, it is a reversed sequence. Every three-phase motor has phase-loss protection.
- **Why:** with a phase lost (blown NH fuse, loose terminal, network fault) a stopped motor doesn't start and overheats; one that was running keeps turning as single-phase, with more current in the other two phases, and burns out if nothing disconnects it. A running motor also "regenerates" voltage on the missing phase: the multimeter can show voltage on that phase even though it is not healthy. The sequence can change after work on the network or the service drop (*acometida*); a centrifugal pump turning backwards gives little flow and is noisy, and in an elevator its controller detects it.
- **How:**
  1. Trained personnel (CAT IV at the main panel incomer, CAT III in sub-panels): measure L1-L2, L2-L3 and L3-L1 (≈ 380 V; tolerance 349.6 to 410.4 V in the Edenor area) and each L-N (≈ 220 V; 202.4 to 237.6 V). Do it with the motor disconnected (its outgoing circuit locked out) so as not to confuse regenerated voltage with a healthy phase.
  2. If a phase is missing at the main panel incomer, it belongs to the utility (ELEC-DIA-24). If it is missing after a user device, it is a loose terminal or a damaged pole (ELEC-DIA-11): check it under lockout/tagout.
  3. Sequence: with a phase rotation tester (IEC 61557-7) at the motor panel. Correct it by swapping two phases at the outgoing terminals of the motor panel, under lockout/tagout; never at the meter or the service drop. In elevators, the elevator maintenance contractor does it.
  4. Protection: phase-loss and phase-sequence relay acting on the contactor, plus a motor protection circuit breaker set to the nameplate current ([special-installations.md](special-installations.md) ELEC-ESP-14).
- **Verify:** three voltages within tolerance; motor that starts with similar currents in the three phases, not higher than the nameplate current; correct rotation; phase-loss relay tested.
- **Don't:** reset the motor protection circuit breaker again and again; bypass the phase-loss relay; swap phases in the utility service box (*caja de toma*) or the meter; measure the "missing" phase with the motor connected and conclude it is there.
- **Source:** phase-loss protection and motor switching: AEA 90364-7-770 Ed. 2017, 770.13.3, according to ELEC-ESP-14; in buildings, AEA 90364-7-772 Ed. 2019 for common services. Phase rotation tester IEC 61557-7: ELEC-MED-7. Tolerances: Subanexo 4 Edenor 2025-2030 (table above). Voltage regenerated by a running motor with an open phase: known behavior of induction machines; its value is **not verified in a primary source** (it depends on the motor and the load).

### ELEC-DIA-29: Single-phase pump or motor that hums, doesn't start or starts every little while
- **Rule:** a single-phase pump (lift or booster pump) or motor that hums and doesn't start is checked in this order: capacitor, seized shaft or impeller, low voltage at starting, open thermal protector. A booster pump that keeps starting with all taps closed indicates a water leak or a pressure tank without precharge: fix that, not the electrical side.
- **Why:** a single-phase motor needs the capacitor to start; with a dried-out capacitor it hums, heats up and ends up tripping the thermal protection. A pump that has been stopped for a long time can seize from scale. With low voltage (long or thin cable) it can't manage to start. The motor's thermal protector resets by itself as it cools, so the fault looks intermittent. Short cycling wears the motor, the pressure switch and the contactor, consumes energy (ELEC-DIA-19) and causes tripping.
- **How:**
  1. If the booster pump starts by itself every few minutes: close everything and see whether the water meter advances or whether a cistern or float valve is leaking; check the pressure tank precharge according to the manufacturer.
  2. Lock out, verify absence of voltage and **discharge the capacitor with a resistor** (not with the screwdriver). Measure its capacitance with a capacitance meter and compare with the µF and the voltage on the label; replace it with one of the same value and voltage.
  3. With the motor locked out, check that the shaft turns freely by hand.
  4. Measure the voltage at the terminals during starting and the running current with a clamp meter (trained personnel), and compare with the nameplate; check the voltage drop at starting ([special-installations.md](special-installations.md) ELEC-ESP-15).
  5. Measure the insulation of the winding to the frame (under lockout/tagout, ELEC-DIA-4) if the RCD trips.
- **Verify:** clean start, running current ≤ nameplate, no short cycling with the taps closed, frame connected to PE, RCD tested.
- **Don't:** replace a capacitor with one of a different value "that worked"; measure the capacitor without discharging it; bypass the thermal protector, the pressure switch or the float switch; free the shaft with the power on.
- **Source:** [special-installations.md](special-installations.md) ELEC-ESP-14 and ELEC-ESP-15 (AEA 90364-7-770 Ed. 2017, 770.13.3 and 770.15.6: drop ≤ 5 % in normal operation and ≤ 15 % at starting); building pumps: [buildings.md](buildings.md) ELEC-EDI-14 (AEA 90364-7-772 Ed. 2019). **Not verified in a primary source:** the recommended precharge of each pressure tank (see the manufacturer's manual).

### ELEC-DIA-30: Generator (*grupo electrógeno*) connected without a changeover switch
- **Rule:** if an installation has a generator (or one will be used during a long outage), verify that it supplies only through a changeover switch or source transfer switch that breaks the network phase and neutral before the generator is connected. A generator plugged into a socket outlet of the house, with a cable with two male plugs, or connected by switching off only the main MCB, is a serious fault: disconnect it until it is corrected.
- **Why:** without a changeover switch, the generator energizes the service drop and, through the utility's transformer, the medium-voltage network. *Worked example:* a generator delivering 380 V between phases on the low-voltage side of a 13.2 kV / 0.4 kV transformer appears on the other side multiplied by the ratio: 380 × 13,200 / 400 ≈ 12,500 V, on a line that the utility's worker believes is disconnected. When the network comes back, generator and network end up in parallel and the generator is destroyed. With the generator's neutral floating, the RCD may also fail to operate.
- **How:**
  1. Check: three-position changeover switch (network, 0, generator) that breaks phase and neutral, or an automatic one with interlock; wall socket outlet for the generator that only feeds the changeover switch.
  2. If there is no changeover switch: generator only for loads plugged directly into it, with suitable extension cords, until a qualified installer fits the changeover switch ([special-installations.md](special-installations.md) ELEC-ESP-25).
  3. With the generator running and the network disconnected, test the RCD with an instrument: if it doesn't trip, check the reference of the generator's neutral to earth (ELEC-ESP-26).
  4. If the generator burned out or everything tripped when the network came back, there was a parallel connection: the changeover switch is missing or faulty.
- **Verify:** with the changeover switch on "generator", no generator voltage at the network-side terminals (measured with the network main switch open); RCD that trips with the generator.
- **Don't:** use the "suicide cord" with two male plugs; switch off only the main MCB instead of using a changeover switch; use a changeover switch that breaks only the phase; run the generator in an enclosed space (carbon monoxide).
- **Source:** [site-safety.md](site-safety.md) ELEC-SEG-21 (Manual TST SRT 2023 (dead work manual), rule 2: prevent backfeed; Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI); [special-installations.md](special-installations.md) ELEC-ESP-25 and ELEC-ESP-26 (AEA 90364-5-55, clause 551, existence confirmed by AEA 90364-7-722:2024). Transformation ratio: calculation; 13.2 kV is a common MV voltage in the AMBA (Buenos Aires metropolitan area). **Not verified in a primary source:** the exact text of clause 551 of the AEA.
### ELEC-DIA-31: Construction site panel (*tablero de obra*) and temporary installation: RCD (*disyuntor*, ID) trips with power tools
- **Rule:** on a construction site, if the site panel's RCD (*disyuntor* / *diferencial*, ID) trips when tools are used or when it rains, find the tool, extension cord (*prolongación*) or socket outlet with leakage using the same one-at-a-time method (ELEC-DIA-2). The leaking tool or extension cord is taken out of service. Don't bypass the site panel's RCD and don't replace it with a 300 mA one.
- **Why:** on a site there is water, mud, conductive dust inside motors (angle grinders, rotary hammers), wet concrete mixers, extension cords run over by wheelbarrows, taped joints lying on the ground and socket outlets without covers. Many tools hanging off a single RCD add up their leakage (ELEC-DIA-6). It is the panel that sees the most accidents.
- **How:**
  1. Unplug everything; switch the RCD back on; plug in one tool and one extension cord (uncoiled) at a time. The one that makes it trip stays out.
  2. Extension cords: visual inspection (cuts, crushing, joints, broken plugs); if in doubt, insulation test them disconnected, between conductors and to PE.
  3. Class I tools: continuity of the PE from the plug to the housing; if it trips the RCD, send it to service. Prefer Class II tools.
  4. If it trips in the rain with nothing plugged in: water in the panel, in socket outlets without covers or in the supply cable (ELEC-DIA-4).
  5. Split the socket-outlet circuits among more than one RCD if there are many tools; type A if there are tools with electronic speed control.
- **Verify:** test button of each RCD at the start of the workday; panel closed, fixed and earthed; current earthing and continuity test report under Res. SRT 900/2015.
- **Don't:** bypass the RCD "porque salta con la hormigonera" ("because it trips with the concrete mixer"); extension cords made of parallel cable without earth or with taped joints; panels on the ground or left open; stripped wires stuck into the socket outlet.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 (construction industry safety regulation), art. 86 (protection by earthing of exposed conductive parts and automatic disconnection devices on site) and art. 87 (inspect before resetting a protection that operated for no known reason), per [panels.md](panels.md) ELEC-TAB-21 and [site-safety.md](site-safety.md) ELEC-SEG-14 to ELEC-SEG-16 and ELEC-SEG-22; Res. SRT 900/2015. **Not verified in a primary source:** the text of AEA 90364-7-704 Ed. 2026 (construction site panel).

### ELEC-DIA-32: Electric shower and instantaneous electric tankless heater (*calefón*): "patadas" (shocks), burnt terminals and cross-section
- **Rule:** an electric shower (*ducha eléctrica*) or an instantaneous electric tankless heater (*calefón*) gets a fixed connection, its own circuit with the cross-section its power requires, PE connected to the appliance's terminal and a 30 mA RCD (*disyuntor* / *diferencial*). If it "patea" (gives shocks), switch it off and don't use it until the fault is found (ELEC-DIA-16).
- **Why:** they are loads of 25 A or more for minutes at a time, in the wettest place in the house. In many showers the heating element is in contact with the water: without PE or RCD, a fault or a broken element puts the water stream at voltage. Burnt terminal blocks and plugs are the classic loose contact (ELEC-DIA-11) with high current.
  *Worked example:* 5500 W shower: I = 5500 / 220 = 25 A (6 mm² cable with a 32 A two-pole MCB (PIA), or whatever the manual says, ELEC-ESP-6). 7500 W instantaneous heater: I = 7500 / 220 ≈ 34.1 A: added to everything else, it can exceed the capacity of the unit's line (ELEC-DIA-23); many large models are three-phase.
- **How:**
  1. "Patada" (shock): switch off; check PE continuity up to the appliance terminal, that an RCD exists and that it passes its test, and the insulation of the appliance (locked out).
  2. Burnt terminals or melted plugs: locked out, replace the terminal block and the damaged section of cable; fixed connection, no plug; tighten to the manufacturer's torque.
  3. Check cross-section, MCB and voltage drop with the calculator and the manual; if the unit's line is not enough, upsize it or choose another appliance.
  4. Location and IP rating according to the bathroom zones ([special-installations.md](special-installations.md) ELEC-ESP-3 and ELEC-ESP-4).
- **Verify:** continuous PE up to the appliance, RCD that trips with a tester, terminals cool after a long use, measured current ≤ In of the circuit.
- **Don't:** install the shower without PE "porque es de plástico" ("because it's plastic"); supply it with 2.5 mm² or through a plug and socket outlet; use the pipes as earth; control it with an ordinary light switch.
- **Source:** [special-installations.md](special-installations.md) ELEC-ESP-6 (dedicated circuit, fixed connection, PE and RCD; AEA 90364-7-770 Ed. 2017, 770.6 and table 770.12.I) and ELEC-ESP-3/ELEC-ESP-4 (bathroom zones). **Not verified in a primary source:** typical ratings of showers and instantaneous heaters on the market (use the nameplate); cross-section and MCB per model (the manual governs).

### ELEC-DIA-33: Cables pierced by nails, wall plugs (*tarugos*) or screws, and damaged by rodents
- **Rule:** if an MCB (*térmica*) or the RCD starts tripping right after something is hung, drilled or screwed into a wall, first suspect a cable pierced at that point. Locate it by measuring section by section and replace the whole conductor between boxes: no joints inside the conduit.
- **Why:** conduits leave the boxes in straight vertical and horizontal runs; drilling directly above, below or beside a socket outlet or switch usually hits them. A wall plug or screw that pierces the insulation may not make contact right away and fail days later, or with humidity. In roof spaces, suspended ceilings and boxes without covers, rodents strip the cables.
- **How:**
  1. Ask what was done and where; identify the circuit with ELEC-DIA-2.
  2. Locked out, measure insulation L-N, L-PE and N-PE (with the neutral separated from the network, ELEC-DIA-4) and split in halves at the boxes until you find the section that includes the pierced point.
  3. With the circuit locked out, remove the nail or wall plug and rewire the whole section between the two boxes (joints go only in boxes, ELEC-CON-21). If the conduit got crushed, repair it.
  4. Rodents: look in ceilings, boxes without covers and runs without conduit; replace the damaged conductors, cover the boxes, run the cables in conduit and seal the access points.
  5. Next time: use a cable and metal detector before drilling, and don't drill in vertical or horizontal line with socket outlets and switches (ELEC-SEG-19).
- **Verify:** circuit insulation of the same order as the other circuits and ≥ 1 MΩ at 500 V; RCD and MCB that hold under load.
- **Don't:** leave the nail in "porque ya no salta" ("because it doesn't trip anymore"); tape the cable inside the wall or the conduit; rely only on the detector (it doesn't see everything).
- **Source:** joints only in boxes: [conductors-and-conduits.md](conductors-and-conduits.md) ELEC-CON-21; vertical and horizontal conduit routes and precautions when drilling: ELEC-SEG-19 and [conductors-and-conduits.md](conductors-and-conduits.md). Minimum insulation: ELEC-DIA-4. Rodent damage: trade practice.

### ELEC-DIA-34: "¿Pongo una térmica más grande?" ("Should I put in a bigger MCB?") or "¿puenteo el disyuntor hasta el lunes?" ("Should I bypass the RCD until Monday?"): no, and what to do today
- **Rule:** when someone asks to raise the rating of an MCB (*térmica*) "para que no salte" ("so it doesn't trip"), or to bypass, remove or "pasar por fuera" ("route around") the RCD (*disyuntor*, ID) even for a few hours, answer **no**, say why with the rule, and in the same message give the safe way out for today: keep everything that has no fault working and isolate the circuit or appliance that is faulty.
- **Why:** the MCB protects the **cable**, not the appliance: with a bigger one, the cable overheats inside the wall with nothing to cut it off (ELEC-PRO-6, ELEC-DIA-10). The RCD is the only thing that trips when a person touches a live part (ELEC-PRO-7); bypassing it "hasta el lunes" ("until Monday") leaves the house without that protection precisely while there is a known leakage. Rushing is when steps get skipped the most (ELEC-EQU-36).
- **How:**
  1. Let the calculator show why: `gateway electricidad coordinate --in 25 --section 2.5` (without `--ib` if the load is unknown) gives ❌ on In ≤ Iz and I2 ≤ 1.45 Iz. If the cross-section is unknown, say so and show the two likely cases (1.5 and 2.5 mm²).
  2. **What anyone can do, without opening covers** (owner or user): unplug everything; tell whether the MCB trips instantly (short circuit) or after a while (overload: add up what was switched on); for the RCD, switch all MCBs off, switch the RCD on and switch the MCBs on one at a time, then plug in the appliances one at a time (ELEC-DIA-2, ELEC-DIA-3; valid only with two-pole MCBs, ELEC-DIA-27); leave the faulty circuit off and the leaking appliance unplugged; don't reset more than twice in a row.
  3. **What is an electrician's job** (with the circuit locked out and verified dead, ELEC-SEG-1): open boxes, insulation test, separate neutrals, find the short circuit, replace the MCB with one suited to the cable or run a new circuit.
  4. If you don't know whether the person asking is an electrician or the owner, answer with the steps in 2 and ask; give the steps in 3 when they say they are in the trade, and always as dead work.
  5. What is realistic for today: everything healthy working, the faulty part disconnected and the people warned; don't promise "hoy queda todo andando" ("everything will be working today").
- **Verify:** the answer has no live step and no bypass; it has the safe way out and the missing questions (whether it trips instantly or after a while, what is plugged in, cable cross-section, whether they trip together).
- **Don't:** give the "solo por hoy" ("just for today"); suggest a 300 mA RCD, an MCB "un poco más grande" ("a bit bigger") or putting the kitchen "por fuera del disyuntor" ("outside the RCD"); send the owner to open boxes without a voltage tester.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.15.2.1 (Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz) and 770.14.2.3 (30 mA RCD on all final circuits), per ELEC-PRO-6 and ELEC-PRO-7; Decreto 911/96 art. 87 (don't reset a protection that operated without knowing the cause), per ELEC-DIA-31; ELEC-DIA-2, ELEC-DIA-3, ELEC-EQU-36. The split between what the owner does and what the electrician does is this guide's own criterion.

## Example case: building with ground floor and 4 floors, single-phase units
Building with single-phase 220 V supplies per unit spread across the three phases, a common meter cabinet on the
ground floor (with each unit's main panel next to its meter and a sub-main circuit that goes up the riser (*montante*)),
a three-phase common services panel with its own meter (elevator, pumps, landings; AEA 90364-7-772 Ed. 2019) and, in each
apartment (*depto*), a sub-panel (TS, *tablero seccional*) with: 2 × 40 A two-pole main switch, 2 × 40 A 30 mA RCD (*disyuntor* / *diferencial*), two-pole MCBs (*térmica*) for
lighting (10 A), general socket outlets (16 A) and a special circuit for the AC (20 A). The values are examples; the real panel
may differ (see [panels.md](panels.md), [buildings.md](buildings.md) and [reviewing-a-panel.md](reviewing-a-panel.md)).

## What this guide does not replace
- On-site diagnosis, repair and signing any certificate are done by an electrician, installer or professional qualified according to the jurisdiction and their professional council. This guide gives guidance; it does not authorize anyone to do live work or to work on panels without training.
- The AEA and IRAM regulations are paid, copyrighted documents: here their clauses are summarized and cited; if there is a difference, the current text governs.
- The utility (distribution company) decides on the service connection, the utility service box, the meter, the capacity of the connection, the tariff category and claims about voltage or damage; the regulator (in the Edenor/Edesur area, ENReGE, successor of ENRE; a provincial regulator elsewhere) decides on unresolved claims; the municipality and the owners' association decide on what concerns them in the building.
