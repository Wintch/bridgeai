# IRAM standards for LV electrical installations

Annotated catalog of the IRAM standards you will see printed on cables, MCBs (*térmicas*), socket outlets, conduits, boxes and
earth rods (*jabalinas*), or cited in a specification. For each one: exact number, current edition according to the IRAM catalog,
what it covers, what it is good for on site and what you must ask for or look at on the product. Each rule has an ID
(`ELEC-IRA-n`) so the agent, a review or a quote can cite it ("does not comply with ELEC-IRA-6").

Three things worth knowing before reading the tables:

1. **An IRAM standard is not mandatory by itself.** It becomes mandatory when a law, a technical regulation or the
   AEA Reglamentación (AEA wiring regulations) cites it. For installation materials, the one that governs today is
   the technical regulation of **Res. SIyC 236/2024**, whose Annex II (in the text set by **Res. SIyC
   26/2025**) assigns an IRAM or IEC standard to each product, and **Disp. DNRT 2/2025**, which sets how products
   are certified (ELEC-IRA-2). Some standards are required by the AEA even though the regulation does not cite
   them: for example, the IRAM 2071 socket-outlet format (ELEC-IRA-8).
2. **For MCBs and RCDs (*disyuntores* / *diferenciales*) there is no current IRAM standard**: they are certified directly against the IEC standard.
   The old IRAM 2169 (MCBs) and IRAM 2301 (RCDs) are **withdrawn**
   (ELEC-IRA-7). For contactors and fuses, very old IRAM standards are still published (IRAM 2240 from 1974 and
   the IRAM 2245 series from 1984), but the regulation does not cite them: it requires IEC 60947-4-1 / IEC 61095
   and IEC 60269, which IRAM has not adopted. Cable trays, enclosures and boxes for protective devices also go
   straight to IEC.
3. **IRAM standards are paid and copyrighted.** This document gives number, short title, edition and what the
   standard is for, not its content. To design or test you need the purchased standard in its current edition.

> How to read the "Edition" column: it is the edition number and the date from which that edition applies, as
> shown by the IRAM catalog consulted on 2026-11-15. An old standard (1980, 1993) is not necessarily
> repealed: if the catalog says "Publicada / Vigente" (published / current), it is still in force. Before citing
> it in a specification, check the catalog again (ELEC-IRA-19).

## Rules

### ELEC-IRA-1: Buy every electrical material with the standard printed on it and the conformity marking
- **Rule:** don't install an electrical installation material that does not show, on the product or on its primary
  packaging, the standard it complies with, the rated data and the **conformity marking**. Anything manufactured or
  imported from **2025-10-01** must carry the **new marking** ("AR" mark with a double tick plus a QR
  code). The **former SC mark** is only accepted on stock manufactured or imported before that date, and that stock
  is proven with the purchase invoice or the import papers. A product covered by Res. 236/2024 with
  neither of the two identifications cannot be sold.
- **Why:** the printed standard tells you which tests it passed (or claims it passed); the marking tells you that a
  certification body checked it and gives you a way (the QR code) to confirm it. If you sign a DCI (installation
  conformity declaration) or an installation certificate, you are declaring that the materials are certified ([regulations-and-permits.md](regulations-and-permits.md), ELEC-NOR-14).
- **How:**
  1. Look on the product for the standard (for example "IRAM NM 247-3", "IEC 60898-1", "IRAM NM 60884-1") and the
     rated data (cross-section and voltage on a cable; In, curve and breaking capacity on an MCB; In
     and IΔn on an RCD). The final table of this document says what to read on each product.
  2. Look for the conformity marking: the mark plus the QR code, on the product or, if it doesn't fit, on the primary packaging.
  3. If it has the old SC mark, ask the seller for the invoice or the customs clearance showing that it is stock from
     before 2025-10-01.
  4. Scan the QR code (ELEC-IRA-3).
- **Verify:** the QR code opens the Declaración Jurada de Conformidad (DJC, sworn declaration of conformity) and,
  for products with mandatory certification, the certificate; brand, model and standard on the certificate match
  what you have in your hand.
- **Don't:** accept "it's the same, but unbranded"; accept material the client brings without checking it; take a
  product as good because the box "says IRAM" (IRAM is the body that writes the standard and is also one of the
  certifiers; the word alone proves nothing); accept a new, recently manufactured product with the old SC mark.
- **Source:** Res. SIyC 237/2024, Annex, item 3.3 (the marking consists of a conformity mark and a QR code that
  leads to the DJC and the certificate; it goes in a visible place on the product or on its primary packaging); Res. SIyC
  26/2025, art. 4 (marking required from 2025-10-01); Disp. DNRT 2/2025, Annex, item 5.3; argentina.gob.ar,
  "Marcado de conformidad" (consulted 2026-11-15: from 2025-10-01 no certified product may be sold
  without identification; anything manufactured or imported before that date may carry the new marking or the
  former SC mark, "acreditando el stock antiguo con factura de compra o documentación de ingreso al país" (proving
  the old stock with the purchase invoice or the import documents); anything later without the new marking does
  not comply).

### ELEC-IRA-2: The current regime is Res. 236/2024 (Annex II as set by Res. 26/2025) with Disp. DNRT 2/2025; Res. 169/2018 is repealed
- **Rule:** when someone cites "Res. 169/2018" to you as the certification rule for electrical materials,
  correct them: it is **repealed**. For installation materials the governing text is the Reglamento Técnico de
  Materiales para la Construcción (technical regulation for construction materials, Res. SIyC 236/2024), with the
  **Annex II set by Res. SIyC 26/2025** (IF-2025-17295098-APN-DNRT#MEC,
  item 4) and with the requirements and procedure of Disp. DNRT 2/2025. For household appliances, lighting and
  power supplies, Res. 16/2025 applies. If a document cites items of the **original** 2024 Annex II (IF-2024-92414137),
  the numbering no longer matches: use the numbering in this document.
- **Why:** the specification, the DCI and market surveillance rely on the current regime. Certificates for
  installation materials issued under Res. 169/2018 had a transition of up to 12 months from
  2025-04-16 (Disp. 2/2025, art. 3), so they **have already expired** (around 2026-04-16). A "Res.
  169/2018" certificate that was not renewed is no good to you today.
- **How:** what the regime for electrical installation materials covers:
  - **Voltage:** materials that operate between 50 V and 1000 V AC, or between 75 V and 1500 V DC.
  - **Excluded:** those for exclusive use in vehicles and transport, in the oil, nuclear or aerospace industry,
    in explosive atmospheres or in medical uses, and **all materials above 63 A, except cables**
    (ELEC-IRA-25).
  - **Products** (current Annex II, item 4, summarized): rigid, pliable, flexible and buried insulating
    conduits; surface trunking (floor, wall, slotted wiring duct for enclosures, columns and floor boxes); steel conduits;
    insulated conduit for the meter pillar (*pilar*); conduit accessories; plastic and metal boxes; meter boxes; surface
    boxes for switches; IEC 60670 enclosures; envelopes (*envolturas*); enclosures (*gabinetes*); cable trays; cables
    (2178-1, 62266, 62267, NM 247-3, NM 247-5 cords, silicone, submersible pumps, rubber, hard-drawn copper);
    cable accessories (terminals, lugs, cable glands, insulating tapes, connectors, heat-shrink,
    terminal blocks, cord switches); manual switches, MCBs, RCDs with and without
    short-circuit protection, contactors, fixed and portable socket outlets, plugs, cable-plug-connector sets,
    LV circuit breakers, industrial connectors, electronic DIN-rail modules, SPDs (*DPS*), isolators, fuses,
    home automation, IEC 60320 connectors, timers, relays and their bases, panel push-buttons and indicators;
    meters; earth rods, exothermic welding and copper-clad steel conductors; capacitors; energy
    monitoring equipment; smoke detectors, alarms and devices for alarm systems.
  - **Dates:** the regulation is enforceable from 2024-12-09 (Annex IV of Res. 236/2024); the current Annex II
    applies from Res. 26/2025 (BO 2025-02-25); Disp. 2/2025 applies from 2025-04-16; the conformity
    marking is required from 2025-10-01.
  - The table of standards per product is further down, in each family.
- **Verify:** the product certificate mentions Res. 236/2024 or Disp. DNRT 2/2025 (or was issued
  after 2025-04-16) and has up-to-date surveillance (ELEC-IRA-4).
- **Don't:** accept a "Res. 169/2018" certificate as valid; confuse the regime for installation
  materials (Res. 236/2024) with the one for appliances (Res. 16/2025); cite in a specification the numbering of the
  original 2024 Annex II.
- **Source:** Res. SIyC 236/2024, arts. 1, 2, 6 and 10 and Annex IV; Res. SIyC 26/2025 (BO 2025-02-25), arts. 1 and 4,
  and its Annex IF-2025-17295098-APN-DNRT#MEC, item 4 (read in full); Disp. DNRT 2/2025, arts. 1, 3 and 4, and Annex,
  items 1 and 2; Res. 16/2025, art. 8 (repeals Res. 169/2018 of the former Secretaría de Comercio; article number
  from secondary sources) and art. 9 (transition for electrical equipment, not for installation
  materials). Secondary confirmation that Res. 236/2024 applies "modificada por la Res. 26/2025" (as amended by
  Res. 26/2025): CDA, "Reglamentos Técnicos Seguridad Eléctrica - Actualización febrero 2026". Consistent with [regulations-and-permits.md](regulations-and-permits.md), ELEC-NOR-14.
### ELEC-IRA-3: How to check that a certificate is genuine
- **Rule:** before accepting a batch or signing, check the certificate in at least two ways: the QR code on the
  product and the register of the certification body that issued it.
- **Why:** a mark can be printed and a PDF can be copied from another product. What is not so easy to fake
  is the match between the product, the certificate and the body's register.
- **How:**
  1. **QR code on the product:** it must lead to the DJC (sworn declaration of conformity) and the certificate.
     Check holder (manufacturer or importer), brand, model or family, standard, certification body, number and
     date of issue.
  2. **Body's register:** search for the certificate or the holder on the website of the body named on the
     certificate. IRAM has a public search tool, "Certificaciones otorgadas" (certifications granted) (iram.org.ar/certificaciones-otorgadas):
     choose the type "Productos" (products) and type at least 3 letters of the holder's company name. IRAM states
     that products with the Sello IRAM (IRAM mark) can also be searched by brand, model, product or standard. If
     you can't find another body's search tool, ask it for written validation.
  3. **Valid body:** the certificate must fall into one of the five cases the regulation accepts:
     1. a body accredited by the OAA for that scope;
     2. a foreign body accredited by a signatory of a multilateral recognition agreement that the OAA
        signed;
     3. a foreign body accredited by an accreditor that did not sign those agreements, with a recognition
        agreement with a national certification body accredited for that scope, ratified by the OAA;
     4. a global scheme named by the regulation: for these materials, **only the IECEE CB scheme**;
     5. a Mutual Recognition Agreement in force between Argentina and another country.
  4. **Doubt or complaint:** the DNRT answers technical queries through Trámites a Distancia (online procedures)
     (service "Realizar consultas técnicas sobre reglamentos técnicos" (make technical queries about technical
     regulations) on argentina.gob.ar).
- **Verify:** the exact model you bought appears on the certificate (not only "the brand"), the certificate
  is valid (ELEC-IRA-4) and the holder matches the manufacturer or importer on the packaging.
- **Don't:** settle for a photo of the certificate sent by the seller; accept a QR code that leads to the
  brand's home page and not to the certificate for that product.
- **Source:** Res. SIyC 237/2024, Annex, items 2.2 B (bodies notify the DNRT of certificates
  issued, suspended or cancelled), 3.2.2 subitems 1 to 5 and 3.3; Disp. DNRT 2/2025, Annex, item 5.2 (accepts
  subitems 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5; of subitem 4 only the IECEE CB scheme); IRAM, page "Certificaciones otorgadas"
  (consulted 2026-11-15); argentina.gob.ar, technical queries service. The wording of subitems 1 to 3 is a
  summary by this guide. **Not verified in a primary source:** the current list of certification bodies
  accredited by the OAA for electrical materials (the OAA website did not respond on 2026-11-15). Bodies that
  often appear on the Argentine market (IRAM, TÜV Rheinland, Lenor, Bureau Veritas, IQC and others): confirm the
  scope of each one with the OAA before taking it as valid.

### ELEC-IRA-4: A valid certificate means less than 2 years old or surveillance up to date
- **Rule:** a product certificate under Disp. 2/2025 is valid for **2 years from its original issue**. After that
  period it only remains valid with the body's **surveillance record** (*constancia de vigilancia*). For manual
  switches, MCBs (*térmicas*), RCDs (*disyuntores* / *diferenciales*), LV circuit breakers, SPDs, isolators and cables, the certificate must be
  **scheme 1b or 5**.
- **Why:** schemes 1b (testing of a batch) and 5 (type testing plus factory and market surveillance)
  are the ones Disp. 2/2025 reserves for products where a failure kills or starts a fire. An old type
  certificate without surveillance does not prove that what is manufactured today is still the same.
- **How:** on the certificate look for the date of issue, the certification scheme (according to ISO/IEC 17067) and,
  if it is more than 2 years old, the latest surveillance record.
- **Verify:** on an MCB, an RCD or a cable, the certificate says "esquema 5" or "1b" (with the batch
  identified, if it is 1b).
- **Don't:** accept a scheme 1b certificate from another batch as if it covered the whole production.
- **Source:** Disp. DNRT 2/2025, Annex, items 5.2.2 (2-year validity and surveillance) and 5.2.3 (list of
  products that only accept schemes 1b or 5: manual switches, MCBs, RCDs,
  RCDs with integrated short-circuit protection, LV circuit breakers, SPDs, LV isolators,
  cables for fixed indoor installations, flexible cords, power, control, signaling and
  command cables); Res. SIyC 237/2024, Annex, item 3.2.2.1. What each scheme means is a summary of ISO/IEC 17067: **not verified in a primary source**.

### ELEC-IRA-5: In fixed installations, single-core IRAM NM 247-3 or IRAM 62267, and sheathed IRAM 2178-1 or IRAM 62266
- **Rule:** in conduit or surface trunking use single-core cables to **IRAM-NM 247-3** (PVC) or **IRAM 62267** (LS0H, halogen-
  free). On cable tray, buried or with a sheath use **IRAM 2178-1** (0.6/1 kV) or **IRAM 62266** (LS0H, 1 kV). The
  **IRAM-NM 247-5** flexible cords are for portable appliances, not for the fixed installation.
- **Why:** these are the standards the technical regulation assigns to those cables and the ones the AEA assumes in its
  current-carrying capacity tables. A cable that does not comply has less copper or an insulation that can't withstand the temperature.
- **How:** look at the printing along the cable: manufacturer, standard, cross-section (mm²) and voltage (450/750 V or
  0.6/1 kV); on LS0H cables, also the halogen-free legend. Usage details in
  [conductors-and-conduits.md](conductors-and-conduits.md) (ELEC-CON-1 to 4). Current-carrying capacities in
  [current-ratings-and-devices.md](current-ratings-and-devices.md).
- **Verify:**
  - The printing repeats along the cable and matches the label on the coil; the coil carries the conformity
    marking.
  - **Don't measure the diameter with a caliper:** almost all single-core cable sold is flexible (class 5) and the bundle of
    strands flattens depending on how hard you squeeze. The typical counterfeit cable ("cable trucho") has the diameter of the nominal cross-section but
    less copper, impure copper or copper-clad aluminum: that is detected by **resistance** (or by weight).
  - **Resistance:** measure the whole coil (for example 100 m) with a micro-ohmmeter or with the four-wire method
    (voltmeter-ammeter) and correct the value to 20 °C: R20 = Rmeasured / (1 + 0.00393 × (t − 20)). Compare with the
    maximum resistance of IRAM-NM 280 / IEC 60228 for the declared class. Reference values, plain annealed
    copper (not tinned), class 5: 1.5 mm² 13.3 Ω/km; 2.5 mm² 7.98 Ω/km; 4 mm² 4.95 Ω/km; 6 mm² 3.30 Ω/km (over 100 m: 1.33;
    0.798; 0.495; 0.330 Ω). Example: 100 m of 2.5 mm² measured at 28 °C gives 0.83 Ω → R20 = 0.83 / (1 + 0.00393 × 8)
    = 0.83 / 1.0314 = 0.805 Ω, more than 0.798 Ω: the cable does not have the declared cross-section or purity.
  - **Weight:** 1 m of bare conductor weighs about 8.9 g per mm² of copper (2.5 mm² ≈ 22 g/m), same as in
    conductors-and-conduits.md (ELEC-CON-1). Scrape a strand: if gray metal shows under the copper color, it is
    copper-clad aluminum.
- **Don't:** use flexible cord ("cable taller", *tipo taller*) or twin flat cord ("paralelo") embedded in the wall; buy cable by "cheap meters" without
  printing; approve a cable because "the diameter is right".
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM-NM 247-3 ed. 1, 2003-11-25 (IEC 60227-3, MOD); IRAM 62267 ed. 2,
  2025-11-10; IRAM 2178-1 ed. 3, 2015-11-30; IRAM 62266 ed. 3, 2026-05-13; IRAM-NM 247-5 ed. 1, 2003-11-25;
  IRAM-NM 280 ed. 1, 2003-11-25. Current Annex II of Res. 236/2024 (text of Res. 26/2025), items 4.3.1
  (2178-1), 4.3.2 (62266), 4.3.3 (62267), 4.3.4 (NM 247-3) and 4.3.5 (NM 247-5). Coefficient 0.00393/°C for annealed
  copper: general materials data. **Not verified in a primary source:** the resistance values of
  IEC 60228 (table of the paid standard; they are the widely published values and match the 7.98 Ω/km in
  conductors-and-conduits.md); confirm them in the manufacturer's data sheet.

### ELEC-IRA-6: A withdrawn standard printed on a new product is a warning sign
- **Rule:** if a **new** cable or device has a withdrawn standard printed as its only reference (IRAM 2183,
  IRAM 2158, IRAM 2268, IRAM 2022, IRAM 2169, IRAM 2301), don't install it until you see a valid certificate against the
  current standard.
- **Why:** it may be very old stock, a manufacturer that did not update the printing or a product that was never
  certified. In any of the three cases you have no proof that it meets the standard the regulation requires today.
- **How:** use the equivalence table below to see which standard replaced the old one and ask for the certificate
  against the new one.
- **Verify:** the certificate (via QR code or from the body) cites the current standard.
- **Don't:** automatically reject an existing installation because its cables say "IRAM 2183": at the
  time it was the correct standard. The warning is for new material entering the site.
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15), status "Anulado" (withdrawn) of each standard in the equivalence table. The
  "replaced by" relation is an inference by this guide from title and scope (IRAM does not publish it in the catalog entry):
  **not verified in a primary source**, except the one already recorded in [conductors-and-conduits.md](conductors-and-conduits.md).

### ELEC-IRA-7: For MCBs (*termomagnéticas* / *térmicas*) and RCDs (*diferenciales*) there is no current IRAM standard: IEC is required
- **Rule:** an MCB (PIA) is certified against **IEC 60898-1**, a plain RCD against
  **IEC 61008-1** and an RCD with integrated overcurrent protection (RCBO) against **IEC 61009-1**. In
  dwellings and in premises operated by unskilled persons, the PIA must be marked with **IEC 60898-1** (with
  the Icn in the rectangle). Many modular PIAs also carry **IEC 60947-2** (dual marking, with Icu/Ics): that is not
  a problem. One that says **only** IEC 60947-2, whether modular or molded-case, is a circuit breaker for use
  by skilled personnel. Contactors are certified against **IEC 60947-1 + IEC 60947-4-1** (industrial) or
  **IEC 61095** (electromechanical contactors for household use, the modular DIN-rail ones used for a
  water heater or a pump). A device that says only "IRAM 2169" (MCB) or "IRAM 2301" (RCD)
  has an old reference: those standards are withdrawn.
- **Why:** IRAM has no current adoption of those IEC standards today (they do not appear in the catalog) and the technical
  regulation points directly to IEC. IEC 60898-1 is meant for devices operated by anyone;
  IEC 60947-2 for skilled personnel, and it states the breaking capacity differently (Icu/Ics in kA instead of Icn
  in A). For contactors there is the old IRAM 2240 (1974), which the regulation does not cite.
- **How:** on the front or the side of the device look for the IEC standard and the rated data. Selection and
  coordination in [protection.md](protection.md).
- **Verify:** in dwellings, the PIA says 60898-1 (alone or together with 60947-2), the RCD 61008-1 or 61009-1;
  the modular contactor says 61095 or 60947-4-1. The certificate for PIA, RCD and LV circuit breaker is scheme
  1b or 5 (ELEC-IRA-4).
- **Don't:** accept an MCB that has only "IRAM" with no number, or only the CE mark (CE is a European
  self-declaration and does not replace the Argentine certificate); reject a good PIA because it also says 60947-2; put in a
  dwelling panel (distribution board) a modular device that says only 60947-2.
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM 2169 "Interruptores automáticos de sobreintensidad para usos
  domésticos y aplicaciones similares" (overcurrent circuit breakers for household and similar uses), **Anulado** (withdrawn) (ed. 1993); IRAM 2301 "Interruptores automáticos de corriente
  diferencial de fuga para usos domésticos y análogos" (residual current circuit breakers for household and similar uses), **Anulado** (ed. 1981); IRAM 2240 "Contactores" (contactors), Publicada (published),
  ed. 1, 1974-11-01; searches for "60898", "61008", "61009", "60947", "61095" and "61439", including withdrawn
  standards, with no results. Current Annex II of Res. 236/2024 (text of Res. 26/2025), items 4.5.2 (IEC
  60898-1), 4.5.3 (IEC 61008-1), 4.5.4 (IEC 61009-1), 4.5.5 (contactors: IEC 60947-1 + IEC 60947-4-1 / IEC 61095)
  and 4.5.11 (LV circuit breakers: IEC 60947-2). **Not verified in a primary source:** the dual-marking criterion and
  the "unskilled persons" scope of IEC 60898-1 versus 60947-2 (summary of the scope of those paid
  standards and of manufacturers' data sheets); that IEC 61095 is the one for household modular devices is a reading of its title.

### ELEC-IRA-8: Fixed socket outlets with earth, IRAM NM 60884-1 by regulation and IRAM 2071 format by the AEA; never "universal"
- **Rule:** the technical regulation requires every fixed socket outlet up to 250 V and 20 A to have an **earth terminal** and
  to comply with **IRAM-NM 60884-1** (or IEC 60884-1), and it **prohibits** fixed socket outlets that accept plugs of different
  geometries at the same time (the "universales" or "multinorma" ones, universal or multi-standard). The **IRAM 2071 format** (10 A and 20 A, flat pins)
  is not required by the technical regulation: the **AEA Reglamentación** requires it for the installation, and that is why
  the DCI and the utilities (distribution companies) check it.
- **Why:** the universal socket outlet accepts plugs without earth or with round pins, and the earth is lost without the
  user noticing. Its contacts also grip plugs of different sizes poorly, heat up and cause arcing.
  Utilities reject the DCI with round-pin socket outlets ([regulations-and-permits.md](regulations-and-permits.md), ELEC-NOR-10).
- **How:** on general-use circuits, IRAM 2071 socket outlet of 10 A; on special-use circuits, 20 A
  ([grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md), ELEC-CIR-21). For industrial socket outlets (three-phase, construction site,
  outdoor), **IRAM-IEC 60309** format.
- **Verify:** on the socket outlet or its packaging: "IRAM 2071" (format), "IRAM NM 60884-1" or "IEC 60884-1" (safety),
  current (10 A or 20 A), 250 V, earth symbol, brand and conformity marking. With the socket outlet installed, the PE
  is connected to the earth terminal (testing in [testing-and-commissioning.md](testing-and-commissioning.md)).
- **Don't:** fit a universal socket outlet "so everything fits"; leave a socket outlet with the earth terminal unconnected;
  accept a socket outlet of foreign format because "it has a 60884-1 certificate": the certificate does not make it suitable for
  the installation under the AEA.
- **Source:** Disp. DNRT 2/2025, Annex, item 4.1 a) (fixed socket outlets with earth contact, IRAM-NM 60884-1 or IEC
  60884-1; those accepting plugs of different geometries simultaneously are prohibited); current Annex II of Res.
  236/2024 (text of Res. 26/2025), items 4.5.6 (socket outlet for fixed installations up to 20 A: IRAM NM
  60884-1 / IEC 60884-1) and 4.5.12 (industrial plugs, socket outlets and connectors: IRAM-IEC 60309-1 / -2). IRAM
  2071 format in AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.6 a) and b), according to [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md) ELEC-CIR-21.
  IRAM catalog: IRAM 2071 ed. 3, 2009-11-12; IRAM-NM 60884-1 ed. 1, 2006-06-27 (IEC 60884-1:1994, MOD); IRAM-IEC
  60309-1 ed. 1, 2001-07-26 and 60309-2 ed. 1, 2005-04-05. **Not verified in a primary source:** the geometric
  difference between 10 A and 20 A plugs of the IRAM 2071/2073 format (it is content of the paid standard).
### ELEC-IRA-9: Plugs (*fichas*) IRAM 2073 (with earth) and IRAM 2063 (without earth); appliance with imported plug, certified adapter with earth
- **Rule:** an earthed appliance (class I) comes with an **IRAM 2073** plug (10 A or 20 A); a double-insulated one
  (class II) of up to 10 A may come with a flat unearthed **IRAM 2063** plug. The regulation also accepts formats
  from IEC TR 60083, provided the plug complies with IRAM-NM 60884-1. If the appliance comes with a plug that is not the
  Argentine one, it must have a label asking for the use of a **certified adapter with earth contact**.
- **Why:** cutting off the original plug or plugging a class I appliance in with an unearthed adapter leaves the metal
  casing without earth; if there is an insulation fault, the RCD (*disyuntor* / *diferencial*) depends on someone touching it to
  trip.
- **How:** if the client has appliances with an imported plug (schuko, American), the solution is a certified
  adapter with earth, or replacing the plug with an IRAM 2073 one, connecting the cable's PE to the earth terminal. Never
  a universal socket outlet on the wall (ELEC-IRA-8). A factory-assembled cable with plug and connector (for example, the
  power cable of a PC) is a "conjunto cable-ficha-conector" (cable-plug-connector set) and is also certified.
- **Verify:** the plug says "IRAM 2073" or "IRAM 2063", the current and the brand; the class I appliance has the PE
  connected to the earth pin.
- **Don't:** cut off the earth pin so it fits an old socket outlet; use an unearthed adapter for a
  class I appliance.
- **Source:** Disp. DNRT 2/2025, Annex, items 4 a) iii (mandatory label: "Este producto no es provisto con
  ficha de alimentación normalizada para la República Argentina. [...] se requiere el uso de un adaptador
  certificado con toma de tierra" (this product is not supplied with a power plug standardized for Argentina. [...] the use of a certified adapter with earth contact is required); for online sales, the legend goes in the listing) and 4.1 c) (plugs). Current Annex II
  (text of Res. 26/2025), items 4.5.8 (plug with earth: IRAM 2073 / IRAM NM 60884-1), 4.5.9 (plug
  without earth: IRAM 2063 / IRAM NM 60884-1) and 4.5.10 (cable-plug-connector set: IEC 60799). IRAM catalog:
  IRAM 2073 ed. 3, 2009-11-12; IRAM 2063 ed. 4, 2009-11-12. **Not verified in a primary source:** that there is a
  specific IRAM product standard for adapters (we found none in the catalog).

### ELEC-IRA-10: Power strips (*zapatillas*), triple adapters and extension cords: 10 A, with earth, with legend and with limiter; on site, rubber cable
- **Rule:** power strips (*zapatillas*), triple adapters (*triples*) and extension cords (*prolongadores*) (portable socket outlets) are rated for **10 A maximum
  simultaneous load**, must be made up of socket outlets with earth, comply with IRAM-NM 60884-1 (or IEC 60884-1) and carry
  the 10 A legend on the body. **Multi-outlet** units (*múltiples*) also need an **automatic limiter at 10 A**. The
  certifier uses as reference the standards **IRAM 2239** (extension cords) and **IRAM 2086** (portable socket
  outlets).
- **Why:** with three or four outlets it is easy to plug 3000 W into a power strip. Without a limiter, the contacts and the
  cord overheat long before the circuit's MCB (*térmica*) trips (which may be 16 A or 20 A). On site,
  the PVC cord ("taller", flexible cord (*tipo taller*), IRAM-NM 247-5) is light duty: stepped on, in the sun and wet, it cracks and leaves the
  conductors exposed in a damp environment.
- **How:** on site and outdoors use extension cords with **rubber or polychloroprene cable type H07RN-F**
  (IEC 60245-4, type 60245 IEC 66) and industrial plugs and socket outlets **IRAM-IEC 60309 IP44 or higher**, fed from
  a construction site panel (distribution board) with a 30 mA RCD. The IRAM-NM 247-5 cord is for light use indoors.
  Fully unwind reel extension cords ([site-safety.md](site-safety.md), ELEC-SEG-15).
- **Verify:** on the product: 10 A legend, socket outlets with earth, conformity marking, and the limiter (a
  reset button) on multi-outlet units. On the site extension cord: the rubber cable designation printed on the
  sheath and the IP rating of plug and socket outlet.
- **Don't:** use a power strip as a permanent installation (behind furniture, for the air conditioner);
  daisy-chain power strips; use triple adapters without earth; use on site an extension cord (*alargue*) made of flexible cord lying in the mud.
- **Source:** Disp. DNRT 2/2025, Annex, item 4.1 b) (includes extension cords in the group of portable socket
  outlets; references IRAM 2239 and IRAM 2086). Current Annex II (text of Res. 26/2025), items 4.3.5 (cords
  IRAM-NM 247-5 / IEC 60227-5), 4.3.8 (rubber-insulated cables: IEC 60245), 4.5.7 (portable socket outlets up to
  10 A: IRAM NM 60884-1) and 4.5.12 (IRAM-IEC 60309). IRAM catalog: IRAM 2239 "Prolongadores eléctricos para uso
  doméstico y similares, enrollables y no enrollables" (electrical extension cords for household and similar use, reel and non-reel), ed. 1, 2004-06-14; IRAM 2086 "Tomacorrientes móviles
  bipolares con toma de tierra [...] de 10 A, 250 V" (two-pole portable socket outlets with earth contact [...] 10 A, 250 V), ed. 3, 2009-11-12; search for "60245" with no IRAM adoption.
  Designation H07RN-F / 60245 IEC 66 according to [site-safety.md](site-safety.md) (ELEC-SEG-15). The criterion
  of not using them as a fixed installation is this guide's.

### ELEC-IRA-11: Conduits, surface trunking and boxes with their standard; orange pipe is not for electricity
- **Rule:** insulating conduits comply with the **IRAM 62386** series (rigid -21, pliable -22, flexible -23,
  buried -24), surface trunking (*cablecanal*) with the **IRAM 62084** series, steel conduits with **IRAM-IAS U 500 2005** (medium, *semipesado*) or
  **IRAM-IAS U 500 2224** (light, *liviano*) (the old IRAM 2005, 2205 and 2224 are also accepted), and boxes with **IRAM 2346**
  (insulating, flush-mounted), **IRAM 2353** (insulating, surface-mounted), **IRAM 62005** (metal, medium) or
  **IRAM 62224** (metal, light). For the **insulated conduit of the meter pillar** (*pilar*) of a service connection, **IRAM 2477**;
  the pillar itself is defined by the utility. The heavy (*pesado*) steel conduit **IRAM-IAS U 500 2100** is still current, but
  it does not appear in the technical regulation.
- **Why:** the conduit system is the mechanical protection and, in case of a fault, the fire barrier around the
  cable. The classification (compression, impact, non-flame propagation) defines where each conduit can be used
  ([conductors-and-conduits.md](conductors-and-conduits.md), ELEC-CON-11 to ELEC-CON-19).
- **How:** in the specification ask for the standard and the classification, not just "3/4 conduit". In slabs and wet
  construction, the conduit must be classified for that (see the usage table in conductors-and-conduits.md). When searching for
  IRAM-IAS standards in the catalog, type the number **without a hyphen** ("U 500 2005"): with a hyphen the search returns zero.
- **Verify:** the conduit has the standard and the classification printed on it; metal conduit has the IRAM-IAS standard and the
  type (light or medium); boxes have brand and standard. The products of Annex II, items 4.1 and 4.2, carry the
  conformity marking: series 62386-21 to -24 and 62386-1 (accessories), 62084, IRAM 2005 / U 500 2005, IRAM
  2205, U 500 2224, IRAM 2224, IRAM 2477, boxes 2346, 2353, 62005, 62224 and 2390. The heavy conduit U 500 2100 is not
  in the Annex: still ask for the printed standard and the voluntary certificate if there is one.
- **Don't:** use water pipe, gas pipe or "orange" pipe for cables; use light conduit in places where
  medium is required.
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM 62386-1 ed. 2, 2008-06-12; 62386-21 and -22 ed. 2, 2009-07-10;
  62386-23 and -24 ed. 2, 2009-09-04; IRAM 62084-1 ed. 2, 2012-08-30; 62084-2-1 ed. 2, 2014-06-06; 62084-2-2 ed. 1,
  2015-07-31; IRAM-IAS U 500 2005 ed. 3, 2001-08-29; IRAM-IAS U 500 2224 ed. 4, 2002-04-12; IRAM-IAS U 500 2100 ed.
  2, 2002-04-12; IRAM 2346 ed. 2, 2019-11-20; IRAM 2353 ed. 2, 2014-07-08; IRAM 62005 ed. 1, 2004-06-14; IRAM 62224
  ed. 1, 2004-03-26; IRAM 2477 "Caño aislado para pilar de conexión eléctrica domiciliaria" (insulated conduit for a household service connection pillar) ed. 1, 2020-10-19.
  Current Annex II (text of Res. 26/2025), items 4.1.1 to 4.1.13 and 4.2.1 to 4.2.6 (numbering in the table
  "Canalizaciones, cajas y gabinetes" (conduits, boxes and enclosures)). In 4.1.12 (light steel conduit) the Annex cites "IRAM 2224 / IRAM 62224",
  but IRAM 62224 is for light metal boxes: it appears to be an error in the Annex.

### ELEC-IRA-12: Enclosures (*gabinetes*) and housings (*envolventes*) with a standard; the MCB (*térmicas*) box goes by IEC 60670-24; the assembled panel is governed by the AEA
- **Rule:** the empty enclosure (*gabinete*) and the boxes for protective devices are certifiable products. The **box for
  MCBs and RCDs** complies with **IEC 60670-1 + IEC 60670-24** (enclosures for protective devices and other
  power-dissipating equipment), with no alternative. The empty enclosure, **IEC 62208**. **IRAM 62670** is the general standard
  for envelopes (*envolturas*) for accessories of household fixed installations and **does not replace** 60670-24 for a
  protective-device box. You design and verify the assembled panel (distribution board) (enclosure plus devices plus connections) according to the AEA
  ([panels.md](panels.md)).
- **Why:** a box that can't withstand the heat of the MCBs deforms, softens and leaves live parts
  within reach. The temperature-rise test for enclosures housing protective devices is the one in -24.
- **How:** when choosing an enclosure, look at the standard, the IP and IK ratings (ELEC-IRA-17), the material and the power it
  can dissipate if the manufacturer declares it. The specific IRAM standards for panels that are still current are
  old: **IRAM 2181-1** (type-tested and partially type-tested assemblies, 1993), **2181-2** (busbar trunking
  systems, 1996), **2181-3** (distribution boards accessible to unskilled persons, 1993), **IRAM
  2200** (metal-enclosed panels, 1985) and **IRAM 2195** (their dielectric tests, 1986). The catalog
  shows no IRAM adoption of IEC 61439.
- **Verify:** the MCB box has brand, **IEC 60670-24** and conformity marking; the metal enclosure,
  IEC 62208.
- **Don't:** build a panel in a junction box or a common flush box; accept as an MCB box a box
  that only declares IRAM 62670; cite "IRAM-IEC 61439" in a specification (we did not find it in the catalog; if you want to
  require conformity with IEC 61439, cite the IEC standard).
- **Source:** Current Annex II (text of Res. 26/2025), items 4.2.7 (surface boxes for switches:
  IRAM NM 60669-1 / IEC 60669-1 / IEC 61439-1 + 61439-2), 4.2.8 to 4.2.10 (IEC 60670-1 + -21, -22, -23), 4.2.11
  ("para elementos de protección y otros equipos eléctricos disipadores de potencia" (for protective devices and other power-dissipating electrical equipment): IEC 60670-1 + IEC
  60670-24), 4.2.12 (envelopes: IRAM 62670), 4.2.13 (enclosures: IEC 62208) and 4.2.14 (cable trays: IEC 61537);
  IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM 62670 "Accesorios para instalaciones eléctricas fijas de baja tensión
  (domésticas y similares). Requisitos generales para sus envolturas" (accessories for low-voltage fixed electrical installations (household and similar); general requirements for their enclosures), ed. 1, 2006-03-24; IRAM 2181-1 ed. 1,
  1993-08-06; IRAM 2181-2 ed. 1, 1996-10-04; IRAM 2181-3 ed. 1, 1993-12-03; IRAM 2200 ed. 1, 1985-06-07; IRAM 2195
  ed. 1, 1986-07-04; search for "61439" with no results.

### ELEC-IRA-13: Earthing materials with a standard: earth rod (*jabalina*) IRAM 2309 or 2310, connections IRAM 2315, 2343 or 2349
- **Rule:** the copper-clad steel earth rod (*jabalina*) complies with **IRAM 2309**, the galvanized steel one with **IRAM 2310** and the
  electrochemical one with **IRAM 2314**. The connection of the conductor to the earth rod is made with exothermic welding (*soldadura cuproaluminotérmica*)
  (**IRAM 2315**), with standardized bolted clamp fittings (**IRAM 2343**, the earth clamp, *tomacable*) or with a compression
  connector (**IRAM 2349**). Soil-enhancing compounds have their own standard, **IRAM 2470**.
- **Why:** an earth rod with too thin a copper coating corrodes in a few years and the earthing resistance
  rises without anyone noticing. A sheet-metal or iron earth clamp rusts and opens the earth
  circuit. Common brass, with a lot of zinc, can dezincify in damp soil and the connection loosens.
- **How:** ask for an earth rod with the standard and dimensions engraved, and a **bronze earth clamp** to standard (or exothermic
  welding IRAM 2315) for buried connections or in an inspection pit. Design and testing in
  [earthing.md](earthing.md); the code of practice is **IRAM 2281** (co-published with the AEA as 95501).
- **Verify:** the earth rod has brand, standard and dimensions engraved; the batch has conformity marking (the
  IRAM 2309, 2310 and 2314 earth rods with their accessories, IRAM 2315 welding and IRAM 2466 and 2467
  copper-clad steel conductors are in Annex II).
- **Don't:** use galvanized pipe or reinforcing bar as an earth rod; tie the cable to the earth rod with
  wire; use iron or common brass clamps on buried connections; leave the buried connection without an inspection
  box.
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM 2309 ed. 4, 2023-12-05; IRAM 2310 ed. 3, 2013-06-14; IRAM 2314 ed. 1,
  2002-09-30; IRAM 2315 ed. 4, 2018-11-05; IRAM 2343 ed. 1, 2009-03-05; IRAM 2349 ed. 1, 2011-08-05; IRAM 2470 ed. 1,
  2020-09-24; IRAM 2466 and 2467 ed. 1, 1992-09-04; IRAM 2281-1 ed. 2, 2026-02-02; 2281-2 ed. 4, 2021-10-01; 2281-3 ed.
  4, 2024-08-01. Current Annex II (text of Res. 26/2025), items 4.6.2 to 4.6.7. The points about galvanized pipe, reinforcing bar
  and brass are this guide's criterion (dezincification of brass: general materials
  knowledge); **not verified in a primary source:** which materials IRAM 2309 and 2343 accept for the earth clamp.
  See earthing.md.

### ELEC-IRA-14: Lightning with IRAM 2184 (AEA 92305) and SPDs (*DPS*) with IRAM 2345 or IEC 61643-11
- **Rule:** the risk assessment and the lightning protection design are done according to the **IRAM
  2184** series (co-published with the AEA as 92305). The LV SPD (*DPS*) complies with **IRAM 2345** or **IEC 61643-11** (alternatives: we did not
  verify that IRAM 2345 is an adoption of the IEC standard). For signal and telecommunication lines, **IEC
  61643-21**; on the photovoltaic DC side, **IEC 61643-31** (adopted by IRAM as **IRAM 2345-31**). They are selected
  according to **IRAM 2345-2** (and, in PV, **IRAM 2345-32**) and section 534 of the AEA (90364-5-534).
- **Why:** without a risk assessment you don't know whether the building needs an external system; an SPD badly chosen or
  without the backup protection required by the manufacturer can catch fire in the panel.
- **How:** design in [buildings.md](buildings.md) and [special-installations.md](special-installations.md);
  selection, Uc, Up and SPD connections in [protection.md](protection.md).
- **Verify:** the SPD has standard, test type or class, Uc, Up, currents (In, Imax or Iimp) and conformity
  marking; the DC one says it is for photovoltaics and its maximum DC voltage.
- **Don't:** install an early streamer emission lightning rod ("con dispositivo de cebado") as if its declared protection radius
  were backed by IRAM 2184: IRAM 2426 only covers laboratory tests of that equipment. Don't fit an
  AC SPD on the DC side.
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM 2184-1, -2, -3 and -4 ed. 4, 2015-06-25; IRAM 2184-11 (selection guide
  for Argentina) ed. 2, 2016-09-01; IRAM 2345 ed. 1, 2014-07-18 (the catalog title does not mention an IEC origin);
  IRAM 2345-2 ed. 1, 2021-11-29; IRAM 2345-31 ed. 1, 2024-05-07 (IEC 61643-31:2018, MOD); IRAM 2345-32 ed. 1,
  2023-04-03 (IEC 61643-32:2017, MOD); IRAM 2428 (Franklin lightning rods) ed. 1, 2002-11-29; IRAM 2426 (early streamer emission
  lightning rods, laboratory tests) ed. 2, 2016-07-27. Current Annex II (text of Res. 26/2025), item 4.5.14
  (SPD: IRAM 2345 / IEC 61643-11, IEC 61643-21, IEC 61643-31). The original 2024 Annex II had the SPD and isolator rows
  shifted; the current text corrected it. AEA 90364-4-443 and 90364-5-534 according to
  [protection.md](protection.md).
### ELEC-IRA-15: Emergency lighting (*iluminación de emergencia*): IRAM-AADL J 2027 (2026 ed.) and luminaires to IRAM-AADL J 2028-2-22
- **Rule:** the emergency lighting design for a building is based on **IRAM-AADL J 2027**
  ("Alumbrado de emergencia en interiores de establecimientos", emergency lighting inside buildings), whose **2nd edition is in force since 2026-06-11**. The
  emergency luminaire complies with **IRAM-AADL J 2028-2-22** (IEC 60598-2-22:2021, MOD) in addition to the general part
  **IRAM-AADL J 2028-1**.
- **Why:** the new edition may change levels, durations or tests compared with what circulates in
  old technical articles. A design signed with values from the 1st edition may be rejected with observations.
- **How:** buy the 2026 ed. before designing; meanwhile, what [buildings.md](buildings.md) says about
  emergency lighting is taken from publications based on the previous edition.
- **Verify:** the self-contained luminaire states the standard, the declared duration (autonomy) and the conformity marking
  (lighting: Res. 16/2025).
- **Don't:** cite "IRAM 2362" (self-contained luminaire for two incandescent lamps, 1985) as the standard for
  current LED emergency luminaires. Also don't confuse **IRAM-AADL J 2020 / J 2021**, which cover
  **street lighting** luminaires, with the indoor ones.
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM-AADL J 2027 ed. 2, 2026-06-11; IRAM-AADL J 2028-2-22 ed. 2, 2025-04-22;
  IRAM-AADL J 2028-1 ed. 4, 2024-06-07 (IEC 60598-1:2020, MOD); IRAM 2362 ed. 1, 1985-12-06; IRAM-AADL J 2020-1 to -4 and

### ELEC-IRA-16: PPE (*EPP*) and tools: gloves IRAM 3604, helmet IRAM 3620 with electrical property, clothing IRAM 3904, ladders IRAM 3634; insulated tools per IEC 60900
- **Rule:** to work near live parts: insulating gloves **IRAM 3604** of the class that
  matches the voltage; helmet **IRAM 3620** **without ventilation holes and with the electrical property declared in the
  marking**; reinforced plastic ladder **IRAM 3634** (not aluminum) and insulated tools certified
  to **IEC 60900** (the IRAM catalog shows no adoption of this standard). For arc risk: gloves
  **IRAM 3676** (2025) and clothing **IRAM 3904** (materials and garments for arc exposure), or its international counterpart
  IEC 61482-2. Electrically insulating footwear: we found no specific IRAM standard; ask for EN 50321 (or ASTM F2413
  with EH mark) declared on the footwear.
- **Why:** the glove, the helmet and the ladder are the last barrier when the isolation procedure fails.
  Each one must be tested for electrical risk, not only mechanical. An industrial helmet with ventilation
  holes or without a dielectric test does not insulate.
- **How:** dead work (*trabajo sin tensión*) procedure and PPE in [site-safety.md](site-safety.md)
  (ELEC-SEG-10 to 12). Footwear: **IRAM 3610** is the general safety footwear standard and **IRAM 3643** covers
  **only the toe caps** against impact and compression; neither of them by itself guarantees electrical insulation.
  Helmet: confirm in the marking which electrical test it declares (typically 440 V AC on EN 397 helmets with the
  optional requirement, or an insulating helmet for LV up to 1000 V per EN 50365).
- **Verify:** on the glove: standard, class, size and date of the last test; on the helmet: standard and electrical
  test marked, no holes; on the ladder: IRAM 3634; on the tool: double triangle, 1000 V and IEC
  60900; on arc-rated clothing: standard and declared protection value.
- **Don't:** use leather gloves, PVC cleaning gloves or "electrician" gloves with no standard; use an aluminum ladder
  near lines or panels; trust "rubber-covered" handles that are not insulated tools (the old IRAM 5264,
  "pinzas y alicates para uso eléctrico" (pliers for electrical use), 1981, does not replace IEC 60900 for live work); use a
  ventilated helmet for electrical work.
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM 3604 "Guantes de material aislante para trabajos eléctricos con

### ELEC-IRA-17: IP ratings with IRAM 2444-1 and IK ratings with IRAM 2444-2
- **Rule:** when a specification (*pliego*) or the utility (distribution company, *distribuidora*) asks for an IP rating (dust and water) or IK (impact), the current
  Argentine reference is **IRAM 2444-1** (IP code) and **IRAM 2444-2** (IK code), both from 2020.
- **Why:** an indoor IP 41 enclosure is not suitable for an outdoor meter pillar (*pilar*), where IP 54 or
  more is usually required; a panel (distribution board, *tablero*) in a place exposed to impacts needs a declared IK.
- **How:** the first digit of the IP is protection against solids and contact, the second against water. Require
  the manufacturer to declare IP and IK in the data sheet or on the product.
- **Verify:** the declared rating is kept with the installation finished: cable glands (*prensacables*) and covers fitted, no
  extra holes.
- **Don't:** drill an IP 65 enclosure with a drill bit and run the cables through without a cable gland.
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM 2444-1 ed. 1, 2020-07-20; IRAM 2444-2 ed. 1, 2020-07-20. The IRAM 2444
  of 1982 (degrees of mechanical protection of enclosures, rotating machines committee) is also still
  published, and it is the one old documents cite. IP values for the meter pillar: [regulations-and-permits.md](regulations-and-permits.md), ELEC-NOR-10.

### ELEC-IRA-18: Drawings with IRAM 2010 symbols and IRAM 4502 lines; signs with IRAM 10005 colors
- **Rule:** draw electrical diagrams with the symbols of the **IRAM 2010** series and building drawing
  lines with **IRAM 4502-23**; mark panels and hazards with the colors and signs of **IRAM
  10005-1 and -2**.
- **Why:** any inspector or colleague can read a single-line diagram (*unifilar*) with standard symbols; an electrical
  hazard sign with the standard colors is recognized even if the text is not read.
- **How:** on the panel: electrical hazard sign (yellow triangle with black border, per IRAM 10005) and
  an up-to-date single-line diagram inside the door ([panels.md](panels.md)). The old "IRAM 4502" (1974) is withdrawn:
  cite the relevant part of the 4502-xx series.
- **Verify:** the drawing states which drawing and symbol standards it uses.
- **Don't:** invent symbols without a reference on the drawing.
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM 2010-1 ed. 1, 1979-09-07; IRAM 2010-3 (control and protection devices)
  ed. 1, 1988-12-02; IRAM 2010-6 ed. 1, 1990-12-07; IRAM 4502-23 (lines for building drawings) ed. 1,
  2008-05-09; IRAM 4502 **Anulado** (withdrawn); IRAM 10005-1 and 10005-2 ed. 1, 2020-05-22. **Not verified in a primary source:**
  the exact shape and colors of the electrical hazard sign in IRAM 10005 (paid standard).

### ELEC-IRA-19: Before citing a standard, confirm number, part and edition in the IRAM catalog
- **Rule:** in a specification, quote, report or certificate, cite the standard with full number, part and year of
  edition (for example "IRAM 2281-3:2024"), and confirm it in the IRAM catalog on the same day.
- **Why:** there are standards with the same base number and different scope (IRAM 2005 of 1972 and IRAM-IAS U 500 2005 of
  2001; IRAM 2444 of 1982 and IRAM 2444-1 of 2020), standards that were split into parts (IRAM 2178, now 2178-1 and
  2178-2) and editions that change (IRAM 62266 ed. 3 of May 2026; IRAM 62267 ed. 2 of November 2025).
- **How:** online IRAM catalog (catalogo.iram.org.ar): search by number or keyword, with a filter to
  include standards no longer in force. The record shows the status ("Publicada" (published) or "Anulado" (withdrawn)), the edition and the date it
  came into force. IRAM-IAS standards are written and searched without a hyphen between "U 500" and the number ("IRAM-IAS U 500 2224").
- **Verify:** the record says "Publicada / Vigente" (published / in force) and the edition matches the one you cited.
- **Don't:** copy standard numbers from an old specification; copy text or tables from an IRAM standard into your own
  document (they are copyrighted).
- **Source:** online IRAM catalog, consulted 2026-11-15 (records cited in this guide; the search "U 500-2005"
  with a hyphen returns no results and "U 500 2005" does).

### ELEC-IRA-20: The meter (*medidor*) belongs to the utility; its standards help you understand it, not touch it
- **Rule:** energy meters are tested against the **IRAM 62052 / 62053** series (static) and, for
  prepaid, **IRAM 62055-31**. As an installer you don't touch it: the meter and the service connection (*acometida*) belong to the utility up to
  the first isolation point.
- **Why:** knowing the meter class helps you discuss a consumption complaint with data. Opening it or
  bypassing it (*puentear*) is illegal and dangerous ([regulations-and-permits.md](regulations-and-permits.md), ELEC-NOR-15).
- **How:** meter cabinets and meter boxes have their own standard (**IRAM 2390** synthetic material boxes,
  **IRAM 2393** modular cabinets) and the utility defines the accepted model.
- **Verify:** the meter box the utility requires appears on its list of approved models.
- **Don't:** change a meter or break seals (*precintos*).
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM 62052-11 ed. 1, 2023-04-03; IRAM 62053-21 ed. 1, 2018-11-09; IRAM
  62055-31 ed. 1, 2022-04-07; IRAM 2390 ed. 1, 2016-04-15; IRAM 2393 ed. 1, 2022-07-25. IRAM 2411, 2412 and 2413
  (induction meters, 1980 to 1994) are still published but cover electromechanical technology. Current Annex II
  (text of Res. 26/2025), items 4.6.1 (meters: IEC 62052-31, safety) and 4.2.6 (boxes IRAM 2390).

### ELEC-IRA-21: Light switches (*llaves de luz*) and manual switches: IRAM-NM 60669-1; isolators (*seccionadores*) per IEC 60947-3
- **Rule:** light switches (*llaves de efecto*: one-way, two-way) and manual switches for fixed installations comply with
  **IRAM-NM 60669-1** (or IEC 60669-1); surface boxes for switches, the same standard (or IEC 61439-1 +
  61439-2). **LV isolators** (switch-disconnectors) are certified to **IEC 60947-3**. Home automation and
  remote control switches, to **IEC 60669-1 + IEC 60669-2-1** (electronic) or **+ IEC 60669-2-5** (home automation). IRAM 2122
  (air-break switches and disconnectors, 1981) is still published but the regulation does not cite it. The old **IRAM
  2007** is withdrawn.
- **Why:** the switch breaks the phase of the outlet point (*boca*); a contact that cannot carry the rated current chars
  and may keep conducting or arc inside the box.
- **How:** choose the switch current by the load (10 A or 16 A are usual) and connect the phase, never the neutral,
  to the switch ([grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md)).
- **Verify:** on the module: standard, current, voltage and brand; conformity marking on the packaging. Manual
  switches and LV isolators are among the products that are certified only under scheme
  1b or 5 (ELEC-IRA-4).
- **Don't:** install an uncertified dimmer or home automation switch on an outlet point with a load higher than the
  declared one; accept an isolator that only declares "IRAM 2122" as if it complied with the regulation.
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM-NM 60669-1 ed. 1, 2005-03-08 (IEC 60669-1, MOD); IRAM 2122
  "Interruptores en aire de baja tensión, seccionadores en aire, seccionadores bajo carga en aire y combinados con
  fusibles" (LV air-break switches, disconnectors, switch-disconnectors and fuse combinations), ed. 1, 1981-10-02; IRAM 2007 **Anulado** (withdrawn) (1995 ed.). Current Annex II (text of Res. 26/2025),
  items 4.2.7, 4.5.1 (manual switches: IRAM NM 60669-1 / IEC 60669-1), 4.5.15 (LV isolators: IEC
  60947-3) and 4.5.17 (remote control and home automation); Disp. DNRT 2/2025, Annex, item 5.2.3 a) and g). The 10 A or 16 A
  is a usual market value, **not verified in a primary source**.

### ELEC-IRA-22: A loose part without QR is exempt only if it goes inside a certified product
- **Rule:** a material from Annex II that enters as an **input** (*insumo*) in the manufacture or assembly of a final product
  covered by a technical regulation, or as a **spare part** (*repuesto*) ordered by the manufacturer or importer of that equipment,
  is **exempt** from certification and from its own marking. If the same material **is sold loose**, at a builders' merchant (*corralón*) or
  online, it must be certified and marked, whatever its destination.
- **Why:** it explains why a terminal block (*bornera*) or a switch that comes inside a piece of equipment has no QR, and it closes the
  "it's a spare part" excuse for selling loose uncertified material.
- **How:** if you are offered loose material without marking "because it's an input", don't buy it for an installation. If
  the spare part is supplied by the equipment manufacturer, ask for it to come with the DJC (sworn declaration of conformity) and the certificate of the final product.
- **Verify:** loose material entering the site has conformity marking (ELEC-IRA-1).
- **Don't:** accept MCBs (*térmicas*), socket outlets (*tomas*) or cable without marking on the argument that "they are for an equipment
  panel" or "they are spare parts".
- **Source:** Disp. DNRT 2/2025, Annex, item 5.4 "Insumos y repuestos" (inputs and spare parts) (exemption from the procedure of Annex III
  of Res. 236/2024 and from marking; proof with DJC and certificate of the final product; "Cuando los insumos y/o
  repuestos [...] se comercialicen en forma independiente [...] deberán dar cumplimiento al procedimiento de
  evaluación de la conformidad [...] independientemente del destino que pudiera tener" (when inputs and/or spare parts are sold independently they must comply with the conformity assessment procedure, regardless of their destination)).

### ELEC-IRA-23: Insulating tape (*cinta aisladora*), cable lugs (*terminales*), terminal blocks (*borneras*) and cable glands (*prensacables*) also have standards and marking
- **Rule:** cable accessories are in the technical regulation: insulating tape **IRAM-NM 60454-3-1** or
  **IRAM 2454** (or IEC 60454); lugs and connectors **IEC 61238-1-1 / 61238-1-2**; cable glands **IEC 62444**;
  rail terminals **IEC 60947-7-1** (feed-through), **-7-2** (earth) and **-7-3** (fuse); connectors **IEC
  61984**; terminal blocks and supply connectors **IEC 60998-1 + 60998-2-1**; heat-shrink **IEC 60684**;
  cord switch **IEC 61058-1 + 61058-2-1**.
- **Why:** the joint is the point where most installations overheat. A tape with no standard loses adhesion
  with heat; a lug that is not for that cross-section or that crimping method works loose; an earth terminal that
  is not an earth terminal does not ensure PE continuity.
- **How:** use lugs matching the cable cross-section and crimp them with the tool and die the
  manufacturer specifies; in the panel, rail terminals with their standard and green-yellow earth terminals for the PE
  ([panels.md](panels.md)). Joints and taps go in boxes, not inside the conduit (*caño*)
  ([conductors-and-conduits.md](conductors-and-conduits.md)).
- **Verify:** the packaging states the standard, the cross-section or accepted range, and the conformity marking.
- **Don't:** use packing tape or "unbranded tape" to insulate; squash a lug with combination pliers;
  use a common feed-through terminal as an earth terminal.
- **Source:** Current Annex II (text of Res. 26/2025), items 4.4.1 to 4.4.11 (4.4.1 feed-through terminals IEC
  60947-7-1; 4.4.2 earth terminals IEC 60947-7-2; 4.4.3 fuse terminals IEC 60947-7-3; 4.4.4 lugs IEC
  61238-1-1 / 61238-1-2; 4.4.5 cable glands IEC 62444; 4.4.6 insulating tapes IRAM NM 60454-3-1 / IRAM 2454 / IEC
  60454; 4.4.7 connectors from 50 V to 1000 V and up to 125 A per contact IEC 61984; 4.4.8 cord switch IEC
  61058-1 + 61058-2-1; 4.4.9 heat-shrink IEC 60684; 4.4.10 interlock sockets IEC 60320; 4.4.11 terminal blocks
  IEC 60998-1 + 60998-2-1). IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM-NM 60454-3-1 (PVC tapes with adhesive) ed. 1,
  2012-09-28; IRAM-NM 60454-1 and -2 ed. 1, 2012-09-28; IRAM 2454-1, -2 and -3 ed. 1, 1989-11-03. The tool and
  green-yellow terminals points are this guide's criterion.
### ELEC-IRA-24: Measuring instruments: CAT category per IEC 61010, testers per IEC 61557 and voltage detectors per IEC 61243-3
- **Rule:** the multimeter (*tester*) or clamp meter (*pinza amperométrica*) you use on panels (distribution boards, *tableros*) must be certified to **IEC 61010-1** (test leads
  per IEC 61010-031) with **CAT III** as a minimum for panels and fixed circuits, and **CAT IV** for the meter pillar (*pilar*), the
  service connection (*acometida*) or the meter (*medidor*). Two-pole voltage testers (*detectores bipolares*), to **IEC 61243-3**. Installation
  testers (insulation, continuity, loop impedance, RCDs (*diferenciales*), earth resistance), to the **IEC 61557**
  series. None of these has an IRAM adoption in the catalog and they are not Annex II products: you do the
  check yourself when buying.
- **Why:** at the meter pillar or the main panel, a transient overvoltage can make a low-category
  instrument explode in your hand. A megohmmeter (megger, *megóhmetro*) that does not comply with 61557 does not guarantee the test voltage
  the verification requires.
- **How:** compare category and voltage together (CAT III 600 V or CAT IV 300 V as a minimum for 220/380 V), as
  [site-safety.md](site-safety.md) ELEC-SEG-13 says. Commissioning measurements are in
  [testing-and-commissioning.md](testing-and-commissioning.md).
- **Verify:** on the instrument and on the test leads: CAT and voltage, standard, brand; the leads have the same or a higher
  category than the instrument.
- **Don't:** measure at the meter pillar with a multimeter with no category or CAT II; use test leads without finger guards or
  probe caps.
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): searches "61010", "61557" and "61243" with no results. Current Annex II:
  only IEC 61010-1 appears, in 4.6.9 ("equipos de monitoreo de consumo eléctrico" (electricity consumption monitoring equipment), installed devices, not
  hand-held instruments). Categories and the CAT III / CAT IV criterion per site-safety.md ELEC-SEG-13.
  **Not verified in a primary source:** the text of IEC 61010-1, 61010-031, 61243-3 and 61557 (paid standards).

### ELEC-IRA-25: Above 63 A there is no mandatory certification: require quality in the specification (*pliego*)
- **Rule:** materials above 63 A (except cables) are **excluded** from Disp. 2/2025: a molded-case
  circuit breaker of 100-250 A, an 80 A RCD or a 125 A isolator (*seccionador*) need no Argentine certificate or
  marking. In the specification ask for: the IEC product standard (IEC 60947-2 circuit breakers, IEC 60947-3 isolators,
  IEC 61008-1 / 61009-1 RCDs, IEC 60269 fuses), the **type test report** from an ISO/IEC 17025
  accredited laboratory or a **voluntary certificate** (for example, from the IECEE CB scheme or from an accredited
  body), and the full rated data (Icu/Ics, curves, settings, In and IΔn).
- **Why:** without mandatory certification, the only thing separating a good device from a counterfeit (*trucho*) one is what you ask for
  and check. At those ratings a breaking failure means a panel fire.
- **How:** in the specification add: "devices above 63 A: compliant with [IEC standard], with type test report
  or certificate from an accredited body, for the same model and rating; panel test record per the
  AEA". Verification of the assembled panel is in [panels.md](panels.md).
- **Verify:** the report or certificate names the model and rating being delivered, the standard and the
  laboratory or body; the device has the standard and the rated data marked.
- **Don't:** accept "it's European, it has CE" as the only proof; assume that because the small MCBs (*térmicas*) in the
  panel are certified, the main switch is too.
- **Source:** Disp. DNRT 2/2025, Annex, item 2 e) ("Todos los materiales, excepto cables, cuya corriente nominal
  de funcionamiento exceda los [...] 63 A" (all materials, except cables, whose rated operating current exceeds 63 A)). The content of the specification is this guide's criterion,
  **not verified in a primary source** as a requirement of any standard.

### ELEC-IRA-26: AFDD, type F or B RCDs (*diferenciales*) and voltage protectors (*protectores de tensión*): what the regulation does not name
- **Rule:** Annex II does not name the **arc fault detection device (AFDD, IEC 62606)** or the **type F
  or B RCD (IEC 62423)**: if you use them, ask for the device to declare that standard and for the base RCD to be certified
  to IEC 61008-1 or 61009-1. The **permanent overvoltage and undervoltage protector** (the one that trips when the
  neutral is lost) does not have its own item in the Annex either; if it is an electronic DIN rail module, it could fall under
  item 4.5.13 (IRAM 63074). In all cases ask for the printed standard and whatever certificate it has.
- **Why:** they are protection devices: not being named does not mean any one will do. The AEA
  requires or recommends them in specific cases (type A as a minimum with power electronics, protection against broken
  neutral in buildings, AFDD in combustible constructions with insulating conduit).
- **How:** selection in [protection.md](protection.md) and
  [special-installations.md](special-installations.md) (ELEC-ESP-2, ELEC-ESP-35).
- **Verify:** the AFDD says IEC 62606; the type F or B RCD says IEC 62423 (and 61008-1 or 61009-1); the
  voltage protector declares its thresholds and tripping times.
- **Don't:** buy an unbranded "protector de tensión" (voltage protector) with no standard and no declared thresholds for a
  building.
- **Source:** Current Annex II (text of Res. 26/2025), complete item 4.5 (IEC 62606 and IEC
  62423 do not appear); IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): searches "62606" and "62423" with no results; IRAM 63074 "Dispositivos
  eléctricos o electrónicos de maniobra, control, señalización, alimentación y/o protección de instalaciones
  eléctricas domiciliarias para usos domésticos y similares. Requisitos generales de seguridad eléctrica" (electrical or electronic switching, control, signaling, supply and/or protection devices for household installations; general electrical safety requirements), ed. 2,
  2008-09-10. **Not verified in a primary source:** that the voltage protector is certified under item 4.5.13
  (this guide's inference from the title of IRAM 63074).

### ELEC-IRA-27: Photovoltaics and EV charging: which standards to ask for
- **Rule:** in distributed generation, modules and inverters only with the certifications the Secretaría
  de Energía (energy secretariat) requires: modules **IEC 61730** plus IRAM 210013-17, IEC 61215 or IEC 61646; grid-tied inverters **IRAM
  210013-21**, or IEC 62109 plus anti-islanding (IEC 62116 or VDE 0126-1-1) plus a grid code (details in
  [special-installations.md](special-installations.md), ELEC-ESP-21). DC-side cables, **IRAM 62930**;
  DC SPDs (*DPS*), **IRAM 2345-31** / IEC 61643-31 (ELEC-IRA-14). For electric vehicle charging, the product
  standards are **IEC 61851** (charging stations) and **IEC 62196** (plugs and connectors), with no IRAM adoption found; the
  installation follows AEA 90364-7-722.
- **Why:** the anti-islanding function and the inverter protections are what prevent the equipment from feeding a
  de-energized network where someone from the utility (distribution company, *distribuidora*) is working.
- **How:** ask for the certificates before buying and include them in the user-generator (*usuario-generador*) application. For the
  EV charger, ask for it to declare IEC 61851-1 and the IEC 62196 connector type, and follow ELEC-ESP-17 and 18.
- **Verify:** the certificates name the exact module and inverter model; the charger states the standard and data
  for current and DC protection.
- **Don't:** connect an "off-grid" inverter, or one without an accepted certificate, to the grid.
- **Source:** IRAM catalog (2026-11-15): IRAM 210013-17 (design qualification and type approval of modules)
  ed. 1, 2003-06-20; IRAM 210013-21 (grid-connected inverters) ed. 1, 2016-12-30; IRAM 210025 (bidirectional
  inverters) ed. 1, 2023-05-19; IRAM 210019 (inverters for stand-alone systems) ed. 1, 2019-02-27; IRAM 62930
  ed. 1, 2023-04-04; searches "61851", "62196", "61730", "61215", "62109" and "62116" with no IRAM adoption. Certification
  requirements of the Secretaría de Energía (Disp. SSERyEE 97/2019) and AEA 90364-7-722:2024 per
  special-installations.md (ELEC-ESP-17, 18, 21). **Not verified in a primary source** in this review: the text
  of Disp. SSERyEE 97/2019; whether charging stations fall under the electrical equipment regime (Res.
  16/2025): they do not appear in the materials Annex II.

### ELEC-IRA-28: Before accepting a batch, check the product alerts
- **Rule:** if a material or device raises doubts (unknown brand, odd price, repeated defects),
  look it up on the official **"Alertas de Productos"** (product alerts) page of Defensa del Consumidor (consumer protection) before installing it.
- **Why:** when a manufacturer or importer starts a market recall for a risk detected after the
  sale, it must report it with brand, model and batch, and it is published there.
- **How:** argentina.gob.ar → Defensa del Consumidor → Alertas de Productos; search by brand or model. If
  you find a product without certification or with a fake certificate, make the inquiry or the complaint to the DNRT
  (ELEC-IRA-3, step 4).
- **Verify:** the brand, model and batch of the material you have do not match any current alert.
- **Don't:** install a product with an alert "because I already bought it".
- **Source:** argentina.gob.ar/produccion/defensadelconsumidor/alertas-de-productos ("Alertas de
  Productos" page, consulted 2026-11-15; its description says it lists products subject to alerts and recalls for faults or
  risks after the sale). Res. of the former Secretaría de Comercio Interior 464/2022 (alert and
  product recall form), per the summary of its publication in the BO (Boletín Oficial, official gazette): **not verified in a primary source** are its
  full text and its validity after the 2025 repeals.

## What this guide does not replace

- **The standards themselves.** IRAM, IEC and AEA are paid, copyrighted documents. Here you get number, edition and what
  they are for; test values, dimensions and requirements are read in the purchased standard, in its edition
  in force.
- **The technical regulation in force.** Res. 236/2024 has already been amended by **Res. SIyC 26/2025** (new Annex
  II IF-2025-17295098-APN-DNRT#MEC and marking enforceable since 2025-10-01), and this guide uses that text. The DNRT and the
  Secretaría may amend it again: before a specification or an expert assessment (*peritaje*), check the Boletín Oficial (official gazette) or
  argentina.gob.ar/normativa for later resolutions or dispositions. In this review we found no
  2026 amendments to the Annex II for electrical materials, but we did not check the BO day by day.
- **Verification of each certificate.** This guide tells you how to verify it; confirmation that a specific
  certificate is valid comes from the QR, the body that issued it and, if in doubt, the DNRT.
- **The design and signature of a qualified professional.** Choosing the right material is one part; the installation
  is designed, carried out and certified by someone with a license (*matrícula*) and the corresponding professional scope (*incumbencia*) ([regulations-and-permits.md](regulations-and-permits.md)).
- **Safety at work.** No certified material protects you if you work live: isolate, lock out,
  verify absence of voltage and earth before touching ([site-safety.md](site-safety.md)).
