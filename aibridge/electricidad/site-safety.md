# Electrical safety at work and on site

Short rules for anyone who works on an electrical installation or uses one on a construction site. Each rule states in **Source** whether it is
a regulatory requirement (decree or resolution, with article) or a recommendation of this guide.
Each rule has an ID so the agent, a daily site report (*parte de obra*) or a report can cite it ("fails ELEC-SEG-4").
**Guiding principle:** in this guide there is no live work. If something cannot be done dead, a site electrician does not
do it: it is done by personnel specifically qualified for live work, with their procedure
(ELEC-SEG-2), or by the utility (*distribuidora*).

The agent does the calculations (fault current, touch voltage, permissible earth resistance) with the
offline calculator `gateway electricidad ...` (current and earthing subcommands) instead of doing them from memory.

## Applicable regulations (status as of 2026-11-15)

**Which decree applies depending on where you work:**
- **Construction, demolition or renovation site with construction employers:** **Decreto 911/96** applies. Its art. 2 says that since it was issued, Decreto 351/79 does not apply to the construction industry.
- **Establishments and buildings in use with workers** (maintenance of an occupied building, a shop, a factory): **Decreto 351/79, Anexo VI** applies. This guide also cites it as a technical reference outside construction sites.
- **Self-employed electrician hired by a private individual:** both decrees bind the employer with respect to its workers, so they do not literally apply to the private individual or to the self-employed electrician. Ley 24.557 mandatorily covers employees; a self-employed worker is only included if the Executive Branch includes them (art. 2, ap. 2 b). The technical rules of this guide are still the ones that prevent the accident, and the agent applies them to everyone.

## Rules

### ELEC-SEG-1: Every installation is live until proven otherwise
- **Rule:** before any work, identify the exact conductor or circuit, and treat it as energized until you have verified the absence of voltage with an instrument (ELEC-SEG-5). "The MCB (*térmica*) is off", "the owner says he cut it" or the light not turning on proves nothing.
- **Why:** the typical accidents are a misidentified circuit, a wrongly labeled MCB, backfeed from another circuit or someone who switched it back on.
- **How:** identify with the drawing or the label, switch off, lock out and verify at the very spot where you will work, not only at the panel (*tablero*).
- **Verify:** you can say which protective device supplies what you are going to touch, and you checked it by measuring.
- **Don't:** work "carefully" on something energized; rely on the non-contact voltage tester (*buscapolo*, a screwdriver with a neon lamp) as proof of absence of voltage.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 78 a) and b); outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI cap. 14, provisions for LV ("Toda instalación será considerada bajo tensión, mientras no se compruebe lo contrario": every installation shall be considered live until proven otherwise).

### ELEC-SEG-2: On construction sites and in homes work is done dead; live work is a separate trade
- **Rule:** always make the part of the installation where you will work dead. Live work on LV is done only by personnel specifically qualified under the regulation of Res. SRT 3068/2014, with a written procedure, arc-rated PPE and supervision. An ordinary site electrician, a helper or the homeowner do not do live work.
- **Why:** on LV the risk is not only electric shock: a short circuit with the tool in your hand produces an arc that burns the face and hands even if the person does not get "stuck".
- **How:** if you are asked to do something "en caliente" (hot: connect an outlet point (*boca*), change an MCB (*térmica*), add a circuit to the panel), the answer is to switch off and apply lockout/tagout (ELEC-SEG-3). If it cannot be switched off because it is upstream of the user's protection (meter terminal block, service connection (*acometida*)), it belongs to the utility (*distribuidora*).
- **Verify:** no task of the day requires voltage to be present, except measuring (ELEC-SEG-13).
- **Don't:** accept "es un minuto" ("it's just a minute"); work live so as not to leave the building's neighbors without power. If a cut is needed, the cut is coordinated (ELEC-SEG-27).
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 78 d) and art. 86 ("la línea deberá ser desenergizada y controlada": the line shall be de-energized and checked); Res. SRT 3068/2014 (BORA 2014-11-20); outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 1.2.2 (inferred numbering: live work is carried out only by personnel specially qualified by the company, with the qualification endorsed by the head of the Health and Safety Service).

### ELEC-SEG-3: The five golden rules, in order, every time
- **Rule:** before working, lock out and tag out the installation (*consignar*) with the five operations: (1) **disconnection** of every voltage source; (2) **locking** of the switching devices in the open position, with the sign "Prohibido maniobrar" ("Do not operate"); (3) **verification of absence of voltage**; (4) **earthing and short-circuiting** of every point through which voltage could return; (5) **signage and marking off** of the work zone. Only once all five are done is the installation locked out (*consignada*).
- **Why:** locking alone does not authorize work: the decree says so expressly. Each step covers a different failure: verification detects the wrong cut; locking stops someone else switching it back on; earthing discharges a backfeed or an erroneous switching operation.
- **How:** legally a **corte visible** (visible break) is required (isolator open, fuse removed). In panels with PIA (MCB) or switches without a visible break, use devices suitable for isolation with positive position indication (PIA according to IEC 60898-1, or a circuit breaker according to IEC 60947-2 marked as suitable for isolation), locked, and verify the absence of voltage downstream. This is a technical interpretation, not an exception provided for in the decree: if a visible break is available (a fuse that can be removed, an isolator), use that. The order 1-2-3-4 does not change; marking off (5) can be done first if there are adjacent live parts. Procedure on a house or building panel: ELEC-SEG-4.
- **Verify:** for today's work you can name which device you switched off, what you locked it with, which instrument you verified with, how you dealt with possible backfeeds and where the signage is.
- **Don't:** skip verification because "I already switched it off"; use the sign as a substitute for locking when the device can be locked.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 79 a) to d) (a) III: "Separar mediante corte visible", separate by means of a visible break); outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI cap. 14, point 1.1.4 (*consignación*, subsections a-e) and point 1.1.3 (inferred numbering; definition of locking: "no autoriza por sí mismo a trabajar sobre él", it does not by itself authorize work on it); SRT, *Manual de Buenas Prácticas TST en transmisión y distribución* (good practice manual for live work in transmission and distribution), 2023 (five rules, checking the tester before and after). Suitability for isolation according to IEC 60898-1 / IEC 60947-2: technical criterion of this guide, **not verified in the text of the IEC standards**. "Corte efectivo" (effective break): appears only in a 2020 draft regulation (Ingeniería Eléctrica), not approved.

### ELEC-SEG-4: Practical lockout/tagout (LOTO) on a house or building panel (*tablero*)
- **Rule:** each person who works puts **their own** padlock and **their own** tag on the switching device; nobody removes someone else's padlock. If the device cannot be physically locked, the sign "Prohibido maniobrar" ("Do not operate") with the name of the person in charge still goes on (the decree requires it), and this guide asks you to add another barrier (panel locked with a key, a guard, removing the fuse).
- **Why:** the classic cause is someone who "saw the MCB (*térmica*) off and switched it on".
- **How:** (1) notify the occupants and, in a building, the caretaker (*encargado*) or the property management; (2) open the circuit's protective device and the main switch of the unit's sub-panel (TS, *tablero seccional*); (3) fit a lockout device for a PIA or DIN rail breaker (an accessory that clips onto the lever) and the personal padlock; if there are several people, use a lockout hasp; (4) hang the tag: "PELIGRO, NO MANIOBRAR, persona trabajando" ("DANGER, DO NOT OPERATE, person working"), name, date, time and how to reach you; (5) lock the enclosure with its key if it has one; (6) verify (ELEC-SEG-5). When finished, each person removes their own padlock (ELEC-SEG-7).
- **Verify:** there are as many padlocks as people working; the tag is legible; the person working holds the padlock key.
- **Don't:** leave the key in the padlock; lock with electrical tape or a cable tie; break the seal of the meter or of the utility's box to "cut further upstream" (the utility cuts its own side).
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 79 a) II (locking and "Prohibido Maniobrar" label with the name of the person in charge); outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 1.1.3 (inferred numbering; definition of locking, which includes signage); SRT, Manual TST 2023, rule 2 ("la señalización [no] podrá sustituir el bloqueo efectivo": signage may [not] replace effective locking, in MV and HV). Personal padlock, lockout hasp, DIN lockout device and additional barrier: recommendation of this guide (usual LOTO practice), **not verified in a primary source** (Argentine) as a textual requirement.
### ELEC-SEG-5: Verify absence of voltage: test, verify, test again
- **Rule:** verify with an LV two-pole voltage tester (*detector bipolar*, IEC 61243-3) or a multimeter of a suitable category (ELEC-SEG-13) in low-impedance mode (LoZ), testing it **before and after** on a known live source.
- **Why:** an instrument with a flat battery, a broken test lead or the selector on the wrong range shows "0 V" on a live circuit. Conversely, an ordinary multimeter (about 10 MΩ input) shows "ghost voltages" of tens of volts, sometimes more than 100 V, on a disconnected conductor that runs next to other live ones in the same conduit (*cañería*), through capacitive coupling. The two-pole tester and LoZ mode load the circuit and those readings disappear; a real voltage does not.
- **How:** (1) test the instrument with a portable voltage proving unit (tester checker) or on a nearby live socket outlet (*toma*); (2) at the work point measure between phase and neutral, phase and earth (PE) and neutral and earth; on three-phase also between phases. Measure **both** live conductors, because it is common to find phase and neutral swapped from the service connection (*acometida*), or a single-pole MCB (*térmica*) that breaks the neutral and leaves the phase live. If there is no PE (typical case in an old building), measure each conductor against a reliable earthed exposed conductive part or against the other live conductor, and record the missing PE as a defect (see [reviewing-a-panel.md](reviewing-a-panel.md)); (3) test the instrument again on the known source.
- **Verify:** the measurements with the two-pole tester or in LoZ read 0 V, and the instrument worked before and after. If an ordinary multimeter reads a voltage that is not clearly zero, **don't dismiss it as induction**: treat it as voltage present until you confirm with the two-pole tester or with LoZ.
- **Don't:** use a non-contact voltage tester (*buscapolo*), a homemade test lamp or "your finger"; verify only at the panel (*tablero*) and not at the work location; measure N-PE and assume the rest; explain an odd reading with "es inducción" ("it's induction") without having confirmed it.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 78 b); outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 1.1.4 c) and safety equipment 2.1.2 e) (voltage detectors or testers); SRT Manual TST 2023, rule 3 (test the tester before use and check it before and after verification); IEC 61243-3 (LV two-pole voltage testers, up to 1000 V AC / 1500 V DC): the 2014 edition is the latest that could be confirmed, **it was not verified whether there is a later one**. Ghost voltages and LoZ mode: technical data from instrument manufacturers, criterion of this guide.

### ELEC-SEG-6: Earthing and short-circuiting, and the sources that can come back
- **Rule:** look for every source that can re-energize what you are going to touch: generator set, UPS, solar inverter or batteries, a second meter or supply, motors coasting by inertia, capacitors. Switch off and lock out each one. Where voltage can return (lines, long feeders, installations with their own generation), fit an LV earthing and short-circuiting device.
- **Why:** earthing and short-circuiting turns a reconnection or a backfeed into a protective device tripping, instead of a shock to the worker.
- **How:** the earthing (PAT) and short-circuiting device is connected **first to earth** and then to the conductors, always with gloves, and removed in reverse order. On an indoor final circuit with no backfeed source at all, locking plus verification is the usual practice; if there is any generation or UPS, fit it. Discharge capacitors (power factor correction banks, variable speed drives) before touching them, waiting the time stated by the manufacturer.
- **Verify:** you made the list of sources and each one is switched off and locked; the PAT device has a cross-section suitable for the short-circuit current at that point.
- **Don't:** improvise the earthing with a thin wire or an alligator clip. With solar panels, believe that with the AC isolator off the system is dead: the direct current side (strings and wiring up to the inverter) is live whenever there is light, even if you cut the grid. It is hundreds of volts DC (each module gives about 40-50 V open circuit; 8 to 20 in series, about 320-1000 V) and a DC arc does not go out by itself: never open a string box or an MC4 connector under load. An on-grid inverter stops injecting AC when the grid fails (anti-islanding), but a hybrid inverter with batteries can keep supplying the backup panel: also switch off and lock out the batteries and the backup output (see [special-installations.md](special-installations.md) and ELEC-SEG-38).
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 79 a) III; outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 1.1.4 d) ("en todos los puntos por donde pudiera llegar tensión": at all points through which voltage could arrive) and 2.1.3 (inferred numbering; discharging the installation); SRT Manual TST 2023, rule 4 (connect to earth first, cross-section sufficient for the short-circuit current). Anti-islanding: requirement for connecting distributed generation (Ley 27.424 and its regulations; test IEC 62116), **article not verified**. Criterion for domestic final circuits with no backfeed: professional practice, **not verified in a primary source**.

### ELEC-SEG-7: Restore service only after checking everything, in person
- **Rule:** before re-energizing, the person in charge of the work checks in person that the earthing devices, tools and leftover material were removed; that the personnel moved away and know the zone is no longer protected; and that the insulation resistance test was done (on construction sites, Decreto 911/96 art. 79 c) IV always requires it before restoring; Anexo VI of 351/79, point 2.1.3 c), does not mention it). Only then does each person remove their padlock, the signs are taken down and the circuit is switched on.
- **Why:** people switch on onto a forgotten tool, a loose conductor or a coworker who still has a hand inside.
- **How:** count the people; walk the zone; test the insulation with the precautions of ELEC-SEG-22 (see [testing-and-commissioning.md](testing-and-commissioning.md)); switch on the main switch first and then the circuits, one at a time; test the RCD (*disyuntor* / *diferencial*, ID) with its button. Outside construction sites, for a minor change without touching the conductors' insulation (for example, an identical socket outlet), this guide's criterion is still to test if you have the instrument.
- **Verify:** no one else's padlocks or PAT devices are left in place; the insulation test was recorded; the RCD trips with its test button.
- **Don't:** switch on "to test" with people working; remove the padlock of a coworker who left (find them; if they cannot be found, the person in charge decides with a written procedure).
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 79 c) I-IV ("IV. Se ha efectuado la prueba de resistencia de aislación": the insulation resistance test has been carried out) and d); outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 2.1.3 c) (inferred numbering; restoring service).

### ELEC-SEG-8: Safety distances to uninsulated live parts
- **Rule:** keep the minimum distances between any uninsulated live part and the body or uninsulated tools, in the most unfavorable situation (swaying, a dropped tool, movement of a bar or a pipe).
- **Why:** on MV the discharge jumps without contact; on construction sites the typical case is the pipe, the rebar, the scaffold or the crane boom that gets close to the overhead line along the sidewalk.
- **How:** values from Decreto 911/96 art. 75 (table 1), for **trained electrical personnel** working on the installation:

  | Voltage level | Minimum distance |
  |---|---|
  | up to 24 V | no restriction |
  | over 24 V up to 1 kV | 0.80 m (1) |
  | over 1 kV up to 33 kV | 0.80 m |
  | over 33 kV up to 66 kV | 0.90 m (2) |
  | over 66 kV up to 132 kV | 1.50 m |
  | over 132 kV up to 150 kV | 1.65 m |
  | over 150 kV up to 220 kV | 2.10 m |
  | over 220 kV up to 330 kV | 2.90 m |
  | over 330 kV up to 500 kV | 3.60 m |

  (1) can be reduced to 0.60 m with suitable insulating screens over the live parts and no earthed metal grilles in between; in 911/96 the reduction appears only in this row, so on MV it is not reduced. (2) note of the decree for work at a distance; work on lines above 1 kV belongs to the utility or the transmission company. Anexo VI of 351/79 has the same table except for the first step (0-50 V, no restriction) and puts the 0.60 m note in the 1-33 kV row; on construction sites 911/96 applies.

  **The table does not apply to scaffolds, cranes, concrete mixers, bars or site personnel.** It does not account for the cable swaying in the wind, the sag that changes with temperature or the inertia of a crane boom. Criterion of this guide: no less than **3 m** between any part of the machine, the scaffold or the load and an MV line. If that is not possible, ask the utility to cut or shield the line before starting; with mobile equipment near MV, the decree requires de-energizing.
- **Verify:** before erecting the scaffold, you located the overhead lines and the distance in the worst case (with the load or the bar raised).
- **Don't:** work near MV trusting that "the cables are covered"; move long pipes or sections under a line.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 75 (table 1) and art. 86 (de-energize with mobile equipment near MV); Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 1.1.5 (inferred numbering; safety distances). The 3 m is a criterion of this guide, **not an Argentine norm**; as an international reference, OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1408 requires about 3 m (10 ft) for cranes near lines up to 50 kV.

### ELEC-SEG-9: Who may do what
- **Rule:** anyone who works on electrical installations must be trained by their employer (hazards, use of safety equipment and tools, rescue of electrocuted persons, first aid, fire and evacuation) and authorized for that task. Live work also requires the specific qualification (ELEC-SEG-2). Signing the design or the installation is something else: it is the professional license that each jurisdiction sets.
- **Why:** Decreto 911/96 requires every operation on high, medium and low voltage to be done by specialized personnel with responsibility for the task.
- **How:** on a site there is a single person in charge of the electrical work per task, who orders the switching off, the locking and the restoration. Helpers do not operate panels on their own. For the license (*matrícula*) or the installer registry, see [regulations-and-permits.md](regulations-and-permits.md).
- **Verify:** it is clear who is in charge of each task, and it is recorded in the daily report (*parte*) or in the site file.
- **Don't:** send the helper "a bajar la térmica que dice cocina" ("to switch off the MCB labeled kitchen"); accept the homeowner restoring the service.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 76 and art. 86 (last paragraph); outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 1.2.2 (inferred numbering; training and qualification for live work: knowledge, experience, consent, psychophysical fitness, low accident rate) and point 1.2.3 (role of a single "Responsable del Trabajo", person in charge of the work).

### ELEC-SEG-10: Insulating gloves: correct class and inflation test before each use
- **Rule:** insulating gloves are used to handle LV cables, even if the cable insulation looks perfect. On LV (220/380 V) the minimum is class 00 (up to 500 V AC) or class 0 (up to 1000 V AC), certified to IEC 60903 / IRAM 3604. Before each use: visual inspection and inflation test.
- **Why:** an invisible pinhole cancels the protection; a rubber cleaning glove or a split leather glove insulates nothing.
- **How:** class reference (IEC 60903):

  | Class | Label color | Maximum use voltage (AC) | Test voltage (AC) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 00 | beige | 500 V | 2 500 V |
  | 0 | red | 1 000 V | 5 000 V |
  | 1 | white | 7 500 V | 10 000 V |
  | 2 | yellow | 17 000 V | 20 000 V |
  | 3 | green | 26 500 V | 30 000 V |
  | 4 | orange | 36 000 V | 40 000 V |

  **Inflation test:** roll the cuff to trap air (or use a glove inflator), squeeze and look, listen and feel whether it leaks anywhere; check especially between the fingers (the crotch). Wear leather over-gloves on top if there is a mechanical hazard. Store them in their bag, unfolded, away from sunlight and solvents.
- **Verify:** label with class, date of manufacture and date of last test legible; the inflation test does not leak. Classes 1 to 4 are not used if more than 6 months have passed without a dielectric test; classes 00 and 0 are checked by visual inspection and air leak test.
- **Don't:** use a glove that has a cut or a patch, or that was treated with a talc the manufacturer does not recommend; use a glove of a class below the working voltage; class 00 covers up to 500 V, but on 380 V three-phase panels the margin is small: prefer class 0.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 86 ("obligatorio el uso de guantes aislantes para manipular los cables de baja tensión": the use of insulating gloves to handle low-voltage cables is mandatory); outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 2.1.2 a); IEC 60903 (classes and voltages, as summarized by INSST, data sheet "Guantes aislantes de la electricidad" (electrical insulating gloves), rev. 3); IRAM 3604 as the counterpart of IEC 60903 according to tester manufacturers (Reflex, Ingeniería Eléctrica 2021/2023): **not verified in the IRAM catalog**. The recommendation of class 0 for 380 V is a criterion of this guide.

### ELEC-SEG-11: Footwear, helmet, face protection and arc-rated clothing
- **Rule:** anyone who works on panels wears safety footwear, a helmet, safety glasses or a face shield, and cotton or flame-resistant clothing (never synthetic clothing that melts); no rings, bracelets, chains or dangling accessories. Electrically insulating footwear and a helmet with no metal parts are recommended. Near a main panel, a switchgear cubicle or a feeder with a high short-circuit current, the clothing and the face shield must be arc-rated according to what the health and safety service defines. At height, a helmet with a chin strap (ELEC-SEG-18).
- **Why:** an electric arc burns with radiation and molten metal; synthetic clothing melts onto the skin. The face and hands are what get injured most. A ring or a chain touching a busbar makes a short circuit and gets red hot.
- **How:** shirt tucked into the trousers, sleeves down and buttoned to the top; no rings, watches or metal bracelets; arc-rated face shield for switching operations on main panels. Use an insulating stool or mat when you operate a panel with a wet or metal floor. The employer supplies the PPE, and it is recorded with the record of Res. SRT 299/11 (on paper or digital).
- **Verify:** PPE certified, in good condition, the correct size, with a delivery record.
- **Don't:** work in wet sneakers or in flip-flops on a wet floor; wear a nylon jacket in front of a panel.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 99 (prohibits "elementos y accesorios (bufandas, pulseras, cadenas, corbatas, etc.)", items and accessories (scarves, bracelets, chains, ties, etc.) that add risk), art. 98 et seq. (provision and use of PPE), art. 111 (safety footwear: toe cap, chemical or thermal protection; does not mention electrical insulation); Res. SRT 61/2023 Anexo I point 20 (chin strap at height); Res. SRT 3068/2014 Annex points 10.1 and 12.2 (arc-rated PPE, as cited by the SRT Manual TST 2023, which also gives the clothing guidelines); Res. SRT 299/2011; outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 2.1.2 (helmet, visor, footwear; face protectors; insulating stools or mats). Insulating footwear (EN 50321), helmet with no metal parts (IRAM 3620) and arc-rated clothing (IEC 61482-2): recommendation of this guide based on product standards, **not verified in a primary source**.
### ELEC-SEG-12: Insulated tools and nothing conductive near live parts
- **Rule:** hand tools for panels (*tableros*) (screwdrivers, pliers, cutters, wrenches) are insulated and certified to IEC 60900 (double triangle symbol and "1000 V"). Near parts that may be live, do not use metal ladders, metal tape measures, oil cans or other conductive items.
- **Why:** even when working dead, the insulated tool is the last barrier if the verification failed or something was re-energized.
- **How:** check the insulation before use: no cuts, burns or bare spots; if the inner layer of a different color shows, discard the tool. Use a fiberglass or dry wooden ladder.
- **Verify:** every tool in the bag has a legible IEC 60900 marking and intact insulation.
- **Don't:** insulate tools with tape or "homemade" heat-shrink; use an aluminum ladder to work on a panel or a ceiling outlet point (*boca*).
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 78 c); outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 2.1.2 f) and LV provisions c); IEC 60900 (tools for live work up to 1000 V AC / 1500 V DC, tested at a much higher voltage; data from manufacturers such as EGA Master): **the standard was not consulted in its primary text**.

### ELEC-SEG-13: Measuring instruments: correct category and use
- **Rule:** the multimeter or clamp meter used on panels must be certified to IEC 61010 with category **CAT III** as a minimum for panels and fixed circuits, and **CAT IV** if you are going to measure at the meter pillar (*pilar*), the service connection (*acometida*) or the meter terminal block. To verify absence of voltage, the IEC 61243-3 two-pole voltage tester (*detector bipolar*) is the most reliable. No Argentine norm that was read requires this in these words: it is the recommendation of this guide.
- **Why:** the category indicates how much transient overvoltage the instrument withstands without exploding in your hand. A CAT II instrument is for things that plug in, not for the panel.
- **How:** compare **category and voltage together**: what counts is the impulse voltage it withstands. CAT III 600 V and CAT IV 300 V both withstand 6 kV; for 220/380 V panels use at least one of those two. Test leads with finger guards and caps (exposed tip of 4 mm or less in CAT III/IV), leads without cuts, selector on V AC before placing the probes; never measure voltage with the selector on current or resistance.
- **Verify:** the instrument has the CAT marking and the voltage on the front, the test leads are sound and have the same category or higher, the internal fuse is the original one.
- **Don't:** measure at the meter pillar with a cheap multimeter (*tester*) with no category; use test leads from another instrument with a lower category.
- **Source:** IEC 61010-1 and 61010-031 (measurement categories and impulse voltages, according to Fluke technical guides "Clasificaciones de categoría IEC" (IEC category ratings)); IEC 61243-3:2014. Recommended minimum and tip length: manufacturer practice and criterion of this guide, **not verified in the text of the standard**.

### ELEC-SEG-14: The site panel (*tablero de obra*) is built like a permanent installation
- **Rule:** the temporary site installation is designed and built to the standard of a permanent installation, according to the AEA Reglamentación (section 771-B.7, construction sites), with: an accessible all-pole main switch; overcurrent protection per circuit (PIA, MCB); a **30 mA RCD (*disyuntor* / *interruptor diferencial*)** for all socket outlet and lighting circuits; all exposed conductive parts earthed; an enclosure with a degree of protection suitable for outdoor use, locked with a key and with the door closed in normal use.
- **Why:** on a construction site tools get wet, cables get stepped on and the floor is damp earth: it is the place where insulation fails most and where the RCD saves lives. In a TT system the MCB (*térmica*) generally does not trip on an earth fault, because the fault current is low: the RCD does.
- **How:** worked example of why the RCD (ID) is needed (guide of Res. SRT 900/15): with phase voltage U = 220 V, utility system earth Rb = 1 Ω and site earth rod (*jabalina*) Ra = 40 Ω, a tool with a fault to the casing produces Id = U / (Rb + Ra) = 220 / (1 + 40) = **5.37 A**, and the casing is at Ut = Id × Ra = 5.37 × 40 = **214.6 V**. A 20 A B curve MCB needs between 3 and 5 × In, that is between 60 and 100 A, to trip instantaneously: **it does not trip**. A 30 mA RCD disconnects in milliseconds. And with a 30 mA RCD, Ra × IΔn = 40 × 0.03 = 1.2 V, far below the limit voltage of 24 V.
  **Watch out:** the RCD condition would be met up to Ra = 24 V / 0.03 A = 800 Ω, but the regulations require Ra ≤ 40 Ω (preferably less) as a backup, because the RCD can fail or be bypassed. Having an RCD is no reason to neglect the earth rod. Calculate other cases with `gateway electricidad earth`. RCD type and selectivity: ELEC-SEG-35. Panel details in [panels.md](panels.md), protection in [protection.md](protection.md), earth rod in [earthing.md](earthing.md).
- **Verify:** RCD test button at the start of every working day; continuity of the PE up to each socket outlet; earth measurement with a protocol (Res. SRT 900/15, valid 12 months); RCD test with an IEC 61557-6 instrument (does not trip at 0.5 × IΔn; trips in 300 ms or less at 1 × IΔn, in 150 ms or less at 2 × IΔn and in 40 ms or less at 5 × IΔn).
- **Don't:** a site panel made from a wooden box and loose MCBs; "bypass" (*puentear*) the RCD because it trips (ELEC-SEG-22); use the water pipe as earth.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 86 (permanent installation according to AEA, earthing of exposed conductive parts and automatic disconnection devices); Res. SRT 900/2015 and its practical guide (SRT, p. 9): numerical example, maximum Ra of 40 Ω in TT conditional on residual current protection with IΔn ≤ 300 mA (citing the RAEA), MCB tripping current that "no se logra en general" (is generally not reached) in TT, RCD ≤ 30 mA mandatory on lighting and socket outlet circuits up to 32 A, RCD tests, application of section 771-B.7 to construction sites. Outside construction sites, as a technical reference: Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 2.1.2 k) (high-sensitivity RCDs as safety equipment) and point 3.3.2 c) (inferred numbering; 30 mA or 300 mA RCD operating in 0.03 s or less: the 30 mA one is for protection of persons; the 300 mA one protects against fire or serves as a selective incoming device). Detailed requirements of 771-B.7 (IP rating, type of socket outlets): **not verified in the AEA text (paid standard)**; see the table and clause in the edition in force.

### ELEC-SEG-15: Site cables, extension cords and socket outlets
- **Rule:** extension cords (*prolongaciones*) are made of flexible double-insulated cable suitable for heavy duty, with no splices, with a plug and a socket outlet at their ends. Indoors, cable runs are suspended or embedded at 2.40 m or more; anything that crosses a path for people or vehicles is buried, raised or mechanically protected. For the site panel socket outlets and the extension cords, IEC 60309 industrial socket outlets with a cover are recommended (or whatever the section 771-B.7 in force requires): blue (200-250 V) for 220 V single-phase; red (380-480 V) for 380 V three-phase.
- **Why:** a cable run over by a wheelbarrow or crushed by a door is the most common failure on site; domestic plugs (*fichas*) break and leave contacts exposed.
- **How:** prefer cable with a rubber or polychloroprene sheath, type H07RN-F (CENELEC designation, EN 50525-2-21; IEC 60245-4 equivalent type 60245 IEC 66) for outdoor use; PVC "cable taller" (flexible cord, *tipo taller*) is for light duty under cover. Fully unwind the reel when the load is large. Overhead lines that cross site roads go at 8 m or more, with a protective mesh.
- **Verify:** no taped splices, no cut sheath, no conductors visible at the plugs; the PE reaches the socket outlet (measure continuity).
- **Don't:** cables on the floor in areas with water; bare wire ends ("patitas") pushed into the socket outlet; extending an extension cord with another; domestic power strips (*zapatillas*) on site.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 86 (indoors at 2.40 m or more, crossings at 8 m or more with mesh, prohibition of bare conductors without covering; materials "que respondan a las normas de validez internacional", that comply with internationally valid standards); outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 3 (double-insulated cables for portable tools, limit the extension by using nearby socket outlets). IEC 60309 socket outlets and colors (IEC 60309-2) and type H07RN-F: recommendation of this guide with manufacturer data, **not verified in a primary source** as an Argentine requirement.

### ELEC-SEG-16: Portable tools and damp or wet locations
- **Rule:** portable electric tools have mechanical protection, insulation in good condition, accessible metal parts earthed or double insulation, and a trigger that cuts off when released; they are unplugged when not in use. In wet locations (pits, tanks, freshly poured concrete slabs, rain), use safety extra-low voltage with a safety transformer (the value is set by the HyS (health and safety) officer, never more than 24 V), a 1:1 separation transformer, or battery tools.
- **Why:** in dry and damp environments the safety voltage is 24 V; in wet ones the health and safety officer sets a value that cannot exceed SELV (MBTS). With 220 V and a wet body, the current that flows can be fatal.
- **How:** portable lamps at 24 V or less with a safety transformer; never autotransformers; double-insulated tools (double square symbol) do not have a PE, class I tools do. The separation transformer supplies **a single appliance**: with two tools on the same secondary, a double fault puts a dangerous voltage between the two casings. The transformer (separation or MBTS) stays **outside** the pit, tank or cistern; in confined conductive locations, prefer MBTS or battery for the lighting and the tool (ELEC-SEG-30). The person in charge checks the tools before use.
- **Verify:** cable and plug sound, trigger that springs back by itself, casing without cracks; safety transformer in use in wet locations, outside the enclosure and with one appliance per separation secondary.
- **Don't:** use an angle grinder with a cracked casing or a plug without earth; leave the tool plugged in on a wet floor; put the transformer inside the tank.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 74 a) (MBTS up to 24 V with respect to earth in dry and damp environments; in wet ones the HyS officer sets it without exceeding MBTS) and art. 208 (mechanical protection, dielectric conditions and cut-off when the operator's action stops). Outside construction sites, as a technical reference: Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 1, Definitions (safety voltage; the sub-number, 1.1.2 or 1.2, could not be confirmed in the copy consulted, same as in [earthing.md](earthing.md)), point 2.1.2 h) to j) (portable lamps, safety transformers of 24 V maximum, 1:1 transformers, autotransformers prohibited) and point 3 (portable tools). One appliance per separation transformer and source outside the enclosure: IEC 60364-4-41 clause 413 and IEC 60364-7-706, **not verified in the AEA**.

### ELEC-SEG-17: Site paperwork: site safety file (*legajo técnico*) and Safety Program
- **Rule:** on a site with construction employers you must notify the ART (workers' compensation insurer) of the start of work and have a Safety Program and a site safety file covering the risks of each stage, including electrical ones (temporary installation, work on panels, nearby lines). If there are several contractors (electrician, plumber, masonry), the main contractor or the client coordinates a single program.
- **Why:** the program defines who checks the temporary installation, the training and the work permits; in an inspection it is the first thing requested.
- **How:** the site safety file is prepared and signed by the site's health and safety officer. Include: drawing or sketch of the temporary installation, earthing protocol (Res. SRT 900/15), PPE records (Res. 299/11), training, the documentation for work at height (Res. 61/2023: PTS, rescue plan and checks if working at 6 m or more) and, if there is excavation or demolition, the survey of buried installations and the service cut requests (Res. 550/11 and 503/14). If you are a private individual hiring an independent electrician, ask for proof of coverage (personal accident insurance) before starting: in the private sector, the ART is mandatory only for employees.
- **Verify:** the site safety file is on site, up to date and signed.
- **Don't:** submit a "template" program that does not mention the site's actual electrical installation.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 20 (site safety file signed by the health and safety officer); Res. SRT 231/96 Anexo I art. 3 (contents of the file); Res. SRT 51/97 (notice of start of work and Safety Program), 35/98 (single coordinated program), 319/99 (repetitive and short-duration works); Res. SRT 550/11 and 503/14; Res. SRT 61/2023 art. 3 and Anexo I points 2, 9 and 24. Validity of 231, 51, 35 and 319: cited in the recitals of Res. SRT 61/2023 (BORA 2023-12-06). Ley 24.557 art. 2 (mandatory coverage of employees; self-employed workers only if the PEN (Executive Branch) includes them). Insurance for the independent electrician: recommendation of this guide, not a norm.

### ELEC-SEG-18: Work at height in electrical tasks
- **Rule:** every edge with a level difference of 2 m or more gets 1 m guardrails with an intermediate rail at 50 cm and a 15 cm toe board; platforms of tubular or wooden scaffolds (except trestles) get those guardrails **also below 2 m**. From 2.50 m a fall arrest system is mandatory: 911/96 says "cinturón" (belt), but Res. SRT 61/2023 specifies it as a **safety harness**; a work positioning belt does not arrest falls. If the task is of short duration and the HyS officer judges that it is not high risk, collective protection may not be mandatory; in that case the minimum is a harness anchored to a fixed point, two workers present and direct supervision by the person in charge of the task. At 6 m or more, add a daily PTS, a rescue plan, an anti-trauma device and a daily equipment check.
- **Why:** electrical tasks combine two risks: a mild shock on a ladder ends in a serious fall. In a 4-story building, the facade, the landing (*palier*) or the light shaft easily exceed 6 m.
- **How:**
  - **Fall arrest:** full body harness, lanyard with energy absorber, strong anchor above the worker and independent of the platform, lanyard as short as the task allows, connectors of 22 kN or more; double lanyard if you have to change anchors; system compatible with the electrical hazard (no parts that can make contact with conductors; wet ropes can conduct). On a MEWP (*PEMP*, elevating work platform), harness with shock absorber to the manufacturer's anchor.
  - **At 6 m or more** (nearest lower level): HyS safe work procedure, daily Permiso de Trabajo Seguro (PTS, safe work permit), Plan de Rescate en Altura (rescue plan at height) in the Safety Program, daily check of the equipment and its anchors with the Anexo III checklists, harness with anti-trauma device, structures with calculations signed by a licensed professional.
  - **Helmet with chin strap** for all work at height.
  - **Ladders:** fiberglass (not metal, ELEC-SEG-12), rungs at 30 cm or less, resting on firm ground and secured at the top and bottom; a single ladder used to reach another level extends 1 m beyond the landing point; a stepladder does not exceed 6 m and has an opening limiter; an extension ladder overlaps 1 m or more. Res. 61/2023 says portable ladders are used only for going up and down and prohibits moving along on them: for tasks that are not brief, use a scaffold or a rolling ladder (*burrito*) with guardrails on its 3 exposed sides, and ask HyS what it allows for working from the ladder.
  - **Scaffolds:** wooden ones cannot exceed 3 m in height; signs "EQUIPO NO HABILITADO" ("EQUIPMENT NOT APPROVED FOR USE") while it is being erected and "EQUIPO LIBRADO A USO" ("EQUIPMENT RELEASED FOR USE") signed when it is ready; wheels with brakes on mobile ones.
  - First you switch off and lock out (ELEC-SEG-3), then you climb. Rescue plan: ELEC-SEG-33.
- **Verify:** equipment checklist (Anexo III of Res. 61/2023) complete; harness without cuts or open seams; anchor point identified; at 6 m or more, the day's PTS signed.
- **Don't:** work on a ceiling outlet point standing on a chair or a bucket; tie the lanyard to an electrical conduit or a cable tray; use a waist belt as fall arrest; move yourself by "walking" the ladder.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 arts. 52, 54, 57 ("Cuando la tarea sea de corta duración y no presente un elevado riesgo a juicio del responsable de Higiene y Seguridad...": when the task is of short duration and does not present a high risk in the judgment of the health and safety officer...), 112 (belts from 2.50 m, lanyard as short as possible), 214-216, 221, 223; Res. SRT 61/2023 arts. 1-8 (BORA 2023-12-06) and Anexo I (IF-2023-144412295-APN-GP#SRT, official PDF read): points 2 (PTS and procedure at 6 m or more), 9 (rescue plan), 20 (chin strap), 25 (guardrails 1 m / 50 cm / 15 cm from 2 m), 26 (guardrails on scaffolds under 2 m), 27 (wood up to 3 m), 28 (signs), 29 (daily check above 6 m), 30 (anti-trauma at 6 m or more), 31 (calculation by a licensed professional), 35 (double lanyard), 37 (22 kN), 39 (independent anchor), 44 and 46 (compatibility with the electrical hazard, wet ropes), 54 (brakes), 56 (rolling ladders with guardrails), 61 (portable ladders only for going up and down), 89 (MEWP). Energy absorber and full body harness as the fall arrest standard: product standards, **not verified in a primary source** (Argentine).
### ELEC-SEG-19: Cutting, drilling or chasing where there may be installations
- **Rule:** before cutting a slab, drilling a wall or chasing a channel, find out where the electrical conduit (*cañería*) runs (and the gas and water pipes) and cut the power to the circuits in that area.
- **Why:** an angle grinder or drill that bites into a live cable electrocutes the worker or burns them with the arc, and the damage to the conduit leaves a hidden fault.
- **How:** look at the drawing or, if there is none, survey: conduits leave the boxes in straight vertical or horizontal runs (see [conductors-and-conduits.md](conductors-and-conduits.md)); use a cable and metal detector as an aid; in slabs, conduits usually run between the ceiling outlet points (*bocas*). With the area's circuits cut and locked out, use double-insulated tools and gloves. In the common areas of a building, what runs through shafts and landings (riser (*columna montante*), common services) is not cut from a unit: ELEC-SEG-27. On sites with excavation, record the buried installations and ask the utility (*distribuidora*) to cut the supply.
- **Verify:** you know which circuits you cut and you verified it at a socket outlet or an outlet point in the area.
- **Don't:** rely only on the detector (it does not see everything); drill directly above, below or beside a socket outlet or a switch (in its vertical or horizontal line).
- **Source:** Res. SRT 503/14 Anexo (survey underground installations, interference drawings, supply-cut requests in the site safety file (*legajo*)); Decreto 911/96 art. 86 (mark areas with underground cables). The rest is site practice, **not verified in a primary source** as normative text.

### ELEC-SEG-20: No open panel and no live part within reach on the site
- **Rule:** panels (distribution boards, *tableros*) with the cover and inner cover (dead front, *contratapa*) in place and the door locked; boxes with their lids; unconnected cables individually insulated and with the circuit cut and locked out; no bare conductors without a cover.
- **Why:** people who are not electricians (bricklayers, painters, plasterers) move around the site, and whatever is left exposed gets touched.
- **How:** at the end of the workday, leave everything closed and marked (ELEC-SEG-37); circuits being worked on stay cut at the panel, with padlock and tag. Protection by obstacles is not removed before the voltage is cut.
- **Verify:** closing walk-through: no open box with voltage, no panel without its inner cover.
- **Don't:** leave "the cable end with tape" in a box on an energized circuit.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 86 (prohibition of bare conductors without a cover or mesh); outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 3 (protection against direct contact by distance, insulation and obstacles; forbids removing obstacles before removing the voltage). Locking and daily walk-through: this guide's criterion.

### ELEC-SEG-21: Generator set: always with a changeover switch, never feed into the grid
- **Rule:** a generator set (*grupo electrógeno*) is connected to a fixed installation only through an interlocked changeover switch or source transfer switch (*conmutador* / *inversor de fuentes*: mains, off, generator) that separates all live conductors, phase and neutral, from the utility network before the generator is connected. Without a changeover switch, the generator feeds only loads plugged directly into it.
- **Why:** without a changeover switch, the generator energizes the service connection and, through the transformer, the street network: the utility worker working "dead" on the line is electrocuted. Also, when the mains returns, the generator and the mains end up in parallel and are destroyed.
- **How:** a three-position manual changeover switch, or an automatic one with mechanical and electrical interlock, installed by a qualified electrician (*electricista habilitado*); outdoors, ventilated (carbon monoxide), and refueled with the engine cold and off. **Generator neutral:** many portable generators have a floating neutral (not bonded to earth or to the frame). Because the changeover switch cuts the mains neutral, if the generator neutral is not referenced to earth according to the installation's system (in TT, connected to a service earth electrode), an earth fault does not drive current through the RCD (*disyuntor* / *diferencial*, ID): the RCD does not trip and the exposed metal part stays dangerous on a second fault. Driving an earth rod (*jabalina*) only at the frame does not solve it. Check it with the generator manual and by testing the RCD with the generator as the source; the qualified installer decides. See [special-installations.md](special-installations.md).
- **Verify:** with the mains cut and locked out upstream of the changeover switch (main panel switch open, with padlock), the generator running and the changeover switch at "generator", measure with the two-pole voltage tester (*detector bipolar*) at the changeover switch's mains input terminals: it must read 0 V. If it shows voltage, the changeover switch backfeeds the grid and must not be used. Also check that the interlock prevents going from "mains" to "generator" without passing through 0, and that the RCD trips with its button and with the RCD tester powered from the generator.
- **Don't:** the cable with two male plugs to "plug the generator into a socket outlet in the house"; switch off only the main MCB (*térmica*) instead of using a changeover switch; measure at the mains terminals with the mains present and draw conclusions.
- **Source:** **Not verified in a primary source:** the requirement to avoid paralleling with the grid is in IEC 60364-5-55 clause 551 (LV generating sets), the basis of the equivalent section of AEA 90364 Parte 5; the AEA text and each utility's supply regulations could not be read. Generator neutral reference and the test with the mains locked out: this guide's technical criterion. Decreto 911/96 art. 79 a) (cut and lock out every source); SRT Manual TST 2023, rule 2 ("Prevenir cualquier posible realimentación o ingreso de una tensión de retorno", prevent any possible backfeed or entry of a return voltage).

### ELEC-SEG-22: If a protective device trips for no known cause, inspect before resetting
- **Rule:** when an MCB or an RCD trips and nobody knows why, the installation is inspected before resetting. Protective devices are not bypassed, not replaced with higher-current ones, and the RCD is not removed "because it trips".
- **Why:** the trip is the symptom of a real fault (damaged insulation, moisture, a shorted tool); resetting blindly exposes the next person who touches it, and closing onto a short circuit produces an arc (ELEC-SEG-29).
- **How:** disconnect all loads, reset, reconnect them one at a time. If it trips with no loads, cut, apply lockout/tagout and test the circuit's insulation: with loads and electronics disconnected, nobody touching the circuit, and discharging it when you finish, because the megohmmeter applies 500 V DC and the wiring stays charged (see [testing-and-commissioning.md](testing-and-commissioning.md)). If it is the RCD that trips, also check for neutral-to-earth faults and neutrals shared with another circuit, which are the most frequent causes. Diagnosis in [troubleshooting-common-cases.md](troubleshooting-common-cases.md).
- **Verify:** the cause was found and corrected, or the circuit stays out of service and locked out.
- **Don't:** bypass with a wire, tie the lever, fit a 32 A MCB where a 16 A one went; test insulation with an electronic device connected.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 87 (protective devices operating for no known cause require a detailed inspection before resetting; repairs must restore the original characteristics). Precautions for the insulation test and typical causes of RCD tripping: professional practice, this guide's criterion.

### ELEC-SEG-23: Electrocuted person: cut, don't touch, call, resuscitate
- **Rule:** with an electrocuted person: (1) **don't touch them** while they may still be in contact with the voltage; (2) **cut the power** (MCB, RCD or the panel's main switch; on the site, the main switch of the site panel); (3) **call emergency services**; (4) if they do not respond and are not breathing normally, **start CPR** and use an AED if there is one; (5) anyone who received a shock goes to the doctor, even if they seem fine. If they are at height, ELEC-SEG-33.
- **Why:** the rescuer who touches the energized victim becomes the second victim. The current can cause cardiac arrest or arrhythmias that appear hours later, and electrical burns are deeper than they look.
- **How:**
  - If you cannot cut (in LV), separate them with a dry insulating object (wooden stick, plastic chair, dry rope), standing on something dry and insulating. In **medium or high voltage** (street line, transformer), **don't approach**: keep everyone away and call the utility and emergency services.
  - Numbers: **107** (medical emergencies, SAME in CABA and in many jurisdictions), **911** (unified emergencies in the Province of Buenos Aires and other jurisdictions), **100** (fire department). Keep the number for the area and the ART's (workers' compensation insurer) number written down on the site.
  - Adult CPR: compressions in the center of the chest, 100 to 120 per minute, 5 to 6 cm deep, without stopping until help arrives or the AED says so.
  - **AED (*DEA*):** Ley 27.159 requires public spaces and private spaces open to the public to have an AED (Decreto 402/2022 sets at least one where more than 1000 people pass per day). A site generally does not fall under that obligation: locate the nearest AED in advance (shop, public building, club) and write it down next to the emergency numbers.
  - Burns: with the power already cut, cool with clean water at room temperature, don't apply creams or ice, don't peel off stuck clothing; cover with a clean dressing. If clothing catches fire: a blanket, or roll the person on the floor.
  - Before re-energizing, the installation stays locked out until it is inspected (ELEC-SEG-22).
- **Verify:** the site has a first-aid kit, the emergency number and the ART number in view, the location of the nearest AED, and at least one person trained in CPR per shift.
- **Don't:** pull the victim with your hands; throw water (on them or on a panel) while the installation may still be energized; let them "go home because they're fine".
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 76 and, outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 1.2 (mandatory training in aiding electric shock victims and first aid); SRT Manual TST 2023 (separate with insulating objects, check breathing, CPR, notify the medical service, blanket or roll; recommends an AED); Ley 27.159 arts. 1, 2 d) and 5 (AED in spaces open to the public, number set by the enforcement authority); Decreto 402/2022 (BORA 2022-07-15; threshold of 1000 people per day according to its text as quoted in secondary sources, **article not read**). Numbers 107/911 by jurisdiction: Hospital Garrahan first-aid guide (secondary source), **they vary by province**. CPR parameters: international resuscitation guidelines, **not verified in a primary source** (Argentine).

### ELEC-SEG-24: After the accident: ART, record and don't touch the scene
- **Rule:** every electrical accident involving a worker is reported: the worker notifies the employer, and the employer reports it to the ART (workers' compensation insurer) and to the SRT. The site's health and safety officer is also notified. The installation involved stays locked out and unmodified until it is investigated.
- **Why:** the right to ART benefits starts with the report, and without an investigation the same fault will injure someone else.
- **How:** call the ART emergency number shown on the card or on the site sign; write down the time, place, task, circuit, witnesses; take photos without touching anything; record the event in the safety file and correct the cause before going back to work.
- **Verify:** there is a record of the report and of the investigation with its corrective measure.
- **Don't:** "fix" the panel before anyone sees what happened; skip the report because "it was minor".
- **Source:** Ley 24.557 art. 31, ap. 2 c) (employers "Denunciarán a la ART y a la SRT los accidentes y enfermedades profesionales", shall report accidents and occupational diseases to the ART and the SRT) and ap. 3 e) (the worker reports them to the employer); art. 43 (benefits are granted from the report); Decreto 911/96 art. 20 (*legajo técnico*, site safety file) for the record. Report procedure and deadlines (current SRT resolutions): **not verified in a primary source**.

### ELEC-SEG-25: Daily check before starting
- **Rule:** every day, before using the site installation or opening a panel, the person in charge does the daily check (section "Daily checklist") and does not authorize the task if anything fails. The decree requires periodic inspection; the daily frequency is this guide's criterion.
- **Why:** on a site, conditions change from one day to the next: it rains, a scaffold is moved, someone takes an extension apart.
- **How:** walk through with the list; write the date and findings in the daily report (*parte*); whatever fails the check is taken out of use and marked.
- **Verify:** the day's report has the signed check.
- **Don't:** do the check "from memory" or leave it for the end of the day.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 87 (periodic inspection and maintenance) and art. 208 (check tools before using them); Res. SRT 61/2023 Anexo I points 22 and 29 (daily inspection of fall-protection equipment); Res. SRT 900/15 art. 4 and its guide (periodic check of the disconnecting devices; testing the RCD monthly is advised; on the site, this guide recommends doing it daily).

### ELEC-SEG-26: Fire in a panel or in an electrical installation
- **Rule:** first cut the power upstream (main switch of the site panel or of the main panel), if you can do it without exposing yourself. Use an extinguisher suitable for electrical fire, **class C** under Argentine regulations: CO2 or ABC/BC dry chemical powder. Never water or foam on parts that may be live. Next to the site panel there must be a suitable extinguisher, accessible and with a current charge.
- **Why:** water and foam conduct: whoever throws them on an energized panel can be electrocuted. A fire in a panel usually continues as long as there is power, because the arc or the overheated conductor keeps feeding it.
- **How:** (1) cut the power; (2) call the fire department (100) or emergency services if the fire is not incipient; (3) attack from the distance shown on the extinguisher, with the exit at your back; (4) with the power cut and confirmed, a fire that continued in ordinary materials (wood, paper) is now class A. Don't re-energize until it is inspected (ELEC-SEG-22). If a lithium battery catches fire, ELEC-SEG-38.
- **Verify:** extinguisher next to the panel, of a suitable class (marked "C" or "BC/ABC"), with nothing in front of it, with the inspection tag up to date; people know where the main switch is.
- **Don't:** throw water or a bucket of wet sand on a live panel; open the door of a smoking panel without cutting first; use a water or foam extinguisher "because it was the one there".
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 88 (the HyS (health and safety) officer defines the type and minimum number of extinguishing devices), art. 90 (inspection at least once a month) and art. 91 (free of obstacles and accessible). Class C as "fuego sobre instalaciones o equipos sometidos a la acción de la corriente eléctrica" (fire on installations or equipment subject to electric current): Decreto 351/79 Anexo VII (fire definitions), **text not verified in this edition**. Location next to the site panel: this guide's criterion.

### ELEC-SEG-27: Building: cutting common services and working in common areas
- **Rule:** cutting a building's common services (*servicios generales*: elevator, water pumps, stairwell pressurization, landing and emergency lighting, intercom, fire alarm panel) is coordinated beforehand with the building management or the caretaker (*encargado*), and for the elevator also with the elevator maintenance company (*conservadora*). The riser (*columna montante*: sub-mains of all the units) and the common-services conductors that run through shafts and landings are not cut or touched from a unit.
- **Why:** cutting the elevator can trap people inside; without pumps the building is left without water, and without landing (*palier*) lighting the stairwell is dark. The riser and common services are fed from the meter room: from an apartment you cannot lock them out, and a drill or angle grinder on the landing can bite into another unit's line.
- **How:** (1) give advance notice and put a sign in the lobby with the day and time; (2) before cutting the elevator, the maintenance company brings it to the ground floor with the doors open and the car is verified to be empty; (3) if work is needed inside the shaft, the equipment stays locked out and tagged (lockout/tagout) and the car is not used as a scaffold; (4) to touch the riser or a common-services circuit, the cut is made in the meter room, with lock and tag (ELEC-SEG-4) and with the caretaker present; if the cut point is upstream of the unit's switch, the utility does it; (5) to chase or drill in common areas, survey first what runs there (ELEC-SEG-19). Building layout in [buildings.md](buildings.md).
- **Verify:** the building management confirmed the cut; the car was empty; the pumps and pressurization came back on restoring power; the emergency light is charging.
- **Don't:** switch off the common-services panel "for a minute" without notice; cut another unit's line to work on yours without its occupant knowing; chase the landing without knowing where the riser runs.
- **Source:** Res. SRT 61/2023 Anexo I point 76 (inside the shaft, using the lifting equipment is forbidden and lockout, stopping, "etiquetado y consignación" (tagging and lockout) are required) and point 78 (forbidden to use the car as a scaffold for construction tasks), points 75 and 77 (shafts and service ducts); Decreto 911/96 art. 79 (cut and lock out every source). Coordination with building management and the maintenance company, and checking the car: this guide's criterion.
### ELEC-SEG-28: What stays live inside the panel even when you switch off the main switch
- **Rule:** when you switch off a panel's (distribution board, *tablero*) main switch, its **input terminals**, the incoming conductors and any busbar or terminal upstream stay live. Before working inside, cut further up (main panel or the utility (*distribuidora*)) or cover those parts with an insulating shield or cap, and verify.
- **Why:** it is a classic accident: the electrician switches off the main switch of the sub-panel (*tablero seccional*), sees everything "dead" and touches the input terminals, which come live from the main panel.
- **How:** identify where the panel's supply comes from; if you have to work on the main switch, its terminals or the incoming line, cut and lock out at the upstream panel (in a building, the switch of the unit's main panel in the meter room). If you work only on outgoing circuits with the main switch open, cover the input terminals and keep your hand away from them. Also measure at the input terminals to know what is live.
- **Verify:** you know which parts of the panel stay live, and they are covered or cut upstream.
- **Don't:** rest tools on the main switch; work on the input terminal block "because the main switch is off".
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 79 a) (separate the installation from every source) and art. 75 (distances to uninsulated live parts); SRT Manual TST 2023 (delimit and protect nearby live parts). Shields and caps over upstream terminals: this guide's criterion.

### ELEC-SEG-29: Arc when switching: stance, and don't close onto a fault
- **Rule:** when operating a circuit breaker or an isolator (*seccionador*), stand to the side of the device, with your face out of its line, and use the hand on the device's side. Don't close a circuit that tripped on a short circuit until you know why it tripped.
- **Why:** if the device fails or closes onto a short circuit, the arc and molten metal come out the front. Closing onto an undiagnosed short repeats the arc and can blow the device apart in your hand.
- **How:** before switching a main panel or site panel: panel door closed if the device is operated from outside, PPE from ELEC-SEG-11 (face shield on main panels), dry floor or insulating mat. If the protective device tripped, follow ELEC-SEG-22 before resetting.
- **Verify:** you know the cause of the trip before resetting; the switching is done standing to the side.
- **Don't:** switch while facing the device head-on; reset "to see if it holds" a main switch that tripped with a bang.
- **Source:** Res. SRT 3068/2014 and Manual TST SRT 2023 (arc risk and arc-rated PPE); Decreto 911/96 art. 87 (inspection before resetting). Side stance: professional practice, **not verified in a primary source** as normative text.

### ELEC-SEG-30: Tanks, cisterns, pits and chambers: conductive locations and confined spaces
- **Rule:** inside a metal tank or cistern, a pit, the elevator pit or the pump chamber, lighting and tools run on SELV (MBTS) (value set by HyS (health and safety), no more than 24 V) or on battery, with the transformer **outside**. If the place has no natural ventilation it is a confined space: before entering you need a permit, measurement of oxygen (18.5 % minimum) and of contaminants or flammable gases, lockout of all external energy (pumps, agitators, valves) and a person outside who watches and can call for help.
- **Why:** in a tight conductive location you cannot move away from the exposed metal: any fault puts your whole body in contact. Without oxygen or with gases, whoever goes in to rescue the first person often becomes the second victim.
- **How:** cut and lock out the pumps that fill or empty the space; ventilate; measure the atmosphere before entering and during the work; safety or separation transformer outside (one per device, ELEC-SEG-16); constant communication with the attendant; the attendant does not go in to rescue without equipment.
- **Verify:** signed permit, oxygen measurement recorded, pumps locked out, attendant present, no 220 V inside.
- **Don't:** go down into the pit with a 220 V floodlight; enter alone; put the transformer inside the tank.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 120 (confined spaces: no natural ventilation), art. 125 (before authorizing: minimum oxygen 18.5 %, no contaminants or flammable mixtures, external energy inputs locked out), art. 74 a) (SELV in wet locations) and art. 84 (underground ducts: hazardous atmospheres); Res. SRT 61/2023 Anexo I point 70 (constant means of communication at inaccessible workstations). Outside attendant and transformer outside: this guide's criterion and IEC 60364-7-706, **not verified in a primary source** (Argentine).

### ELEC-SEG-31: Storm, rain and wind: stop work
- **Rule:** when a thunderstorm is imminent, work on overhead lines, at height, on the meter pillar (*pilar*) and on any outdoor installation is stopped. In rain, no work is done on panels, meter pillars or exposed connections. With wind gusts of 30 to 40 km/h, work at height needs HyS authorization; above 40 km/h, it is stopped.
- **Why:** lightning induces overvoltages in the lines even when they are cut, and water cancels the insulation of the body and of the PPE. Wind moves scaffolds, cables and ladders.
- **How:** before starting, check the forecast and alerts from the Servicio Meteorológico Nacional (national weather service); if a storm is forecast, leave the installation closed and safe (ELEC-SEG-20) and come down from height. Any of the workers can stop the task because of the weather.
- **Verify:** the daily report records the day's weather; after rain, the site panel is dry inside and the RCD (*disyuntor* / *diferencial*) trips with its button before the installation is used.
- **Don't:** "finish quickly" the meter pillar connection with the storm overhead; open a wet panel.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 84 e) ("Se suspenderá el trabajo cuando exista inminencia de tormentas", work shall be stopped when storms are imminent, overhead lines); Res. SRT 61/2023 Anexo I point 5 (the HyS service checks the weather conditions; anyone can stop work for thunderstorms, rain, wind) and point 6 (30-40 km/h with HyS authorization; above 40 km/h work is stopped). Rain on panels and meter pillars: this guide's criterion.

### ELEC-SEG-32: Never alone on electrical work
- **Rule:** on any work on an electrical installation there must be a second person in sight or within earshot, who knows where and how to cut the power and how to call for help.
- **Why:** someone "stuck" to the voltage cannot let go or call for help; the minutes without CPR decide whether they survive.
- **How:** before starting, show your companion the disconnecting device and the emergency number; on higher-risk tasks (main panel, height, confined spaces) the companion does nothing else. If you work as an independent in a house, tell someone in the house what you are going to do and where the main switch is.
- **Verify:** the companion knows how to cut the power and has a phone.
- **Don't:** stay alone on the site at night to "finish the panel".
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 57 (two workers present on short tasks without collective protection); Res. SRT 61/2023 Anexo I point 72 (minimum crew of two on bosun's chairs). As a general rule for all electrical work: this guide's criterion, **not verified in a primary source** as a textual requirement.

### ELEC-SEG-33: Rescue at height and suspension trauma
- **Rule:** before climbing, it must be decided how to bring down someone left hanging from the harness or electrocuted at height. At 6 m or more a written Plan de Rescate en Altura (work-at-height rescue plan) is mandatory.
- **Why:** a person hanging from the harness can suffer harness syndrome (suspension trauma) within minutes, from compressed circulation in the legs. If someone is electrocuted at height, cutting the power may make them let go and fall: the cut and the restraint are planned together.
- **How:** the plan defines who rescues, with what equipment (ladder, MEWP (*PEMP*), descent system) and how emergency services are called; harness with anti-trauma straps at 6 m or more; rescue drill according to the program. With someone electrocuted at height: cut the power (ELEC-SEG-23) with the fall-arrest system or the rescue already set up for when they let go; never climb up to touch them while live. Tell emergency services that the person was suspended.
- **Verify:** the rescue plan is in the site safety file and the rescue equipment is on the site.
- **Don't:** rely on "calling the fire department" as the only plan; leave a worker hanging while waiting.
- **Source:** Res. SRT 61/2023 Anexo I point 9 (Plan de Rescate en Altura at 6 m or more), point 12 (annual training with a rescue drill every six months), point 30 (anti-trauma straps; harness syndrome) and point 67 (anchors for rescue maneuvers). Coordinating the cut with the possible fall: this guide's criterion.

### ELEC-SEG-34: Meter pillar and temporary site connection
- **Rule:** the temporary construction supply (*luz de obra*) is requested from the utility by the site owner, with the meter pillar and the panel that its regulations require, normally with the involvement of a qualified installer or licensed professional. The site electrician works from the meter's output terminals inward: they do not work upstream of the meter or break seals.
- **Why:** what is upstream of the meter belongs to the utility and cannot be cut from the site; a direct or "hooked-on" connection (*conexión colgada*) leaves the site without RCD protection and is illegal.
- **How:** ask the local utility for the site meter pillar requirements (location, service conduit, meter box, panel with main switch and RCD, earthing) and the documents it requires. Downstream of the meter, the site panel follows ELEC-SEG-14. If work is needed on the service connection (*acometida*) or the meter, ask the utility to cut the supply. Procedures in [regulations-and-permits.md](regulations-and-permits.md).
- **Verify:** the meter pillar is approved by the utility; the site panel has a main switch, a 30 mA RCD and measured earth.
- **Don't:** "hook on" to the grid or to the neighbor; touch the meter terminal block; use the temporary connection without a panel.
- **Source:** **Not verified in a primary source:** the site meter pillar requirements are set by each utility's supply regulations (Edenor, Edesur, EPEC, EPE, EDEA, etc.); none were read in this review. Decreto 911/96 art. 86 (site installation according to AEA, with earthing and automatic disconnection).

### ELEC-SEG-35: RCD type and selectivity in the site panel
- **Rule:** on the site use 30 mA **type A** RCDs (*diferenciales*) as a minimum for circuits that feed tools with electronics (angle grinders with speed control, drills, inverter welders, single-phase drives). If there is an upstream (incoming) RCD, make it **selective (type S)** and with a higher rated residual current (for example 300 mA), so that a fault at one socket outlet does not leave the whole site in the dark.
- **Why:** type AC only detects leakage in sinusoidal AC. Electronic equipment can leak with a pulsating DC component, which a type AC may not see or may be "blinded" by. Without selectivity (discrimination), the main RCD trips together with the socket outlet's one and the site lighting is lost, with the risk of falling in the dark.
- **How:** type A for tool socket outlets; type F for single-phase equipment with a variable frequency drive; type B for three-phase drives or vehicle chargers (see [protection.md](protection.md)). For selectivity between two RCDs in series: the upstream one type S and with an IΔn at least 3 times that of the downstream one (for example, 300 mA S upstream and 30 mA downstream). Test each one with its button.
- **Verify:** the type is marked on the front of the RCD (waveform symbol or letter); the upstream one says "S"; pressing the button of the socket-outlet RCD does not trip the main one.
- **Don't:** put two instantaneous 30 mA RCDs in series; replace a type A with an AC "because it's the same".
- **Source:** **Not verified in a primary source:** types AC/A/F/B according to IEC 60755, IEC 61008-1 and IEC 62423; selectivity with type S and IΔn ratio according to manufacturers' practice (Schneider, ABB, Siemens). The AEA Reglamentación covers the topic in its protection section (see [protection.md](protection.md)); this guide's recommendation.

### ELEC-SEG-36: Temporary site lighting
- **Rule:** temporary lighting (festoon lights, lampholders, floodlights) goes on circuits with a 30 mA RCD, with lampholders of insulating material and weatherproof outdoors, lamps protected against impact, cables hung out of reach and out of the way. In wet locations or tight spaces, 24 V or less or battery (ELEC-SEG-16, 30).
- **Why:** a festoon with broken lampholders or stripped cables at hand height is a live part within reach; a halogen floodlight gets hot enough to set fire to a tarp, expanded polystyrene (*telgopor*) or insulation.
- **How:** class I floodlights with PE or double-insulated, fixed and aimed away from combustible materials; prefer LED (it heats less); the lighting circuit separate from the tool circuit, so that a tool trip does not leave the site in the dark (ELEC-SEG-35); portable lighting with a safety transformer.
- **Verify:** no lampholder without a lamp or broken while live; floodlights fixed, with their guard, and nothing combustible in front.
- **Don't:** bakelite lampholders hanging by their own cable outdoors; a halogen floodlight resting on wood or expanded polystyrene; a 220 V lamp inside a tank.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 86 (conductors without bare parts, installation at 2.40 m or more) and art. 74 a) (SELV); outside construction sites, Decreto 351/79 Anexo VI point 2.1.2 h) (portable lamps). The rest: this guide's criterion, **not verified in a primary source**.

### ELEC-SEG-37: Electrical hazard signs and access to the site panel
- **Rule:** the site panel, panels under construction and areas with buried cables or nearby lines carry electrical hazard signs, with wording in Spanish and pictograms, and access to the site panel is limited to authorized personnel.
- **Why:** people who don't know what is live move around the site; the sign and the lock are what stop them.
- **How:** electrical hazard warning sign (yellow triangle with black border and lightning bolt) and "Prohibido el acceso a personal no autorizado" (no access for unauthorized personnel) on the panel door; a "Prohibido maniobrar" (do not operate) tag on whatever is locked out (ELEC-SEG-4); tape or barriers to delimit the work area (ELEC-SEG-3, step 5).
- **Verify:** the signs are legible, in Spanish, and the person in charge holds the panel key.
- **Don't:** handwritten signs on cardboard that wash off in the rain; leave the key hanging on the panel.
- **Source:** Decreto 911/96 art. 66 (signs according to recognized national or international standards), art. 68 (wording in Spanish, pictograms, contrasting colors) and art. 86 (mark areas with underground cables). Colors and shape according to IRAM 10005: **not verified in the text of the IRAM standard (paid)**.

### ELEC-SEG-38: Batteries: battery tools and UPS or solar battery banks
- **Rule:** treat a battery bank (UPS, solar, hybrid) as a source that cannot be switched off: cut and lock out its isolator before touching the inverter or the backup panel, use insulated tools and don't bridge terminals. The lithium batteries of tools are charged on a non-combustible surface, with their original charger and under supervision.
- **Why:** in direct current there is no zero crossing, so an arc does not extinguish by itself; a battery bank can deliver short-circuit currents of thousands of amperes, and a wrench that falls across two terminals melts and burns. A lithium pack that has been struck or badly charged can go into thermal runaway: it catches fire, reignites and gives off toxic gases.
- **How:** take off rings and watch; cap the terminals you are not using; disconnect the negative first, or the isolator the manufacturer indicates; work on one terminal at a time. For tool packs: discard swollen, impact-damaged or overheating ones; don't charge them at night with nobody on the site. If a pack catches fire, move away, move combustibles away and call the fire department (100); don't pick it up by hand. See [special-installations.md](special-installations.md).
- **Verify:** battery isolator open and locked out; DC voltage measured at 0 V on the work side; charging area clear.
- **Don't:** open a DC connector under load; rest tools on the batteries; charge damaged packs.
- **Source:** **Not verified in a primary source:** this guide's criterion with technical data from battery and inverter manufacturers; DC arcs and string voltage are also covered in ELEC-SEG-6. Decreto 911/96 art. 79 a) (cut every source) and art. 99 (accessories).
## Example case: locking out a circuit in an apartment of a 4-story building, single-phase supply

Typical situation: a building with a ground floor and 4 floors, each unit with a single-phase supply (phase and neutral) from its
meter in the ground-floor meter room; next to each meter, the unit's main panel (TP) with a two-pole
switch; inside the apartment (*depto*), the sub-panel (TS, *tablero seccional*) with a two-pole main switch, a
30 mA RCD (*disyuntor* / *diferencial*) and an MCB (*térmica*) per circuit; common services (*servicios generales*: landing, pumps, elevator) on another meter and panel. Typical panel in
[panels.md](panels.md), building layout in [buildings.md](buildings.md). A socket outlet in the living room has to be replaced.
It is a building in use: if the person working is an employee, Decreto 351/79 applies (not 911/96); if self-employed, the rules
of this guide are the same.

1. **Identify.** On the drawing or the sub-panel labels, locate the living-room socket-outlet circuit (ELEC-SEG-1). Tell the occupants and someone who can cut the power if something happens to you (ELEC-SEG-32).
2. **Cut.** Switch off the circuit's MCB and the sub-panel's main switch. If the work is inside the sub-panel or on the sub-main (feeder), also switch off the switch of the unit's main panel, on the ground floor, because the main switch's input terminals stay live (ELEC-SEG-28): coordinate with the caretaker (*encargado*), because the meter room is a common area (ELEC-SEG-27).
3. **Lock out and tag.** DIN breaker lockout device and personal padlock on the device you cut (on the ground floor too); tag with name and time (ELEC-SEG-4).
4. **Verify.** Test the two-pole voltage tester (*detector bipolar*) with a portable voltage proving unit or on a nearby live socket outlet (for example, the one on the landing). Measure at the living-room socket outlet L-N, L-PE and N-PE; if the box has no PE, measure against a reliable earthed metal part and note the defect. Test the voltage tester again (ELEC-SEG-5). A reading that is not clearly zero on an ordinary multimeter is not dismissed as "induction".
5. **Backfeeds and other circuits' conductors.** Ask whether there is a UPS, solar panels or a generator in the unit; if so, cut and lock them out (ELEC-SEG-6, ELEC-SEG-21). In old buildings, check that there is no neutral shared with another unit or a circuit from another apartment running through the same conduit: also measure on any other circuits' conductors you see in the box. **If any conductor in the box is still live after your cut: stop.** Don't work in that box until that circuit is cut and locked out at its origin (another unit or common services, coordinating with the caretaker or the building management). If that is not possible, the work is not done dead, and it is not a job for an ordinary site electrician (ELEC-SEG-2).
6. **Work** with gloves, insulated tools and a fiberglass ladder if it is a high outlet point (*boca*).
7. **Restore.** Walk-through, removal of tools, insulation test (ELEC-SEG-7), your padlock off, closing the main switch and the circuit standing to the side (ELEC-SEG-29), testing the RCD with its button and testing the socket outlet with the instrument.

What you do **not** touch: the meter terminal block, the meter and everything upstream of the main panel's switch.
Those terminals stay live even if you switch everything off: if work is needed there, ask the utility (*distribuidora*) to cut the supply.

## What this guide does not replace
- The design, execution and sign-off of the installation (temporary or permanent) are done by a licensed professional or qualified installer according to the jurisdiction; see [regulations-and-permits.md](regulations-and-permits.md).
- A site's Safety Program (*Programa de Seguridad*), site safety file (*legajo técnico*), work permits, rescue plan, training and the selection of PPE and extinguishers are defined and signed by the health and safety officer, with the ART.
- What is upstream of the meter (service drop, meter, street line) belongs to the utility: only the utility cuts, connects or approves. The municipality decides its own matters (building permits, inspections). In a building, the building management and the elevator maintenance company (*conservadora*) decide on the common services.
- The AEA, IRAM and IEC standards cited are paid: detailed values must be confirmed in the current edition.
- In an emergency, the instructions given by the emergency service over the phone take precedence over this guide.
