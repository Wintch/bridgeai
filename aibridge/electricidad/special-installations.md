# Special installations and loads

Rules for the places where the electrical risk goes up (water, wet body, outdoors) and for the loads that cannot
be hung on an ordinary socket-outlet circuit (air conditioning, cooking, shower, motors, electric car,
own generation). Each rule has a stable ID so that the agent, a review or a site report can cite it
("does not comply with ELEC-ESP-7"). If a rule conflicts with the AEA Reglamentación in force, the AEA governs and this guide gets corrected;
if it conflicts with what the utility (distribution company, *distribuidora*) or the municipality requires for their part (metering, connection, approval), their requirement governs.

**Which edition to use (as of 2026-11-15, AEA catalog).** Bathrooms: **AEA 90364-7-701 Ed. 2012**. Pools and fountains:
**7-702 Ed. 2018**. Photovoltaics: **7-712 Ed. 2015** (the base IEC is already at Ed. 3.0 of October 2025; the AEA has not
adopted it yet). Electric vehicle: **7-722 Ed. 2024**. Dwellings: the catalog lists **7-770 Ed. 2026** as the
edition on sale (same title: single-family up to 63 A, BA2 and BD1), but we did not find its date of entry into
force or whether it coexists with the 2017 edition. **All "770.x" citations in this guide are from Ed. 2017**, the last text we
consulted: before signing, verify the numbering, the tables and the values against the 2026 edition (ELEC-NOR-4).
Apartments in a building: 770 or 771 depending on whether the unit falls within the scope of 770 and what the jurisdiction accepts
(ELEC-NOR-3, ELEC-EDI-26). Common services: **7-772 Ed. 2019**. Offices, shops and dwellings outside the scope of 770:
**7-771 Ed. 2006**. Stationary batteries: **90364-5-57 Ed. 2026**. UPS: **IT AEA 95511 Ed. 2023**. Part 7
**is not self-contained**: whatever a special section does not say is governed by Parts 1 to 6 and the applicable dwelling
section. AEA standards are paid: here we summarize and cite the clause, we do not copy.

**Safety first.** All mounting, connection or repair work is done **dead**, with the isolation verified
with an instrument. Three traps specific to this document: (1) a **solar panel delivers dangerous DC voltage
whenever there is light**, even if you open everything at the panel (distribution board, *tablero*); (2) a **generator set, a hybrid inverter or a UPS**
can keep a circuit energized with the grid cut off (see ELEC-ESP-38); (3) a **motor capacitor** stores charge
after isolation. Some checks in this document are done **live** (measuring voltage, current with a
clamp meter, testing the RCD (*disyuntor* / *diferencial*) with an instrument, Voc of a photovoltaic string, inverter disconnection test): only
the installer does them, with an instrument of the right category (CAT III 600 V or higher for AC; for photovoltaics, rated for DC and
1000 V or higher), shrouded probes and PPE, **without dismantling anything or touching live parts** (ELEC-MED-1, ELEC-MED-14,
ELEC-SEG-13). Live work proper requires specific authorization (Res. SRT 3068/2014) and
**this guide does not cover it**. See [site-safety.md](site-safety.md).

The agent does not calculate from memory: for current, cross-section, voltage drop and demand it uses the offline calculator
`gateway electricidad` and shows the calculation. The examples here are for understanding the method.

## Things common to all special loads

### ELEC-ESP-1: Identify the special location and apply its Part 7 section on top of the dwelling section
- **Rule:** if there is water, outdoors, wet body, motor, electric car or own generation, in addition to the dwelling section (770 or 771) you apply the special section: bathroom **701**, pool/fountain **702**, construction site **704**, photovoltaics **712**, electric vehicle **722**, common areas **772**. The special section **supplements, modifies or replaces** the general one; where it is silent, the general one governs.
- **Why:** in those places the body's resistance drops (wet skin, solid contact with earth) or a second energy source appears; ordinary protection is not enough.
- **How:** in the technical report, list per room or load which section applies and with which edition. For loads with no special location (AC, cooktop, shower, pump) the circuit comes from 770/771: TUE if each load is ≤ 20 A per socket outlet; **specific-use circuit** when the load exceeds that or does not fit IUG/TUG/TUE (770 refers them to 771, which defines the types, e.g. ACU for a single load). The outlet point (*boca*) can be a socket outlet or a fixed connection (770.6.6, note 1).
- **Verify:** that the report cites the edition in force (701:2012, 702:2018, 712:2015, 722:2024, 770 in the applicable edition); that each special load has its circuit identified on the single-line diagram (*unifilar*).
- **Don't:** hang an AC, an oven and a water heater (*termotanque*) on the same general socket-outlet circuit "because the socket outlet can take it"; use 770 alone for a pool.
- **Source:** AEA digital catalog (editions, consulted 2026-11-15); AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.6.6 c) (specific uses → 771), 770.6.6 note 1 (outlet point with socket outlet or fixed connection) and 770.8.2 (demand of dedicated circuits MBTF, APM, ATE, MBTS, ACU, IUE, ITE, OCE); Editores SRL, "Todo sobre AEA 90364" (Sept 2024) for the supplementary nature of Part 7.

### ELEC-ESP-2: With power electronics, RCD (*diferencial*) type A as a minimum; with a risk of smooth DC, type B or a DC detector
- **Rule:** equipment with a rectifier or drive (inverter AC unit, induction cooktop, inverter washing machine, pumps with a drive, photovoltaic inverter, car charger) can produce leakage with a DC component. Protect it with an ID **type A** as a minimum; **type F** for single-phase drives; **type B** (or type A/F + 6 mA RDC-DD detector) where smooth DC above 6 mA can appear, as in a wallbox with an IEC 62196 connector that does not include its own detection (ELEC-ESP-17).
- **Why:** type AC only guarantees tripping with sinusoidal leakage; pulsating leakage does not make it operate reliably, and smooth DC "blinds" an AC or an A and leaves every circuit hung from it without protection.
- **How:** per the tripping-conditions table of 722 (table 722.I): type **A** still trips correctly with up to **6 mA** of superimposed smooth DC; **F**, with up to **10 mA**; **B**, with up to **0.4 IΔn**; and **only B** also trips with a pure smooth DC leakage (between **0.5 and 2 IΔn**). That is why B is the one to use when DC can exceed 6 mA. In the panel, group the circuits with electronics under the type A (or F) ID and don't hang ordinary circuits from an ID that may be blinded. Never put a type B ID downstream of an A or an AC: the upstream one must also be B (IEC 60364-5-53 and manufacturers' criterion). Check the equipment manual: many manufacturers state the type.
- **Verify:** the type marking on the front of the ID (sine-wave symbol = AC; sine + pulsating = A; F; B); that the instrument test is done with the waveform of the type.
- **Don't:** put a type AC upstream of a car charger or an inverter; replace a type A with an AC "because it's the same and cheaper".
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-722:2024, 722.531.2.101 (type A minimum; DC measures if there is an IEC 62196 socket outlet or connector: type B, or A/F + RDC-DD per IEC 62955) and table 722.I that follows (tripping conditions; printed as "Tabla 772.I"). AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.14.2.3 note (type AC is the most common in that text). **Not verified in a primary source:** that the AEA requires type A for inverter AC units or induction in dwellings (it is a recommendation of manufacturers and IEC 60364-5-53), and the rule of type B upstream of another B (IEC 60364-5-53, 531.3.3, cited from manufacturers; we did not read the equivalent AEA text). Check whether 770 Ed. 2026 changed the minimum type. More detail in [protection.md](protection.md).

### ELEC-ESP-35: Equipment with electronics: check whether an SPD (*DPS*) and a voltage protector (*protector de tensión*) are needed
- **Rule:** the inverter AC unit, the photovoltaic inverter, the car charger and pumps with a drive are the first to burn out from overvoltages. Apply the mandatory-SPD cases of 770 (always if the building has a lightning rod; with an overhead service drop in an area with more than 25 thunderstorm days per year, except for the exceptions in the table) and, in buildings, the protector against permanent overvoltage due to a broken neutral (*corte de neutro*). If the photovoltaic inverter **does not have a built-in SPD**, the SE requires external SPDs on the **DC and AC** sides, close to the inverter, per IRAM 2184 parts 4 and 11.
- **Why:** a switching or lightning overvoltage damages electronics even if no protection trips; a broken neutral in a building puts up to 380 V into an apartment.
- **How:** choose, mount and coordinate the SPDs as ELEC-PRO-16 to ELEC-PRO-18 say (type, Up, Uc, connections ≤ 0.5 m, configuration on the correct side of the ID); voltage protector (over/undervoltage relay, *protector de tensión*) per ELEC-PRO-19. In photovoltaics, the DC SPD must be rated for photovoltaic DC and for the maximum string voltage.
- **Verify:** SPD status indicator; length of the connections; that the DC SPD is marked for PV.
- **Don't:** put an AC SPD on the DC side; assume "the inverter already has it" without seeing the datasheet.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.15.4 and table 770.15.III (mandatory-SPD cases; see [panels.md](panels.md) ELEC-TAB-11 and [protection.md](protection.md)); Disposición SSERyEE 97/2019, Annex 2.4 (external SPDs on DC and AC per IRAM 2184-4 and -11 if the coupling equipment does not include them).
### ELEC-ESP-39: Before adding a large load to an existing installation, check the incoming switch, the line and the power
- **Rule:** to add an AC, a cooktop (*anafe*), a water heater (*termotanque*) or a charger to an installation that already works, first verify that they fit within the **contracted power**, the **incoming protection** and the **main line or sub-main (feeder)** that feeds the panel (distribution board, *tablero*); only then design the new circuit. If it does not fit, limit the load, stagger its use or ask the utility (distribution company, *distribuidora*) for more power (or three-phase).
- **Why:** the new circuit can be perfect and the sum still trips the main switch, overheats the sub-main in the riser (*montante*) or makes the utility cut the supply for excess.
- **How:** (1) survey the existing panel (incoming switch, ID, PIA, cross-sections; [reviewing-a-panel.md](reviewing-a-panel.md)); (2) recalculate the demand with the new load (`gateway electricidad demand`, [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md)); (3) compare with the incoming switch and with the Iz of the line; (4) verify space in the panel and the ID it will hang from; (5) test the existing parts that are affected (ELEC-MED-20). Example: apartment with a 32 A incoming switch (≈ 7 kW at 220 V) to which you want to add a 7.2 kW cooktop (32.7 A): the cooktop alone already equals the incoming switch; it does not fit without limiting it or without more power. Step-by-step cases: ELEC-DIA-21 (AC), ELEC-DIA-22 (water heater), ELEC-DIA-23 (cooktop); in buildings, ELEC-EDI-28.
- **Verify:** demand recalculated and signed; incoming switch ≥ demand; Iz of the line ≥ incoming switch; contracted power updated if needed.
- **Don't:** "raise the main MCB (*térmica*)" so it does not trip; add the new circuit without recalculating the riser line.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.8 (total load and demand) and 770.4.2 e1) (incoming switch ≤ 63 A); AEA 90364-7-722:2024, 722.6.4.1 (verify the affected existing installation when adding a charging point). See [buildings.md](buildings.md) and [testing-and-commissioning.md](testing-and-commissioning.md).

## Bathrooms and locations with a shower or bathtub (AEA 90364-7-701)

### ELEC-ESP-3: Respect zones 0, 1 and 2 of the bathroom and what each one allows
- **Rule:** around the bathtub or shower, zones are defined with restrictions that increase toward the water:
  - **Zone 0:** the inside of the bathtub or shower tray (in showers without a tray, per the base IEC, a layer **0.10 m** high above the floor with the same horizontal extent as zone 1). Only fixed equipment made for that location, supplied at **MBTS ≤ 12 V AC** with the source outside the zones.
  - **Zone 1:** above zone 0, up to **2.25 m** from the floor, bounded by the edge of the bathtub or tray (in showers without a tray, a radius of **1.2 m** from the water outlet). Only fixed equipment suitable for that zone (electric shower, water heater, extractor fan, luminaire, whirlpool pump) and switches or socket outlets only at MBTS.
  - **Zone 2:** a strip **0.60 m** beyond zone 1, up to 2.25 m high. Suitable luminaires, extractor fans, heated towel rails; **no 220 V socket outlets** (only a shaver socket with an isolating transformer or MBTS); no junction or branch boxes.
  - **Outside the zones:** 2P+E IRAM 2071 socket outlets and switches, with the circuit's 30 mA ID. **Pending confirmation:** the base IEC and its European transpositions add a minimum horizontal distance for 220 V socket outlets measured from the edge of zone 1 (on the order of 2.5 m to 3 m); confirm in AEA 701:2012 whether it applies and with what measurement before placing the vanity unit (*vanitory*) socket outlet.
- **Why:** with a wet body and feet in the water, the body's resistance drops several times; a touch voltage that is tolerable in the living room is deadly in the shower.
- **How:** draw the zones on the bathroom detail drawing (plan and section) before placing outlet points (*bocas*). The socket outlet on the vanity countertop goes at least outside zone 2, under a 30 mA ID, as far as possible from the shower or bathtub, with its box ≥ 0.10 m above the countertop. A **fixed** partition or screen limits the zone: the distance is measured along the shortest path around it (the "stretched string" criterion); a curtain, a movable screen or a suspended ceiling does not limit it (IEC criterion; confirm it in AEA 701).
- **Verify:** measure from the edge of the tray or from the shower head: no 220 V socket outlet or switch inside zones 0, 1 and 2; in zones 1 and 2, only fixed equipment suitable for that zone; luminaire above the shower with suitable IP (ELEC-ESP-4).
- **Don't:** put a socket outlet "for the hair dryer" next to the bathtub; use an ordinary socket outlet for the electric shower.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-701 Ed. 2012 (text not consulted: the standard is paid and the index preview is not available); AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.7.1 e) and k) (refers to 701) and 770.7.2 (0.10 m above countertops; distances to water sources per 701). **Not verified in a primary source:** the measurements 2.25 m, 0.60 m, 1.2 m and 0.10 m, the fixed-screen criterion and the horizontal distance for socket outlets come from IEC 60364-7-701 (Ed. 2006 and later) and its British transposition (BS 7671, 701.512.3: 3 m in the 2008 ed., 2.5 m in the 2018 ed., cited from technical memory), which the AEA adapts; earlier Argentine teaching material (UTN FRRO, 2014) still speaks of an unrestricted "volumen 3" (zone 3), which the IEC removed. Confirm against the text of 701:2012.

### ELEC-ESP-4: In the bathroom: IP according to the zone, 30 mA ID and never the panel (*tablero*)
- **Rule:** equipment in **zone 0** must be at least **IPX7**; in **zones 1 and 2**, **IPX4** (**IPX5** if cleaned with a water jet, as in changing rooms). Every circuit that enters the bathroom goes under an **ID ≤ 30 mA**. The **panel does not go in the bathroom**.
- **Why:** the IP ensures that splashes or immersion do not reach live parts; the ID disconnects before fibrillation if the insulation fails anyway.
- **How:** choose luminaires and extractor fans with the IP printed on the box or rating plate; embedded conduit with boxes outside zones 0-2 when possible; Class II appliances or with the PE connected.
- **Verify:** IP on the rating plate of each fitting; ID test; that there are no junction boxes inside the zones.
- **Don't:** install an ordinary ceiling lamp (IP20) inside the shower; leave the apartment's sub-panel (TS, *tablero seccional*) inside the bathroom or the damp laundry.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.14.2.3 (ID ≤ 30 mA on every final circuit), 770.16.2.1 (panel not in bathrooms; see [panels.md](panels.md) ELEC-TAB-3). **Not verified in a primary source:** the IP per zone comes from IEC 60364-7-701, the basis of AEA 701:2012.

### ELEC-ESP-5: Bond the metal parts of the bathroom to earth (supplementary equipotential bonding)
- **Rule:** extraneous conductive parts that enter the zones (metal water and drain pipes, metal bathtub, metal grates) are connected to each other and to the **protective conductor (PE)**.
- **Why:** if a fault energizes a pipe on another floor, the person who touches the faucet and the bathtub at the same time must not end up between two different potentials.
- **How:** green-yellow copper conductor from an earth terminal in a bathroom box to each metal part, with a suitable earth clamp (*morseto*) or clip, accessible for inspection. With plastic pipes (heat-fusion PPR, PVC) there is normally nothing to bond, except a metal bathtub.
- **Verify:** continuity with a milliohmmeter, dead, between the part and the PE bar of the panel (see [testing-and-commissioning.md](testing-and-commissioning.md), ELEC-MED-3).
- **Don't:** use the water pipe as the earth electrode; wrap the cable around the pipe without a clamp.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.14.4.6 (green-yellow copper PE branches ≥ 2.5 mm²). Supplementary equipotential bonding in the bathroom is covered by AEA 90364-7-701:2012 (**not consulted**). **Not verified in a primary source:** its exact scope (IEC 2006 allows omitting it under certain conditions; Argentine secondary sources give it as mandatory). When in doubt, do it.

### ELEC-ESP-37: Whirlpool bathtub and spa: own circuit, pump accessible only with a tool and everything bonded to the PE
- **Rule:** the whirlpool bathtub is treated as fixed equipment in zone 1 (ELEC-ESP-3): **own circuit** with PIA per the rating plate and a **30 mA ID**, PE to the pump and the heater, equipotential bonding of the bathtub if it is metal (ELEC-ESP-5). The pump and the connection box under the bathtub stay behind a **cover that only opens with a tool**. An outdoor spa or jacuzzi follows the manufacturer's manual and the pool criterion (ELEC-ESP-7 to ELEC-ESP-9).
- **Why:** the pump and its heating element are centimeters from the water and the body; if the space under the bathtub is accessible by hand, it becomes part of zone 1.
- **How:** equipment connection box with the IP of the zone; cable from a box outside the zones; panel controls at MBTS (supplied with the equipment); inspection hatch with screws.
- **Verify:** equipment rating plate (current, IP); ID test with the equipment running; PE continuity to the pump housing (dead).
- **Don't:** plug the whirlpool pump into a socket outlet under the bathtub; leave the hatch cover press-fit, without screws.
- **Source:** **Not verified in a primary source:** IEC 60364-7-701 criterion (space under the bathtub accessible only with a tool is outside zone 1) and manufacturers' manuals; the 30 mA ID is from AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.14.2.3. Confirm in AEA 701:2012 and, for spas, in 702:2018.

### ELEC-ESP-6: Electric shower and electric water heater: own circuit, PE and what the manufacturer requires
- **Rule:** the **electric shower** goes on a specific-use circuit, with the PE connected and a 30 mA ID; the usual practice (and what many manufacturers require) is a **fixed connection with a terminal block**, without a socket outlet. The **electric water heater** goes on its own circuit or a TUE, with PE; if it is in zone 1 or 2, it must be suitable for that zone.
- **Why:** a 5500 W shower draws 25 A for minutes: neither a TUG (10 A per socket outlet) nor a 20 A socket outlet can take it; water flows over the heating element and an insulation fault puts the water stream live if there is no PE and ID.
- **How:** current = power / voltage.
  - Shower 5500 W: I = 5500 / 220 = **25 A**. 6 mm² cable (36 A) with a 32 A two-pole PIA, or whatever the manual says (the manual governs if it asks for more).
  - Water heater 2000 W: I = 2000 / 220 = **9.1 A**. It fits on a 10 A socket outlet, but it is a continuous load for hours: prefer a TUE or own circuit with a 16 A PIA and 2.5 mm² cable.
- **Verify:** that the PE reaches the equipment's earth terminal; ID test with the equipment connected; the thermostat and the safety valve of the water heater (not electrical, but without it the unit can burst).
- **Don't:** install the shower without PE "because it's plastic"; feed the shower with 2.5 mm² cable; disable the safety thermostat.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.6.6 a) and b) (limits of 10 A and 20 A per socket outlet), 770.6.6 c) (specific uses → 771), table 770.12.I (current-carrying capacities). **Not verified in a primary source:** the fixed connection as a requirement (770 does not require it; it is practice and a manufacturers' request; 771 was not consulted for this); cross-section and PIA of electric showers, which depend on the model (use the manufacturer's manual).

## Pools and ornamental fountains (AEA 90364-7-702)

### ELEC-ESP-7: In pools and fountains, in the water and next to the edge only 12 V MBTS
- **Rule:** in zones **0** (the water) and **1** (the edge) the only protection allowed is **MBTS of 12 V nominal**, with the **safety transformer outside zones 0, 1 and 2**. Underwater luminaires are 12 V, suitable for pools and for immersion.
- **Why:** with the body immersed and in contact with earth potential, the shock risk is at its maximum; the only safe way is for the voltage to be unable to cause harm even if everything fails.
- **How:** safety transformer (IEC 61558-2-6), with separate primary and secondary (never an autotransformer), in the plant room or in an enclosure outside the zones; secondary cable in its own conduit up to the luminaire niche. Zones per the base IEC: zone 1 up to **2 m** horizontally from the edge and **2.5 m** high; zone 2 up to **1.5 m** beyond zone 1. Ornamental fountains have only zones 0 and 1.
- **Verify:** primary-secondary separation (insulation between them with a megohmmeter, dead); secondary voltage (12 V; live measurement, done by the installer); luminaire IP (IPX8 in zone 0); location of the transformer outside the zones.
- **Don't:** light the pool with 220 V luminaires "with good sealing"; put the transformer next to the edge; use 24 V LED power supplies in zone 0.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.14.1 a) (12 V where the body may be immersed) and final paragraph and note 5 (12 V MBTS mandatory in zones 0 and 1, source outside 0, 1 and 2); 770.7.1 g) (no autotransformers). AEA 90364-7-702:2018, index: 702.41 (electric shock, MBTS and MBTP), 702.415 (supplementary equipotential bonding), 702.53 (panels), 702.55 (other equipment), annex A (example of zones). **Not verified in a primary source:** the measurements 2 m / 2.5 m / 1.5 m and the IP ratings come from IEC 60364-7-702; confirm with the AEA text.

### ELEC-ESP-8: Pool pump, filter, heater and socket outlets: outside the zones, with ID and PE
- **Rule:** the filter pump, the chlorinator, the heater and their panel go **outside zones 0 and 1** (ideally in a plant room or shed outside zone 2). 220 V equipment in zone 2, and socket outlets, only with an **ID ≤ 30 mA**, MBTS or electrical separation. All 220 V equipment has a PE.
- **Why:** the pump is a motor in a wet location, with chlorinated (corrosive) water and barefoot people around.
- **How:** own circuit from the house panel with a 30 mA ID and motor protection (ELEC-ESP-14); pool panel with an IP suitable for outdoors (IP54 at least, like any outdoor panel); level controls or float switches at MBTS (ELEC-ESP-16). A **heat pump** for heating the pool is sized like an air conditioner (ELEC-ESP-10 and ELEC-ESP-11): rating-plate current, own circuit, C curve PIA, 30 mA ID (type A if it is inverter), unit outside zones 0 and 1 and preferably outside zone 2. Ventilated plant room with drainage: if it floods, the panel must not end up under water.
- **Verify:** that there are no 220 V socket outlets in zone 1; ID test; PE continuity to the pump and heater housings; motor insulation (megohmmeter, dead).
- **Don't:** plug in the pump with an extension cord from the house; put the pump panel on the edge; leave a socket outlet for the pool vacuum inside zone 1.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-702:2018 (index 702.41, 702.53, 702.55) and AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.7.1 h), 770.13.3, 770.14.2.3, 770.16 (IP54 for outdoor panels). **Not verified in a primary source:** the exact conditions for equipment in zones 1 and 2 in 702:2018 (the IEC 60364-7-702 criterion was summarized); the location of the heat pump is a practical and manufacturers' criterion.
### ELEC-ESP-9: Bond everything metallic in the pool to the equipotential system
- **Rule:** all conductive parts of zones 0, 1 and 2 (mesh or reinforcement of the pool shell and of the surrounding paving if accessible, metal ladders and handrails, luminaire niches, metal nozzles and pipes, pump housing) are connected to each other and to the PE with protective conductors.
- **Why:** even if everything is protected, a fault or a voltage that arrives through the ground from outside must not create a potential difference between the water, the edge and the handrail.
- **How:** leave the connection to the reinforcement **before pouring the concrete** (cable or flat bar (*planchuela*) welded or with an earth clamp (*morseto*), with an accessible terminal); bond niches and ladders with green-yellow copper conductor; record everything on the drawing.
- **Verify:** continuity between each part and the PE bar (milliohmmeter, dead); photos of the bonds before filling.
- **Don't:** leave the mesh unconnected "because it's buried"; use the pump pipe as the earth path.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-702:2018, 702.415 (additional protection: supplementary equipotential bonding, per the index published by AEA). **Not verified in a primary source:** the exact list of parts to bond and the cross-sections; summarized from IEC 60364-7-702.

## Air conditioning, cooking, appliances, heating

### ELEC-ESP-10: For the air conditioner, take the current from the rating plate, not from the frigorías
- **Rule:** you size the circuit with the **rated current on the rating plate** (and the maximum, if given), not with the frigorías. Frigorías are **thermal** power; the **electrical** power is about three times lower.
- **Why:** confusing 3000 frigorías (3.5 kW thermal) with 3.5 kW electrical leads to oversizing; ignoring the starting of an on/off unit leads to PIA trips.
- **How:** worked example, 3000 frigorías/h split:
  1. Thermal power: 3000 × 1.163 W = **3489 W ≈ 3.5 kW** (≈ 12 000 BTU/h).
  2. Electrical power with EER = 3.2 (W/W): P = 3489 / 3.2 = **1090 W**.
  3. Current: I = P / (U × cos φ) = 1090 / (220 × 0.9) = **5.5 A** (the agent gets it with `gateway electricidad current --power 1090 --pf 0.9`; cos φ 0.9, as in ELEC-CIR-9). Compare with the rating plate (the rating plate governs).
     If the person only knows the frigorías, this number serves as a **provisional estimate**, stated as such ("unos 8 A para 4500 fg, según catálogos típicos; confirmalo con la placa" (about 8 A for 4500 fg, per typical catalogs; confirm it with the rating plate)), to get ahead on cross-section and MCB (*térmica*); the circuit is finalized with the rating plate.
  4. Starting: an on/off compressor draws several times the rated current for fractions of a second; an inverter starts softly.
  Typical values (indicative, check the rating plate): 2250 fg ≈ 2.6 kW thermal ≈ 4 A; 4500 fg ≈ 5.2 kW thermal ≈ 8 A; 6000 fg ≈ 7 kW thermal ≈ 11 A.
- **Verify:** rating plate of the outdoor unit (rated and maximum current, voltage, protection recommended by the manufacturer).
- **Don't:** add frigorías as if they were watts in the demand; choose the PIA "by eye" without seeing the rating plate.
- **Source:** definition of the kcal (1 kcal/h = 1.163 W). EER values and currents: **Not verified in a primary source:** they are typical of catalogs; each model comes with its rating plate and its efficiency label.

### ELEC-ESP-11: Air conditioner on its own circuit, C curve PIA, cable between units protected
- **Rule:** each air conditioner above about 2250 frigorías goes on its **own circuit** (specific-use, or a TUE with a single unit) with a two-pole PIA per the rating plate; the cable that joins the indoor unit to the outdoor unit is part of the appliance only up to **3 m** and with a single slave unit: if it is longer, or if there are several indoor units, it is installation and is sized and run in conduit as such. **Inverter** units go with a type A RCD (*disyuntor* / *diferencial*) (ELEC-ESP-2).
- **Why:** two units on a TUG saturate it in the first heat wave; the starting of an on/off unit trips a small B curve PIA; a long, thin interconnection cable without protection is a fire risk.
- **How:** for a unit up to ~11 A on the rating plate: 2.5 mm² cable (21 A) and a 16 A C curve PIA (C curve: magnetic trip between 5 and 10 times In, tolerates starting). For more current, 4 mm² and a 20 A PIA, always with In ≤ Iz of the cable. Voltage drop: with 20 m of 2.5 mm² and 5.5 A, ΔU = 15 V/(A·km) × 5.5 A × 0.020 km = **1.65 V (0.75 %)**, well below 3 %. Outdoor unit with its own visible isolation if it is far from the panel (distribution board, *tablero*) (usual practice), PE connected to both units, conduit and IP44/IP54 sealed box outdoors (ELEC-ESP-28), firm support and condensate drain away from electrical outlet points. Overvoltage protection: ELEC-ESP-35.
- **Verify:** In of the PIA ≤ Iz of the cable ≤ what the manufacturer requires; ID test with the unit running; tightness of the terminals in the outdoor unit (dead).
- **Don't:** plug a split into a TUG with a power strip (*zapatilla*); leave the interconnection cable exposed, without conduit, across the balcony; hang the AC on the lighting circuit.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.1 (definition of electrical equipment: cables between units of split air conditioners, part of the appliance up to 3 m and a single slave unit), 770.6.6 a) II (10 A per socket outlet on TUG), tables 770.12.I and 770.15.IV, 770.15.6 (3 %). The C curve (5-10 In) is from IEC 60898-1. **Not verified in a primary source:** the threshold of "own circuit from 2250 fg" is a practical criterion, not an AEA figure; the AEA requires that no load exceed the circuit limit (10 A on TUG).

### ELEC-ESP-12: Induction cooktop (*anafe*), electric range and oven: dedicated circuit; above 20 A, specific-use circuit
- **Rule:** cooktop, electric range and electric oven go on a **dedicated circuit**. Up to 20 A per appliance, TUE with a 2P+E 20 A IRAM 2071 socket outlet; **above 20 A**, specific-use circuit (770.6.6 c), per 771), with cross-section and PIA according to the current. The usual practice, and what cooktop manufacturers require, is a **fixed connection** in a box with an accessible terminal block. 30 mA ID, type A recommended for induction.
- **Why:** a 4-zone cooktop draws more than 30 A; no domestic socket outlet is made for that, and an overheated socket outlet behind the cabinet is a hidden fire.
- **How:** examples:
  - Induction cooktop 7200 W: I = 7200 / 220 = **32.7 A**. Set the cooktop's power limiter (if it has one) to ≤ 32 A, and feed it with 6 mm² (36 A) and a 32 A two-pole PIA; or choose a lower-power model. Note that this load alone can equal the apartment's incoming switch: it goes into the demand (ELEC-ESP-39 and [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md)).
  - Electric oven 3500 W: I = 3500 / 220 = **15.9 A**. TUE with a 20 A socket outlet and 4 mm² cable (28 A) with a 20 A PIA; or 2.5 mm² (21 A) with a 20 A PIA if the voltage drop works out and the conduit is not shared.
  - Gas range or cooktop with electric ignition: it is a fixed-location appliance; a 10 A socket outlet on the kitchen circuit is enough.
- **Verify:** that the limiter is set and documented; with the cooktop at full load, current measured with a clamp meter at the panel (installer, without opening anything); afterwards, dead, tightness and condition of the terminals in the connection box (no discoloration or burning smell); PE connected.
- **Don't:** plug in the cooktop with a cut-down 10 A plug; put the connection box inside the cabinet with no access.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.6.6 b) (TUE: 20 A IRAM 2071 socket outlets, protection ≤ 32 A), 770.7.1 i) (fixed-location appliances in kitchens), 770.6.6 c) (specific uses → 771); table 770.12.I. **Not verified in a primary source:** cooktop powers and limiters (they depend on the model); the fixed connection as a normative requirement (it is a manufacturer's request and usual practice; 771 was not consulted for this); type A ID for induction is a manufacturers' recommendation.

### ELEC-ESP-33: Washing machine, dryer, dishwasher and refrigerator: check the rating plate and separate the refrigerator from what trips often
- **Rule:** the washing machine, the dishwasher and the dryer connect to a socket outlet of the appropriate circuit according to the rating-plate current: **≤ 10 A**, 10 A socket outlet on a TUG; **above 10 A** (typical for heated dryers), 20 A socket outlet on a TUE. The **refrigerator** goes on a 10 A socket outlet, on a circuit and under an **ID different** from that of outdoor areas, the laundry and equipment with heating elements that tend to leak, so that a trip does not leave it without power for hours. No circuit is left without an ID (770.14.2.3).
- **Why:** these appliances combine water, heating elements and a motor: they are the ones that accumulate the most leakage; if they share an ID with the refrigerator, the first night-time trip ruins the food and the temptation is to "take the *disyuntor* out".
- **How:** current = power / voltage. Washing machine with 2200 W heating: I = 2200 / 220 = **10 A** (right at the TUG limit; if the rating plate says more, TUE). Heated dryer of 2500 W: I = 2500 / 220 = **11.4 A** → TUE with a 20 A socket outlet. Dishwasher of 1800 W: I = 1800 / 220 = **8.2 A** → TUG. **Inverter** washing machines and dishwashers: type A ID recommended (ELEC-ESP-2). In the laundry, socket outlets ≥ 0.30 m from the floor if it is hosed down (ELEC-ESP-29). Distribute the circuits so that the standing leakage of each ID stays below ~0.3 IΔn (ELEC-PRO-11).
- **Verify:** rating plate of each appliance; that the refrigerator does not share an ID with outdoor areas or the laundry; test of each ID.
- **Don't:** take the refrigerator off the RCD "so it doesn't cut out"; plug the washing machine and the dryer into the same power strip; use adapters without earth (if it "gives a shock" (*patea*), see ELEC-DIA-16).
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.6.6 a) II (single loads ≤ 10 A on TUG) and b) (up to 20 A on TUE), 770.14.2.3 (ID ≤ 30 mA on every circuit), 770.7.2 (0.30 m in rooms that are hosed down). **Not verified in a primary source:** typical powers (they depend on the model) and the separation of the refrigerator, which is a practical criterion.

### ELEC-ESP-13: Electric heaters: each one on its correct socket outlet, no power strips
- **Rule:** fan heaters, oil-filled radiators and panels up to 2200 W (10 A) plug **directly** into a wall socket outlet on the correct circuit; higher-power fixed equipment (large panels, electric radiant slab or underfloor heating, towel rails) on their own circuit or a TUE. In bathrooms, only appliances suitable for the zone where they end up (ELEC-ESP-3).
- **Why:** two 2000 W fan heaters on the same TUG are 18 A sustained: the 20 A PIA does not trip and the cable and socket outlets work at their limit all night. Power strips and extension cords are the classic cause of overheating.
- **How:** distribute the heaters among circuits; for radiant floor or ceiling heating use heating cable or foil with an earthed mesh and its thermostat, on its own circuit with a 30 mA ID, per Annex E of 771.
- **Verify:** sum of loads per circuit in winter ≤ the PIA and ≤ Iz; socket outlets without heat marks.
- **Don't:** use power strips for heaters; cover a fan heater; install an ordinary panel inside zone 1 of the bathroom.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.6.6 a) and b) (loads up to 10 A on TUG, up to 20 A on TUE) and 770.7.6 (heating by radiant cables and foils embedded in floors and ceilings: refers to Annex E of AEA 90364-7-771). **Not verified in a primary source:** the content of Annex E of 771 (not consulted).

## Motors and pumps

### ELEC-ESP-14: Every fixed motor: switching that breaks phase and neutral, its own overload protection
- **Rule:** each motor in a fixed installation has: a **switching device** that opens **phase and neutral** (single-phase) or all phases at once (three-phase); a **dedicated overload protection** (thermal overload relay or motor protection circuit breaker (*guardamotor*) set to the motor current); short-circuit and earth-leakage protection (may be shared). On three-phase motors, also **phase-loss protection**.
- **Why:** a PIA or a fuse **does not protect a motor against overload**: it is chosen for the cable, not for the winding; a jammed motor or one with a lost phase burns out with the PIA closed.
- **How:** motor protection circuit breaker (thermal + magnetic in one device) set to the rating-plate current, plus a **contactor** if there is automatic control (float switch, pressure switch, timer); the circuit's 30 mA ID. For building lift pumps, a panel with alternation and protection against lack of water (see [buildings.md](buildings.md), ELEC-EDI-14).
- **Verify:** thermal setting = rating-plate current (not "a bit more so it doesn't trip"); that the contactor breaks both poles on single-phase; trip test of the thermal relay if the device has a test button.
- **Don't:** protect a pump with only a 20 A MCB; bypass the thermal relay because "it trips in summer" (it trips for a reason: bearings, capacitor, low voltage).
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.13.3 (phase and neutral switching, phase loss, dedicated overload; fuses and circuit breakers do not count as motor overload protection). In buildings, AEA 90364-7-772:2019 for common services.

### ELEC-ESP-15: Single-phase motor: capacitor, starting and voltage drop
- **Rule:** size the circuit of a single-phase motor with the rating-plate current and check the voltage drop **≤ 5 % in running and ≤ 15 % at starting**; the capacitor is checked **dead and discharged**. Housing always to PE.
- **Why:** with low voltage the motor does not start, heats up and burns out; a dried-out run capacitor makes the pump hum and trip the thermal relay; a charged capacitor gives a shock even if the circuit is isolated.
- **How:** example, 0.75 kW (1 HP) single-phase pump:
  1. Estimated current: I = P / (U × η × cos φ) = 750 / (220 × 0.72 × 0.85) = **5.6 A** (compare with the rating plate).
  2. Starting: 4 to 7 times In (≈ 25-40 A during starting) → C or D curve PIA per manufacturer, motor protection circuit breaker set to 5.6 A.
  3. Drop at starting, 30 m of 2.5 mm², cos φ 0.3 (770.15.6): ΔU ≈ 2 × I × L × (R cos φ + X sin φ) = 2 × 35 A × 0.030 km × (8.9 × 0.3 + 0.1 × 0.95) Ω/km ≈ **5.8 V (2.6 %)**: meets the 15 %.
  4. Capacitor: isolate, verify absence of voltage, **discharge it with a resistor** (not with the screwdriver), measure the capacitance with a capacitance meter and compare with the µF and voltage on the label.
- **Verify:** actual current with a clamp meter while running, measured by the installer without opening anything (compare with the rating plate); winding insulation to earth (megohmmeter, dead).
- **Don't:** replace a capacitor with one of a different value "that worked"; measure the capacitor without discharging it; feed a distant pump with 1.5 mm².
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.15.6 (5 % / 15 % for motors; cos φ 0.30 at starting) and 770.13.3. Copper resistance at 70 °C ≈ 8.9 Ω/km for 2.5 mm² (typical manufacturers' value; the `gateway electricidad` calculator uses tables). **Not verified in a primary source:** efficiency and cos φ of the example (typical of small motor rating plates).

### ELEC-ESP-34: Booster pump and submersible well pump: motor protection, dry-running and voltage drop over the full run
- **Rule:** the **booster pump** of an apartment or house goes on a socket outlet or circuit suited to its rating-plate current, with thermal motor protection (built in or a motor protection circuit breaker) and PE. The **submersible well pump** goes on its own circuit from the panel, with a **control panel** at the surface (motor protection circuit breaker or thermal overload relay, contactor, capacitor if it is a three-wire single-phase unit, and **dry-running protection**), drop cable **suitable for immersion in drinking water** and a watertight submersible splice; the voltage drop is calculated with the **total** length: panel → wellhead → pump depth.
- **Why:** a submersible works far away and deep down: with thin cable it starts poorly, heats up and burns out at the bottom; without protection against lack of water it is destroyed in minutes when the water table drops.
- **How:** example, 1 HP single-phase submersible with 7 A on the rating plate, 40 m from panel to well + 60 m depth = **100 m** of cable:
  1. Running (table 770.15.IV, cos φ 0.8): with 2.5 mm², ΔU = 15 × 7 × 0.100 = **10.5 V (4.8 %)**, at the limit of 5 %; with 4 mm², ΔU = 10 × 7 × 0.100 = **7 V (3.2 %)**.
  2. Starting (5 × 7 A = 35 A, cos φ 0.3, R at 70 °C ≈ 8.9 Ω/km for 2.5 mm² and ≈ 5.5 Ω/km for 4 mm², X ≈ 0.1 Ω/km): 2.5 mm² → 2 × 35 × 0.100 × (8.9 × 0.3 + 0.1 × 0.95) ≈ **19.4 V (8.8 %)**; 4 mm² → 2 × 35 × 0.100 × (5.5 × 0.3 + 0.1 × 0.95) ≈ **12.2 V (5.6 %)**. Both meet the 15 %, but in running 2.5 mm² is borderline: choose **4 mm²**.
  3. Dry running: level relay with electrodes (probes at MBTS, ELEC-ESP-16) or underload/cos φ relay per the pump manufacturer.
  4. Booster pump: if the rating plate says ≤ 10 A it can go on a 10 A socket outlet of a TUG, with the unit's pressure switch or flow switch; if it starts and stops very often, check the pressure tank before touching anything electrical.
- **Verify:** insulation of the cable and the motor to earth with a megohmmeter before lowering the pump and after splicing (dead); running current with a clamp meter (installer); that the lack-of-water protection trips in the test.
- **Don't:** splice the drop cable with ordinary insulating tape; lower or raise the pump hanging from the cable; work in a well or cistern without confined-space precautions (ELEC-SEG-30).
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.13.3 (motor protection), 770.15.6 (5 % / 15 %; cos φ 0.30 at starting), table 770.15.IV, 770.7.1 h) (controls at MBTS in wet locations). **Not verified in a primary source:** immersion-rated cable, splices and control panel with capacitor were summarized from pump and cable manufacturers' manuals; copper resistances at 70 °C (8.9 and 5.5 Ω/km) are typical manufacturers' values; rating-plate current and starting factor of the example are illustrative.
### ELEC-ESP-16: Float switches (*flotantes*) and controls in tanks: in MBTS
- **Rule:** control circuits in wet locations (float switches of the roof storage tank and the cistern, signaling, alarms) are supplied in **MBTS**; no 220 V inside the tank.
- **Why:** the float switch lives in the water, and whoever checks it puts a hand in the tank.
- **How:** pump panel (distribution board, *tablero*) with a safety transformer for the control that acts on the contactor coil: up to **24 V** in a wet location; since whoever checks the float switch puts a hand in the water, it is better to use the **12 V** limit for locations where the body is immersed (recommended). Float switches or probes suitable for drinking water and for MBTS. If you find 220 V float switches that cut the pump directly (very common in old buildings), that is a **does not comply with ELEC-ESP-16**: replace them with MBTS float switches or probes that drive a contactor coil from the pump panel, or with an extra-low-voltage electronic tank controller.
- **Verify:** voltage at the float switch terminals (≤ 24 V, or ≤ 12 V if that criterion was adopted; measured by the installer); separation of the transformer (primary-secondary insulation, dead).
- **Don't:** use a 220 V float switch that cuts the pump's phase directly inside the tank.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.7.1 h) (controls in wet locations, including cistern and elevated tanks, in MBTS) and 770.14.1 a) (24 V in dry, damp and wet locations; 12 V where the body or part of it is immersed).

## Electric vehicle charging (AEA 90364-7-722 Ed. 2024)

### ELEC-ESP-17: Each charging point on its own circuit, with its own MCB (*térmica*) and RCD (*diferencial*)
- **Rule:** each electric vehicle connection point has a **specific-use circuit** with its **own MCB (*térmica*, PIA)** and an **individual RCD (*diferencial*, ID) of ≤ 30 mA, at least type A**. If the point has an **IEC 62196 socket outlet or connector** (Mode 3 wallbox), protection against **DC** leakage is also needed: **type B RCD**, or **type A (or F) + RDC-DD** (IEC 62955), unless the station already includes it. For an **IRAM 2071 socket outlet** (Mode 1/2) the AEA does not require DC detection in the fixed installation (the IC-CPD of the Mode 2 cable usually includes it); adding a type B or an RDC-DD is a recommendation. The RCD cuts **all live conductors**. In a TN system, the circuit **has no PEN**.
- **Why:** the charger has electronics that can leak DC and blind the RCDs upstream; the car charges for hours at full current; an RCD shared with other loads leaves all of them unprotected.
- **How:** wallbox (Mode 3) with built-in 6 mA DC detection → 30 mA type A RCD and MCB in the panel. Wallbox without detection → type B RCD, or type A + RDC-DD. IRAM 2071 socket outlet → individual 30 mA type A RCD (recommended: type B or RDC-DD). **Watch the RCD that already exists upstream:** if the panel has a general 30 mA RCD (often type AC) ahead of all the MCBs, there is no selectivity (discrimination) between two 30 mA RCDs, and a type AC can be blinded. Branch the charger circuit **before** that RCD (from the main switch / incoming switch, with its own MCB and RCD), or replace the general RCD with an incoming **300 mA selective "S"** one and leave each circuit with its own 30 mA RCD (ELEC-PRO-12). The upstream RCD must be at least type A (never AC) and, if the charger's is type B, also type B. With a wallbox that includes a 6 mA RDC-DD, a type A upstream is acceptable. The circuit demand is taken with **factor 1** (the car charges at its rated current or at the configured maximum).
- **Verify:** charger data sheet ("RDC-DD 6 mA DC" or similar); RCD type in the panel and of the upstream RCD; RCD test; that the maximum current configured in the charger is the design value and can only be changed with a tool or software by trained personnel.
- **Don't:** hang the charger from the house socket-outlet RCD; use a type AC RCD on the circuit or upstream; put a type B RCD downstream of a type A or AC; use the "in-cable" device (IC-CPD) of the portable charger as fixed protection.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-722:2024, 722.311 (factor 1 and configuration by trained persons), 722.312.2.1 (no PEN), 722.314.101 (specific-use circuit), 722.411.3.3 (individual RCD ≤ 30 mA), 722.531.2.101 (type A minimum; DC measures only if the station has an IEC 62196 socket outlet or connector and does not include them), 722.531.2.1.1 (cut all live conductors), 722.533.101 (overcurrent per point), 722.535.3 (selectivity), 722.530.3.101 note 2 (IC-CPD not suitable for a fixed installation). **Not verified in a primary source:** the rule of type B upstream of another B (IEC 60364-5-53, 531.3.3, cited from manufacturers) and that the Mode 2 IC-CPD includes DC detection (depends on the product; IEC 62752).

### ELEC-ESP-18: Prefer Mode 3 (wallbox); with an ordinary socket outlet, a dedicated IRAM 2071 and no extension cords
- **Rule:** the AEA **recommends Mode 3** (fixed charging station). If charging with the cable that comes with the car (Mode 1 or 2), the socket outlet is a **2P+E IRAM 2071 of 10 A or 20 A** according to the charging current, identified with the IEC 60417-6171 symbol, on its own circuit; **no** extension cords (*prolongaciones*), loose female connectors or adapters between the connector and the car are used. One socket outlet supplies **only one car** at a time. Outdoors, equipment **IPX4 and IP4X**; in public places, **IK08** or mechanical protection.
- **Why:** Mode 1 has no control or protection of its own; household socket outlets are not made for 10 A for 8 hours; the extension cord on the garage floor is the typical failure.
- **How:** Mode 1: max. 10 A with a 10 A IRAM 2071 socket outlet. Mode 2: the cable includes control and protection (IC-CPD), up to 32 A. Mode 3: fixed wallbox, power according to the circuit: at 32 A single-phase it is about **7.0 kW** (32 A × 220 V = 7040 W; the catalog "7.4 kW" are calculated at 230 V). Mode 4: DC fast charging, not residential. Socket outlet or charger as close as possible to the parking spot.
- **Verify:** symbol and type of socket outlet; that the cable reaches without an extension cord; IP of the outdoor equipment.
- **Don't:** charge with a power strip or a construction-site extension cord; install chargers in areas classified as explosive (near fuel dispensers, for example).
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-722:2024, 722.314.101 and note 2 (Mode 3 recommended; symbol 6171), 722.55.101.1 (socket outlet types: IEC 62196, IRAM-IEC 60309, IRAM 2071 10/20 A), 722.55.101.2 to .4 (proximity, no portable plugs, one vehicle per socket outlet), 722.512.2.101 to .103 (IPX4, IP4X, IK08), annex A (adapters and hazardous areas), annex D (charging modes; summary table). Phase-to-neutral voltage of 220 V: AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.4.2 b).

### ELEC-ESP-19: Building garages: factor 1 or load management, and check the existing installation
- **Rule:** in buildings, add up all charging points with **simultaneity 1**, unless there is **load control** (in the chargers or upstream). Before adding chargers, **verify the existing installation** (line, protective devices, meter, contracted power). Stations are inspected periodically according to the jurisdiction. Each apartment (*depto*) has **only one normal supply**: supplying its charger from common services (*servicios generales*) is an exception that must be justified and agreed.
- **Why:** ten cars charging at night at 16 A are 160 A; if nobody planned for it, the sub-main (feeder, *línea seccional*) trips or the riser (*montante*) burns.
- **How:** decide with the owners' association (*consorcio*) and the utility (distribution company, *distribuidora*) where each charger is supplied from: (a) **own line branched at the output of the unit's meter**, in the ground-floor meter cabinet (in the compartment of that unit's main panel (TP), with its MCB and RCD; in agreement with the utility, which seals the cabinet), up to a garage sub-panel (TS, *tablero seccional*) for that unit; each user pays for it and the run is short, because meters and garages are usually on the ground floor; the alternative of coming down from the apartment's panel doubles the run along the riser and adds the drop of the sub-main to that of the circuit; or (b) from a charger panel in common services with sub-metering and load management (exception of 770.4.1.3; see ELEC-EDI-18). Voltage drop example, 32 A wallbox at 40 m from the panel (cos φ 0.8, table 770.15.IV): with 6 mm², ΔU = 6.5 × 32 × 0.040 = **8.3 V (3.8 %)** → not advisable; with 10 mm², ΔU = 3.8 × 32 × 0.040 = **4.9 V (2.2 %)** → fine. Configuring the wallbox at 16 A (16 × 220 ≈ 3.5 kW, ≈ 28 kWh in 8 h) is usually enough for daily use and does not overload the unit's single-phase service connection (*acometida*).
- **Verify:** demand calculation of the panel that supplies the garages; selectivity between the RCD of each point and the incoming one (ELEC-ESP-17); record of the current configured in each charger.
- **Don't:** plug the car into the garage cleaning socket outlet (it belongs to common services and everyone pays for it); add chargers without recalculating the line.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-722:2024, 722.311 (diversity 1 without load control), 722.6.4.1 (verify the affected existing installation), 722.6.5.1.1 (periodic inspections according to jurisdiction), annex B (diagrams for a single point and multiple points in a building; it is informative and the multiple-outlet figure shows shared protection: when in doubt, normative clause 722.411.3.3 governs, one RCD per point); AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.4.1.3 (only one normal supply per dwelling; exception for dwelling installations that must be supplied from the building's common services) and table 770.15.IV. The 3 % criterion for the charger circuit is taken from 770.15.6 by analogy. See [buildings.md](buildings.md).

## Photovoltaic distributed generation (Ley 27.424, AEA 90364-7-712)

### ELEC-ESP-20: Connecting panels to the grid is a formal procedure: user-generator, utility and bidirectional meter
- **Rule:** equipment that can **inject** into the grid is only connected with the utility's authorization, under the **Ley 27.424** (national distributed generation law) regime (or the provincial regime where the province has not adhered), with a **qualified installer** (*instalador calificado*) and a **bidirectional meter** installed by the utility. The coupling power (inverter) goes **up to the contracted power**; more only with prior authorization from the regulator (*ente regulador*). In single-phase supplies, in addition, the sum of coupling powers does not exceed **5 kVA** (ELEC-ESP-31).
- **Why:** an inverter connected without notice can energize the grid during an outage and electrocute a utility worker; also, the utility needs to measure what is injected.
- **How:** national flow through the Plataforma Digital de Acceso Público (public digital access platform, via TAD): the user requests the **power reservation** (*reserva de potencia*) (the utility answers: form 1B); the qualified installer installs and uploads **form 2A** (bidirectional meter request) with equipment, certificates, commissioning protocol and sworn statement (*declaración jurada*); the utility installs the meter (installation cost paid by the user); the Secretaría de Energía (national Energy Secretariat) issues the **user-generator certificate** (form 2C). Categories by coupling power: **UGpe** up to 3 kW (LV); **UGme** more than 3 kW and up to 300 kW; **UGma** more than 300 kW and up to 12 MW. The utility cannot charge extra fees for the injection or for its studies. **Jurisdictions:** check the current list of adhering jurisdictions and registered utilities at argentina.gob.ar/economia/energia/generacion-distribuida; the SE provincial implementation guide (Oct 2020) cites as examples the adhesion laws of Tierra del Fuego (Ley 1.276), Córdoba (Ley 10.604) and Corrientes (Ley 6.503). Where the province has its own regime, its regulator and its utility govern; for example, Santa Fe has its provincial prosumer program with its own technical procedure (santafe.gob.ar/ms/prosumidores).
- **Verify:** power reservation approved before buying the inverter; inverter power ≤ the reserved power (and ≤ 5 kVA if the supply is single-phase); user-generator certificate issued before enabling injection.
- **Don't:** connect an on-grid inverter "plugged into a socket outlet"; enable injection before the bidirectional meter.
- **Source:** Ley 27.424 and Decreto 986/2018; Res. ex-SGE 314/2018, Annex ch. 2 replaced by **Res. SE 287/2025** (BO 2025-07-04; repeals Res. 235/2024): categories, power ≤ contracted, 12 MW cap, no additional charges. Secretaría de Energía, "Instructivo para el instalador calificado" (instructions for the qualified installer) (Dec 2020): forms 1B, 2A and 2C. FAQ of argentina.gob.ar (meter paid by the user; installer with degree and license (*matrícula*); the page still mentions the old 2 MW cap). SE, "Guía de implementación provincial" (provincial implementation guide) (Oct 2020 version), examples of adhesion laws. **Not verified in a primary source:** the complete, current list of adhering jurisdictions (the SE pages consulted on 2026-11-15 do not list them, and the list of registered utilities could not be opened) and the details of the provinces' own regimes (Santa Fe, and others we could not confirm).

### ELEC-ESP-21: Only certified panels and inverters, and a signed commissioning protocol
- **Rule:** only equipment with the certifications required by the Secretaría de Energía is accepted. **Modules:** IEC 61730 (1 and 2) plus, depending on the type, IRAM 210013-17 (crystalline silicon; Disp. 97/2019 exempts the torsion test), IEC 61215 (crystalline silicon or thin film) or IEC 61646 (thin film); the 2020 instructions summarize it as "IEC 61730 + IRAM 210013, IEC 61215 o IEC 61646". **Grid-connected inverters:** IRAM 210013-21, or IEC 62109(-2) + anti-islanding protection (**IEC 62116 or VDE 0126-1-1**) + a grid code (**VDE-AR-N 4105 or RD 1699**); the instructions list the combinations IEC 62109 + IEC 62116 + RD 1699, IEC 62109 + IEC 62116 + VDE 4105 and IEC 62109 + VDE 0126-1-1 + VDE 4105. Commissioning is documented with the protocol required by form 2A.
- **Why:** the anti-islanding function is what disconnects the inverter when the grid goes down; without a tested inverter there is no guarantee that it does.
- **How:** commissioning protocol (details and values in ELEC-ESP-31): (1) protective devices installed and verified; (2) disconnection test when opening the main switch; (3) reconnection time verified; (4) earth continuity of supports, frames and enclosures; (5) generator-grid (GR) protection settings verified; (6) access to those settings sealed or password-protected. The utility may ask for a professional engagement registration (*encomienda profesional*) or a DCI (installation conformity declaration).
- **Verify:** certificates attached in the application; real grid-cut test (open the main switch) → the inverter stops injecting in less than 2 s and does not reconnect before 180 s (live test, done by the qualified installer).
- **Don't:** buy an inverter without an accepted certificate "because it's the same"; leave the inverter configuration with the factory password.
- **Source:** Disposición SSERyEE 97/2019, Annex 3.1.1 (modules), 3.1.2 (inverters) and 3.4 (commissioning); Secretaría de Energía, "Instructivo para el instalador calificado" (Dec 2020), 3.1.1.4 (modules), 3.1.3.5 (inverters) and 3.2 (commissioning protocol). AEA 92559-3-1 Ed. 2019 (renewable generation connected to the LV grid; designation as it appears in the AEA catalog) and AEA 90364-8-82 Ed. 2025 (LV prosumers) as AEA references: their content is **not verified in a primary source** (catalog only).
### ELEC-ESP-31: User-generator connection protections: GR protection, interface switch and times
- **Rule:** all distributed generation equipment connected to the grid has: **generator-grid (GR) protection** with disconnection on undervoltage, overvoltage, underfrequency, overfrequency and islanding (built into the inverter if its output power is ≤ **30 kVA**; otherwise, central, with a test button); an **interface switch** (*interruptor de interfaz*) controlled by the GR, automatic and without delay, made up of **two groups of relays in series** and cutting **all poles** (two-pole in single-phase); and a **protection panel** (distribution board, *tablero de protecciones*) near the connection point with an **RCD** (*diferencial*, ID) (IEC 61008-1 / 61009-1; IRAM 2301 is withdrawn, ELEC-IRA-7) and overload and short-circuit protection. Maximum opening and reconnection times in the Reference values table. Single-phase: up to **5 kVA** of coupling in total; three-phase: up to 5 kVA per phase with single-phase units; more than **15 kVA**, three-phase generator.
- **Why:** these settings are what guarantee that the inverter does not keep feeding a disconnected grid and does not reconnect in the middle of a utility (*distribuidora*) switching operation.
- **How:** in a certified inverter the GR and the interface switch are built in: the installer selects the applicable grid code, checks the settings and protects them. Settings (single-phase, measured phase-to-neutral): **U < 187 V (85 %) → opens in ≤ 1.5 s; U ≥ 253 V (115 %) → ≤ 0.2 s; f > 51 Hz or f < 47 Hz → ≤ 0.5 s; island → ≤ 2 s; reconnection ≥ 180 s**. Loss of the GR's auxiliary supply must open the interface switch. DC side: isolator suitable for the DC voltage; outdoor cable with double insulation and a sheath resistant to UV, ozone and weather. Exposed conductive parts and panel structures to an earth **independent of the neutral and of the distribution grid's earth**: in a TT dwelling, the installation's earthing (PE), never the neutral ([earthing.md](earthing.md)). SPD (*DPS*): ELEC-ESP-35.
- **Verify:** inverter display or software with the thresholds and the selected grid code; disconnection test (< 2 s after opening the main switch) and reconnection test (≥ 180 s after closing it), done by the qualified installer; settings sealed or password-protected; earth continuity of frames and structure (dead).
- **Don't:** "adjust" the inverter's voltage thresholds so it does not disconnect when the grid voltage is high; bond the panel structure to the neutral.
- **Source:** Disposición SSERyEE 97/2019, Annex (updates the one in Disp. 28/2019), ch. 2: 2.1 (GR protection, functions, table of times, 30 kVA, protected access), 2.2 (interface switch), 2.3 (protection panel), 2.4 (SPD); ch. 3: 3.2.1 (5 kVA single-phase, 15 kVA three-phase), 3.2.2 (earthing), 3.2.3 (DC isolator and cables), 3.4.1 and 3.4.2 (2 s and 180 s). Res. ex-SGE 314/2018, Annex 3.3 and 3.4, delegates the technical installation and commissioning rules to the SSERyEE. Texts downloaded from argentina.gob.ar (distributed generation legislation, consulted 2026-11-15). **Not verified in a primary source:** whether there were later amendments to the technical annex of Disp. 97/2019 (the SE list shows none, but it is not up to date either: it does not include Res. 287/2025).

### ELEC-ESP-22: The DC side never "turns off": treat it as live
- **Rule:** in daylight, the panels generate DC voltage (in a string, hundreds of volts). Work on the DC side **with the DC isolator open, the panels covered or at night, and verifying absence of voltage**; MC4-type connectors **are not disconnected under load**. DC side with: DC isolator suitable for photovoltaics next to the inverter, **gPV fuses per string when the possible reverse current exceeds what the module can withstand**, DC SPD according to the risk, solar cable, and connectors of the same brand and model at each joint.
- **Why:** a DC arc does not extinguish by itself as in AC: a connector unplugged with current flowing can sustain an arc and start a fire; and opening the AC MCB (*térmica*) does not remove the voltage from the panels.
- **How:** string fuses: they are needed when (No. of strings in parallel − 1) × module Isc exceeds the module's permissible reverse current (data sheet value). Example: 3 strings with Isc = 13 A and permissible reverse current of 25 A → (3 − 1) × 13 = 26 A > 25 A → fuses required; with 2 strings, 13 A < 25 A → not needed. In practice, from 3 strings in parallel they are usually necessary. Double-insulated solar cable (EN 50618 / IEC 62930, type H1Z2Z2-K) in UV-resistant conduit or trunking; polarity and open-circuit voltage measured on each string **before** connecting to the inverter (and verifying that the Voc at the site's minimum temperature does not exceed the inverter's maximum voltage); frames and structure earthed.
- **Verify:** Voc and polarity per string (live measurement: instrument suitable for DC and 1000 V or more, done by the installer); insulation of the DC side with an instrument suitable for DC and photovoltaics; that the DC isolator has a DC rating category (DC-PV).
- **Don't:** join connectors of different brands; unplug an MC4 with the inverter generating; use ordinary AC single-core cable on the roof.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-712 Ed. 2015 (text not consulted); Disposición SSERyEE 97/2019, Annex 3.2.3 (DC isolator; double-insulated, UV-resistant cable) and 3.4 (earthing of supports and frames). **Not verified in a primary source:** the fuse criterion based on reverse current (IEC 60364-7-712:2017, 712.433, cited from technical memory; Ed. 3.0 of 2025 was not consulted); SPD and solar cable were summarized from IEC 60364-7-712 and manufacturers' data sheets; the Isc and reverse current values in the example are illustrative.

### ELEC-ESP-23: AC side of the inverter: own circuit, suitable RCD and "two sources" signs
- **Rule:** the inverter connects to the panel on its **own circuit** with an MCB (PIA) and isolation, with no socket outlets or other loads; RCD as required by the inverter manufacturer (at least type A, or type B if the inverter does not guarantee DC separation); and the panel and the meter are marked to show that **there are two sources** of energy.
- **Why:** an electrician who opens the main protective device expects everything to be dead; with an inverter (or with batteries) some circuit may still be live.
- **How:** MCB sized for the inverter's maximum output current (e.g. 5 kW / 220 V ≈ 23 A → 6 mm² cable (36 A) and a 25 A or 32 A MCB according to the manual; with power factor 1, 5 kW is 5 kVA, exactly the single-phase cap of ELEC-ESP-31); interface switch and GR protection according to ELEC-ESP-31 and the manual; sign on the main panel and on the inverter's sub-panel (*tablero seccional*); single-line diagram (*unifilar*) updated with the inverter; written isolation procedure (ELEC-ESP-38).
- **Verify:** that there are no other loads on the inverter circuit; visible signs; the written isolation procedure on the panel.
- **Don't:** connect the inverter to an existing socket-outlet circuit; omit the sign.
- **Source:** Disposición SSERyEE 97/2019, Annex 2.1 to 2.3 (GR protection, interface switch, protection panel with RCD and overcurrent protection); Secretaría de Energía (national Energy Secretariat), "Instructivo para el instalador calificado" (instructions for the qualified installer) (Dec 2020), 3.2.2 and 3.2.5. **Not verified in a primary source:** dedicated circuit, RCD type and dual-source signage in AEA 712:2015; they were summarized from IEC 60364-7-712 and inverter manuals.

### ELEC-ESP-36: Panels on a roof terrace with lightning protection (*pararrayos*): inside the protected zone, separated or bonded according to the design
- **Rule:** if the building has a lightning protection system (SPCR), the photovoltaic array is placed **inside the zone protected** by the air terminals and respecting the **separation distance** to air terminals and down conductors set by the SPCR design; if that distance cannot be met, the panel structure is **bonded to the SPCR** according to the design, with matching SPDs (type 1 where applicable). With lightning protection, the SPD in the installation is mandatory.
- **Why:** lightning striking the air terminal jumps to the structure or the DC cables if they are too close, and enters the building through the photovoltaic installation.
- **How:** ask the SPCR designer for the protected zone and the separation distance before defining the layout; DC cables as short as possible and without forming large loops; SPDs on DC and AC (ELEC-ESP-35); common building earthing (ELEC-EDI-20, ELEC-EDI-21). If there is no SPCR, don't let the panels turn the roof terrace into an unassessed strike point: the need for lightning protection comes out of the risk assessment.
- **Verify:** SPCR drawing with the PV array drawn on it; distances measured on site; SPDs installed and with a healthy indicator.
- **Don't:** use the panel structure as an air terminal without a design; run the DC cables tight against a lightning protection down conductor.
- **Source:** AEA 92305-1 to -4 / IRAM 2184-1 to -4 (ed. 2015) and 92305-11 (2016), according to the AEA catalog (see [buildings.md](buildings.md) ELEC-EDI-21); Disposición SSERyEE 97/2019, Annex 2.4 (SPD according to IRAM 2184-4 and -11); AEA 90364-7-770:2017, table 770.15.III (SPD mandatory with lightning protection). **Not verified in a primary source:** the calculation of the separation distance and the protected zone (criterion of IEC 62305-3, the basis of AEA 92305-3, not consulted).

### ELEC-ESP-24: Off-grid and hybrid systems with batteries: the backup output never touches the grid
- **Rule:** an **off-grid** system (no grid) does not inject and does not fall under Ley 27.424 (national distributed generation law); a **hybrid** with batteries may supply loads during an outage only if its internal switching **isolates the backup output from the grid**; if it also injects, it goes through the procedure of ELEC-ESP-20. Batteries are installed according to **AEA 90364-5-57 Ed. 2026**.
- **Why:** an ordinary on-grid inverter shuts down during an outage (anti-islanding); whoever wants backup buys a hybrid and sometimes wires it wrong, joining the backup output with the grid.
- **How:** a separate "critical loads" panel, supplied only from the inverter's backup output; batteries in a ventilated place, with their DC protection (fuse or DC breaker near the bank) and away from heat sources.
- **Verify:** with the grid cut (main protective device open) and the hybrid supplying the critical loads, the installer measures, with a CAT III instrument and without opening anything, that no voltage appears on the grid side of the main panel or on the line to the meter.
- **Don't:** connect the backup output to a circuit that also receives the grid; put lithium batteries inside a closed closet (*placard*) without ventilation or distance from combustible materials.
- **Source:** AEA catalog (90364-5-57 Ed. 2026, stationary batteries); Disposición SSERyEE 97/2019, Annex 2.1 (autonomous operation only with automatic protection and switching that isolate from the grid); FAQ of argentina.gob.ar (to enter the regime you must be connected to the grid). **Not verified in a primary source:** content of 90364-5-57:2026.

## Generator sets and UPS

### ELEC-ESP-25: Generator set (*grupo electrógeno*) only with a changeover switch that prevents connecting it to the grid
- **Rule:** the generator supplies the installation through a **changeover switch** (*conmutador*) that makes it impossible for generator and grid to be connected at the same time: manual (3-position I-0-II changeover switch (*llave conmutadora*), cutting **phase and neutral** in single-phase) or automatic (two contactors with **mechanical and electrical interlocking**, or an automatic transfer switch). Operating in parallel with the grid only with the utility's approval and interface protections. The generator is a **standby supply**, not a second "normal" supply of the dwelling.
- **Why:** a generator connected "through a socket outlet" or without changeover feeds voltage back into the grid through the meter and the transformer: it electrocutes whoever is working on the line believing it is cut, and it destroys the generator when the grid comes back.
- **How:** changeover switch after the meter and before the main panel (or an essential-loads panel); inlet for a portable generator with an IEC 60309 male plug on the wall that only feeds the changeover switch; size the generator for the load and for motor starting.
- **Verify:** (1) **dead**: grid cut at the main protective device or the meter, generator stopped and with starting locked out, absence of voltage verified with a voltage tester; with an ohmmeter, and with the changeover switch in I, in 0 and in II, that there is **never** continuity between the RED (grid) input terminals and the GRUPO (generator) input terminals, on phase and on neutral. (2) **Live functional transfer test**: done by the installer with the panel closed, operating only the changeover switch, checking with a CAT III voltmeter on the grid side that no generator voltage appears.
- **Don't:** the "suicide cord" (*cable suicida*, two male plugs) to plug the generator into a house socket outlet; a changeover switch that cuts only the phase; measure continuity with the grid or the generator present.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.4.4 (standby supply → see AEA 90364-5-55) and 770.4.1.3 (only one normal supply per dwelling); AEA 90364-5-55 (Part 5, Ed. 2006), clause 551 (generator sets), including 551.7 (parallel with the public grid); the existence of 551 and 551.7 is also confirmed because AEA 90364-7-722:2024 modifies them (722.551, 722.551.7.2). **Not verified in a primary source:** the exact text of 551 in the AEA (not consulted); the criterion of changeover without paralleling is that of IEC 60364-5-55 551.6. See also ELEC-PRO-27 and ELEC-SEG-21.

### ELEC-ESP-26: Neutral, earth and location of the generator
- **Rule:** when the generator supplies alone (islanded), its **neutral must be referenced to earth** so that the RCD works as on the grid (that is why the changeover switch cuts the neutral); the generator frame goes to the installation's PE. The **single N-PE link** of the generator always goes **on the generator side** of the changeover switch's neutral pole (on the generator itself or at the GRUPO input terminal block), **never** on the panel's neutral bar or on the common side. With the changeover switch in RED, the grid neutral must not touch the installation's PE. The generator is installed **outdoors or in a ventilated room**, with the exhaust directed outside.
- **Why:** with the generator neutral floating, a first earth fault does not make fault current flow and the RCD may not trip; if the link is on the load side, when returning to the grid the utility's neutral ends up joined to the earth rod (*jabalina*) and the PE (prohibited in TT): neutral current flows through the PE and the RCD trips or does not protect properly. And carbon monoxide kills more people than electricity.
- **How:** follow the generator manual and the design (TT in a dwelling): many portable generators come with the neutral bonded to the frame from the factory, check it in the manual before deciding where the link goes; the generator neutral goes to earth at its **own earth electrode of 10 Ω or less, separate from the public grid's earth**, and the frame to the installation's PE (AEA 771, 771-D.8.2; [earthing.md](earthing.md) ELEC-PAT-21); 30 mA RCD downstream of the changeover switch.
- **Verify:** dead and with the changeover switch in I (RED), that there is no continuity between the panel's N and the PE bar; with the generator running and the grid disconnected, RCD test with an instrument (it must trip; done by the installer); frame-PE continuity (dead).
- **Don't:** run the generator in a closed garage or on the landing (*palier*); leave two N-PE links (one on the generator and another in the panel) that make neutral current flow through earth; put the generator's N-PE link on the panel's neutral bar.
- **Source:** **Not verified in a primary source:** criterion of IEC 60364-5-55 (551.4) and manufacturers' manuals for the location of the N-PE link. Own earth electrode ≤ 10 Ω for the generator neutral: AEA 90364-7-771 Ed. 2006, 771-D.8.2 (via ELEC-PAT-21). The TT system for dwellings and the 30 mA RCD come from AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.14.2.3 and 770.14.3.2 (and [earthing.md](earthing.md): in TT the grid neutral is not joined to the PE inside the installation).
### ELEC-ESP-27: UPS: no backfeed to the grid and a permanent-voltage sign
- **Rule:** the UPS is installed according to **IT AEA 95511 Ed. 2023** and the manual; it must have **backfeed** protection (it must not return voltage to its input during an outage); the circuits it supplies are identified because they **stay live with the grid cut**.
- **Why:** whoever switches off the main switch and starts working on a socket outlet supplied by the UPS gets a surprise.
- **How:** a UPS of more than a few kVA in its own panel (distribution board, *tablero*) with a maintenance bypass; PE continuous from input to output; RCD (*diferencial*, ID) downstream if the UPS galvanically separates the neutral (according to the manual); sign "circuito alimentado por UPS" (circuit supplied by UPS) on the panel and on the socket outlets (a different color is common practice).
- **Verify:** with the grid cut, which socket outlets stay live (and that they are identified) and that the UPS input has no voltage: the installer measures it with a CAT III instrument, without opening anything.
- **Don't:** plug one UPS into another; hang refrigerators or heaters from a small computer UPS.
- **Source:** AEA catalog (IT 95511 Ed. 2023, UPS installation conditions). **Not verified in a primary source:** content of IT 95511; backfeed protection is a product requirement of IEC 62040-1.

### ELEC-ESP-38: Installation with two sources: isolate, lock out and verify each source, in order
- **Rule:** with photovoltaics, batteries, a generator set or a UPS, cutting the grid **is not enough**: before working, each source is opened and **locked out with a padlock and tag** (lockout/tagout, LOTO), in the order indicated by the manufacturer and the panel sign, and absence of voltage is verified **on both sides** of each isolation point. The five golden rules apply to each source.
- **Why:** the typical accident is opening the grid main switch, seeing the light go off and touching a circuit that is still live from the inverter, the battery, the generator or the UPS.
- **How:** indicative order (the equipment manual governs):
  1. **Photovoltaics:** switch off the inverter from its control; open the inverter's MCB (*térmica*, PIA) or AC switch; open the **DC isolator**; wait the discharge time of the internal capacitors indicated in the manual; remember that the DC cables between panels and isolator **are still live** if there is daylight (ELEC-ESP-22).
  2. **Batteries and hybrids:** also open the bank's DC fuse or DC breaker; disconnect the backup output.
  3. **Generator:** changeover switch in 0 and locked; generator stopped with **automatic start disabled** (key removed, starter battery terminal disconnected).
  4. **UPS:** switch off, move the maintenance bypass to the position indicated in the manual and open its battery.
  5. **Grid:** open and lock out the main protective device.
  At each point: personal padlock and tag with name and date; verify with a voltage tester tested before and after (ELEC-SEG-5). When finished, the sources are restored in reverse order; only the person who locked out removes their padlock.
- **Verify:** that the "two sources" sign and the procedure are on the panel (ELEC-ESP-23); absence of voltage at the work point with all sources open.
- **Don't:** trust that "the inverter shuts down by itself when the grid is cut"; leave the generator on automatic while working; disconnect DC connectors under load.
- **Source:** general isolation and lockout procedure of [site-safety.md](site-safety.md) (ELEC-SEG-3, ELEC-SEG-4, ELEC-SEG-6, ELEC-SEG-28) and ELEC-NOR-6; Res. SRT 3068/2014 (live work only with specific authorization). **Not verified in a primary source:** the opening order and discharge times depend on each manufacturer; the order given is practical criterion.

## Outdoors, wet locations, gardens and *quinchos* (covered barbecue areas)

### ELEC-ESP-28: IP according to location: semi-covered IP44, outdoors IP54, with water jets IP55
- **Rule:** fittings (luminaires and appliances) in **semi-covered** spaces (gallery, open *quincho*, covered balcony) at least **IP44**; **outdoors**, **IP54**; conduits, boxes and outlet points (*bocas*) outdoors **IP44**, and **IP55** if jet washing is expected (outdoors or semi-covered). Outdoor panel **IP54**. If the job is handed over without fittings, the technical report (*memoria técnica*) states what IP they must have.
- **Why:** wind-driven rain, splashes from the hose and condensation end up in the box; water in a socket outlet means leakage current (trips the RCD) or arcing.
- **How:** outdoor socket outlets with a cover that keeps the IP **with the plug inserted** (self-closing cover or box with a transparent cover); conduit entries from below, cable glands, gaskets; luminaires with the IP printed; outdoor boxes made of synthetic material or hot-dip galvanized (not enameled sheet steel). **Ceiling fans and heavy luminaires** in galleries and *quinchos*: hung from a hook or bracket fixed to the structure and suitable for the weight and vibration, never from the box screws; IP44 equipment in semi-covered areas.
- **Verify:** IP on the rating plate of the fitting and of the box; cable glands tightened; boxes without water inside; fan fixing firm and with its safety catch.
- **Don't:** install an ordinary indoor socket outlet on the boundary wall (*medianera*); drill the box from the top; hang a fan from the lighting box.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.6.6 a) (IP44 semi-covered, IP54 outdoors, technical report), 770.6.6 note 2 (material of outdoor boxes; IP44, IP55 with jets), 770.16 (outdoor panels IP54; see [panels.md](panels.md)). **Not verified in a primary source:** fixing of fans and heavy luminaires is site practice and manufacturers' instructions.

### ELEC-ESP-29: Locations washed down with buckets (*baldeo*) and laundries: high socket outlets or IP54
- **Rule:** in rooms cleaned by washing down the floor (laundries, garages, changing rooms, storerooms), socket-outlet boxes go with their lower edge **≥ 0.30 m** above the floor (there IP20 is enough); if they go lower, the box-socket-cover assembly must be **IP54**. Socket outlets above countertops in bathrooms, kitchens and laundries at **≥ 0.10 m** above the countertop, and respecting the distances to water sources, which the 770 refers to the 701.
- **Why:** the wash-down water runs across the floor and enters the low box.
- **How:** set the height on the detail drawing; building garages with IP54 service socket outlets if washed with a hose. In bathrooms, see ELEC-ESP-3; in kitchen and laundry, the 770 gives no figure of its own: place the socket outlets away from the sink and out of reach of splashes (practical criterion).
- **Verify:** height measured from the finished floor (not from the rough slab).
- **Don't:** leave socket outlets flush with the floor in a laundry; put the countertop socket outlet right next to the faucet.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.7.2 (0.30 m, IP20/IP54; 0.10 m above countertops in bathrooms, kitchens and laundries, "respetando las distancias respecto a las fuentes de agua (ver Sección 701)" (respecting the distances to water sources, see Section 701)). **Not verified in a primary source:** which distance from 701:2012 applies to kitchen and laundry sinks (the 701 was not consulted).

### ELEC-ESP-30: Gardens and *quinchos*: own circuit for outdoors, sheathed buried cables and water lights in MBTS
- **Rule:** outdoor circuits should preferably be **independent** of indoor ones, with a 30 mA RCD; buried cables must be **sheathed cables only** (IRAM 2178-1 or IRAM 62266), with mechanical protection and warning tape; submerged lights (fountains, ponds) in **MBTS 12 V** (ELEC-ESP-7).
- **Why:** the garden is where cables get cut most often (shovel, lawn mower) and where most leakage appears; if the outdoors shares an RCD with the refrigerator, a rainstorm cuts the kitchen.
- **How:** sub-panel (TS, *tablero seccional*) or group of outdoor circuits with its own RCD; sheathed IRAM 2178-1 cable (underground cable type, *subterráneo*), directly buried at **0.70 m** minimum with bricks or slabs and warning tape, or in a duct, according to ELEC-CON-19 of [conductors-and-conduits.md](conductors-and-conduits.md); garden luminaires IP65 or higher if they sit in the watered lawn; in the *quincho*, extractor fan and luminaires IP44, socket outlets away from the washing sink (*pileta de lavar*) and from the grill (*parrilla*). **Decorative lights and string lights** (Christmas or permanent on balconies and galleries): suitable for outdoors (IP44 or higher), preferably in MBTS with their transformer or power supply under cover, plugged into an outdoor socket outlet with an IP cover; if they stay all year, they are part of the installation and go in a box, with fixed cable and connections, not with extension cords. The RCD tripping when it rains on a string of lights is not an RCD fault: it is real leakage.
- **Verify:** insulation of the buried cables (megohmmeter, dead) before backfilling; RCD test; drawing with the route of the buried cables.
- **Don't:** bury single-core cables (IRAM NM 247-3) even if they run inside a conduit or a hose: only sheathed cable is buried; supply the garden lights or the string lights with a permanent extension cord; hang the *quincho* from an indoor TUG with exposed cable; "bypass" (*puentear*) the RCD because it trips when it rains.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.6.6 a) (recommendation of independent circuits for outdoors), 770.10.2 and 770.10.4.1 (sheathed buried cables; see ELEC-CON-19), 770.14.1 (MBTS 12 V with the body immersed), 770.14.2.3 (30 mA RCD). **Not verified in a primary source:** IP65 for lawn luminaires and the criterion for decorative lights (manufacturers' practice).

### ELEC-ESP-32: Electric fence (*cerco eléctrico*): independent circuit with RCD, at 2.5 m or more and with signs
- **Rule:** electric fences or deterrent barriers comply with **IEC 60335-2-76**, are mounted according to the manufacturer at a **minimum height of 2.5 m** above the finished floor, are supplied from an **independent circuit** with an **RCD ≤ 30 mA** (it cannot be omitted), carry warning **signs**, and are operated by authorized personnel.
- **Why:** the energizer emits high-voltage pulses in a place reachable from the sidewalk and the neighbor's side; badly installed or with non-compliant equipment it can kill.
- **How:** certified energizer with its manual; own circuit from the panel (it counts in the demand according to the power given by the manufacturer); earthing of the energizer as the manual indicates; signs along the fence, visible from both sides; also check what the municipality requires.
- **Verify:** height measured from the finished floor; energizer certificate; circuit RCD tested; signs in place.
- **Don't:** build a homemade "energizer" or one with an ordinary transformer; hang the fence from a TUG; place it within hand reach from the sidewalk.
- **Source:** AEA 90364-7-770:2017, 770.7.7 (IEC 60335-2-76, 2.5 m, independent circuit, RCD ≤ 30 mA, demand according to manufacturer, signage and operation by authorized persons). **Not verified in a primary source:** municipal requirements (they vary by jurisdiction).

## Example case: 4-story building with single-phase supplies
The guide's case: ground floor + 4 upper floors, 8 single-phase apartments (*deptos*) (2 per floor), meters in the shared
ground-floor meter cabinet and a common services (*servicios generales*) meter that is usually **three-phase** ([buildings.md](buildings.md)).
Each apartment has its main panel (TP) on the ground floor (2×32 A incoming MCB + 300 mA "S" RCD) and its sub-panel inside,
like the one in [panels.md](panels.md) (ELEC-TAB-18). The apartments are designed with the 770 if each unit falls within its
scope and the jurisdiction accepts it, and otherwise with the 771 (ELEC-EDI-26; [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md) uses the
771); common services, with the 772. Typical special loads and which rule applies:

| Load | Where | Quick calculation | Circuit and protection | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Split AC 3000 fg (frigorías) on/off | each apartment | 3489 W thermal ≈ 1.1 kW electrical ≈ 5.2 A (see rating plate) | own circuit 2.5 mm², 2×16 A C curve MCB, 30 mA RCD (type A if inverter) | ELEC-ESP-10, 11, 2 |
| Induction cooktop 7.2 kW | kitchen | 32.7 A → limit to ≤ 32 A | specific-use circuit, fixed connection at a terminal block (the usual), 6 mm², 2×32 A MCB, type A RCD | ELEC-ESP-12, 39 |
| Electric water heater (*termotanque*) 2 kW | laundry or bathroom | 9.1 A | TUE or own circuit, 2.5 mm², 2×16 A MCB, PE | ELEC-ESP-6 |
| Washing machine 2.2 kW and refrigerator | laundry and kitchen | 10 A; refrigerator < 10 A | 10 A socket outlet on a TUG; refrigerator under an RCD different from the laundry's | ELEC-ESP-33 |
| Bathroom | each apartment | — | zones 0-2, vanity socket outlet outside zone 2 (confirm distance in the 701), IPX4 shower luminaire, no panel | ELEC-ESP-3, 4, 5 |
| Single-phase lift pump (x2 alternating; example 0.75 kW) | common services | 5.6 A; starting 25-40 A | motor protection circuit breaker (*guardamotor*) set to the rating plate + contactor, float switch control in MBTS, drop ≤ 5 % / 15 % | ELEC-ESP-14, 15, 16 |
| Elevator, landings, emergency lighting, door entry system | common services (three-phase meter) | see buildings.md | see ELEC-EDI-13 to ELEC-EDI-16 and AEA 90364-7-791-T1:2018 | — |
| Garage gate | common services | according to rating plate | own circuit with control that cuts phase and neutral, and motor protection | ELEC-ESP-14 |
| Car charger in garage | per unit (or common services, as an exception) | wallbox at 16 A ≈ 3.5 kW | own line from the unit's TP on the ground floor, own MCB and own RCD type A (wallbox with RDC-DD) or type B, branched without being downstream of a 30 mA RCD; incoming 300 mA "S" RCD at least type A; 10 mm² if it is 40 m away at 32 A | ELEC-ESP-17, 18, 19 |
| Panels on the roof terrace | common services (most common) | inverter ≤ contracted power of common services (SG) | UG (user-generator) procedure, own inverter circuit, GR protection and interface switch, dual-source signs, coordination with the lightning protection | ELEC-ESP-20 to 23, 31, 36 |
| Roof terrace or yard with socket outlets | common or unit | — | IP54 fittings, IP44 boxes and socket outlets with cover (IP55 if jet washed), own outdoor circuit | ELEC-ESP-28, 30 |

**Does everything fit in a single-phase apartment?** Adding the special loads in the table at full current:
AC 5.2 A + limited cooktop 32 A + water heater 9.1 A + charger 16 A = **62.3 A** (62.3 × 220 ≈ 13.7 kW), **not counting**
lighting or socket outlets. With the case's 2×32 A incoming device (≈ 7 kW) it doesn't come close, and even applying the
diversity (simultaneity) factor, the cooktop and the charger are long loads that coincide at night. "Upsizing the MCB" doesn't solve it either: the
contracted power and the single-phase supply limit set by the utility (*distribuidora*) govern (and above 63 A incoming
the unit falls outside the scope of the 770). Do the full demand calculation (ELEC-ESP-39, [grades-and-circuits.md](grades-and-circuits.md))
before promising that "everything fits". If it doesn't fit, the options are to limit (cooktop to 16-20 A, charger to 10 A or with
load management), stagger the use, or ask the utility for more power or a three-phase supply.

## What this guide does not replace
- The **design**, the calculation and the **signature** are done by a professional or qualified installer with the legal competence (*incumbencia*) required by the jurisdiction; for distributed generation, the **qualified installer** (*instalador calificado*) registered on the Secretaría de Energía platform. The agent guides and reviews; it does not sign.
- The **utility** decides the connection, the power, the meter (including the bidirectional one) and whether it accepts a generator in parallel; the **municipality** and the regulator decide the approval (*habilitación*). See [regulations-and-permits.md](regulations-and-permits.md).
- The current AEA sections (701:2012, 702:2018, 712:2015, 722:2024, 770 in its current edition, which the catalog already lists as Ed. 2026) and the **manufacturer's manual** of each piece of equipment govern over this guide; where this guide says "not verified in a primary source", consult the text of the standard.
- No step in this guide authorizes live work, nor work on the DC side of a photovoltaic installation in daylight.
