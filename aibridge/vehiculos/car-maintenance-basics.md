# Car maintenance basics

For owners of any petrol car who do basic jobs themselves or who want to know what to ask a workshop. Each rule has an ID
(`VEH-MAN-n`). Model-specific figures (oil capacity, torque values, belt intervals, part numbers) are in
[vw-gol-1994-specs.md](vw-gol-1994-specs.md) and [vw-gol-1994-repairs.md](vw-gol-1994-repairs.md); for long trips see
[trip-preparation.md](trip-preparation.md).

**Safety first, and information only.** A repair that can kill: brakes, steering, fuel system, work under a raised car, airbags,
a hot cooling system, battery acid and the gas it gives off, high-pressure tyres. If you are not sure, a workshop does it. Intervals
and values here are typical figures: the owner's manual of your car is the authority. This is information, not professional advice.

## Working safely

### VEH-MAN-1: Support the car on jack stands, never on the jack alone
- **Rule:** never get under a car, or put any part of your body under it, if it is supported only by the jack; raise it with the jack, then place rated jack stands under the structural points and lower the car onto them.
- **Why:** a jack is made to lift, not to hold; it can slip, sink in soft ground or be knocked, and a falling car kills.
- **How:** (1) Hard, flat, level ground (concrete); not on asphalt in summer heat, grass or an incline. (2) Use the lifting and support points in the owner's manual. (3) Raise, place two stands of adequate rating (at least half of the car's weight each, with margin), lower the car gently on them and shake the car to test it. (4) Leave the jack under the car as a second safety. (5) Put the removed wheel under the sill as a last-resort safety. (6) Do not use bricks, concrete blocks or wooden logs.
- **Verify:** the car does not move when you push it firmly at the corner; the stands are vertical and on solid ground.
- **Don't:** run the engine in gear with a wheel in the air; work under a car on the roadside jack.
- **Source:** general workshop safety practice (vehicle manuals and jack makers); Not verified in a primary source (no specific Argentine regulation for private owners). Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-2: Chock the wheels and secure the car before lifting
- **Rule:** before lifting a wheel, park on the level, apply the handbrake, put the car in first gear (manual) or P (automatic), and put chocks in front of and behind a wheel that stays on the ground, on the opposite end of the car.
- **Why:** a car can roll off the jack when a wheel is lifted, especially on a slope or when the wheel nuts are loosened.
- **How:** (1) Chocks (or a block of wood) diagonally opposite the wheel you lift. (2) Loosen the wheel nuts a half turn with the car still on the ground, before lifting. (3) Lift, change, lower, and tighten the nuts crosswise to the torque in the manual; re-check the torque after 50-100 km. (4) The handbrake does not hold a car which has the rear wheels in the air: use the chocks.
- **Verify:** chocks in place, handbrake applied, nuts crosswise tight and checked after the first drive.
- **Don't:** change a wheel on the carriageway of a fast road; tighten nuts with an impact tool without a torque stick on alloy wheels.
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-3: Let the engine cool and never open a hot cooling system
- **Rule:** wait for the engine to cool before touching the exhaust, the radiator, hoses or the oil, and never open the radiator cap or the expansion tank cap while the engine is hot.
- **Why:** the cooling system is under pressure at temperatures above 100 C; opening the cap releases scalding steam and coolant. Exhaust manifolds and the engine oil cause serious burns.
- **How:** (1) Wait at least 30-45 minutes after the engine is switched off, until the upper hose is cool to the touch. (2) Cover the cap with a thick cloth and turn it a quarter to let the pressure out, then remove it. (3) For an oil change warm the engine for a few minutes only: the oil flows better but is not scalding. (4) Remove rings, watches and loose clothes; the cooling fan of some cars can start by itself even when the engine is off (electric fans): keep hands away.
- **Verify:** the hose is cold; the cap turns without hissing.
- **Don't:** pour cold water into a hot engine; lean over the cap.
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-4: Treat the battery with respect: acid, hydrogen, sparks and short circuits
- **Rule:** wear glasses and gloves when working near a battery, avoid sparks and flames, never short the terminals with a tool, and disconnect the negative terminal first and reconnect it last.
- **Why:** lead-acid batteries contain sulphuric acid and produce hydrogen, which is explosive; a spanner across the terminals welds and burns; the battery can deliver hundreds of amperes.
- **How:** (1) Remove rings and watches. (2) Disconnect the negative (-, usually black) terminal first, then the positive; reconnect positive first, then negative. (3) Ventilate when charging (VEH-MAN-31). (4) Keep metal tools away from the positive terminal. (5) Acid on skin: wash with plenty of water for 15 minutes; in the eyes, water and a doctor at once. (6) Carry a damaged or leaking battery in a plastic tray; never throw it in household waste: return the old battery to the shop that sells you the new one or to a battery recycler.
- **Verify:** no tools resting on the battery; terminals tight and clean.
- **Don't:** smoke or use a flame near a battery; connect the jumper leads in the wrong order (VEH-MAN-30).
- **Source:** general practice from battery makers and owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Used batteries are hazardous waste (lead and acid) under Ley 24.051; read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-5: Prevent fire when working with fuel
- **Rule:** work on fuel lines, the carburettor, the pump or the tank outdoors or in a well-ventilated place, with the battery disconnected, no flame or spark source, and an extinguisher within reach.
- **Why:** petrol vapour is heavier than air, collects on the floor and ignites from a spark or a pilot light, such as a water heater, in the garage.
- **How:** (1) Disconnect the negative terminal of the battery. (2) Work with the engine cold. (3) Have rags and a container to catch the fuel; seal the open lines. (4) Never smoke; no cooking or heater pilot in the room. (5) Keep a dry-powder (ABC) extinguisher at hand. (6) Do not drain a tank in a closed garage. (7) On an injected car the fuel line stays pressurised after switching off: follow the manual's depressurisation procedure; on a carburetted car the pressure is low but the fuel still flows. (8) Leaking fuel lines and tanks are a workshop job.
- **Verify:** no smell of petrol after the job; all clamps tight; no seepage after running the engine for 5 minutes.
- **Don't:** use a naked light to look into the tank; store petrol in soft-drink bottles.
- **Source:** general practice (firefighters and owner manuals); Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-6: Dispose of used oil and other fluids correctly in Argentina
- **Rule:** collect used engine oil, filters, coolant, brake fluid and old batteries and deliver them to a workshop or quick-lube centre (*lubricentro*) that has a licensed waste manager, or to a municipal collection point; never pour them down a drain, on the ground or into the household rubbish.
- **Why:** used mineral oil is a hazardous waste (category Y8 of the national Hazardous Waste Law); one litre contaminates a large volume of water and soil.
- **How:** (1) Drain the oil into a clean container with a lid (a plastic jug), never mix with brake fluid, coolant or solvent. (2) Take it to the place where you bought the oil, a lubricentro or the municipal environment office; ask where used-oil collection is in your area (local rules vary by province and municipality). (3) Old filters: drain 24 hours into the container, then hand over. (4) Coolant: collect in a labelled container. (5) Used tyres: return to the tyre shop. (6) If you pay a workshop to change oil, ask what it does with the old oil.
- **Verify:** the container is delivered to someone who takes it; you can name the place.
- **Don't:** pour oil on dirt roads "to settle the dust" or into the drain; burn it.
- **Source:** Ley 24.051 de Residuos Peligrosos, annex I category Y8 (wastes of mineral oils unfit for their original use) and Decreto 831/93 (regulates the law for national jurisdiction; provinces adhere with their own rules), as shown in search results read 2026-11-15; the collection points and procedures for individuals: Not verified in a primary source; ask the provincial or municipal environment authority.

### VEH-MAN-7: Know which jobs are for a workshop
- **Rule:** leave to a professional the jobs where an error can kill or is hard to detect: brake hydraulics and bleeding if you have no experience, steering and suspension parts, airbag and seat-belt pretensioner work, fuel tank and high-pressure fuel lines, tyre mounting and balancing, wheel alignment, welding structure, air-conditioning refrigerant, and a natural gas (*GNC*) installation.
- **Why:** these systems fail without warning or are regulated: a badly bled brake or a loose steering part causes a crash; an airbag can fire during the work and injure; a GNC installation has mandatory certification.
- **How:** (1) Airbags: do not probe the circuit with a test lamp; disconnect the battery, wait the time in the manual (often 10 minutes or more) and leave the work to the workshop. (2) GNC: the regulator and the cylinder must be checked by a workshop authorised by the national gas regulator (ENARGAS), with the periodic checks of the cylinder. (3) Safety-critical parts: keep the invoice. (4) If a repair needs a second person to pump the brake pedal, learn the procedure first (see VEH-MAN-34).
- **Verify:** the work order lists what was done, with the parts and the guarantee.
- **Don't:** reuse safety parts (brake hoses, tyre valves, steering bolts marked "replace"); work near airbag connectors without disconnecting the battery and waiting the time in the manual.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 29 reg. item 6 (GNC vehicles must comply with ENARGAS norms; consolidated text, read 2026-11-15); general practice for the rest; Not verified in a primary source.

## Routine checks and intervals

### VEH-MAN-8: Follow the maintenance schedule of the manual, and shorten it for hard use
- **Rule:** service the car by the intervals in the owner's manual (km or time, whichever comes first), and use the shorter "severe use" interval for short trips, dust, heat, towing, stop-and-go traffic and old cars.
- **Why:** oil, rubber and fluids age with time as well as with kilometres; a car that does 3,000 km a year still needs its oil and brake fluid changed.
- **How:** the table gives typical values; the manual overrides.

| Item | Typical interval | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Engine oil and filter | 10,000 km or 12 months (5,000 km for older engines or severe use) | mineral oil in old engines: shorter |
| Air filter | 15,000-30,000 km (check every service) | dusty roads: much sooner |
| Fuel filter | 20,000-30,000 km | fuel of poor quality: sooner |
| Spark plugs | 20,000 km (ordinary) to 60,000+ km (long-life) | per manual |
| Coolant | 2-5 years | per type: do not mix |
| Brake fluid | 2 years | VEH-MAN-14 |
| Timing belt | 60,000-100,000 km or 4-5 years | per manual; VEH-MAN-15 |
| Accessory belt | inspect every service; 60,000 km or 5 years | |
| Tyres | rotate 8,000-10,000 km; check pressure monthly | VEH-MAN-17 |
| Battery | 3-5 years life; test yearly | VEH-MAN-29 |

- **Verify:** the date and km of each job in a log book or in the service book (VEH-MAN-47).
- **Don't:** follow only the kilometre figure when the car has been parked for a long time.
- **Source:** typical figures from owner manuals and workshop practice: Not verified in a primary source; use the manual of the vehicle (for the Gol see vw-gol-1994-specs.md). Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-9: Check the engine oil monthly and change oil and filter on schedule
- **Rule:** check the oil level every month and before a long trip; change the oil and the oil filter at the recommended interval with the grade and specification the manual gives.
- **Why:** oil lubricates, cools and cleans; low or old oil is the commonest cause of engine wear and failure.
- **How:** (1) Level ground, engine off 5 minutes, dipstick wiped and re-inserted; the level must be between MIN and MAX. (2) Colour: dark is normal after a few hundred km; milky means coolant in the oil; a smell of petrol means dilution. (3) Change: warm the engine, drain into a container, replace the sump plug washer, fit a new filter (smear the gasket with oil, tighten by hand plus three quarters of a turn), refill with the right quantity and viscosity (for example SAE 15W-40 or 20W-50 for old engines, check the manual) and run for a minute, then check the level and for leaks. (4) Dispose of the oil properly (VEH-MAN-6).
- **Verify:** no drip under the filter or the plug after 10 minutes of running; the level at MAX on the dipstick after settling.
- **Don't:** overfill; change only the oil and leave the old filter on; over-tighten the sump plug (strips the thread).
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-10: Replace the air filter when dirty
- **Rule:** inspect the air filter at every service and replace it when it is dirty, oily or damaged.
- **Why:** a clogged filter raises consumption, lowers power and, on a carburetted engine, enriches the mixture; a missing or damaged filter lets dust into the engine.
- **How:** (1) Open the air box (clips or screws), take out the paper element. (2) Hold it against a light: if no light passes, replace it. (3) Do not clean paper elements with compressed air or water; tap off loose dirt in an emergency only. (4) Check that the box closes tight and the rubber ducts are not split. (5) Oil-bath or foam types follow their own procedure.
- **Verify:** the new element sits flat in the box; the clips are closed.
- **Don't:** run without the element; fit a wrong-size filter.
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-11: Change the fuel filter at the interval and when the car stumbles
- **Rule:** replace the fuel filter at the interval in the manual, and sooner when the car loses power under load, stumbles or the fuel is of dubious quality.
- **Why:** the filter traps dirt and water; a clogged filter starves the engine and, on a carburetted car, blocks the carburettor jets.
- **How:** (1) Note the direction of flow (an arrow on the filter). (2) Work with the engine cold, the battery disconnected, a rag and a container (VEH-MAN-5). (3) Pinch or clamp the inlet hose, change the filter and replace worn hose clamps. (4) Start the engine and check for leaks around the connections. (5) Injected cars have a pressurised line: use the manual's procedure or a workshop.
- **Verify:** no fuel on the filter or the hoses after 5 minutes of running.
- **Don't:** fit the filter backwards; reuse old crumbling hoses or clamps.
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-12: Change spark plugs at the interval and read them
- **Rule:** replace spark plugs at the manual's interval with the correct type and heat range, set the gap with a feeler gauge, and read the old plugs to learn about the engine.
- **Why:** worn plugs cause hard starting, misfire, high consumption and high emissions; the colour of the electrode shows the mixture and the engine's condition.
- **How:** (1) Engine cold. (2) Remove one lead at a time (do not mix the order), blow out dust around the plug, unscrew with a plug spanner. (3) Read the plug: light tan-grey is normal; black and sooty means rich mixture (or too cold plug); white and blistered means lean or overheating; wet with oil means oil burning; wet with petrol means flooding. (4) Fit the new plugs gapped to the value in the manual, thread by hand, tighten to the torque (or one-quarter turn after seating with a crush washer). (5) Refit the leads in the same order; check the leads and the cap for cracks.
- **Verify:** the engine starts easily and idles smoothly.
- **Don't:** cross-thread the plug (stop if it does not turn by hand); change the heat range without a reason.
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals and plug makers; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15. Gap and type for the Gol: [vw-gol-1994-specs.md](vw-gol-1994-specs.md).

### VEH-MAN-13: Keep the coolant the right type, mix and level
- **Rule:** keep the coolant between the MIN and MAX marks of the expansion tank when cold, use the type of coolant the maker specifies mixed with distilled water as the manual says (usually 50/50), and replace it every 2-5 years.
- **Why:** coolant lowers the boiling point and protects against freezing, corrosion and scale; plain water corrodes and boils sooner.
- **How:** (1) Check cold only. (2) Top up with the same type; do not mix the colours or the chemistries (OAT, HOAT, IAT) unless the label allows it. (3) A falling level means a leak (hoses, radiator, water pump, head gasket) or burning in the engine: find it. (4) Renew by draining the radiator and the block drain (if any) and refilling with the mix; bleed the air (heater on hot, cap off or bleed screw). (5) Collect the old coolant (VEH-MAN-6).
- **Verify:** level stable for a month; the heater blows hot; the gauge reads in the middle.
- **Don't:** use tap water permanently; open the system hot (VEH-MAN-3).
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals and coolant makers; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-14: Change the brake fluid every two years and use the right DOT type
- **Rule:** replace the brake fluid at least every 2 years (or the manual's interval), using the DOT grade specified by the maker; never use DOT 5 (silicone) in a system designed for glycol fluids.
- **Why:** glycol-based fluid absorbs water from the air; water lowers the boiling point, causes vapour in the lines under hard braking (spongy or no pedal) and rusts the system from inside.
- **How:** (1) DOT 3, DOT 4 and DOT 5.1 are glycol-based and can be mixed in practice (DOT 4 is usual; DOT 5.1 has a higher boiling point); DOT 5 is silicone-based and is not compatible. (2) Minimum boiling points (US FMVSS 116): DOT 3 dry 205 C, wet 140 C; DOT 4 dry 230 C, wet 155 C; DOT 5 and 5.1 dry 260 C, wet 180 C. (3) Use a sealed new bottle; an opened bottle absorbs water, so discard it after a few months. (4) The fluid is changed by bleeding each wheel in the sequence of the manual, starting at the wheel farthest from the master cylinder: a workshop job unless you have experience (VEH-MAN-34). (5) Brake fluid damages paint: wash off spills.
- **Verify:** the date of the change is written in the log book; the new fluid is clear.
- **Don't:** open the reservoir cap for long; mix DOT 5 with DOT 3 or 4; ignore a pedal that goes soft.
- **Source:** 49 CFR 571.116 (FMVSS 116, minimum boiling points of DOT 3, 4, 5 and 5.1 brake fluids), known standard; read as general knowledge on 2026-11-15, the text was not downloaded; the 2-year interval is a common maker's recommendation: Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-MAN-15: Replace the timing belt by age or kilometres, with its tensioner and the water pump
- **Rule:** replace the timing belt at the interval in the manual (by kilometres or by years, whichever first), and replace the tensioner and water pump at the same time if the pump is driven by the belt; if the history is unknown, replace it.
- **Why:** a timing belt that snaps stops the engine at once and, in an interference engine, makes the valves hit the pistons (expensive damage); rubber ages even in a car that does not run.
- **How:** (1) Find the interval in the manual (typical: 60,000-100,000 km or 4-5 years). (2) Look at the belt: cracks, oil, fraying, missing teeth. (3) Changing it needs the timing marks set exactly: unless you have the data and experience, leave it to a workshop. (4) Replace with a branded kit (belt, tensioner, idler, pump). (5) Write the date and the km on the belt cover or in the book. (6) For the Gol see vw-gol-1994-specs.md and vw-gol-1994-repairs.md.
- **Verify:** the invoice names the kit and the date; the timing marks are aligned after the job.
- **Don't:** postpone it "until it fails"; reuse the tensioner; turn the engine backwards.
- **Source:** general practice from manufacturers' manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-16: Inspect the accessory belt and replace it when worn
- **Rule:** inspect the accessory belt(s) at each service and replace them when they show cracks, glazing, fraying or noise; adjust the tension as the manual says.
- **Why:** the belt drives the alternator, water pump (in some engines) and the power steering; a failed belt means no charge, overheating or heavy steering.
- **How:** (1) Look at the ribs and the back of the belt in daylight; cracks across more than three ribs in a 10 cm length mean replace. (2) Press midway: about 10 mm of deflection is common, but the manual gives the exact figure. (3) A squeal at start-up or when turning the wheel means a loose or glazed belt, or a pulley with wear. (4) Replace the belt as a set if two belts run together. (5) Check the pulleys turn freely and are aligned.
- **Verify:** no squeal at the start in wet weather; the charge warning light stays off.
- **Don't:** spray belt dressing to silence it; over-tighten.
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-17: Check the tyres: pressure, rotation, alignment and the legal tread depth
- **Rule:** check the pressure once a month (cold) and before a trip, rotate the tyres every 8,000-10,000 km, check the alignment if the car pulls or wears unevenly, and replace tyres before they reach the legal minimum tread depth of 1.6 mm in the central zone.
- **Why:** the law forbids driving with tyres with faults or without the legal depth; the grip on wet roads falls long before 1.6 mm; wrong pressure wears the tyre unevenly and increases the risk of a blowout.
- **How:** (1) Use the pressure printed on the door pillar sticker or in the manual, cold; check the spare too. (2) Measure the depth in the central grooves with a depth gauge (a coin is only a rough guide); the tread wear indicators (TWI bars) are flush with the tread at 1.6 mm. (3) For wet-weather safety, change at 3 mm. (4) Rotate the tyres following the pattern in the manual (front to rear, crossing for non-directional tyres); the best tyres go on the rear axle if you mix pairs. (5) On the same axle the tyres must be of the same type and size. (6) Look at the wear: wear on the edges means low pressure; on the centre, high pressure; one side only, alignment; patchy wear, worn shock absorbers. (7) Reconstructed and re-grooved tyres have restrictions in the regulation.
- **Verify:** the gauge reads at least 1.6 mm everywhere (3 mm preferred); no bulges or cuts.
- **Don't:** mix radial and diagonal tyres on the same axle; use tyres with cuts or with a damaged sidewall.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 29 reg. items 4.2-4.6 (wear indicators, tread not less than 1.6 mm, same type on the same axle, ban on tyres with cuts), art. 48 l) (prohibition to drive with tyres with faults or without the legal depth of the grooves), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; Decreto 196/2025 art. 10 (new art. 29: 1.6 mm), partial read via argentina.gob.ar on 2026-11-15. The 3 mm wet-weather advice and the rotation interval: Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-MAN-18: Read the tyre date code (DOT) and replace old tyres
- **Rule:** read the DOT code on the sidewall to know the age of the tyre, inspect tyres older than 5 years every year, and replace tyres older than about 10 years even if they look new.
- **Why:** rubber hardens and cracks with age; an old spare that has never been used can fail; the law requires tyres with the regulation markings.
- **How:** (1) Find the sidewall marking starting with DOT; the last four digits are the date: the first two the week and the last two the year (for example 2319 means week 23 of 2019). (2) Tyres made before the year 2000 have three digits. (3) The tyre manufacturers and car makers advise replacement at around 6-10 years from manufacture: check the maker's advice. (4) Inspect for cracks in the tread and sidewall, and bulges. (5) When buying, check the DOT date so that you do not receive an old stock, and, for a pricey set, look at the whole code to detect counterfeits (see VEH-MAN-41).
- **Verify:** all five tyres including the spare have a date of manufacture less than 6-10 years ago.
- **Don't:** put an old spare on the car and drive at highway speed; buy "new" tyres of unknown age.
- **Source:** 49 CFR 574.5 (US tyre identification number: the last four digits show the week and the year of manufacture; the DOT code is also printed on tyres sold in Argentina), standard knowledge, text not downloaded; read 2026-11-15; Ley 24.449 art. 29 reg. item 4.2 (tyres with the regulation markings). The 6-10 year advice: manufacturers' guidance, Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-MAN-19: Check the spare wheel and wheel fastenings after every tyre job
- **Rule:** after any wheel change, re-torque the wheel nuts after 50-100 km, check the valve caps and the spare wheel's pressure, and check that the wheel brace and the locking key are in the car.
- **Why:** wheels that come off are caused by loose or wrong nuts; a spare that is flat does not help.
- **How:** (1) Torque value in the manual (typically 80-120 Nm for small cars, check). (2) Cone-seat nuts for steel and alloy wheels differ: use the right type. (3) Check for cracks in alloy wheels and bent steel rims. (4) Replace damaged valves with the tyre. (5) Keep the spare pressure 0.3-0.5 bar above the normal.
- **Verify:** the nuts hold their torque on the second check.
- **Don't:** use grease on the nut threads unless the manual says so; use an impact wrench at full power.
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Ley 24.449 art. 29 reg. item 4.9 (rims with cracks or missing fixings may not be used on the public road), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15.

## Dashboard warning lights

### VEH-MAN-20: Read warning lights by colour and act
- **Rule:** treat a red light as "stop safely now", an amber light as "get it checked soon", and a green or blue light as information; check the manual for the exact symbols of your car.
- **Why:** a red oil-pressure or temperature light means damage in seconds or minutes; an amber light often warns of a developing fault.
- **How:** use the table.

| Light | Colour | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oil can (oil pressure) | red | low oil pressure | stop at once, engine off; check the level; do not drive if the level is right: tow |
| Thermometer or coolant | red | engine overheating | stop, VEH-MAN-23; never open the cap hot |
| Battery | red | charging fault (belt or alternator) | drive to the nearest workshop only if the belt is intact; switch off consumers |
| Brake (circle with exclamation) | red | handbrake on, low fluid or hydraulic fault | check the handbrake; if off, check the fluid; if low, stop and tow |
| ABS | amber | ABS disabled; normal brakes work | have it checked; drive carefully |
| Engine or injection (*check engine*) | amber | fault in the engine management (injected cars only) | have it scanned; flashing means misfire: reduce load and go to the workshop |
| Airbag | red or amber | fault in the airbag system | workshop; airbags may not work |
| Low fuel | amber | reserve | refuel within 40-60 km |
| Tyre pressure (TPMS) | amber | low pressure in a tyre (cars with the system) | stop and check |
| Seat belt | red | belt not fastened | fasten |

- **Verify:** all lights come on for a moment when the key is turned on and go off after starting; a light which stays on is a fault.
- **Don't:** cover or remove a warning bulb; keep driving with a red light on.
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15. Cars of the early 1990s have few lights (oil, charge, brake, high beam): see vw-gol-1994-specs.md.

## Diagnosis by symptom

### VEH-MAN-21: Diagnose a car that will not start: battery, starter, fuel or spark
- **Rule:** classify the symptom first (nothing, click, crank without starting) and then check the likely cause in the order below.
- **Why:** most no-start problems are simple (battery, connection, fuel) and can be solved with a few minutes of checks.
- **How:** (1) Nothing when turning the key and the lights are dim or off: flat battery or loose or corroded terminal; try the headlight, the horn; check the terminals; jump-start (VEH-MAN-30). (2) A single loud click or a rapid clicking: weak battery or poor connection; a single click with a good battery points to the starter or the solenoid. (3) The engine cranks briskly but does not start: it lacks fuel (empty tank, clogged filter, failed pump, carburettor without fuel, choke not set) or spark (wet or fouled plugs, failed coil, distributor or ignition module, wet leads) or compression/timing (the belt snapped; the engine turns too freely). (4) Fuel smell and the engine floods: wait 10 minutes and try with the accelerator pressed to the floor (carburetted engines, flood clear). (5) Check the spark: hold a plug lead near a metal part with an insulated tool (only if you know how, risk of shock and fire): a strong blue spark is good. (6) Check an anti-theft immobiliser light on newer cars. (7) If none of this finds it, call assistance.
- **Verify:** you have found whether the fault is electrical, fuel or ignition before you buy any part.
- **Don't:** keep cranking for more than 10 seconds at a time (it overheats the starter and drains the battery); tow-start an automatic car.
- **Source:** general mechanical practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15. Model-specific faults: [vw-gol-1994-repairs.md](vw-gol-1994-repairs.md).

### VEH-MAN-22: Diagnose smoke from the exhaust by colour
- **Rule:** use the colour of the exhaust smoke as a first clue: white thick smoke that does not clear, blue-grey smoke, black smoke; thin white vapour on a cold morning is normal condensation.
- **Why:** each colour points to a different failure and a different cost.
- **How:** use the table.

| Smoke | Likely cause | First checks |
|---|---|---|
| Thin white vapour, disappears after warm-up | condensation | none |
| Thick white, sweet smell, coolant level drops | coolant burning (head gasket, cracked head) | stop driving; workshop |
| Blue or blue-grey | oil burning (worn valve seals, rings, over-full oil, PCV) | oil level and consumption; workshop |
| Black | too rich mixture (choke stuck, dirty air filter, carburettor fault, injector fault) | air filter, choke, carburettor adjustment |
| Grey | various: automatic transmission fluid, too lean | workshop |

- **Verify:** note the colour when the engine is cold, warm and under load; check the oil and coolant levels.
- **Don't:** ignore a rising coolant or oil level; drive far with thick white smoke.
- **Source:** general mechanical practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15. A car with smoke beyond the limits cannot pass the inspection and the regulation prohibits circulating with excessive emissions (Ley 24.449 art. 48 w).

### VEH-MAN-23: Handle and diagnose overheating
- **Rule:** if the engine overheats, stop safely, switch off, wait for it to cool, check the level and look for the cause before you go on; recurring overheating needs a workshop.
- **Why:** overheating warps the head and blows the head gasket. Usual causes: low coolant (leak), a stuck thermostat, a fan that does not run, a clogged radiator, a loose or broken belt, a bad water pump, a faulty cap, or a blown head gasket.
- **How:** (1) Heater on full and ease off; stop; engine off (see also trip-preparation.md). (2) When cold, check the level and the hoses for leaks; check the belt. (3) With the engine running (cold start), see if the electric fan starts when the gauge rises; look at the upper hose: if it stays cold while the engine is hot, the thermostat is stuck; if the hose is hard and swollen, there may be a gas leak into the coolant (head gasket). (4) Test: a CO test kit in the expansion tank is a workshop test. (5) Bubbles in the expansion tank, white smoke and milky oil mean a head gasket failure.
- **Verify:** the cause found and fixed, then 30 km with the gauge steady.
- **Don't:** drive with a failed water pump or fan "just to get home"; add stop-leak additives as a permanent repair.
- **Source:** general mechanical practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-24: Interpret noises when braking
- **Rule:** use the type of noise to decide how urgent the brake job is: squeal, grinding, clunk, pulsating pedal.
- **Why:** brake noises are usually the wear warning; metal on metal destroys the discs and brakes badly.
- **How:** (1) High squeal that stops when you press hard: the pad wear tab touches the disc, or glazed pads; inspect the pads soon. (2) Metallic grinding: the friction material is gone, the backing plate scores the disc: stop driving and have it repaired; the discs probably need replacement. (3) Clunk when you start braking or release: loose caliper, worn pad slides, or suspension looseness. (4) Pulsating pedal and steering shake while braking: warped or uneven discs (or drums). (5) Squeal only in the morning or in damp weather and then gone: usually surface rust, normal. (6) Pedal to the floor or spongy: see VEH-MAN-34.
- **Verify:** inspect the pads, discs and drums at every service; the new parts silent after the first 200 km of bedding in.
- **Don't:** keep driving with the grinding; fit cheap pads without chamfers or shims.
- **Source:** general mechanical practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-25: Interpret noises when turning and over bumps
- **Rule:** classify the noise: clicking when turning at low speed, a hum that changes with speed, clunks over bumps, a groan when steering.
- **Why:** the steering and suspension are safety systems; a worn ball joint or a failed CV joint can let the wheel collapse.
- **How:** (1) Rhythmic clicking in full-lock turns: worn outer CV joint (front-wheel-drive cars); check for a torn rubber boot and grease. (2) A hum or growl that increases with speed and changes when you swerve left or right: wheel bearing. (3) Clunks over bumps: worn shock absorber, anti-roll bar link, bushes, ball joints or strut top mounts. (4) A groan or a whine when turning the wheel: low power steering fluid, a slipping belt, or the pump. (5) Vibration or looseness in the wheel: tie rod ends. (6) A knock from the rear over bumps: shock absorbers, bushes, exhaust touching. (7) These parts are checked in the VTV: the workshop can lift the car and check the play.
- **Verify:** the play check of VEH-MAN-45.
- **Don't:** postpone a noisy wheel bearing or a torn CV boot; it only gets more expensive.
- **Source:** general mechanical practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-26: Diagnose vibration
- **Rule:** note when the vibration occurs (at a particular speed, under braking, at idle, under acceleration) and where you feel it (steering wheel, seat, floor).
- **Why:** the pattern shows the cause: wheels, brakes, driveshafts, engine mounts or the engine itself.
- **How:** (1) Steering wheel shake between 80 and 110 km/h: front wheel out of balance, bent rim or a tyre bulge; balancing is cheap. (2) Seat or floor vibration at speed: rear wheels or the driveshaft. (3) Vibration only when braking: warped discs or drums. (4) Shake at idle that goes when you rev: misfire, a failed engine mount or a poorly adjusted carburettor. (5) Shake when accelerating: worn CV joints or driveshaft joints. (6) A tyre with a bulge or separation: replace at once. (7) Check the wheel nuts and the pressure first.
- **Verify:** after balancing and rotating, the vibration disappears; if not, check the suspension and the bearings.
- **Don't:** drive at speed with a bulge in a tyre.
- **Source:** general mechanical practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-27: Diagnose a car that pulls to one side
- **Rule:** find out whether the car pulls with the brakes off, under braking, or when accelerating, and check the tyre pressure, the brakes and the alignment in that order.
- **Why:** a car that pulls is unsafe in an emergency; the cause may be as simple as an unequal pressure or as serious as a seized brake caliper.
- **How:** (1) Check that the pressures are equal. (2) Pull to one side while driving straight with the hands off the wheel on a flat road with no crown (carefully): suspect the alignment, a tyre (conicity) or a sticking brake. (3) Pull under braking: a sticking caliper, a collapsed brake hose or contaminated pads on one side. (4) A hot wheel after a drive or a burning smell: a stuck caliper or a seized shoe. (5) Pull only when accelerating: the driveshaft or the engine mount. (6) Have the alignment checked after replacing tyres or after hitting a pothole.
- **Verify:** after the repair the car runs straight with light hands on the wheel.
- **Don't:** fix a pull by adjusting the steering wheel; drive with a hot, stuck brake.
- **Source:** general mechanical practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-28: Identify leaks by colour and location
- **Rule:** identify any fluid under the car by colour, smell and position (put a clean white cardboard under the engine overnight), and act according to the urgency.
- **Why:** a leak is the first sign of a failing seal or hose; a brake fluid or fuel leak is dangerous.
- **How:** use the table.

| Colour | Fluid | Where | Urgency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clear water | air-conditioning condensation | under the cabin front | none |
| Green, orange, pink, sweet smell | coolant | front, radiator, hoses, water pump | high; check the level |
| Brown to black, slippery | engine oil | under the engine, sump, valve cover | medium; check the level |
| Red or brown, thin | automatic transmission or power steering fluid | front or middle | medium |
| Clear to yellow, oily, near wheels | brake fluid | at a wheel or under the pedal area | very high: do not drive |
| Yellow-brown, smelling of petrol | fuel | under the tank, the lines or the carburettor | very high: fire risk; do not drive |
| Dark, thick and smelly | gear oil | near the transmission or axles | medium |

- **Verify:** the cardboard is dry after the repair and the levels are stable for a week.
- **Don't:** use your fingers to taste or smell brake fluid; ignore a fuel smell.
- **Source:** general mechanical practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

## Battery

### VEH-MAN-29: Test the battery and the charging system
- **Rule:** test the battery's rest voltage and its voltage during cranking, and check the alternator's output, once a year and before winter or a long trip.
- **Why:** a battery loses capacity with age (3-5 years of typical life), and the alternator may be failing or overcharging.
- **How:** (1) With a multimeter on DC volts: engine off, rest 30 minutes after the last drive or charging: 12.6-12.8 V full; 12.4 V about 75%; 12.2 V about 50%; below 12.0 V discharged. (2) While cranking: the voltage should not drop below about 9.6 V; a larger drop suggests a weak battery or a bad connection. (3) Engine running at 2,000 rpm, lights on: 13.8-14.7 V; below 13.5 V a charging fault; above 15 V an overcharging regulator. (4) Check the electrolyte level in serviceable batteries (distilled water to the mark); check the date code on the case. (5) Test the connections: voltage drop between the post and the terminal clamp should be near zero. (6) A shop load test is more reliable.
- **Verify:** the readings are recorded with the date.
- **Don't:** top up with tap water or acid; ignore a swollen case.
- **Source:** general practice from battery makers and workshop manuals (typical voltages); Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-30: Jump-start a car correctly
- **Rule:** connect the jumper leads in the correct order (positive to positive, then good battery negative to a bare metal part of the engine of the dead car, away from the battery), start the donor, then the dead car, and remove in the reverse order.
- **Why:** a wrong connection causes sparks and battery explosions, or burns electronic modules; the final connection to the dead battery's negative post can ignite the hydrogen gas.
- **How:** (1) Park the cars close but not touching, engines off, handbrakes on. (2) Check that both batteries are 12 V and that the dead battery is not frozen or leaking. (3) Red clamp to the positive of the dead battery; the other red clamp to the positive of the good battery. (4) Black clamp to the negative of the good battery; the last black clamp to an unpainted metal part of the engine block or a strong bracket of the dead car, away from the battery and moving parts. (5) Start the donor and let it run for 2-3 minutes at a fast idle; then try the dead car for no more than 10 seconds; wait a minute between tries. (6) Once running, remove the clamps in reverse order (black from the engine, black from the donor, red from the donor, red from the dead car) without letting them touch. (7) Drive at least 20-30 minutes to recharge, and then test the battery and the alternator (VEH-MAN-29).
- **Verify:** the car starts, the warning lights go off and it re-starts after a stop.
- **Don't:** let the clamps touch each other; connect the last clamp to the dead battery's negative; jump-start a battery that is cracked or frozen; tow-start a car with a catalytic converter.
- **Source:** general procedure from owner manuals (every maker publishes it; follow the manual of your car); Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-31: Charge a battery safely and know when to replace it
- **Rule:** charge a flat battery with a proper charger in a ventilated place, at a current of about one tenth of its capacity (for example 4-6 A for a 45-60 Ah battery), and replace it when it no longer holds charge.
- **Why:** fast or unattended charging overheats the battery, boils off the electrolyte and releases explosive hydrogen; a deeply discharged old battery sulphates and will not hold a charge.
- **How:** (1) Disconnect the negative terminal (or the battery) before charging; (2) clean the posts; (3) connect the charger off, positive to positive, negative to negative; then switch on; (4) use a smart charger that stops at full charge; (5) a full charge takes 8-12 hours at C/10; (6) in a car parked for months disconnect the negative or use a maintainer. (7) After charging, rest 30 minutes and measure: 12.6 V or more is good; if the battery drops below 12.4 V in a day, it is failing. (8) Replace a battery of 4-5 years that is weak; buy a battery of equivalent capacity (Ah) and cold-cranking current (CCA) and the right size for the tray, and return the old battery to the seller.
- **Verify:** 12.6 V or more after 30 minutes rest, and a clean start.
- **Don't:** charge in a closed room with sparks; charge a frozen battery; throw an old battery away.
- **Source:** general practice from battery makers; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15. Disposal: Ley 24.051 (hazardous waste), see VEH-MAN-6.

## Lights and fuses

### VEH-MAN-32: Replace bulbs and fuses with the same type and rating
- **Rule:** replace a failed bulb with the same type and wattage, and a blown fuse with a fuse of the same amperage; if a new fuse blows again, find the fault instead of using a bigger fuse.
- **Why:** a fuse is the weakest point that protects the wire from burning; a bigger fuse or a wire in its place can start a fire.
- **How:** (1) Fuse box: look at the lid for the diagram or the manual; pull the fuse with the puller; a blown fuse has a broken strip; use a test lamp or a multimeter to check. (2) Replace with the same colour and number (amps). (3) Bulbs: switch off, let the bulb cool, do not touch the glass of halogen bulbs with the fingers (oil causes hot spots); clean it with alcohol if touched. (4) Replace in pairs for headlights to keep the colour and brightness equal. (5) Check the earths: a dim light can be a rusty earth. (6) Relays: swap with the same type to test. (7) Check that the headlights are aimed (a workshop does it, VTV checks it).
- **Verify:** all lights work, a spare set of bulbs and fuses is in the car.
- **Don't:** fit a nail, foil or a larger fuse; ignore a fuse that blows repeatedly (a short circuit); use "xenon" kits in a headlamp not designed for it (dazzle, and it fails the inspection).
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 31 and art. 47 (lighting system and use of the lights; consolidated text, read 2026-11-15); the fuse and bulb practice is general practice from owner manuals: Not verified in a primary source.

## Brakes

### VEH-MAN-33: Check pads, shoes, discs and drums, and know the wear limits
- **Rule:** inspect the brake pads and shoes, discs and drums at every service or every 10,000-15,000 km, and replace the pads before they reach the wear limit.
- **Why:** braking is a safety system; the friction material wears away and the backing plate then cuts into the disc.
- **How:** (1) Pads: look through the wheel spokes or remove the wheel (on stands); replace at about 3 mm of friction material (the manual gives the minimum, usually 1.5-2 mm at the very least). (2) Discs: check the thickness against the minimum stamped on the disc, look for deep grooves, a lip at the outer edge, cracks and heavy rust; replace discs in pairs. (3) Drum brakes (usually the rear on cars of the 1990s): the shoes are checked through the inspection hole or with the drum removed; check the springs and the wheel cylinder for leaks. (4) Replace pads or shoes in axle sets, never one wheel only. (5) Pads and shoes of reputable brands; after the replacement bed in with 10-20 gentle stops from 50 km/h without a full stop.
- **Verify:** measure and write down the thickness with the date.
- **Don't:** replace the pads of one wheel only; use glazed or oily pads.
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Ley 24.449 art. 29 a) 1 of the regulation (braking system safe and effective), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-34: Recognise brake pedal faults and when it is a workshop job
- **Rule:** if the brake pedal is spongy, goes to the floor, sinks slowly or is hard, or the car takes longer to stop, stop driving the car in traffic and have the brake system inspected; bleeding and hydraulic repairs are workshop jobs unless you have experience.
- **Why:** the brake hydraulics have no redundancy in old single-circuit systems; air, a leak or a failing master cylinder or servo mean loss of braking.
- **How:** (1) A spongy pedal: air in the lines, old fluid or a flexible hose swelling: bleed and replace the fluid. (2) The pedal sinks slowly under steady pressure: a leaking master cylinder or a leaking wheel cylinder or caliper. (3) A hard pedal: servo (vacuum booster) failure; check the vacuum hose. (4) A low pedal: pads and shoes worn or the drum brakes out of adjustment. (5) A falling fluid level in the reservoir: leak or worn pads; look at each wheel. (6) A long pedal travel and a dragging brake: stuck caliper. (7) Bleeding: two people, the sequence from the farthest wheel from the master cylinder, the reservoir never allowed to empty; a brake pressure bleeder or a vacuum bleeder helps. (8) After the job test the brakes at low speed in a safe place before driving.
- **Verify:** the pedal is firm after the repair and the fluid level stays stable.
- **Don't:** drive after a brake fluid leak; bleed with the reservoir empty; use the engine brake alone as a substitute.
- **Source:** general practice from workshop manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Ley 24.449 art. 29 a) 1 of the regulation (braking system), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-35: Adjust and test the handbrake
- **Rule:** test the handbrake monthly and adjust it when the lever travels more than the manual allows (typically 4-8 clicks) or the car does not hold on a slope.
- **Why:** the handbrake is the second braking system and the one that keeps the car still; cables stretch and rust.
- **How:** (1) On a gentle slope with the lever fully applied the car must hold; (2) count the clicks; (3) adjust at the lever or at the equaliser under the car following the manual (rear wheels must turn freely with the lever released); (4) if the cable is seized or the rear shoes are worn, repair them; (5) in cold climates avoid parking with the handbrake on in frost: leave in gear.
- **Verify:** the car holds on a slope, the rear wheels turn freely with the lever released.
- **Don't:** adjust it so tight that the rear brakes drag; rely on it to hold the car with the wheels lifted (VEH-MAN-2).
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15. The inspection checks the parking brake effectiveness (secondary, iProfesional).

## A car that has been parked for months

### VEH-MAN-36: Before starting a car that has been parked for months, inspect fuel, oil, battery and rodents
- **Rule:** before turning the key, check for rodent nests and damage, the battery, the fuel and the oil and coolant levels; do not start on old fuel without a decision.
- **Why:** a car that sits for months suffers old fuel, a dead battery, perished rubber, rodent damage and moisture; starting it blind can damage the engine.
- **How:** (1) Walk round: leaks on the ground, tyres, damage by rodents (chewed wires, nests in the air box, the glove box and the engine bay: remove all). (2) Oil: check the level and the colour; if the oil is very old (a year or more), change the oil and filter before starting if possible. (3) Coolant and brake fluid: check the levels; the brake fluid is old if the car is over 2 years from the change. (4) Fuel: petrol older than 3-6 months loses volatility and forms gum and varnish, especially with ethanol blends; for a long storage the best action is to drain the tank and carburettor and put fresh fuel; a few litres of fresh fuel added to a nearly full tank of old fuel is a compromise. (5) Battery: measure the voltage; charge it (VEH-MAN-31) or replace it; an old battery that has sat for months is probably dead. (6) Check the air filter and the exhaust tip for nests. (7) Check the lights, wipers and rubber seals. (8) Pull the plugs and turn the engine by hand (using a spanner on the crank pulley, in the direction of rotation) and put a few drops of oil in the cylinders if it has been parked a very long time.
- **Verify:** the fluid levels are good, there are no nests, the battery shows 12.4 V or more and the fuel is fresh.
- **Don't:** start the engine with a nest in the air box; start the engine immediately after a long storage with old oil and a dry engine, and rev it hard.
- **Source:** general practice (workshops and classic-car clubs); Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-37: Free seized brakes and check the tyres of a car parked for months
- **Rule:** before the first drive, check that every wheel turns freely, that the brakes are not seized and that the tyres have not flat-spotted or cracked, and refill the pressure.
- **Why:** pads and shoes rust to the discs and drums; calipers, wheel cylinders and cables seize; tyres left under load develop flat spots and lose pressure.
- **How:** (1) Release the handbrake (if the cable is seized, do not force it; use penetrating oil and leave on the gear); (2) Lift each wheel on stands and spin it by hand: it must turn almost freely; (3) a wheel that does not turn: take the wheel off, inspect the caliper and the pad; (4) light surface rust on discs is normal and cleans after a few brake applications; (5) brake pedal firm and does not sink; inspect the master cylinder and the brake hoses for cracks; (6) tyres: inflate to the correct pressure plus a little; look for cracks in the sidewalls and the tread (VEH-MAN-18); a flat spot makes a thumping that goes away after a few km, but deep cracks mean replacement; (7) replace the tyres if older than 6-10 years; (8) if the car stood on a damp surface, check the underside for rust.
- **Verify:** all wheels turn freely, the brake pedal is firm and the tyres hold pressure over two days.
- **Don't:** drive on tyres with cracked sidewalls; free a seized brake by driving hard.
- **Source:** general practice (workshops and classic-car clubs); Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-38: Do the first start and the first drive of a long-parked car in steps
- **Rule:** start the engine, let it idle, check for leaks and noises, and make a first short drive at low speed before any long trip; have the car serviced and the brakes checked before you use it on the road.
- **Why:** rubber seals dry out and leak when pressure returns; the first drive reveals problems in brakes, clutch, steering, fuel and cooling.
- **How:** (1) Fresh fuel in a carburetted car: pump the throttle once, set the choke as normal, and crank in short bursts of up to 10 seconds with rests; let the oil pressure light go out; (2) idle for 5 minutes, watching the temperature, oil light and charging light, and looking under the car for leaks and checking the exhaust smoke; (3) check the lights, the horn and the wipers; (4) test the brakes at walking pace in a safe private place, then at 30 km/h; (5) drive 5-10 km at low speed, return, recheck the levels and the leaks; (6) change the oil and the filter, the fuel filter and the brake fluid after the car has been parked more than a year; (7) check the insurance and the technical inspection (VTV) status before driving on the road (ownership-and-paperwork.md and insurance.md); (8) a long trip only after a service and a test run (see trip-preparation.md).
- **Verify:** no leaks, normal temperature, firm brakes and a clean start after the test drive.
- **Don't:** rev the cold engine; take the car on a motorway straight away.
- **Source:** general practice (workshops and classic-car clubs); Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

## Choosing a workshop and parts in Argentina

### VEH-MAN-39: Choose a workshop and get a written estimate
- **Rule:** choose a workshop by reputation, specialisation and transparency; ask for a written estimate before the work and a written receipt (with the parts and the labour) afterwards, and keep the old parts.
- **Why:** the consumer-protection law gives the user rights over the repair service (a written certificate of the repair and a guarantee), and an estimate prevents disputes over price.
- **How:** (1) Ask owners of the same make for a recommendation; look for specialists for your model (old VW air- or water-cooled engine, fuel injection and so on); (2) ask for the estimate on paper with the parts (brand and quality), labour and time; (3) agree that nothing extra is done without your approval; (4) ask for the replaced parts back (it shows they were changed); (5) get an invoice (*factura*) and a repair certificate with the date and the guarantee period; (6) for a safety repair (brakes, steering) a re-check after the first 100-200 km; (7) if there is a dispute, the consumer-protection office (*defensa del consumidor*) of the province or municipality is the place to complain; (8) do not leave the car with documents and valuables inside.
- **Verify:** the estimate and the invoice exist and match; the guarantee period is stated.
- **Don't:** accept "we will see when we open it" without a limit; pay everything in advance.
- **Source:** Ley 24.240 de Defensa del Consumidor, provisions on the repair service and the repair certificate (arts. 12-17 as recalled; the article numbers were not confirmed): Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15; check the text on argentina.gob.ar/normativa.

### VEH-MAN-40: Choose between original and aftermarket parts
- **Rule:** use original (genuine, OEM) parts or aftermarket parts of a reputable first-line brand that state the compatibility with your car; for safety parts (brakes, steering, suspension, tyres, belts, filters on a demanding engine) do not buy unbranded parts.
- **Why:** quality differs widely; a cheap brake pad or timing belt can fail; but some original parts for old cars are no longer produced or cost many times more than a good aftermarket part.
- **How:** (1) Terms: genuine (made by or for the car maker and sold in its network), OEM or "original equipment" (made by the maker's supplier, sold under its own brand), first-line aftermarket (brands of the supply chain with a catalogue and a guarantee), and low-cost unbranded. (2) Use the part number from the old part, the manual or the catalogue of the brand with the car's make, model, year and engine; (3) buy from a dealer, a recognised parts distributor or a shop that gives an invoice and a guarantee; (4) for an old car (such as a 1990s Gol) look at the specialised suppliers and the vehicle clubs, and check which parts remain available (vw-gol-1994-specs.md); (5) keep the box and the invoice; (6) used parts from a scrap yard (*desarmadero*) are legal only from registered dismantlers; do not buy safety parts (airbags, belts) used.
- **Verify:** the part number matches; the box, label and the packaging are intact; there is an invoice.
- **Don't:** buy the cheapest safety part from a street seller; fit a part "that looks the same" without checking the number.
- **Source:** general practice (parts trade and workshops); Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-MAN-41: Avoid counterfeit parts
- **Rule:** buy parts only from reputable sellers, check the packaging, the part code, the QR or traceability code where it exists and the price, and ask for an invoice; do not buy safety parts from informal sellers or at suspiciously low prices.
- **Why:** counterfeit filters, brake pads, shock absorbers, spark plugs and tyres are common in Argentina; a federal operation (2019) seized about 194,000 counterfeit parts (shock absorbers, water pumps and electronic parts); counterfeit brake and suspension parts can fail in use.
- **How:** (1) Buy from the brand's official distributor, a dealer or a large parts chain; online only from the official store of the brand. (2) Compare the packaging with the one on the brand's site: printing quality, spelling, logo, the hologram or the QR; (3) filters are often falsified by changing the last letter of the code: check the full code against the catalogue for your car; (4) scan the QR code: since 2025 a national regime requires manufacturers and importers to put a QR traceability code on safety parts (manufacturer, brand, model, country of origin and certification); the rules were changed in 2026 and old stock could be sold until 2026-09-01, so a missing QR on a new part is a warning sign (secondary report, check the current norm); (5) for tyres, read the DOT code and the date (VEH-MAN-18); (6) a price far below the usual is a warning; (7) keep the invoice and the packaging; (8) if you suspect a fake, report to the brand and to the consumer-protection authority.
- **Verify:** the part has the manufacturer's code, a QR or a traceability mark and the invoice names the seller.
- **Don't:** buy brake pads or tyres on marketplaces without the official seller's badge; accept a part without packaging.
- **Source:** argentina.gob.ar/node/167841 (federal operation "Repuestos Falsos": 30 raids, 45 people detained, about 194,000 parts seized; page metadata dated 2019-08-27), read 2026-11-15; QR regime for safety parts and the 2026 changes: El Sol (Mendoza), 2026-06-03, secondary: Not verified in a primary source (norm number not found); falsified filter codes: Perfil Parabrisas citing SKF Argentina, secondary.

## The technical inspection (VTV)

### VEH-MAN-42: Know what the technical inspection checks and the rules that apply
- **Rule:** every car must have a valid technical inspection certificate (*VTV*, *RTO* in the national text) at the interval set by its jurisdiction; know the systems that are checked and the rules for your province or city.
- **Why:** driving without it is a violation and the insurance may be affected; the inspection checks the safety systems and the emissions.
- **How:** (1) The national regulation requires the inspection of the safety systems and the emissions, with a certificate and a possible roadside rapid check (*RRA*). (2) The Decreto 196/2025 changed the national rules (first inspection of a private new car after up to 60 months; validity 24 months for cars up to 10 years old and 12 months for older cars; private garages and dealers as inspection centres) and invited the provinces to adapt; the province of Buenos Aires announced it keeps its own system (Ley provincial 13.927: annual from the second year), and the City of Buenos Aires has its own rules (reported: from 3 years or 60,000 km); other provinces differ: check the one where the car is registered (see ownership-and-paperwork.md for the dates and fees). (3) The areas checked (secondary report): chassis, bumpers, windscreen, wipers, seat belts and front head restraints, extinguisher and triangle, steering and front end, suspension, tyres and rims, service and parking brakes, exhaust (emissions, smoke and noise), and lights. (4) Possible results: approved, conditional (re-inspection in 60 days for private cars, free of charge in the reports) or rejected (not allowed to drive until fixed).
- **Verify:** the jurisdiction's official site (for the Province: vtv.gba.gob.ar, the provincial regulator) lists the current schedule and the checklist.
- **Don't:** assume the inspection rules of another province apply; leave the pre-check for the day of the appointment.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 34 (technical inspection; the regulation as in the consolidated text with 24 months validity up to 7 years of age and 12 months for older cars; text read 2026-11-15); Decreto 196/2025 art. 12 (new art. 34: 60 months first inspection, 24 months up to 10 years, 12 months over 10 years, conditional result 60 days for private cars), read via argentina.gob.ar on 2026-11-15 (partial read); PBA: La Nación 2026-03-13 and Ámbito (secondary: PBA keeps the Ley 13.927 annual scheme); list of inspected items and results: iProfesional (2023), secondary: Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-MAN-43: Pre-check the lights, horn, wipers and glass at home
- **Rule:** before the appointment check every light, the horn, the wipers and washers, the mirrors and the glass.
- **Why:** these are the commonest and cheapest causes of rejection; a bulb costs less than a second inspection.
- **How:** (1) Park facing a wall or use a helper: low beam, high beam, position lights, brake lights (with the pedal), indicators, hazards, reversing lights and plate light, and the fog lights if fitted. (2) Only the approved lights: no added coloured or non-approved lamps; (3) headlights aimed (a workshop can check the aim) and the lenses not cracked or fogged; (4) horn: a single continuous tone (no musical or siren horns); (5) wipers: blades not cracked and clearing the glass; the washer sprays; (6) windscreen: no cracks in the driver's field of view, and the stickers only where allowed (the regulation limits the stickers on glass to the bottom of the windscreen and the rear and fixed side glass); (7) mirrors: present, not cracked; (8) tyres see VEH-MAN-45.
- **Verify:** each item is working, a list is ticked, and the spare bulbs are in the car.
- **Don't:** go to the inspection with a cracked windscreen, a missing mirror or a non-approved horn.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 34 (technical inspection of the systems that affect safety), art. 48 q) of the regulation (stickers only at the lower part of the windscreen and on the fixed glass) and art. 48 v) (horn or siren not authorised), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; windscreen requirements in the inspection: Not verified in a primary source (Cronista, secondary).

### VEH-MAN-44: Pre-check brakes, emissions and the exhaust
- **Rule:** before the inspection check the brake effectiveness and the parking brake, the exhaust for leaks, smoke and noise, and the engine for fluid leaks; have the engine tuned if the smoke or the CO is high.
- **Why:** brakes and emissions are the commonest reasons for rejection; a car that exceeds the limits for gases, smoke or noise cannot pass and may not circulate.
- **How:** (1) Brakes: the pedal is firm, the car stops straight without pulling; the handbrake holds on a slope; pads and shoes above the minimum thickness (VEH-MAN-33); (2) the exhaust: no holes or leaks, the silencer fixed, no excessive noise; (3) smoke: no blue or black smoke at idle or when revved (VEH-MAN-22); (4) emissions: on a carburetted car the idle mixture (CO) and the idle speed are adjusted by a workshop with a gas analyser before the inspection, after replacing the spark plugs and the air filter and with the engine at the operating temperature; on an injected car a failing oxygen sensor or catalytic converter or a lit check-engine light fails; (5) fluids: no oil, fuel or coolant leaks that drip; (6) the car warmed up for the test.
- **Verify:** gas analyser readings of the workshop (CO and HC) within the limits of the regulation applied by your jurisdiction.
- **Don't:** remove the catalytic converter or the silencer; pass a smoking car "to see".
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 48 w) (prohibition to circulate with vehicles that emit gases, smoke, noise or other emissions exceeding the regulation limits), art. 34 (technical inspection includes the emissions), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; the specific CO and HC limits and tests of each jurisdiction: Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-MAN-45: Pre-check tyres, steering, suspension and play at home
- **Rule:** check the tyres and rims, the steering play and the suspension for looseness, leaks and noises before the inspection.
- **Why:** the inspection uses a pit and a play detector and rejects loose or worn ball joints, tie rod ends, bushes, leaking shock absorbers and worn tyres.
- **How:** (1) Tyres and rims: tread 1.6 mm minimum (3 mm better), no cuts, bulges or cracks, rims not bent or cracked, same type on each axle, all wheel nuts present (VEH-MAN-17). (2) Steering play: with the engine running (power steering) turn the wheel until the front wheels start to move; more than about 2-3 cm of play at the rim is too much; a loose steering box or column. (3) Wheel play: car on stands, grab each front wheel at 12 and 6 o'clock and rock it: play means a worn wheel bearing or ball joint; at 3 and 9 o'clock: play means a tie rod end. Rock it only when the car is on stands (VEH-MAN-1). (4) Check the rubber boots of the CV joints and the steering rack for tears, the shock absorbers for oil leaks, and the springs for cracks. (5) Bounce test: press the corner of the car down and release: it should settle after one or two bounces. (6) The seat belts and head restraints: belt retracts and locks; a head restraint fitted if the seat was designed for one; (7) the number of belts must match the seats. (8) Check the fire extinguisher (1 kg, in date, with a gauge) and the warning triangles are in the car.
- **Verify:** no play or noise found, or the worn parts replaced and an alignment done.
- **Don't:** go with a car that has worn tie rod ends: it is a safety risk, not only a rejection.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 29 of the regulation (steering, suspension, tyres: items 4.1-4.9) and art. 40 f) and k) (extinguisher, triangles, belts), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; the home play test is general workshop practice; the list of items checked: iProfesional (2023, secondary): Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-MAN-46: Act on the result and the deadline of the technical inspection
- **Rule:** if the result is conditional or rejected, repair the faults and return for the re-inspection within the deadline, which is 60 days for private cars under the national text; a rejected car should not be driven (except to the workshop and the inspection, if the local rules allow it).
- **Why:** missing the deadline means paying again and driving with an invalid certificate exposes you to fines and insurance problems.
- **How:** (1) Read the report: it lists each defect by section. (2) Fix the items (the cheap ones first: bulbs, wipers, horn). (3) Ask the workshop for the part and labour invoice. (4) Return with the report to the same plant within the deadline; the re-inspection checks only the failed items, according to the reports (the first re-check is free in the reports). (5) Keep the certificate and the windscreen sticker (or the digital certificate) with the car's documents. (6) The certificate of a car that suffered a serious accident with damage to the brakes, steering, front end or chassis loses its validity and requires a new inspection after the repair.
- **Verify:** the new certificate with the date and the expiry; the sticker or the digital record visible.
- **Don't:** let the deadline lapse; fix a defect by a trick that hides it (it fails again).
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 art. 12 (new art. 34 item 17: conditional result, 60 days for private vehicles, read via argentina.gob.ar on 2026-11-15, partial read); Ley 24.449 art. 34 reg. items 5 and 6 (loss of validity of the certificate after an accident that damages safety elements), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; the inspection results and the free re-check: iProfesional (2023, secondary). Check the rules of your jurisdiction.

### VEH-MAN-47: Keep a maintenance log and the invoices
- **Rule:** keep a written (or digital) log of every service and repair with the date, the km, the parts (brand and part number) and the cost, and keep the invoices.
- **Why:** it tells you when each item is due, helps diagnose problems, proves the maintenance to the next buyer and to the insurer, and supports a claim under the repair guarantee.
- **How:** (1) A small notebook in the glove box or a spreadsheet: date, km, job, parts, cost, who did it, guarantee. (2) Photograph the invoices. (3) Note the fuel consumption at every fill: a sudden rise shows a problem. (4) Write on the dashboard or the engine bay sticker the date and km of the last oil and timing belt change. (5) Put reminders for the next due dates (the VTV, the insurance, the oil and the belts).
- **Verify:** you can answer "when did I last change the oil, the brake fluid, the coolant and the belt?" from the log.
- **Don't:** rely on memory; throw away the invoices of safety parts.
- **Source:** general practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.
