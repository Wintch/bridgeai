# VW Gol "cuadrado" 1994: identification, specifications, maintenance and manuals

Facts about the first-generation Gol (the BX platform, nicknamed *Gol cuadrado*, "square Gol") that an owner or a mechanic needs: how to
read the numbers on the car, what engine and fuel system it has, which fluids and how much, tightening torques, the fuse box and bulbs, the
maintenance plan the factory gave (adapted here to a car that is driven little), parts, and where the manuals are.
It is for owners and drivers of old cars, some of whom do their own basic work.

**How the figures were obtained.** The main source is an Argentine workshop manual, "Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol" (3rd edition, 388 pages, supplied by the owner). It covers the second-generation Gol ("Gol 95") **and** the earlier "Gol GL" carburettor models ("modelos anteriores a 1996"), whose sections apply to the first-generation car; the G2-only sections (injection, hydraulic tappets) do not. Almost every manual of this car that can be found, this one included, is a **scan without text
layer**, so the numbers below were read from page images. Where documents disagree (for example engine power 82 / 85 CV, tyre pressures, gearbox oil grade), both values are shown and the rule says which to trust (see also VEH-GOL-43). The older Argentine factory data found are for the **Gacel / Senda**
(same BX platform, same AP engines, same gearbox, same front suspension) and for a **Gol GTi (1993)**; the Gol-specific tables
(fuses, tyres, capacities, alignment) come from the **Brazilian 1992 Gol/Saveiro/Voyage/Parati manual**. Treat a value that is only
in a Gacel/Senda or Brazilian manual as "very likely the same on the Gol, confirm on the car".

**Safety first.** Brakes, fuel system, cooling system, battery, jacking and working under the car can injure or kill: use axle stands (never
only the jack), work with the engine cold, no flames or sparks near petrol or battery, and have brake and fuel-line work done or checked by a
professional. Nothing here is a substitute for the workshop manual or a qualified mechanic.

## Identification

### VEH-GOL-1: Confirm it is a first-generation (BX) Gol before using any figure in this document
- **Rule:** use this document only if the car is the "square" Gol of the BX family: three-door hatchback with a squared-off body and a spare wheel carried upright in the luggage area (Autocosmos 2024); the second-generation Gol (AB9, rounded shape, "Gol 95", GLi, CLD/GLD) has different parts, engines tuned for injection and different data.
- **Why:** both are called "Gol" in classified ads and in parts catalogues, and a cross-reference for the wrong generation gives parts that do not fit.
- **How:** (1) look at the shape: the first generation has the squared-off body (the second generation, road-tested by *Parabrisas* in 1995, moved the spare wheel to the boot floor and has a rounded body); (2) read the type code in the VIN, positions 7-8: **30** is the BX family (Gol, Voyage, Gacel/Senda, Saveiro, Parati), see VEH-GOL-2; (3) confirm with the dealership (*concesionario*) parts department using the VIN. Production overlap in Argentina: the BX Gol was sold from 1991 and the second-generation Gol replaced it from 1995 (secondary sources).
- **Verify:** VIN positions 7-8 read 30 and the engine code matches one of VEH-GOL-6.
- **Don't:** buy parts by the word "Gol" and year only; do not use the figures of a Gol 1995-2003 for this car.
- **Source:** gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf (cover, all models of the book are BX), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 49 (manual p. 92, VIN example with type code 30), read 2026-11-15; Parabrisas 1995 Gol road test (second generation). Not verified in a primary source: the exact dates of the changeover in Argentina (secondary sources give 1994 or 1995).

### VEH-GOL-2: Read the VIN: country code 8AW (Argentina) or 9BW (Brazil), type code 30, check character, model-year letter, plant letter, serial
- **Rule:** the vehicle identification number (*número de chasis* / VIN) has 17 characters. On a BX Gol the first three characters tell where the car was built: **8AW** = Volkswagen built in Argentina, **9BW** = Volkswagen built in Brazil. Positions 4-6 are filler (ZZZ), positions 7-8 are the type code (**30** for the BX family), position 9 is a check character, position 10 is the model-year letter (VEH-GOL-3), position 11 is the plant letter and positions 12-17 are the serial number.
- **Why:** the first characters separate an Argentine-built car from an imported Brazilian one, which matters for the engine (VEH-GOL-8), for parts and for the paperwork (see ownership-and-paperwork.md).
- **How:** invented example with the same layout: `8AW ZZZ 30 Z R J 000000` = Argentine-built (8AW), BX family (30), model year 1994 (R), plant letter (J in the factory's own example, meaning not confirmed), serial 000000. Position 9 is a check character (a Z in the factory's own example). Write the 17 characters from the stamping, not from the registration card, and compare both (see VEH-GOL-4 for where it is stamped).
- **Verify:** the VIN is 17 characters, starts with 8AW or 9BW, has 30 at positions 7-8, and the stamping on the body, the label and the registration card (*cédula verde*) agree.
- **Don't:** accept a VIN with a different country code without a documented reason; do not post the full VIN of your car in public listings or messages.
- **Source:** gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 49 (manual p. 92: the factory's picture of the plate and the stamping, WMI 8AW, type 30), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: 9BW for the Brazilian VW WMI and the meaning of position 11 (plant letter) are from Brazilian secondary pages (autoideia / autopapo) that also list 30 = Gol, Parati, Voyage, Saveiro.

### VEH-GOL-3: Decode the model-year letter in position 10 (N, P, R, S, T)
- **Rule:** position 10 of the VIN is the **model year**, not necessarily the year of manufacture. For these cars: **M = 1991, N = 1992, P = 1993, R = 1994, S = 1995, T = 1996, V = 1997**. The letters I, O, Q, U and Z are never used.
- **Why:** a car sold in 1994 can carry P (1993 model year, built in 1993) or R (1994 model year); the letter decides which year's parts and manual page apply, and it must match the registration card.
- **How:** the sequence is A=1980, B=1981, C=1982, D=1983, E=1984, F=1985, G=1986, H=1987, J=1988, K=1989, L=1990, M=1991, N=1992, P=1993, R=1994, S=1995, T=1996, V=1997. The 30-year cycle repeats (a letter S can also mean 2025), but position 7 is numeric on cars of this era, which puts it in 1980-2009. The factory's own Gacel/Senda example shows P (1993). Argentine production of this car started in 1993 (VEH-GOL-8), so a Gol with a P or R letter is a normal Argentine-built car and one with N or earlier is a Brazilian import.
- **Verify:** the letter matches the model year on the registration card and the year range of the version (VEH-GOL-7).
- **Don't:** read the letter as the exact build date, or assume the model year is the year of the first registration.
- **Source:** gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 49 (manual p. 92, example with P), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the letter sequence is from ISO 3779 / general VIN practice (consistent with L = 1990 and V = 1997 seen in secondary pages), not from a VW document; whether VW Argentina used the letter for the model year or the build year should be checked with a dealership for a given car.

### VEH-GOL-4: Find the places where the VIN is stamped and the labels, and check they are intact
- **Rule:** the VIN is stamped on the body in the engine compartment and appears again on a number of labels and on the glass. On the Gacel/Senda it is stamped on the rear transverse panel of the engine compartment, next to the battery; the Gol manual shows the "main stamping" in the engine compartment plus two destructible labels (one under the carpet at the door sill, one on the door pillar).
- **Why:** the technical inspection (*VTV*), the registry (*Registro del Automotor*) and any police check compare these numbers; a stamping that has been ground, painted over or does not match is a legal problem.
- **How:** (1) open the bonnet and find the stamping on the rear transverse panel of the engine compartment, next to the battery (Gacel/Senda; on the Gol check the same area); (2) lift the carpet flap at the front door sill (the manual shows a cut-out in the carpet) to read the destructible label (*etiqueta destruible*: it tears if removed); (3) look at the label on the door pillar; (4) the windscreen, rear window and side windows carry the chassis number etched in the glass; (5) photograph each. The factory also prints a second code, the vehicle section indicator (VIS), on the destructible labels (second-generation manual, same practice).
- **Verify:** the stamped number is clean and readable, the labels are not torn, all places show the same 17 characters.
- **Don't:** repaint or grind the stamped area; do not buy a car whose stamping looks reworked without a legal check (see ownership-and-paperwork.md).
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 11 ("Identificación de la unidad": vehicle number inside the engine bay on the left of the upper front crossmember; body number stamped on the rear transverse panel of the engine compartment next to the battery; engine number on the left of the block next to the fuel pump), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 49 (manual p. 92), read 2026-11-15; gol-1993-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 1-2 (manual p. 3-4: "Identificación", Chasis, "Grabación principal", "Etiqueta destruible"), read 2026-11-15; gol-1996-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 1 (manual p. 13: VIN and VIS, second generation, same practice), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GOL-5: Read the identification plate in the engine compartment (model, engine, gearbox, colour, upholstery)
- **Rule:** the factory plate (*chapa de identificación*) on the upper front crossmember, on the left side of the engine compartment, repeats the VIN and carries the car's own codes: model, engine code, transmission, colour code and upholstery code, plus a service-package field.
- **Why:** it is the quickest way to know the exact engine and the paint code (*código de pintura*) without opening anything.
- **How:** the Senda plate in the factory's picture has these headings in order: **MOD** (model), **MOTOR**, **TRANS**, **L.C.** (equipment code), **AC** (air conditioning), **COLOR** (paint code), **TAPIZ** (upholstery code), **PK SERV** (service package), and below them the vehicle identification number. In the factory's own example the engine field reads UN and the model N (Senda 1.6). Photograph the plate, read the COLOR field and give it to the paint shop together with the VIN.
- **Verify:** the engine code on the plate matches the code stamped on the engine (VEH-GOL-6) and the colour matches the car.
- **Don't:** match the paint by eye from a faded panel; do not assume the plate survived a repaint or a swapped bonnet or crossmember.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 11 (vehicle number on the left of the upper front crossmember, inside the engine bay), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 49 (manual p. 92: plate location and headings read from the enlarged picture), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the exact location and field layout on a Gol (the Gol manual in hand, gol-1993-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf PDF p. 2, only labels it "Identificación del fabricante" without legible text).

### VEH-GOL-6: Find the engine number and read the engine code (UN, UD; AE / CHT on early imported cars)
- **Rule:** the engine number is stamped on the left side of the cylinder block, near the fuel pump. The first letters are the **engine code**: **UN** = 1.6 petrol (AP-1600), **UD** = 1.8 petrol (AP-1800), **UQ** = 2.0 (AP-2000, GTi), **BE** = 1.6 diesel (Senda D only). The code and the serial that follows are the "engine number" (*número de motor*) that the registration card and parts catalogues use.
- **Why:** the engine code decides which parts fit (pistons, head, gaskets, carburettor, plugs) and is checked by the registry and the technical inspection.
- **How:** (1) with the engine cold, look on the left side of the block, beside the fuel pump (the factory's photo shows an engine number of the form "UD 910647" there on a 1.8); (2) clean it with a rag, not a wire brush, and photograph it; (3) compare the first two letters with the table in VEH-GOL-9 (the engine code letters are UN, UD and UQ in the Argentine workshop manual); (4) engines made before 1986 had the code **UC** and a 79.5 mm bore (the 1986-onward engine has a bigger 81 mm bore); (5) an early Gol imported from Brazil with the **AE-1600** (CHT) engine has a different code that is not in the sources found.
- **Verify:** the code on the block, the plate and the registration card all agree; if they do not, the engine has been changed and the change should be documented.
- **Don't:** assume any "1.6 Gol" has the AP-1600 engine: the cheap early imports used the Ford-Renault CHT/AE-1600 (VEH-GOL-10).
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 11 (engine number on the left side of the block next to the fuel pump; codes AP 1.6 = UN, AP 1.8 = UD, AP 2.0 = UQ), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 49 (manual p. 92, "Número de motor": UN / UD / BE, "UD 910647" in the picture), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 26 (manual p. 58: engine code UN replaces UC from 1986, bore 81 mm instead of 79.5 mm), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GOL-7: Tell the version: CL, GL (1.6 / 1.8), GTS, GTi, Copa
- **Rule:** identify the version from the plate or catalogue code, the engine and the equipment, not from badges: in Argentina around 1994 the range was, according to secondary sources, the **GL 1.6** (base), **GL 1.8** and **GTi 2.0** (the sporty injection model); the **CL** was the basic trim and the **GTS** (1.8 S, higher-power AP-1800S engine, 185/60 HR 14 tyres) is a Brazilian version and the **Copa** a Brazilian 1994 1.6 series (secondary source); it is not confirmed that either was sold in Argentina. The pickup based on the same car is the **Saveiro**; the estate is the **Parati**.
- **Why:** the version changes the engine, the tyres (155 SR 13, 175/70 SR 13 or 185/60 HR 14), the brakes (ventilated front discs only on the GTi) and the parts list.
- **How:** use this table (Brazilian 1992 manual, Gol column; Argentine range from period magazines):

| Version | Engine | Front tyre size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gol 1.6 / 1.8 CL | AP-1600 (UN) in Argentina or AP-1800 (UD); AE-1600 in Brazilian 1.6 cars | 155 SR 13 | basic trim, carburettor, 5-speed |
| Gol 1.6 / 1.8 GL | AP-1600 (UN) or AP-1800 (UD) | 175/70 SR 13 | carburettor |
| Gol 1.8 S (GTS) | AP-1800S | 185/60 HR 14 | Brazilian, higher power |
| Gol GTi | AP-2000i, injection | 185/60 HR 14 | ventilated front discs |
| Gol Furgão | AE-1600, 4-speed | 155 SR 13 | Brazilian van |

No source was found for a "Dacota" Gol; the BX pickup is the Saveiro.
- **Verify:** engine code (VEH-GOL-6) and tyre size on the tyre sidewall match one row of the table.
- **Don't:** sell or insure the car as a GTi or GTS from a badge alone; do not mix GTi brakes or wheels into a GL without checking.
- **Source:** gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 79 and 82 (manual p. 7-06 and 7-09: models, tyres, weights by version), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: which versions were officially sold in Argentina in 1994 (Parabrisas 1995 and Autocosmos 2024 are secondary and describe the second generation or give only the range).

### VEH-GOL-8: Know whether the car was imported from Brazil or built in Argentina, and when
- **Rule:** the BX Gol reached Argentina in 1991 as an import from Brazil; local production of the CL and GL versions began at the General Pacheco plant in May 1993 with the AP-1600 engine (secondary source); the GTi, with its injection engine, was most likely still brought from Brazil.
- **Why:** an early imported GL has a different engine (the CHT/AE-1600, not the AP) and different parts; a Pacheco-built car has the 8AW VIN and the AP-1600 UN engine.
- **How:** (1) VIN starts with 8AW = built in Argentina, 9BW = built in Brazil (VEH-GOL-2); (2) engine code UN or UD = AP engine, anything else on a 1.6 needs checking; (3) a car with model-year letter M or N (1991-92) can only be an import; P, R or later is most likely, but not surely, a Pacheco car. A car with model-year letter R is most likely local.
- **Verify:** the VIN country code and the engine code give the same story as the registration card.
- **Don't:** assume a "national" GL has the same engine as an imported GL from 1991-92.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 184 and 197 (Weber DMPV carburettor produced until October 1991 and from November 1991) read 2026-11-15, which lists the Gol GL carburettor models before 1996. Not verified in a primary source: secondary only, es.wikipedia "Volkswagen Gol AB9" (read 2026-11-15: "En agosto de 1991 se comenzó a exportar el Gol a Argentina"; "En mayo de 1993 se empezó a fabricar en General Pacheco las versiones CL y GL con el antiguo AP1600 de 82 CV"; early GL "importados con el motor CHT"); Autocosmos 2024 (arrived 1991 with a 1.6 engine, local plant in 1995 for the next generation). The GTi's Brazilian origin is stated in the Parabrisas 1995 test only for the second-generation GTi.

## Engines

### VEH-GOL-9: Engine range around 1993-1996: figures per engine
- **Rule:** the BX Gol could be had with four engine families: **AP-1600** and **AP-1800** (belt-driven overhead camshaft, mechanical tappets, carburettor), **AP-2000i** (GTi, multipoint injection, hydraulic tappets; engine code UQ) and the **AE-1600 / CHT** (Ford-Renault origin, camshaft in the block driven by a chain, pushrods). A 1.6 diesel exists only on the Senda sedan (not on the BX Gol).
- **Why:** each engine has different oil, plug, clearance and timing-belt rules; using the 1.8 figure on the 1.6 gives a wrong plug or clearance.
- **How:** read the column of your engine. For the Gol GL the Argentine workshop manual is the main source; the Senda/Gacel owner's manual (1990) gives slightly different power, so both are shown:

| | AP-1600 (UN) | AP-1800 (UD) | AP-2000i (UQ, GTi) | AE-1600 (CHT) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Displacement | 1596 cm3 | 1781 cm3 | 1984 cm3 | 1555 cm3 |
| Bore x stroke | 81 x 77.4 mm | 81.4 x 86.4 mm (81 x 86.4 in the Senda manual) | 82.5 x 92.8 mm | 77.0 x 83.5 mm |
| Compression ratio | 9:1 | 8.5:1 (Gol GL); 9:1 (Gacel 1.8 manual) | 10:1 | 9:1 (petrol) |
| Net power | Gol GL: 62.5 kW (85 CV) at 6000 rpm; Senda 1990: 60.3 kW (82 CV); Senda 1987: 56 kW (76.2 CV) | Gol GL: 64.1 kW (87.1 CV) at 5200; Gacel 1.8 1990: 70.4 kW (95.8 CV) at 5500 | 82.3 kW (112 hp) at 5600 | 53 kW (72 hp) at 5400 |
| Torque | 126.5 Nm at 3000 (Senda 1990); 129 Nm (1987) | 143.2 Nm at 2700 (Gacel 1990) | 171.6 Nm at 3000 | 123 Nm at 3400 |
| Fuel system | double-barrel Weber carburettor (VEH-GOL-11) | Brosol 2E-CE carburettor (Gol GL 1.8) | LE-II Jetronic multipoint injection | double-barrel carburettor |
| Valve train | belt-driven OHC, mechanical tappets | same | belt-driven OHC, hydraulic tappets | chain-driven cam in block, pushrods |
| Firing order | 1-3-4-2 | 1-3-4-2 | 1-3-4-2 | 1-3-4-2 |

The 1.6 diesel (Senda D only): 76.5 x 86.4 mm, 1588 cm3, compression 23.5:1, 40 kW (54 CV) at 4800 rpm. The Gol diesel (CLD/GLD) only came with the second-generation Gol.
- **Verify:** compare the engine code (VEH-GOL-6) and displacement to the column; a compression test (VEH-GOL-15) should be in range.
- **Don't:** mix figures between the columns; do not take the Brazilian AE-1600 power for the AP-1600; the figures differ between documents, see VEH-GOL-43.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 11-12 (Gol GL 1.6 and 1.8 data: AP 1600 / AP 1800, bore, stroke, kW and CV at rpm, compression ratio, firing order, engine codes UN, UD, UQ), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 50 (manual p. 94-95: petrol and diesel tables, DIN 70020), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 26 (manual p. 58), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 78 and 75 (manual p. 7-05 and 7-02: AE-1600, AP-2000i, valve train descriptions), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: that the Argentine GL used the Senda torque figures (the Gol manual gives no torque).

### VEH-GOL-10: Tell an AP engine from an AE / CHT engine by looking at it
- **Rule:** an **AP** engine has a toothed rubber belt (*correa dentada*) behind a plastic cover on the front of the engine; an **AE-1600 / CHT** engine has no toothed belt cover and its camshaft is driven by a chain inside the block.
- **Why:** the two share the Gol name and 1.6 L size but have almost no common parts; the timing-belt rules of this document apply only to the AP.
- **How:** (1) open the bonnet and look at the front of the engine, in the direction of travel, left side of the car: a belt cover with two or three bolts and a visible toothed belt = AP; (2) look at the top of the engine: an AP has a camshaft cover with the oil filler on it and spark plugs on the exhaust side; (3) the oil filler position and dipstick differ, as the Brazilian manual's pictures of the two engine compartments show; (4) read the engine code (VEH-GOL-6).
- **Verify:** engine code UN/UD and a belt cover present = AP.
- **Don't:** order a timing belt kit for an engine that has no timing belt.
- **Source:** gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 75 (manual p. 7-02: AP engines "eixo comando de válvulas no cabeçote, acionado por correia dentada"; AE-1600 "eixo comando das válvulas no bloco, acionado por corrente"), read 2026-11-15; PDF p. 47-48 (manual p. 5-06 to 5-07, engine compartment pictures of both), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the visual details (cover, bolts, plug side) are general knowledge of these engines.

### VEH-GOL-11: Carburettor: Weber DMPV double-barrel (Gol GL 1.6), Brosol 2E-CE (Gol GL 1.8)
- **Rule:** the **Gol GL 1.6** has a **Weber DMPV double-barrel carburettor** whose second barrel opens automatically (part **029 129 015.26** until October 1991, part **029 129 015.37** from November 1991, so a car sold in 1994 is the later one); the **Gol GL 1.8** has a **Brosol 2E-CE** double-barrel with automatic choke and an electronic idle-speed control. The earlier Gacel/Senda 1.6 used a Weber-type "Webcarbs" carburettor with a manual choke. The choke of the Gol GL 1.6 appears to be manual too (a choke indicator lamp in the workshop manual p. 283 and a choke lever with a vacuum opener on p. 189-190); confirm on the car (VEH-GRP-15).
- **Why:** this is the part most often out of adjustment on an old car that stands unused; most no-start, rough-idle and fuel-smell complaints are carburettor complaints.
- **How:** key figures for the 1991-onward Weber DMPV: idle **900 to 1000 rpm**, CO **0.5 to 1.5 %**, main jets 100 in both barrels, venturi 21 / 22 mm, float height **8 to 9 mm**, float travel 41 to 43 mm, choke flap opening 3.7 to 4.3 mm, accelerator pump delivery **2.25 to 3.75 cm3 per 5 strokes**, positive throttle opening 0.55 to 0.75 mm. The pre-November 1991 carburettor has other jets (main 105 / 102) and CO 1 to 2 %. Brosol 2E-CE (1.8): idle 850 to 950 rpm, CO 0.5 to 1.5 %, float height 26.5 to 28.5 mm. The Gol GL data table (p. 12) prints idle **900 +/- 50 rpm** and CO 2 % for the 1.6 and **850 +/- 50** for the 1.8, while the Argentine Senda manual (1990) prints 750 +/- 50 for its 1.6: set idle with a tachometer and CO with a gas analyser to the Gol GL values. Do not adjust without those two instruments.
- **Verify:** engine idles smoothly at the specified speed warm, with and without the choke; no petrol smell, no black smoke; CO reading within the range.
- **Don't:** open the main jets or "turn all the screws" by ear; do not use the Gacel/Senda "Webcarbs" figures (float 13.5-14.5 mm, idle 700-800) for a Weber DMPV Gol.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 184 (Weber DMPV up to October 1991, specifications), p. 197 (Weber DMPV from November 1991, specifications), p. 198 (Brosol 2E-CE for the 1.8) and p. 12 (idle 900 +/- 50, CO 2 %), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 50 (manual p. 94: idle 750 +/- 50 / 850 +/- 50), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 31-37 (manual p. 69-81, Webcarbs carburettor), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GOL-12: Ignition: firing order, initial advance (9 or 13 degrees: sources conflict), timing marks, inductive pickup and module
- **Rule:** firing order **1-3-4-2**. The ignition of the carburettor Gol GL is **electronic, with a Bosch distributor that has an inductive pickup coil (*bobina captora*) and a reluctor** (not a Hall sender), an external ignition module with six terminals, and a coil. **Initial advance: the sources conflict.** The Gol GL data page prints **9 +/- 0.5 degrees before top dead centre (BTDC)** (workshop manual, p. 12), and the Senda/Gacel owner's manual (1990) and the Brazilian AP-1800 manual also give 9 degrees; the distributor specification page of the carburettor chapter prints **13 degrees** (workshop manual, p. 196, Bosch part 86AU-12100-H). **Use 9 degrees unless the car's own sticker or distributor part number says otherwise.**
- **Why:** timing too advanced causes pinking (detonation) and can hole a piston; too retarded gives weak power, heat and fuel use. A failing module or pickup causes sudden no-spark stops. The Hall sender found in the workshop manual (p. 173) belongs to the injected second-generation engine. The Brazilian 1992 manual describes a Hall sender for its AP-1800 and AE-1600 too, and the Argentine GTi manual for the AP-2000i, so a Brazilian-built car may differ: identify yours by the distributor (a reluctor ring and pickup coil means inductive; a metal shutter blade and a Hall chip means Hall).
- **How:** why 9 is adopted: it is printed on the data page for this exact model, in the 1990 Argentine owner's manual (9 degrees at 1000 rpm, vacuum hose disconnected) and in the Brazilian manual (9 or 6 degrees), against one 13-degree line on a distributor page; the 13 degrees may refer to a different distributor or to advance at a higher speed, which the manual does not say. If you do not have the sticker: (1) warm the engine, connect a timing light to cylinder 1, vacuum hose off, about 1000 rpm, set 9 degrees BTDC; (2) refit the hose; (3) road-test on a hill in a higher gear: no pinking (a metallic rattle under load) means the setting is acceptable; (4) check CO at idle with a gas analyser (VEH-GOL-11); if the car pinks at 9 degrees retard slightly, never advance towards 13 without a mechanic confirming it. Marks: the **crankshaft pulley mark** against the **arrow on the lower timing-belt cover** at top dead centre of cylinder 1; the distributor rotor points at the mark on the distributor body. Mark the distributor position before removing it. Electrical checks (workshop manual p. 196): pickup coil resistance **990 to 1210 ohm**, air gap between rotor (reluctor) and pickup **0.2 to 0.6 mm**, rotation clockwise; coil primary 1.9 to 2.1 ohm, secondary 5500 to 7500 ohm; coil lead 1200 to 2800 ohm; spark plug leads 2800 to 4400 ohm.
- **Verify:** the engine idles at the specified speed, starts hot and cold, does not pink under load; the resistances above are in range.
- **Don't:** set timing by ear; do not set it with the vacuum hose connected; do not copy the 13 degrees of one table into a car that has run for years at 9; do not swap the module or distributor for one of another model without checking the part number.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 12 (initial advance 9 +/- 0.5, electronic ignition), p. 194 (external ignition circuit: battery, switch, distributor, module with terminals 1-6, coil), p. 195-196 (distributor with pickup coil and reluctor; specification: initial advance 13 degrees, resistances), p. 61 and p. 66 (crankshaft pulley mark and arrow on lower cover; rotor to distributor mark), p. 173 (Hall sender, injected engine), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 50 (manual p. 94: 9 degrees BTDC at 1000 rpm vacuum disconnected), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 77 (manual p. 7-04: 9 or 6 degrees for AP-1800), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: which of the 9 and 13 degree values the Argentine GL 1.6 was calibrated to in 1994; the guide recommends 9 degrees on the strength of three sources against one, and a mechanic with a timing light and gas analyser should confirm.

### VEH-GOL-13: Spark plugs: thread, gap and heat range
- **Rule:** the Gol GL carburettor workshop page (p. 196) lists the plugs **Autolatina VW N32 RC, Bosch W8D, NGK BCPR5ES** (gap 0.6 to 0.8 mm), and p. 174 lists **NGK BPR5EX / Bosch WR7DS** for the AP 1600i and 1800i; the older Argentine and Brazilian manuals give the equivalents below. The plug thread is **M14 x 1.25** and the electrode gap **0.6 to 0.8 mm** on the AP-1600 and AP-1800 (Argentine manual; the Brazilian table gives 0.7 to 0.8 mm for the AP engines, AP-2000i included). The original plug is VW part **040 905 999.4** (1.6) or **056 905 999 ZA** (1.8); equivalent heat ranges: **Bosch WR7DS / NGK BPR 6EVX** class for the AP-1800 petrol, **Bosch FR8DS / NGK BKR 6EVX** class for the AE-1600 petrol.
- **Why:** wrong reach or heat range burns the plug or the piston; a fouled plug is the first cause of misfire.
- **How:** (1) engine cold; (2) blow dirt from the plug wells; (3) loosen and remove one plug at a time; (4) check gap with a feeler gauge, adjust only the side electrode; (5) screw in by hand, then tighten **25 to 30 Nm (2.5 to 3 kgm)**; (6) fit leads to the right cylinders (order 1-3-4-2). The 1987 workshop manual lists an NGK plug for the 1.6 (reads like NP6ES in the scan) and another for the 1.8 (reads BP6ES), too blurry to quote.
- **Verify:** engine idles evenly; plug tips are light tan.
- **Don't:** overtighten (the head is aluminium); do not fit a plug of a hotter or colder grade "because the shop had it" without the cross-reference.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 12 (plugs M14 x 1.25, gap 0.6 to 0.8 mm), p. 174 and p. 196 (plug brands and types), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 50 (manual p. 94, "Bujías de encendido": parts, M14 x 1.25, 0.6 to 0.8 mm), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 78 (manual p. 7-05: Bosch / NGK types per engine), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 161 (manual p. 270-271: plugs 25 to 30 Nm), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GOL-14: Valve clearance: adjustable mechanical tappets on the AP-1600 and AP-1800 (not hydraulic)
- **Rule:** the carburettor AP-1600 (engine code UN) and AP-1800 (UD) of the Gol GL use mechanical tappets (*taqués*), so the clearance must be checked and adjusted: **cold (engine at ambient): intake 0.15 to 0.25 mm, exhaust 0.35 to 0.45 mm; hot (about 80 C coolant): intake 0.20 to 0.30 mm, exhaust 0.40 to 0.50 mm**. The injected AP engines of the second-generation Gol (codes with a third letter, for example UNC, UDC, UQD in the table on p. 55 of the workshop manual; hydraulic tappets, check on p. 56-57) and the AP-2000i (GTi) have hydraulic tappets with no adjustment. The Gol GL data page (p. 11-12) lists mechanical tappets (*botadores mecánicos*) with the clearances above, which is why the clearance rule applies to this car. The full adjusting method is in VEH-GOL-45.
- **Why:** too tight burns valves, too loose gives a tapping noise and lost power.
- **How:** (1) Tell which type you have: read the engine code (VEH-GOL-6): UN or UD without a third letter is the carburettor engine with mechanical tappets; a third letter or an injected engine means hydraulic. With the valve cover off, a mechanical engine shows a round shim (thin steel disc) in the top of each bucket tappet between the bucket and the cam; hydraulic tappets have no shim (practical identification, see the Source note). (2) Check cold at the 30,000 km service (VEH-GOL-36), measuring each valve with a feeler gauge with the cam lobe pointing up and away from the tappet; the clearance is corrected by changing the shim that sits in the top of the bucket (VEH-GOL-45). The valve cover gasket is replaced when adjusting. The valve cover nuts tighten to **10 Nm (1 kgm)**.
- **Verify:** all eight clearances inside the range after adjustment; no tapping when warm.
- **Don't:** adjust a hot engine to the cold figures; do not skip the check on a low-mileage car, clearances move with age; do not try to "adjust" a hydraulic tappet engine.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 11 (engine codes: AP 1.6 is UN, AP 1.8 is UD, AP 2.0 is UQ), p. 11-12 (valve clearance with mechanical tappets, "botadores mecánicos": cold 0.15-0.25 intake and 0.35-0.45 exhaust, hot 0.20-0.30 and 0.40-0.50), p. 55 (engine prefix table UDC / UQD / UNC with hydraulic tappet, item 24) and p. 56-57 (hydraulic tappet check), read 2026-11-15; AP engine workshop manual (compilation), PDF p. 38-40, same text; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 50 (manual p. 94, "Luz de válvulas": cold and hot figures), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 28 (manual p. 63, valve clearance table read from the page image: hot 80 C 0.20-0.30 and 0.40-0.50, cold 0.15-0.25 and 0.35-0.45) and PDF p. 158 (manual p. 265, torque), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 75 and 77 (manual p. 7-02 "tucho mecânico" for AP-1800 and "tucho hidráulico" for AP-2000i; 7-04 clearances), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: that the clearance is corrected by shims (none of the Gol or Senda pages read describes the adjustment; taken from the VW Golf Mk1 and Golf 2 workshop texts for the carburettor 1.6/1.8 engine, which print the same four clearance figures; secondary, workshop-manuals.com and vwmanual.ru, read 2026-11-15), and the "shim visible under the cam" test and the reading of "UNC" in the p. 55 table (inference).

### VEH-GOL-15: Compression test: expect about 9 to 14 bar, 1 bar maximum difference between cylinders
- **Rule:** a healthy AP-1600 shows about **9 to 14 bar** at cranking, with a maximum difference of **1 bar** between cylinders (Senda/Gacel workshop manual). The Gol workshop manual gives, for the later injected AP engines, 154 to 184 psi (about 10.6 to 12.7 bar) for the AP 1600i and a maximum difference of 15 psi (about 1 bar).
- **Why:** it is the cheapest way to know the real condition of an engine that has stood for years.
- **How:** (1) run the engine until the cooling fan has switched on a second time; (2) disconnect the coil lead from the distributor cap (or the coil supply) so the engine cannot start (the workshop manual says to disconnect the Hall connector, which applies to the injected engines); (3) remove all four plugs; (4) fit the gauge in one plug hole; (5) crank with the throttle fully open until the needle stops rising and note it; repeat for each cylinder; (6) if a cylinder is low, squirt a spoon of oil into it and repeat: if the pressure rises the rings are worn, if not the valves or head gasket leak.
- **Verify:** all four readings written down, in range and within 1 bar of each other.
- **Don't:** do the test with a flat battery or with the plug leads connected near petrol; do not skip disconnecting the ignition.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 31 (procedure written for the injected engines, compression and difference values for AP 1600i / 1800i / 2000i), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 26 (manual p. 58: "9 a 14 bares", difference 1 bar for the carburettor 1.6), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: that a carburettor Gol 1.6 matches the injected AP 1600i psi figure (different compression and cam).

### VEH-GOL-16: Timing belt (AP engines): no petrol interval printed in the Argentine plan, so replace by age and distance
- **Rule:** the Argentine maintenance chart prints "replace the timing belt" only for the diesel (60,000 km); for the petrol AP it prints no interval. Replace the toothed belt, the tensioner roller and the water pump on a petrol Gol on a **conservative interval of 50,000 km or 5 years, whichever comes first**, because rubber ages even when the car is parked; and always when the car is bought without proof of the last change. Assume a broken belt can damage valves.
- **Why:** the belt drives the camshaft, the intermediate shaft (distributor and oil pump) and the water pump; a snapped belt stops the engine at once, and a decades-old original belt is a time bomb.
- **How:** parts: belt, tensioner roller, water pump. Tightening values from the factory torque table: crankshaft sprocket bolt **200 Nm (20 kgm)**, intermediate shaft sprocket bolt **80 Nm (8 kgm)**, camshaft sprocket bolt **80 Nm (8 kgm)**, tensioner roller nut **45 Nm (4.5 kgm)**, belt cover bolts **10 Nm (1 kgm)**, crankshaft pulley bolt **20 Nm (2 kgm)**, water pump bolts **18 to 22 Nm**. Valve timing must be set with the marks on the crankshaft, intermediate shaft and camshaft sprockets aligned (see below); a belt fitted one tooth off gives poor running or valve contact. Marks: the crankshaft pulley mark lines up with the arrow on the lower timing cover (cylinder 1 at top dead centre) and the distributor rotor points to its mark on the distributor. The Gol workshop manual (p. 61, Fig. II.60) sets belt tension by rotating the tensioner roller clockwise through its eccentric with the covers off (the figure shows a spanner on the roller nut and a tool turning the eccentric) and checking by twisting the belt, then fits the cover, tensions the V-belt and re-checks the ignition timing; it prints no tension number, so the 90-degree twist check of the Golf workshop text applies (VEH-GRP-30). After any belt change turn the engine two full turns by hand and recheck the marks. Have it done by a workshop with the workshop manual.
- **Verify:** receipt with date and odometer; marks aligned after two turns by hand before starting.
- **Don't:** reuse the old tensioner or belt "because it looks fine"; do not tighten the crankshaft bolt with an impact wrench without the flywheel locked.
- **Source:** gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 158-161 (manual p. 265-271: sprocket, tensioner, cover and pulley torques), read 2026-11-15; workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 53 (valve-train drawing with 80, 45, 30, 25 and 10 Nm values) and p. 61 (belt tension adjustment and marks: crankshaft pulley notch at the arrow of the lower cover, distributor rotor at the distributor mark, tension by rotating the tensioner clockwise and twisting the belt; Fig. II.50 torques read from the page image: camshaft sprocket 80 Nm, intermediate shaft sprocket 80 Nm, tensioner nut 45 Nm, covers 10 Nm), read 2026-11-15; AP engine workshop manual (compilation), PDF p. 36 and 43-44 (same pages), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 11 (maintenance chart "Edición 01-94", operation 6 "Correa dentada de distribución: reemplazar" marked D (diesel) at 60,000 km only), read 2026-11-15. Still not verified in a primary source (searched again in all local manuals and on the web, 2026-11-15, nothing found for the petrol AP): the 50,000 km / 5 years interval (general practice, not from VW Argentina; Brazilian belt makers quoted by O Mecanico for the EA111 engine say 60,000 km, a different engine) and whether a broken belt always damages valves (the 1987 Senda manual warns of piston-to-valve contact on the diesel version only).

### VEH-GOL-17: GTi 2.0 (AP-2000i): injection, hydraulic tappets, different data
- **Rule:** the Gol GTi has the **AP-2000i** engine with **LE-II Jetronic multipoint injection**, digital mapped ignition with a Hall sender, hydraulic tappets and an electric fuel pump; its idle ignition advance is **12 degrees** (apms), CO at idle **0.8 to 1.2 %**, plug gap 0.7 to 0.8 mm, and its idle speed is 850-950 rpm (Brazilian manual).

- **Don't:** fit a carburettor-engine part by cross-reference to a GTi; do not run the fuel pump dry.

### VEH-GOL-19: Engine oil: grade, quantity and how to check
- **Rule:** the Argentine factory specification is **SAE 15W-40, API SG (CCMC G2)** or better (the Gol workshop manual says "15W40 API SG ó superior"), usable from -18 C to above 40 C, never thicker than SAE 40; the Brazilian 1992 Gol manual gives SAE 30 or 20W-40 API SE/SF and SAE 40 above 25 C ambient. **Quantity 3.0 L without filter, 3.5 L with filter.** Check the level with the car on the level, engine stopped 3 to 5 minutes, dipstick between MIN and MAX. Never go above MAX. Consumption up to 1.0 L per 1,000 km is accepted as normal in the Brazilian manual.
- **Why:** oil thicker or thinner than specified wears an old engine; today's oils are better than the 1990 ones, so a modern 15W-40 or 10W-40 API SJ or better mineral/semi-synthetic oil meets the specification.
- **How:** (1) warm the engine, switch it off, wait 5 minutes; (2) place a container under the sump, remove the drain plug (**30 Nm / 3 kgm** when refitting, new seal) and the filler cap; (3) when drained, refit the plug; (4) remove the old filter, smear the new filter's seal with oil and tighten it by hand; (5) add 3.5 L, run the engine for a minute, stop, wait and check the level. The Argentine oil filter is VW **049 115 561 2** (also "RTA-115561").
- **Verify:** the level reads at MAX after the check; no leak under the plug or filter.
- **Don't:** use a multigrade of a lower viscosity than the thin range of the car "to save fuel"; do not tighten the drain plug on the old seal.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 14 (lubricants: 15W40 API SG or higher), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 48 (manual p. 91, "Guía de lubricantes": 15W40, API SG, CCMC G2, "en ningún caso ... mayor a SAE 40") and PDF p. 50 (manual p. 94, oil filter VW 049 115 561 2), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 48-49 (manual p. 5-07 and 5-08: level check; SAE 30 / 20W40, API SE or SF; 3.0 / 3.5 L), PDF p. 81 (consumption up to 1.0 L per 1,000 km), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 159 (manual p. 266: drain plug 30 Nm), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the suitability of modern oil grades (my own recommendation, not in the manual).

### VEH-GOL-20: Gearbox oil: SAE 80W-90 API GL-4, 2.0 L, no scheduled change
- **Rule:** the five-speed gearbox (*caja de velocidades* / transaxle, "caja puente") takes **SAE 80 or 80W-90 hypoid oil**, **2.0 L**. The Argentine owner's manual (1990) and the Brazilian manual specify **API GL-4 (MIL-L-2105)**; the Gol workshop manual prints **API GL5**. Use GL-4 as the owner's manual says unless the dealership confirms a GL-5 oil is approved for the box. The factory documents treat the oil as permanent lubrication: check the level, and top up only if there is a leak.
- **Why:** gear whine or hard shifting often start from low oil after an unnoticed leak from a driveshaft seal.
- **How:** check the level at the filler plug (**25 Nm**) at each service when the car is level and cold; if oil comes out of the hole, the level is right. On a car older than 25 years the oil is long past its life: a drain and fill with GL-4 (not GL-5 with high sulphur additive that harms the synchro rings of some boxes) is reasonable.
- **Verify:** oil reaches the filler hole; no wet patch under the gearbox or driveshaft cups.
- **Don't:** use automatic transmission fluid or an unspecified GL-5 in the box; do not over-fill.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 14 ("80 ó 80W90 API GL5"), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 48 (manual p. 91: API-GL4, MIL-L-2105, SAE 80 to 80W90) and PDF p. 51 (manual p. 97: 2 L), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 50 (manual p. 5-09: "A transmissão dispensa troca de óleo"), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 158-159 (manual p. 265-267: filler and drain plugs 25 Nm), read 2026-11-15. ### VEH-GOL-21: Coolant: VW antifreeze additive, 40 to 55 % depending on temperature, and the "never remove the cap hot" rule
- **Rule:** use the VW antifreeze additive **052 922 10** (Argentine manual; Brazilian code N 052 774 B0) mixed with water: **40 % additive / 60 % water for -25 C**, **50/50 for -35 C**, **55/45 for -40 C**; the Brazilian manual refills with 40 % additive (3 parts water to 2 parts additive in its text, i.e. 60 / 40). Capacity 5.6 L (1.6) to 6.6 L (1.8, A/C). Never remove the expansion tank cap with the engine hot.
- **Why:** the system is sealed and pressurised; the additive protects against corrosion of the aluminium head as well as freezing; hot coolant can scald.
- **How:** with the engine cold the level must lie between MIN and MAX of the reservoir; refill only with the premixed solution; the Brazilian manual allows plain water only "in extreme need" and then replace it soon. Replace the whole coolant **every 50,000 km or 2 years, whichever first** (Argentine chart). The electric fan is switched by a thermal switch at the radiator.
- **Verify:** level between the marks cold; no sweet smell or wet hose clamps.
- **Don't:** open the cap hot; do not mix different antifreeze types or top up with tap water regularly; do not add "common additives".
- **Source:** gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 50 (manual p. 94, "Líquido refrigerante": VW 052 922.10, 40/60, 50/50, 55/45) and PDF p. 51 (manual p. 97: capacities), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 50 (manual p. 5-09: level check, N 052 774 B0, "Não remova a tampa do reservatório com o motor quente"), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 11 (chart "Edición 01-94", operation 19: replace every 50,000 km or 2 years), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GOL-22: Brake fluid: DOT 4, 0.34 L, replace on time
- **Rule:** the brake and clutch hydraulic system takes **brake fluid DOT 4** (minimum boiling point printed 250 C in the Argentine manual). System volume **0.34 L**. The factory chart says replace the fluid **every 50,000 km or 3 years**; for a car driven little, replace it **every 2 years** because the fluid absorbs moisture even when parked.
- **Why:** moisture lowers the boiling point; a boiled brake fluid means a pedal that goes to the floor on a long descent, and moisture corrodes the cylinders.
- **How:** check the level in the transparent reservoir, between MAX and MIN (a slow drop is normal as pads wear; a quick drop is a leak, see vw-gol-1994-repairs.md); bleeding or changing the fluid is done by a workshop or by a person with experience, starting from the rear wheel farthest from the master cylinder, with a helper; the brake system has two diagonal circuits.
- **Verify:** fluid is nearly colourless or light amber; pedal firm at the first press.
- **Don't:** mix mineral oil or silicone (DOT 5) fluid; spill brake fluid on paint (it strips it); reuse an opened bottle left for a year.
- **Source:** gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 51 (manual p. 96: "Tipo DOT 4 (Punto de ebullición min. 250 C)"; manual p. 97: 0.340 L), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 11 (chart: operation 24 "Fluido: drenar y reemplazar líquido de freno" every 50,000 km or 3 years), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 51 (manual p. 5-10: reservoir, level marks, "Utilize somente Fluido de Freio Original VW"), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the 2-year interval for a low-use car (my own recommendation based on general practice).

### VEH-GOL-23: Fuel: "nafta especial" and what to use now
- **Rule:** the Argentine factory manual specifies **"nafta especial"** for the AP-1600 and AP-1800 (compression 9:1); the Brazilian manual specifies unleaded Brazilian "gasolina tipo C" (which in Brazil contains 21 to 23 % anhydrous ethanol), or hydrated ethanol for the alcohol versions. Use today the grade the pump sells as **Súper (about 95 RON)** and avoid the cheapest grade on a 9:1 engine.
- **Why:** pinking (detonation) damages pistons; modern petrol contains ethanol, which dries and swells old rubber fuel hoses and gaskets and attracts water in a tank that stands.
- **How:** (1) fill at a busy station; (2) if the car stands more than 2 months, keep the tank near full; (3) inspect the rubber fuel hoses, carburettor float chamber and fuel pump diaphragm for hardening, cracks and leaks at each service; (4) the fuel filter is replaced every 20,000 km on the Argentine chart.
- **Verify:** no petrol smell in the engine bay; no pinking under load.
- **Don't:** run on fuel that has stood in a tank for more than 6 months; do not add old cans of petrol to the tank.
- **Source:** gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 50 (manual p. 94: "Combustible requerido: nafta especial", 9:1), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 78 (manual p. 7-05, fuel note), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: Súper (95 RON) as the modern equivalent of "especial" and the effect of today's ethanol blend on old parts (general practice).

### VEH-GOL-24: Tyres: sizes by version and pressures
- **Rule:** the Gol GL runs **175/70 SR 13** tyres on **5J x 13 steel wheels** (ET 38, wheel bolts M12 x 1.5); the basic version uses **155 SR 13** (4.5J or 5J wheel) and the GTS and GTi use **185/60 HR 14** (6J x 14). The Gol GL data page prints **24 psi front and 26 psi rear at half load; 24 psi front and 32 psi rear at full load**. Other pages disagree, see the table.
- **Why:** pressure and size drive braking, steering feel and fuel use; old tyres can be cracked even with deep tread.
- **How:** cold pressures in psi (1 atm is about 14.7 psi, 1.01 bar):

| Source and tyre | Half load front / rear | Full load front / rear |
|---|---|---|
| Workshop manual, Gol GL data (175/70 R13), p. 13 | 24 / 26 | 24 / 32 |
| Workshop manual, suspension chapter, 1.6 engine (175/70 R13), p. 243 | 26 / 26 | 26 / 30 |
| Workshop manual, suspension chapter, 1.8 engine (175/70 R13), p. 243 | 27 / 28 | 28 / 32 |
| Brazilian manual 1992, Gol on 155 or 175/70 SR 13 | 24 / 26 | 24 / 30 (spare 30) |
| Argentine Senda 1990, 155 SR 13 | 24 / 26 | 24 / 32 |

Why the p. 13 values are adopted: that page is the data sheet of this exact model (Gol GL, 175/70 SR 13) and agrees with the Brazilian manual and the Senda manual at half load, whereas the p. 243 lines are in the suspension chapter, are written for "R13" tyres of the 1.6 and 1.8 injected cars and give different numbers for the two engines, so they most likely describe the second-generation car. ## Tightening torques

### VEH-GOL-25: Wheel bolts: 100 Nm, and the safe way to change a wheel
- **Rule:** wheel bolts (*bulones de rueda*) tighten to **100 Nm (10 kgm)**, front and rear, in a cross pattern; the car is supplied with a full-size spare and bolts specific to the wheel; only fit bolts of the same type.
- **Why:** loose wheels come off; overtightened bolts stretch or strip.
- **How:** (1) park on firm level ground, hand brake on, first gear, hazard triangle behind; (2) loosen the bolts a turn while the wheel is still on the ground; (3) place the jack only at the jacking point shown in the manual (on a soft surface put a board under it); (4) raise the car, remove the top bolt last when taking the wheel off, fit the spare, hand-tighten in a cross pattern; (5) lower the car to the last turn, tighten to 100 Nm with a torque wrench; (6) re-check after the first 50 km. The jack is only for wheel changes: never go under the car on the jack.
- **Verify:** torque wrench click at 100 Nm; spare wheel pressure 2.1 atm.
- **Don't:** work under a car held only by the jack; do not use an impact wrench to final-tighten.
- **Source:** gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 159-160 (manual p. 267 and 268: "Bulón de fijación de la rueda en la maza" and "... en la campana del freno ... 100 Nm (10 kgm)", front and rear), read 2026-11-15; gol-1993-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 23 (manual p. 44: "Cambio de rueda": hand brake, triangle, jack point, loosen bolts first, "bulones específicos"), read 2026-11-15.

## Mechanical and chassis

### VEH-GOL-27: Gearbox ratios and clutch
- **Rule:** the Gol has a **five-speed synchromesh manual gearbox**, front-wheel drive, with a single dry-plate clutch with diaphragm spring, mechanically (cable) actuated. Ratios (Gol GL workshop manual): **1.6: 3.45 / 1.94 / 1.29 / 0.91 / 0.73, final drive 4.11**; **1.8: 3.45 / 1.79 / 1.13 / 0.83 / 0.68, final 4.11**; reverse 3.17 in the Senda and Brazilian manuals (the Gol GL page prints 4.11 in the reverse row, probably a misprint). The 1990 Senda/Gacel table prints 1.94 for second gear of the 1.8 where the other two sources print 1.79. The van version has a 4-speed box.
- **Why:** to identify the correct gearbox and to check for a swapped box (the 1.6 and 1.8 differ in the upper gears).
- **How:** the clutch pedal rests 0 to 10 mm lower than the brake pedal; free play is checked at every service; the clutch disc is about 200 mm diameter. Count the teeth on the box if in doubt: a 1.6 box fitted to a 1.8 engine (or the reverse) changes the upper gears.
- **Verify:** no slip under load, clean gear selection into reverse; the pedal height matches the figure.
- **Don't:** adjust the clutch cable by feel without checking free play; do not fit a 1.8 gearbox to a 1.6 without matching the driveshafts.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 12 (clutch 200 mm; ratios; handbook gear ratios for 1.6 and 1.8), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 51 (manual p. 96: ratios and clutch diameter 200 mm), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 76 and 77 (manual p. 7-03: "embreagem ... monodisco a seco e platô com mola tipo membrana", "alavanca de mudanças no assoalho"; 7-04: pedal 0 to 10 mm), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 4 (manual p. 15: ratios, 190 / 210 mm clutch), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: cable actuation (the Brazilian text says "mechanical", the cable inference is general knowledge).

### VEH-GOL-28: Brakes: front disc, rear drum, servo, diagonal circuits
- **Rule:** service brakes act on all four wheels: **solid floating front discs (ventilated discs on the GTi) and self-adjusting rear drums**, with a vacuum servo (*servofreno*) on the bulkhead and **two diagonal hydraulic circuits**; the handbrake is mechanical on the rear wheels, lever between the front seats.
- **Why:** a diagonal system means one failed circuit still brakes two wheels (one front, one rear) at reduced force; pads and shoes decide stopping distance.
- **How:** at every service check pad thickness, drum linings, hoses and the handbrake travel (the Argentine chart lists "Sistema de frenos: controlar desgaste en pastillas y cintas de freno, pérdidas, estado de los flexibles, regular y lubricar freno de estacionamiento" at every service); asbestos-free pads were fitted to the second generation, so ask for non-asbestos pads for any old car; torque of caliper bolts is in VEH-GOL-26.
- **Verify:** pedal firm, car stops straight, handbrake holds on a slope with 3 to 5 clicks. Figures from the workshop manual: front pad wear limit **9.8 mm**, pads always replaced in pairs on the same axle, caliper guide bolts **40 Nm**, wheel bolts 100 Nm, minimum disc thickness after machining **10.8 mm**.
- **Don't:** work on hydraulic brake parts without experience; do not drive with a soft pedal.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 12 (hydraulic dual diagonal circuit, front disc, rear drum, vacuum servo, mechanical handbrake on the rear wheels, DOT 4) and p. 200-201 (pad wear limit 9.8 mm, guide bolts 40 Nm, minimum disc thickness 10.8 mm), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 76 (manual p. 7-03, "Freios": hydraulic, four wheels, front floating disc, GTi ventilated disc, rear self-adjusting drum, servo, diagonal circuits), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 11 (chart operation 25) and PDF p. 51 (manual p. 96), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the "3 to 5 clicks" figure (general practice).

### VEH-GOL-29: Suspension, steering and alignment values
- **Rule:** front **MacPherson struts** with lower triangular arms, coil springs and an anti-roll bar; rear **semi-independent V-profile twist beam** with tubular trailing arms, progressive coil springs and double-acting telescopic dampers; steering **rack and pinion**, mechanical (no power assistance on the base cars), **3.65 turns lock to lock** (Brazilian Gol) or **3.4** (Senda), turning circle **9.7 m** (Brazilian Gol) or **10.2 m** (Senda).
- **Why:** worn dampers, bushes and ball joints cause wandering, noise and bad braking; alignment affects tyre wear and fuel use.
- **How:** alignment figures (vehicle unladen). **Gol (Argentine workshop manual): front toe -20 minutes to 0, or -2 to 0 mm; front camber -1 degree 10 minutes to -30 minutes (max difference 30 minutes); caster 1 degree 15 minutes to 2 degrees 45 minutes; toe-out on turns (wheels turned 20 degrees) -1 degree 40 minutes to -40 minutes; rear camber -2 to -1 degrees; rear toe 15 to 35 minutes (max difference left-right 10 minutes).** Gacel/Senda, front: camber **-0 degrees 50 minutes to -0 degrees 10 minutes**, caster **1 degree 20 minutes to 2 degrees**, toe **0 to -2.0 mm**; rear: toe **+1.5 to +3.5 mm** per axle, camber **-1 degree to -2 degrees**. Brazilian Gol, front: camber -50 minutes to -10 minutes (left-right difference max 15 minutes), toe -20 minutes to 0 (or -2 mm to 0); rear toe 15 to 35 minutes, camber -2 to -1 degrees. The Argentine chart lists the inspection of dust boots, ball joints and dampers at every service.
- **Verify:** an alignment printout at a tyre shop with the figures above; even tyre wear.
- **Don't:** weld or straighten a bent suspension arm; do not align a car with worn ball joints.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 13 (rack and pinion, 3.65 turns, 9.70 m, front MacPherson, rear suspension) and p. 243 (alignment values), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 76 and 77 (manual p. 7-03 and 7-04: suspension, steering, alignment) and PDF p. 79 (manual p. 7-06: 3.65 turns, 9.70 m), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 49 (manual p. 93: alignment), PDF p. 51 (manual p. 96: 3.4 turns, 10.2 m), read 2026-11-15.

## Electrical

### VEH-GOL-31: Battery, alternator, regulator and jump-starting
- **Rule:** the system is **12 V, negative earth**, factory battery **36 Ah (petrol, without A/C)** and **54 Ah with A/C**, alternator **14 V 45 A (Brazilian AP-1800 petrol)** or **55 A (Argentine Senda and Gacel; 65 A is listed for the Senda D with A/C)**, with an **electronic voltage regulator inside the alternator** (no external regulation), controlled voltage **13.7 to 14.6 V**; starter 12 V 0.80 to 0.95 kW.
- **Why:** an old battery and a lightly used car lead to a flat battery; undercharging or overcharging shows in the regulated voltage.
- **How:** with the engine running at 2500 rpm a multimeter at the battery should read 13.7 to 14.6 V; check electrolyte level (the cap-fill batteries need distilled water to the marks) every service; when removing the battery, disconnect the **negative first**, reconnect it **last**; jump-starting with a cable set long enough to keep the two cars apart (the cars must not touch): follow the factory sequence, cable 3 from the positive (+) of the good battery to the positive (+) of the flat one, cable 4 from the negative (-) to the negative (-); the manual says the terminal of one cable must never touch the other, to remove rings, watch and keys and to switch off all consumers; safer general practice is to make the last connection on bare engine metal of the flat car, away from the battery; a modern battery of about 45 Ah fits if the tray and clamps allow.
- **Verify:** 12.4 to 12.7 V at rest after a day; 13.7 to 14.6 V running.
- **Don't:** disconnect the battery with the engine running; do not smoke or make sparks near the battery (explosive gas); if electrolyte splashes, rinse with plenty of water and see a doctor if it reaches the eyes (the manual says the same).
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 14 (sealed maintenance-free 12 V battery, 36 Ah without A/C, 54 Ah with A/C; alternator 55 A; regulator 13.7 to 14.6 V), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 78 (manual p. 7-05: battery 12 V / 36 Ah or 54 Ah, alternator 14 V 45 / 55 A, starter), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 51 (manual p. 97: regulator, 13.7-14.6 V, 55 / 65 A, battery 36 / 54 / 63 Ah), read 2026-11-15; gol-1993-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 19 and 24 (manual p. 38: battery precautions; manual p. 46: jump-start connections 1-4 and cautions), read 2026-11-15. ### VEH-GOL-33: Relays: the workshop manual's table and the Gacel/Senda box
- **Rule:** the Gol workshop manual lists eleven relay positions on the central panel, marked by colour on the relay covers and on the panel: **I** windscreen washer-wiper, **II** turn signal and hazard flasher, **III** rear washer-wiper, **IV** auxiliary fog light, **V** window timer, **VI** function X (cuts supply of heavy loads during starting), **VII** auxiliary horn, **VIII** window thermal breaker, **IX** central locking, **X** air conditioning, **XI** auxiliary depth light. A base GL 1.6 has only some of these fitted. The Gacel/Senda box (21 fuses, like the Gol GL box in VEH-GOL-32) lists 11 positions with different numbering: 2 and 3 A/C, 5 function X, 6 interior light timer, 7 horn, 9 wipers, 11 flasher.
- **Why:** an unexplained dead horn or flasher is usually a relay, not the unit.
- **How:** the manual says the relays are fitted vertically with the pins down and the positions are marked by colour on the panel; relays for the injection, the fuel pump and full power sit behind the glove box and exist only on the injected cars, not on a carburettor Gol; relays are fitted vertically with the pins down; swap a suspect relay with a known good one of the same type (the flasher and the horn relay are different parts); the community Gol quadrado tutorial describes plug colours of the fuse box but is unofficial.
- **Verify:** the relay clicks when the circuit is switched on; the unit works with the substitute.
- **Don't:** bridge a relay socket with a wire; do not trust an unofficial pin table without checking with a multimeter.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 272 (relay positions I to XI and functions), p. 270 (central distribution panel), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 52 (manual p. 99, Gacel relays 1-11), read 2026-11-15; gol-quadrado-esquema-eletrico-onlycars-unofficial.pdf, PDF p. 2 (unofficial), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GOL-34: Bulbs: sizes and wattage (Gol workshop manual table, matches the Gacel/Senda table)
- **Rule:** use these bulb types (Gol workshop manual, "Luces" table): **headlights: halogen double-filament 55/60 W** (2); **front position 4 W single-filament** (2); **front turn / hazard 21 W single-filament** (2); **rear brake and position: one double-filament 21/5 W bulb per side** (2); **rear turn / hazard 21 W** (2); **reverse 21 W** (2); **engine bay 10 W tubular** (1); **boot 15 W tubular** (1); **number plate 4 W** (2); **interior 10 W tubular** (1); **glove box 5 W tubular** (1); hazard-switch key, digital clock, lighter and each of the two air-conditioning panel bulbs 1.2 W; the warning lights for choke, handbrake, oil pressure, charging, high beam, turn signal and temperature are LEDs.
- **Why:** a wrong wattage overheats the socket and the lens, or is too dim to pass the technical inspection (*VTV*).
- **How:** the headlight bulbs are replaced from the engine compartment: remove the connector, release the bulb fixing (a single clip, two clips or a metal ring depending on the lamp), never touch the glass with fingers; the rear lamps open from inside the boot (press the tabs, remove the bulb holder; the owner's manual numbers the three lamps 1 reverse, 2 brake / position with two filaments, 3 turn); the bulb in the ignition key is a separate small bulb. The manuals give wattage and filament type but not the bulb base code (for a 55/60 W halogen it is usually H4, check the old bulb before buying). Keep a full set of spare bulbs. If your car has the Gacel/Senda-style rear lamps (separate brake 21 W and position 5 W) the filament split is the same.
- **Verify:** all lights tested before a trip, including the number plate lights; the double-filament rear bulb lights both brightnesses (position and brake).
- **Don't:** install higher-wattage headlight bulbs, or add cheap "LED conversion" bulbs that dazzle in a reflector for halogen.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 283 ("Luces": description, quantity, lamp type and power; table read from the page image), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 52 (manual p. 98, "Luces": the earlier Gacel/Senda table, same values for the shared items, brake 21 and position 5 listed separately), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 58 (manual p. 5-17: how to change headlight bulbs), read 2026-11-15; gol-1993-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 17-18 (manual p. 34-35: bulb replacement, rear lamp identification 1 reverse, 2 brake / position, 3 turn), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: that the Gol table (printed in the electrical chapter next to a G2-era audio table) is exactly the bulb list of the 1994 BX Gol (it agrees with the Gacel/Senda table and with the 1993 rear-lamp description, but no pre-1996-only table was found), and the bulb base codes.

### VEH-GOL-35: Headlight aim
- **Rule:** adjust the headlights on level ground with tyres at the right pressure, the car 5 m from a wall, using the horizontal and vertical screws.
- **Why:** a mis-aimed headlight dazzles others or does not light the road; the technical inspection (*VTV*) checks it.
- **How:** (1) mark two crosses on the wall at the height of each lamp centre minus 70 mm for the dipped beam (A = h - 70), 100 mm for the fog lamp (A = h - 100); (2) the car's centre line must point at the middle between the crosses; (3) with the dipped beam on, cover the opposite lamp, turn the vertical screw (A): clockwise raises the beam; horizontal screw (B): clockwise moves the beam to the right for the left lamp; (4) the fog lamp has its own screw (C).
- **Verify:** the cut-off of the dipped beam lies on the line at the wall; the beam does not dazzle in the mirror of a car 20 m ahead.
- **Don't:** adjust with a loaded boot or on a slope.
- **Source:** gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 57 (manual p. 5-16, "Regulagem dos faróis"), read 2026-11-15; gol-1993-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 17 (manual p. 33), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the unit of the "70" and "100" offsets (the pictures give "A = h - 70"; mm assumed).

## Maintenance

### VEH-GOL-36: The manufacturer's plan: a service every 10,000 km or 6 months
- **Rule:** the VW Argentina preventive maintenance plan (chart edition 01-94, for Gacel and Senda, the same engine and platform) prescribes a **service every 10,000 km or 6 months, whichever comes first**, from 10,000 km to 100,000 km. The factory warns that the 6-month limit is about the **loss of the additives in the lubricants**: when the 6 months arrive, the engine oil must be replaced even if the odometer has not reached 10,000 km.
- **Why:** an owner who uses the car little never reaches the odometer interval, but rubber, oil and fluids age with time.
- **How:** the operations of the chart for **petrol (N)** cars:

| Operation | Interval |
|---|---|
| Engine oil: drain and replace (with sump plug seal) | every service (10,000 km / 6 months) |
| Oil filter: replace | at 10,000 and every 20,000 km after (30, 50, 70, 90 thousand) as read from the chart; simplest rule: with every oil change |
| Spark plugs: replace | every 20,000 km |
| Carburettor: clean, lubricate, adjust | every 20,000 km |
| Carburettor: check mixture (CO) and idle | every service |
| Drive belts: check tension and condition | every service |
| Distributor: lubricate, check timing, points and dwell | every 20,000 km |
| Air filter: clean the element | at 10,000 and every 20,000 km after (30, 50, 70, 90) |
| Air filter: replace the element | every 20,000 km (20, 40, 60, 80, 100 thousand) |
| Fuel filter: replace; check for leaks | every 20,000 km |
| Radiator: check cleanliness and fins | every 20,000 km |
| Coolant: check for leaks and top up | every service |
| Coolant: drain and replace | every 50,000 km or 2 years |
| Valves: check and adjust clearance, replace valve cover gasket | every 30,000 km |
| Crankcase ventilation: clean | every 20,000 km |
| Brake fluid: check level; replace | check every service; replace every 50,000 km or 3 years |
| Brakes: pads, linings, hoses, handbrake | every service |
| Suspension and steering: check boots, ball joints, dampers and fixings | every service; steering box play and front alignment every 20,000 km from 10,000 |
| Wheels and tyres: rotate, balance, pressures, bolts | every service |
| Gearbox oil: check level | every service |
| Clutch: check free play | every service |
| Battery and charging system: check terminals, electrolyte, charge | every 30,000 km |
| Instruments, lights and controls; lubrication of hinges, bonnet, boot, cables | every 30,000 km |
| Road test | every service |

The timing belt is marked D (diesel) in the chart; see VEH-GOL-16 for the petrol belt.
- **Verify:** a logbook or the stamped service pages with date and odometer, or receipts.
- **Don't:** wait for the odometer when the car has done 2,000 km in a year; do not skip the oil change because the oil "looks clean".
- **Source:** gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 10 (manual p. 19: "no debe exceder 10.000 km ó 6 meses ... pérdida de la capacidad específica de los aditivos"), PDF p. 11 ("Planilla de mantenimiento preventivo SENDA", 36 operations, "Edición 01-94") and PDF p. 12-13 (manual p. 20: "Cumplimiento del plan de mantenimiento", 10,000 to 100,000 km), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the exact marks per column (the chart was read from a scan with dots that are hard to separate) and that VW Argentina printed the same plan for the Gol; the Gol 1993 GTi manual in hand lacks the plan pages.

### VEH-GOL-37: Severe service: when to shorten the intervals
- **Rule:** the plan marks several operations "severe service": under severe service shorten the interval of the marked operations (engine oil, filters and the like). The factory defines severe service as: **frequent travel in dusty areas; long trips on mud; long stretches in low gears, high revs and high engine temperature; repeated short trips of 15 km or less with long stops and ambient temperature below -10 C; sustained high speed with ambient above 30 C; towing a trailer on long trips; long periods idling**.
- **Why:** these conditions load the oil with fuel, water and dust; short trips are the most common severe condition of an urban car in Argentina.
- **How:** if even one of the conditions applies, change engine oil and oil filter every **5,000 km or 6 months** (the factory's own diesel interval of 5,000 km shows the order of magnitude) and check the air filter more often.
- **Verify:** a dated note of the conditions in the logbook.
- **Don't:** treat "city only" as normal service; do not extend intervals because the car "feels fine".
- **Source:** gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 10 (manual p. 19, "Servicio severo": the list above) and PDF p. 11 (footnote "En condiciones de Servicio Severo ... aumentar la frecuencia"), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the 5,000 km figure for petrol under severe service (the chart prints 5,000 km only for diesel oil filter; my own recommendation).

### VEH-GOL-38: A car used little: what to do by time, not by kilometres
- **Rule:** for a Gol that does under 5,000 km a year, adapt the factory plan as follows: **engine oil and filter every 6 months or 5,000 km**; **brake fluid every 2 years**; **coolant every 2 years**; **tyres inspected for cracks every 6 months and replaced by age**; **timing belt by age (5 years, VEH-GOL-16)**; **start and drive the car at least 20 to 30 minutes every 2 weeks**; check the battery charge monthly or fit a smart maintainer.
- **Why:** an old car that stands loses the battery, gums up the carburettor float chamber, cracks hoses and tyres and corrodes brake cylinders; most failures of a car "in the garage" are age failures.
- **How:** worked example: a car with 3,000 km a year. The plan: each April and October change oil and filter (two a year, 6,000 km equivalent); once a year check valve clearance and adjust if needed; every 2 years replace coolant and brake fluid; every 5 years replace the timing belt, tensioner and water pump; at the October service inspect tyres, hoses and belts. Before a long trip run the pre-trip checks in trip-preparation.md and the basics in car-maintenance-basics.md.
- **Verify:** a service card with the date and odometer of each item.
- **Don't:** drain the carburettor by letting it run dry as "storage"; do not leave the handbrake on during a long stand (the pads and shoes can stick).
- **Source:** gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 10 and 11 (manual p. 19, the 6-month rule; chart edition 01-94, brake fluid 3 years, coolant 2 years), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the 5,000 km / 6 months oil and the fortnightly drive are my own recommendations for a low-use car, based on the factory's 6-month additive rule and general practice.

### VEH-GOL-40: Shared parts with the Senda, Gacel, Voyage, Saveiro and Parati
- **Rule:** the BX family shares many parts: same **wheelbase 2358 mm**, same **front suspension and brakes**, same **AP-1600/AP-1800 engines** (Gacel/Senda) and **five-speed gearbox ratios** (3.45 / 1.94 / 1.29 / 0.91 / 0.73 for the 1.6), same **oil filter, air filter and plugs**, and the same wheel size 13 inch with 155 SR 13 or 175/70 SR 13 tyres.
- **Why:** parts for the Senda and Gacel (four-door sedans of the same family) are often easier to find than parts for the Gol, and fit the same mechanical group.
- **How:** engine, gearbox, brakes, front suspension, alternator, carburettor and filters are interchangeable in principle between Gol 1.6/1.8, Senda, Gacel and Voyage; body panels, glass, lights, seats and wiring harnesses are not (the Gol is a hatchback with a different rear body); check rear springs and dampers by part number because the body and load differ. Always verify with the VIN and the engine code before buying.
- **Verify:** a part bought for another model is compared with the old part in the shop.
- **Don't:** assume an electrical or body part from a Voyage fits a Gol; do not assume a part from a diesel Senda fits a petrol one.
- **Source:** gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 79 and 81 (manual p. 7-06, 7-08: Gol, Saveiro, Voyage, Parati in the same book with same wheelbase and tyres), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 50-51 (manual p. 94-97: ratios, brakes, suspension identical for Gacel 1.8, Senda 1.6 and 1.8), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the interchangeability list is general knowledge of the BX family.

### VEH-GOL-41: Brands sold in Argentina and how to cross-reference
- **Rule:** the usual aftermarket brands sold in Argentina for this car are filters from **Mann, Fram, Tecfil and Wega**, plugs from **NGK and Bosch**, belts from **Gates and Dayco**, brake pads and shoes from **Fras-le and Ferodo**, dampers from **Cofap and Monroe**, oil from **YPF, Shell, Castrol and Total**. - **Don't:** buy brake or timing parts of unknown brand from a market stall; do not trust a cross-reference from a single sticker.

### VEH-GOL-43: When two sources disagree, use this order
- **Rule:** when a figure differs between documents, use, in order: the **plate and stamped data on the car**, the **Argentine VW owner's manual of the Gol (or Senda)**, the **Argentine Gol workshop manual ("Gol GL / before 1996" sections)**, the **Argentine Gacel/Senda workshop manual**, the **Brazilian Gol manual** and finally secondary sources (magazines, data sheets, forums).
- **Why:** the Gol was sold with different engines in Argentina and Brazil; a Brazilian manual describes the AE-1600 with 9.0:1 and different torque, and a Brazilian data sheet for the 1994 Gol CL shows an AP-1600 at 8.5:1 and 80 CV, while the Argentine 1990 owner's manual gives 82 CV, the 1987 workshop manual 76.2 CV and the Gol GL workshop manual 85 CV. Initial ignition advance is 9 +/- 0.5 degrees on p. 12 of the Gol workshop manual but 13 degrees on p. 196 (VEH-GOL-12 adopts 9 and says why). Tyre pressures differ between pages of the Gol workshop manual itself (24/26 psi on p. 13, 26/26 on p. 243), and the gearbox oil is GL-4 in the owner's manuals but GL5 in the workshop manual.
- **How:** in the repair note, write the figure used and its source ("compression 9:1, gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, p. 94"); mark anything read from a blurred scan as "to confirm".
- **Verify:** every critical number (torque, clearance, oil, timing) has a source line and, for safety items such as brakes, a second source or a mechanic's confirmation.
- **Don't:** copy a number from a forum post into a torque wrench; do not use a figure from a manual of another generation.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 11-14 and p. 243 (85 CV; tyre pressures; GL5), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 50 (manual p. 94: 82 CV, 9:1) and gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 26 (manual p. 58: 76.2 CV, 9:1), read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 78 (manual p. 7-05), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the 8.5:1 and 80 CV of the Brazilian data sheet (fichacompleta.com.br, secondary).

## Additions after the second source pass (2026-11-15)

### VEH-GOL-44: Cylinder head bolts on the AP engine: three stages, angle, order and renewal
- **Rule:** the AP head has ten bolts. Fit **new bolts every time the head is removed**, and tighten them in the figure order 1 to 10 in three passes: **40 Nm (4 kgm), then 60 Nm (6 kgm), then a further 180 degrees** (Gol workshop manual). Loosen in the reverse order, 10 to 1.
- **Why:** the third stage is an angle stage, so the bolts stretch and are not meant to be used twice; an uneven or reused set warps the aluminium head or lets the gasket leak (coolant into oil, VEH-GRP-29).
- **How:** (1) Cold engine, battery earth disconnected. (2) Loosen in the order of the removal figure (II.63: left to right on the top row 1, 7, 9, 5, 3 and on the bottom row 4, 6, 10, 8, 2), which is the exact reverse of the tightening numbers; the 1987 Gacel/Senda manual says the same, "loosen 10 to 1, tighten 1 to 10". (3) Check the head: flatness 0.10 mm maximum, height 133 +/- 0.1 mm, do not machine below 132.6 mm. (4) Fit the new gasket on the guide pins with the word OBEN (TOP) up; set cylinder 1 at top dead centre (crankshaft pulley notch at the arrow of the lower timing cover) and the camshaft with the cylinder-1 cam lobes up; seat the head. (5) Tighten with the figure II.71 order (front row 7, 5, 1, 3, 9 and rear row 10, 4, 2, 6, 8 as drawn; the sequence is 1 and 2 in the middle, then outwards): stage 1 all ten to 40 Nm; stage 2 all ten to 60 Nm; stage 3 all ten a further 180 degrees using a 12-point (dodecagonal) socket and an angle gauge, in the same order, with no stopping part way. (6) The 1987 Gacel/Senda manual (read from the page image) prints stage 1 as 4 kgm, stage 2 as 6.1 +/- 0.6 kgm and stage 3 as 200 +/- 20 degrees, and says these new-type bolts need no re-tightening after a running period; 180 degrees lies inside that window. (7) The table of 40 / 60 Nm followed by two extra 90-degree steps (hot, warm) in the Gol manual belongs to the diesel engine: do not apply it to the petrol AP.
- **Verify:** every bolt of the new set went through all three stages, in order, and the sequence is written in the job sheet; after warming up and cooling, no coolant or oil weep at the gasket edge.
- **Don't:** reuse the old bolts "because they are not stretched"; do not lubricate the threads beyond what the bolt maker says; do not tighten in a circle or by rows; do not use the diesel figures.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 55 (head bolt item 19: 1st 40 Nm, 2nd 60 Nm, 3rd 180 degrees, page image read), p. 63 (removal list and Fig. II.63), p. 64 (flatness, height), p. 66 (OBEN up), p. 67 (Fig. II.71), p. 323 (diesel bolt table, 90 + 90), read 2026-11-15; AP engine workshop manual (compilation), PDF p. 38, 46-50 (same pages), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 29 (manual p. 64: order 10 to 1 / 1 to 10, 4 kgm, 6.1 +/- 0.6 kgm, 200 +/- 20 degrees, 12-point key, do not reuse the bolts) and PDF p. 158 (manual p. 265: "deben ser reemplazados cada vez que sean removidos"), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: which angle (180 or 200) the factory used on a 1994 carburettor engine, since the two Argentine manuals differ and the 1987 scan is low resolution; the clean-and-dry thread condition is general practice.

### VEH-GOL-45: Adjust the valve clearance of the AP-1600 with shims: method, warm or cold
- **Rule:** on the mechanical-tappet AP (engine code UN, VEH-GOL-14) measure each valve with a feeler gauge when the cam lobe points up, compare with **cold 0.15-0.25 (intake) / 0.35-0.45 (exhaust) mm or warm 0.20-0.30 / 0.40-0.50 mm**, and correct a wrong valve by changing the shim in the top of its bucket tappet; adjust to the middle of the range.
- **Why:** the clearance shrinks as valves and seats wear, so an engine that is never checked ends with burnt valves (too tight) or noise and weak power (too loose).
- **How:** (1) Cold engine, air filter, upper timing cover and valve cover off. (2) Do the cylinders in the firing order 1, 3, 4, 2. Turn the crankshaft by its pulley bolt (never by the camshaft sprocket bolt, which strains the belt) until the two cam lobes of the cylinder point up and are not pressing on the tappets. (3) Slide the feeler gauge between cam and shim: it should drag slightly. Note intake and exhaust. (4) A valve out of range: new shim thickness = old shim thickness + measured clearance - target clearance (aim for the middle: cold 0.20 intake, 0.40 exhaust; warm 0.25 and 0.45). The VW shims of this family run from 3.00 to 4.25 mm in 0.05 mm steps, thickness engraved on the underside. (5) Turn the crankshaft a quarter turn back so no valve is open near a piston, turn the bucket so its notch is accessible, press the bucket down with the special tool (VW 2078 / 10-208 or an equivalent), take out the old shim with pliers, fit the new one with the engraving DOWN towards the bucket. (6) Turn two full turns and re-measure all eight. (7) Warm or cold: the Gacel/Senda 1987 manual defines warm as 80 C coolant; the Golf texts say a clearance may be set warm when the coolant is above 35 C and the head is just hand-warm, and cold after head work with a recheck after about 1,000 km. To avoid mixing, measure cold with the cold column, or fully warm with the warm column, never one against the other. (8) Valve cover nuts 10 Nm (Gacel/Senda torque table) with a new gasket (VEH-GRP-32). (9) Hydraulic tappet engines (a third letter in the code, injected cars, GTi) have no shims; the check there is the push test of the Gol manual p. 56-57 (VEH-GRP-33).
- **Verify:** all eight valves inside the range on the second measurement; no tapping after warm-up; recheck after the first 1,000 km if the head or the camshaft was worked on.
- **Don't:** use cold figures on a warm engine or the other way round; do not turn the engine on the camshaft bolt; do not use an old shim without measuring its thickness; do not force the special tool and drop a shim into the engine.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 11-12 (clearances for the mechanical tappets, cold and hot), p. 56-57 (hydraulic check), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1987-manual-taller-es.pdf, PDF p. 28 (manual p. 63: clearances, "motor caliente (80 C)" and "motor frío", page image read), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the shim method, the 3.00-4.25 mm shim range, the order and tools: none of the Gol, Senda or AP compilation pages read describe the adjustment of the mechanical AP; steps taken from the VW Golf Mk1 workshop text for the carburettor 1.5/1.6/1.8 engine (workshop-manuals.com, valve clearance) and the Golf 2 1.6/1.8 text (vwmanual.ru, "Valve clearance adjustment"), both secondary, same engine family and identical clearance figures, read 2026-11-15; confirm shim thickness against the shim catalogue for the AP before buying.

### VEH-GOL-46: Wiring diagram and parts catalogue for the first-generation Gol: what was found and where they are sold
- **Rule:** no free, official wiring diagram or parts catalogue of the first-generation (BX) Gol with the AP engine could be downloaded in the search of 2026-11-15; use the fuse and relay tables of the workshop manual and the unofficial community harness guide for the car's wiring, and get the official diagram and the parts lists through a Volkswagen dealership, from a paid workshop-manual or diagram compilation, or from a second-hand paper manual.
- **Why:** a repair on the harness without the right diagram (a swapped feed, a wrong fuse) can start a fire; and a wrong part number wastes money.
- **How:** (1) Local material: the Gol workshop manual (p. 270-277) gives the central panel, fuse and relay positions, connector colours and harness layout but not a full circuit diagram for the carburettor GL; the Gacel/Senda 1987 workshop manual has a circuits chapter (same platform, AP engine, check it against the car); the file of the community tutorial (Clube do Gol Quadrado, Portuguese, plug colours of the fuse box and how to wire battery, starter, alternator, coil, electronic ignition, fan, signals and wipers, 1987-1996) is unofficial and must be checked with a multimeter. (2) Web search results (Portuguese and Spanish, 2026-11-15): free diagrams exist for later Gol generations (Brazilian trade magazine O Mecanico, G4 and G5), none for the BX AP; paid compilations of "esquema elétrico geral do Gol quadrado AP e CHT 1985-1994" are sold by Brazilian sellers on marketplaces (a reader must judge the seller; Brazilian wiring can differ from the Argentine car). (3) Official route: ask a Volkswagen dealership (*posventa*) or the brand's customer line whether they hold archived literature for the Gol or Senda by VIN; they work from the VIN and may supply part numbers. (4) Parts: an ETKA-style web catalogue of Volkswagen parts for Brazil exists on third-party sites, but it is not confirmed to cover the BX Gol (do not rely on it without the VIN or the old part number); the Weber DMPV has an exploded view (images only) in a Spanish-language carburettor blog (secondary). (5) A generic Haynes manual on Weber carburettors is on sale new (it does not cover the DMPV by name); the Ferrer series book is a second-hand item (VEH-GOL-42).
- **Verify:** any diagram you use names Gol (not Gol G2, G3 or Golf) and covers the AP engine and the year; its fuse table matches the count and amperage of the box in the car (VEH-GOL-32).
- **Don't:** trust a diagram labelled only "Gol" without year and engine; do not cut or splice a harness from an unofficial colour table; do not use pirate download sites.
- **Source:** workshop manual (Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol), p. 270-277, read 2026-11-15; gol-quadrado-esquema-eletrico-onlycars-unofficial.pdf (unofficial tutorial, PDF p. 1-2), read 2026-11-15; web searches by the author on 2026-11-15 (WebSearch; O Mecanico, 7zap, carburacion.blogspot.com "Despiece carburador Weber 32 DMPV, Volkswagen Gol, Saveiro motor 1600", 2009, secondary). Not verified in a primary source: that no official Argentine diagram is currently on sale (only that none was found free); nothing was downloaded.
