# Road signs, markings and traffic lights in Argentina

This file is for drivers who want to know what a sign, a line or a light means and what they must do, and for people who think a ticket was
unfair because the signage was missing or wrong. The legal source is **Anexo L** of Decreto 779/95, replaced as a whole by Decreto 196/2025
(art 24). Codes in this file (R.27, P.1, H.4, T.5, I.15, F.3) are the codes of that annex: R = regulatory (*reglamentarias*), P = warning
(*preventivas*), I = informative, H = horizontal markings, T = temporary roadworks, F = special rail signs. The annex gives shapes, colours and
meanings in words; the drawings are on the official pages and in the manuals listed in the sources (the pictures in the old ISEV edition are
representative only). Traffic-law consequences (speed limits, priorities, fines) are in [traffic-law.md](traffic-law.md) and
[fines-and-points.md](fines-and-points.md).

**Safety and legal caveat.** Obey an officer, then the signs, then the written rule (VEH-SEN-3). If a sign is missing or wrong, you must still
drive with care and to the general rules: the dispute happens afterwards (VEH-SEN-42 to VEH-SEN-45), never by ignoring the danger. This is
information, not legal advice.

## How the system works

### VEH-SEN-1: Anexo L is the only legal signage code, and it was replaced in 2025
- **Rule:** the Sistema de Señalización Vial Uniforme (Anexo L of Decreto 779/95, regulating art 22 of Ley 24.449) describes the meaning, shape, colours and placement of all traffic-control devices; every datum sent to road users must go through this system and "no symbols or signs not contemplated in it can be used" (Anexo L point 2). Decreto 196/2025 art 24 (Boletín Oficial 2025-03-18) replaced the whole annex; the main novelty is a group of new level-crossing signs (VEH-SEN-36).
- **Why:** the old drawings circulating in manuals and on websites differ in some details; the legal text is the 2025 one. Existing signs that differ from the approved ones are replaced only when they need renewal by wear or end of life (Anexo L point 1).
- **How:** consult the annex on argentina.gob.ar > Normativa > Decreto 196/2025 (text) or the Boletín Oficial notice 322652 of 2025-03-18 (annex 3). For pictures use the national or CABA manuals; they illustrate but do not replace the annex.
- **Verify:** an older sign still standing is not an infringement by the road authority until it is renewed; a sign with a symbol not in the annex can be challenged (VEH-SEN-42).
- **Don't:** treat a drawing found on a blog as the law; and don't assume a "weird" sign is invalid without checking the annex.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 art 24 and Anexo L points 1-2, https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/decreto-196-2025-410682/texto and https://www.boletinoficial.gob.ar/detalleAviso/primera/322652/20250318 (read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-SEN-2: Families of signs: shape and colour tell you the kind of message
- **Rule:** the colours allowed on sign plates are white, black, yellow, red, blue, green and orange (Anexo L 7 a.3). The families are:

| Family | Shape and colour (Anexo L) | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Regulatory, prohibition (R.1-R.10) | round, white with red border, black symbol, red diagonal bar | forbids; mandatory |
| Regulatory, restriction (R.11-R.26) | round, white with red border and black symbol, or white symbol on blue | limits (speed, weight, size) and exclusive uses; mandatory |
| Priority (R.27-R.30) | STOP: red octagon; CEDA EL PASO: white triangle with red border | changes who passes first; mandatory |
| Warning (P) | yellow square on its corner, black symbol; max-danger signs: white triangle with red border | warns, no order, but you must adapt |
| Informative (I) | green or blue rectangles, white rectangles for route shields | guides, no legal order unless it contains an R or P sign |
| Roadwork (T) | orange diamonds and rectangles with black symbols | warns and orders in the work zone |

- **Why:** you can tell what a sign demands before you read it: red border = order, yellow = warning, green or blue = information, orange = roadworks.
- **How:** learn the border colours first; then the symbol. Regulatory signs (*señal reglamentaria*) are 0.6 to 0.9 m in diameter (the larger on fast roads) (Anexo L 8 a); warning squares 0.7-0.9 m; STOP at least 0.75 m between parallel sides; CEDA EL PASO at least 0.9 m a side.
- **Verify:** a regulatory sign may be a rectangle with a white lower panel giving a legend or hours (Anexo L 7 d).
- **Don't:** read an informative sign (*señal informativa*) as a prohibition, or a warning as an order to stop.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L points 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13, 19 (read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-SEN-3: Order of priority and what makes a sign binding
- **Rule:** in the street you follow, in this order, the indications of the authority, the traffic signs and the legal rules (Ley 24.449 art 36). Orders given through the system "are mandatory for the road user they are addressed to, and failing to comply is an infringement as long as they conform to this annex"; and "it is not an infringement to fail to comply with a provision that had to be stated through the Uniform Signage System and is not" (Anexo L point 3). The meaning of the system is presumed known by all users, but this presumption does not cover accessory local provisions or exceptions to a general rule, which must be announced through the system (Anexo L point 3). Local exceptions must be clearly stated at the place where they apply to be valid (Ley 24.449 art 2).
- **Why:** this is the legal basis of every signage defence: no valid sign (or rule posted in the wrong form) means no infringement for the thing that needed the sign.
- **How:** (1) obey an officer even against the sign; (2) obey a conforming sign; (3) when there is no sign, the general rule of the law applies (default speeds, right-hand priority (*prioridad de paso*), etc.).
- **Verify:** whether the prohibition is a general rule of the law (no sign needed, e.g. no parking on a crossing) or a local exception (needs a sign, e.g. no parking on one side of this street).
- **Don't:** assume "no sign, no obligation": the law itself already imposes many rules without signs (VEH-SEN-45).
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 arts 2 and 36 (biblioteca.afip.gob.ar/dcp/LEY_C_024449_1994_12_23, read 2026-11-15); Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L point 3.

### VEH-SEN-4: Where signs stand and how far a sign reaches
- **Rule:** vertical signs normally stand on the right side of the road (sometimes the left), tilted 8-15 degrees from the perpendicular to the axis, or are overhead on gantries (Anexo L 7 c). A regulatory sign (*señal reglamentaria*) must be far enough from the object it refers to so that a driver can stop before it; in cities it is usually at the height of the object and not more than 15 m from it (Anexo L 8 c). A no-parking sign (R.8) covers the side of the block where it stands, for the whole length of the block, or the stretch between two signs if the prohibition is short (Anexo L R.8 b). A prohibition ends where its end sign R.31/R.32 stands (VEH-SEN-17); a warning for an extended hazard ends at a P.34 sign (VEH-SEN-17).
- **Why:** to decide whether a sign applied to you or not.
- **How:** look at the sign position relative to your lane and the junction; supplementary plates below give hours, distances and conditions (Anexo L 7 d).
- **Verify:** reflective material is mandatory on paved roads and "high reflectivity" at curves, bridges, roundabouts (*rotonda*), crossings and motorways (*autopista*) (Anexo L 7 a).
- **Don't:** assume a sign applies on the whole street: check the arrows and plates.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L points 7 and 8 and R.8 (read 2026-11-15). Not verified in a primary source: the exact stretch covered by signs other than R.8.

## Regulatory signs (R)

### VEH-SEN-5: Prohibition signs at a glance (R.1 to R.10)
- **Rule:** prohibition signs (*señales de prohibición*) are white discs with a red border and a red diagonal bar from upper left to lower right, with a black symbol. Anexo L lists:

| Code | Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| R.1 | black arrow pointing up | No entry/advance on this road, usually with a time or day period |
| R.2 | red disc with a white horizontal bar | Wrong way (*contramano*): the road has the opposite direction |
| R.3 | silhouette of a car, motorcycle, bicycle, lorry, trailer, pedestrian, animal cart, animals, handcart or tractor (crossed) | that type of traffic may not use the road |
| R.4 | right-angled arrow | No turn to that side (the bar runs lower-left to upper-right only for left) |
| R.5 | horseshoe arrow | No U-turn |
| R.6 | two cars seen from behind | No overtaking |
| R.7 | horn | No noise (horn or other sound) |
| R.8 | letter E | No parking |
| R.9 | letter E crossed by a second bar (an X) | No parking or stopping |
| R.10 | lane line with a double arrow | No lane changes |

- **Why:** the symbol is the content, the border says it is an order.
- **How:** read the supplementary plate for hours; at an R.1 the time or date range is part of the order.
- **Verify:** most of these signs must be accompanied by the matching marking (for R.10 a continuous line).
- **Don't:** treat the red bar as decoration: the crossed-out symbol is what is prohibited.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L points 9 (R.1-R.10) (read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-SEN-6: Wrong way (R.2) and no entry (R.1)
- **Rule:** R.2 (red disc with a white horizontal bar, *contramano*) means the road ahead runs the other way, so you cannot enter. R.1 (black arrow in a prohibition disc) prohibits advancing on that road, mostly at certain hours or days (Anexo L R.1, R.2). When a street stops being two-way, the previous junction should also carry a P.23 sign (Anexo L R.2 d; R.26 marks the start of two-way). Driving the wrong way is a grave fault (*falta grave*) (Ley 24.449 art 77 w) and in CABA it leads to licence (*licencia de conducir*) retention (Ley 2148 art 5.6.1 b.14).
- **Why:** wrong-way entries are among the most dangerous manoeuvres.
- **How:** if you enter by mistake, stop on the right if possible, put the hazards on and reverse out slowly only if no traffic is coming, or wait for help; never continue.
- **Verify:** look for the red disc at the entry of one-way streets and ramps.
- **Don't:** enter an exit ramp of a motorway (*autopista*) or reverse into the lane.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.1, R.2, R.26 (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 art 77 w; Ley 2148 CABA art 5.6.1 (juristeca h-tit-5, read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-SEN-7: No turns, no U-turns, no overtaking, no lane change: signs and lines go together
- **Rule:** R.4 forbids turning to the side the elbow arrow points; R.5 forbids U-turns on the same road; R.6 forbids overtaking (*adelantamiento*) (its end is marked with R.31, see VEH-SEN-17); R.10 forbids changing lane in the marked zone and must be accompanied by a continuous line (Anexo L R.4, R.5, R.6, R.10). If a prohibition applies only at certain hours, a supplementary plate must say so (Anexo L R.4 d). On two-way roads with traffic lights (*semáforo*), left turns are forbidden unless a sign allows them (Ley 24.449 art 44 f; Anexo L 32 f).
- **Why:** most tickets for these signs come from camera or officer observation at junctions.
- **How:** if you miss the turn, continue and take the next legal one; look for I.21 (permitted turn) signs, which allow a turn where it is normally forbidden.
- **Verify:** the plate with days and hours; the markings on the pavement.
- **Don't:** make a U-turn "because the road is empty": R.5 or a continuous line is enough for the infraction.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.4-R.6, R.10, point 32 f (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 art 44 f.

### VEH-SEN-8: STOP (R.27): full stop, even if nobody is coming
- **Rule:** the STOP sign (*PARE*) is a red regular octagon with a white border and the word PARE in white, at least 0.75 m between parallel sides. It means you must stop completely before the junction, without invading the pedestrian crossing (*senda peatonal*), and move off only when no vehicle or pedestrian is using the cross road. "The stop is mandatory even if nobody is on the cross road" (Anexo L R.27). It may be reinforced by the pavement word PARE (H.10) and the stop line (*línea de detención*) (H.4), which must be crossed only after stopping (Anexo L H.4, H.10).
- **Why:** a rolling stop is an infringement even with no traffic: cameras and officers record whether the wheels stopped.
- **How:** brake so that the car is still at the line; look left, right, left; check pedestrians; move off. The stop line is a white continuous line 0.5 m wide; if there is no line, stop before the crossing, or if none, before the cross road.
- **Verify:** the octagon and the word on the pavement are both used; a plate with "4" or "ALL WAY" is a local addition (not in Anexo L).
- **Don't:** stop beyond the line or on the crossing, or "creep" forward.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.27, H.4, H.10 (read 2026-11-15). Note: Anexo L H.10 says the pavement word is equivalent to "R.29": in the full text R.27 is the STOP sign (R.29 is the narrow-road sign, VEH-SEN-10).

### VEH-SEN-9: CEDA EL PASO (R.28): you lose priority, you need not stop if it is safe
- **Rule:** the give way sign (*CEDA EL PASO*) is an equilateral triangle, point down, white with a red border and the words "CEDA EL PASO" in black, at least 0.9 m per side. You lose the priority you would have by the general rule; it is not necessary to stop provided you secure the passage of those crossing on the other road (Anexo L R.28). It may be accompanied by a pavement triangle with the point against the traffic direction (H.12).
- **Why:** it changes the right-hand priority (*prioridad de paso*) of Ley 24.449 art 41 a (specific signs prevail over the general rule).
- **How:** slow down enough to stop if needed; look; go when the road is clear; if the cross road has traffic, stop and wait.
- **Verify:** whether the cross road has a STOP too; the advance sign P.33 may announce one.
- **Don't:** treat CEDA EL PASO as a STOP, or as an invitation to speed through without looking.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.28, H.12 (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 art 41 a.

### VEH-SEN-10: Narrow road: "no priority" sign (R.29) and other priority rules
- **Rule:** R.29 is a white disc with a red border showing two vertical arrows in opposite directions, the ascending one red and the descending one black. The vehicle that finds the sign in front has no priority; if both vehicles have entered the narrow part where only one can pass, the one that had no priority must reverse, except what art 41 g says about steep narrow slopes (Anexo L R.29; Ley 24.449 art 41 last paragraph: on steep narrow slopes the one going down reverses, unless he pulls a trailer and the one going up does not). On roads with no sign, the right-hand priority (*prioridad de paso*) applies (traffic-law.md VEH-LEY-33, and the PBA change in VEH-LEY-34).
- **Why:** it organises alternating traffic on narrow bridges and roadworks.
- **How:** if you see the red arrow facing you, stop before the narrow stretch and wait for the oncoming car.
- **Verify:** the sign is placed at the entry of the narrow stretch; look at which side shows the red arrow.
- **Don't:** enter the narrow stretch hoping the other car will give way (*ceda el paso*).
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.29; Ley 24.449 art 41 (read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-SEN-11: Speed limit signs (R.15, R.16) and when no sign is needed
- **Rule:** R.15 is a white disc with a red border and the maximum speed in km/h in black (it may carry the legend "VELOCIDAD MÁXIMA"); R.16 is a blue disc with a red border and a white number: the minimum speed, below which you cannot drive on that road (Anexo L R.15, R.16). The sign sets the limit for the signed stretch (Ley 24.449 art 52 b); without a sign the legal limits of art 51 apply (traffic-law.md VEH-LEY-29). The annex gives no fixed reach for a speed sign: treat the limit as in force until a new speed sign or the end sign R.31 (VEH-SEN-17). Where speed cameras (*cinemómetro, fotomulta*) are used, PBA requires a vertical sign 500 m before when the limit is lower than the legal maximum (VEH-SEN-39).
- **Why:** exceeding the limit by more than the tolerance is a grave fault (*falta grave*) (Ley 24.449 art 77 n).
- **How:** know the default speeds for the road type (40 street, 60 avenue, 110 rural for cars) so that you notice when a sign gives a different number.
- **Verify:** the speed sign applies from its location onward.
- **Don't:** assume a limit sign "expires" at the next junction: the text read does not say so.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.15, R.16, R.31; Ley 24.449 arts 51, 52, 77 n (read 2026-11-15). Not verified in a primary source: whether a speed sign loses effect at junctions.

### VEH-SEN-12: No parking (R.8), no parking or stopping (R.9), reserved parking (R.17)
- **Rule:** R.8 shows a black capital E on the prohibition disc: no parking; the hours it applies are written under the E and a short stretch is marked "entre discos" (between two signs) or with arrows; stopping to load or unload goods or let passengers on and off is still allowed, with the hours when loading is not permitted shown on a plate (Anexo L R.8). R.9 adds a second bar forming an X: no parking and no stopping; not even to let passengers on or off or load goods; "the only stop possible is the one required by traffic" (Anexo L R.9). R.17 is a blue disc with a red border and a white E: parking only for the vehicles listed on the plate (Anexo L R.17). A permitted parking sign (I.20) shows a white E with the angle (45 or 90 degrees), hours and payment mode (Anexo L I.20).
- **Why:** the difference between "parking" and "stopping" decides whether you may wait with the engine on and the driver inside.
- **How:** if the sign says E with a bar only: you may stop briefly with the driver in the car (loading, passengers); with an X: keep moving. Both signs are repeated every block or at each end of the stretch.
- **Verify:** the plate with hours and days (the prohibition applies only then); the painted kerb (*cordón*) (VEH-SEN-29).
- **Don't:** leave the car "just to buy something" where the sign has an X; a car without its driver is parked, not stopped.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.8, R.9, R.17, I.20 (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 art 49.

### VEH-SEN-13: Exclusive lanes (R.18) and the markings that go with them
- **Rule:** R.18 is a blue disc with a red border and white vehicle figures: the lane is exclusive for those vehicles (public transport, motorcycles, bicycles, pedestrians, riders). Exclusive lanes are bounded by a double continuous white line (H.2 a.1) and must also have the vertical or luminous sign, with the vehicle figure and a diamond painted on the pavement (Anexo L R.18, H.2, H.12). Lanes only "exist and have effect when they are marked" (Anexo L H.2 d). The lane use is set by art 45 of Ley 24.449.
- **Why:** driving in a bus lane means disturbing public transport and, in CABA, is a ground for licence (*licencia de conducir*) retention when it is the Metrobus lane (VEH-SEN-38).
- **How:** cross a bus lane only where permitted (to turn right at the junction, where the markings show it) and only for the shortest distance.
- **Verify:** the figure on the sign (bus, motorcycle, bicycle) and the hours on the plate.
- **Don't:** stop or park in an exclusive lane even for a moment.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.18, H.2, H.12 (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 art 45.

### VEH-SEN-14: Weight, height, width and length limits (R.11 to R.14) and the matching warnings
- **Rule:** R.11 limits weight: a number with "tns" (total weight) or the same with an axle symbol (per axle); R.12 and R.13 limit height and width (arrows meeting from above and below, or from the sides, the number in metres); R.14 limits vehicle length, with the vehicle silhouette, a double arrow and the length in metres (Anexo L R.11-R.14). The legal maximum for height is 4.1 m and for width 2.6 m, so a restrictive sign shows lower numbers; a higher limit is shown with the warning signs P.18/P.19 instead (Anexo L R.12/13 d; P.18/19).
- **Why:** low bridges and light bridges are a common cause of damage to vans and campers.
- **How:** read your vehicle card (*cédula de identificación del automotor*) for gross weight and the actual height with roof racks or boxes; plan the route around R.12.
- **Verify:** a van with a roof box can exceed a 2.5 m limit.
- **Don't:** trust satnav routes for vans; check the limit signs.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.11-R.14, P.18/P.19 (read 2026-11-15). The text of R.12/13 d gives the figures as "4,1 mm and 2,6 m" (typo in the annex for 4.1 m and 2.6 m).

### VEH-SEN-15: Mandatory direction signs (R.20 to R.24) and one-way streets
- **Rule:** R.20 (right-angle arrow in a disc): you must turn in the arrow's direction; R.21/R.22 (arrow on a white disc with red border, or white arrow on a black, green or blue panel): you must travel in the arrow's direction (R.22 is mounted on obstacles and islands: only one direction allowed); R.23: heavy traffic keeps right; R.24: pedestrians walk on the left (Anexo L R.20-R.24). Urban street-name panels may show the direction of the one-way flow (Anexo L I.1.4).
- **Why:** a one-way arrow on a corner is the only information that tells you the street's direction.
- **How:** read the arrow on the street-name panel at the corner; R.22 on an island means keep to that side.
- **Verify:** the arrow on each corner; wrong-way is VEH-SEN-6.
- **Don't:** assume the street is one-way in the same direction as the previous block.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.20-R.24, I.1.4 (read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-SEN-16: Control post (R.25), horn ban (R.7), snow chains (R.19)
- **Rule:** R.25 is a white disc with a red border and a black horizontal rectangle: "when you see this sign the driver must stop" (control post; the sign can carry a legend naming the kind of control) (Anexo L R.25). R.7 forbids the horn (*bocina*) and any other noise in the zone. R.19 makes snow chains compulsory in the zone and in the snow season (Anexo L R.19; the regulation tells drivers to fit chains when snow, frost or ice change the normal conditions: reg art 48 u in the text of Decreto 32/2018).
- **Why:** at a signed control post you stop whatever the time; passing it is an infringement and may lead to a stop by the officer.
- **How:** if you see R.25, stop where the officer indicates; at R.19 fit the chains before the steep section.
- **Verify:** the legend under the sign.
- **Don't:** fit chains on bare asphalt for long distances.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.7, R.19, R.25 (read 2026-11-15); reg art 48 u in Decreto 32/2018 (read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-SEN-17: End of the prohibition (R.31/R.32) and end of the warning (P.34)
- **Rule:** R.31 and R.32 are white discs crossed by a grey band (black and white stripes for prohibitions, R.31; a red band for impositions, R.32): from the sign on the prohibition, imposition or order represented by the crossed-out figure ends (Anexo L point 12). P.34 is the basic warning plate in white with a black symbol crossed by a grey band: end of the zone with the risk warned (Anexo L P.34).
- **Why:** you can resume overtaking (*adelantamiento*) or normal speed only after the end sign (or after the stated distance).
- **How:** look for the end signs after a no-overtaking or speed-limit sign; the annex does not say that a prohibition ends at the next junction, so without an end sign or a new sign treat the prohibition as still in force and ask the authority if in doubt.
- **Verify:** the figure inside the sign is the one ended.
- **Don't:** accelerate before the end sign.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L point 12 (R.31/32) and P.34 (read 2026-11-15).

## Warning signs (P)

### VEH-SEN-18: Warning signs: the yellow diamond does not forbid, but you must adapt
- **Rule:** the generic warning sign (*preventiva*) is a square placed on its corner, yellow with a black line round the edge and a black symbol, 0.7 to 0.9 m a side; the maximum-danger sign is a white triangle with a red border; special signs are the St. Andrew's cross, approach panels and directional arrows (Anexo L 13). "They warn of the proximity of a circumstance or variation of the normal state of the road that may be surprising or dangerous; they give no directions, but before a warning an appropriate attitude or conduct must be adopted" (Anexo L 13 b).
- **Why:** driving at the same speed through a signed hazard can still be a fault under the precautionary speed rule (Ley 24.449 art 50).
- **How:** slow down before the hazard, not in it; the sign is placed at a distance suitable for the road speed.
- **Verify:** the symbol shows the hazard (curve, slope, school, animals); a plate may add a distance.
- **Don't:** read a warning as optional: ignoring it can make you responsible for a crash.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L points 13-18 (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 art 50.

### VEH-SEN-19: Level-crossing signs: P.1, P.3 (St. Andrew's cross), R.30 barriers, H.13 marking
- **Rule:** P.1 (level crossing (*paso a nivel*)) is a white triangle with a red border and a black steam locomotive in side view pointing left, in high-reflectivity material: slow down and watch for trains. P.3 is the St. Andrew's cross (*Cruz de San Andrés*): two crossed arms with points, white with a red border (the older black-bordered cross is accepted), marking the start of the crossing zone, inside which the train has priority; if more than two tracks are to be crossed a plate gives the number of tracks. R.30 is the barrier: a bar red and white in 0.4-0.5 m stripes at 45 degrees, which when horizontal on the carriageway prohibits entry even if it leaves a gap. H.13 is an X on the pavement with the letters F and C, warning of the crossing (Anexo L P.1, P.3, R.30, H.13). In rural areas approach panels (P.2.1) count down 100 m per red stripe (Anexo L P.2).
- **Why:** it is the one place where the vehicle with priority cannot stop.
- **How:** slow down at P.1, stop before the stop line (*línea de detención*) (H.4) or before the cross or barrier if there is no line, look both ways, and cross only if the exit is clear (VEH-SEN-35 gives the full procedure).
- **Verify:** the number of tracks and the existence of barriers or lights.
- **Don't:** stop between the cross and the track or on the rails.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L P.1, P.2, P.3, R.30, H.4, H.13 (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 art 48 k.

### VEH-SEN-20: Road geometry warnings (P.4 to P.24)
- **Rule:** the annex lists, among others: P.4 sharp curve (max-danger triangle with a curved arrow); P.7 curve (curve, sharp curve, curve and counter-curve, sharp S); P.8 winding road (three or more successive curves); P.9 slope (triangle with an inclined hypotenuse and a car on a downhill); P.10 narrowing; P.11 irregular profile (rough road, dip or *badén*, hump or *lomada*); P.12 slippery road; P.13 loose stones thrown by vehicles; P.14 rockfall; P.15 tunnel; P.16 narrow bridge; P.17 movable bridge; P.18/P.19 limited height or width; P.20 start or end of divided carriageway; P.21 roundabout (*rotonda*) (three arrows counter-clockwise); P.22 merging traffic; P.23 start of two-way traffic; P.24 crossroads, T or fork in several diagrams; P.5 pedestrian crossing (*senda peatonal*) (rural zones only) (Anexo L 14-15).
- **Why:** most are placed at a distance that lets you slow to the safe speed before the hazard.
- **How:** brake before the curve (not in it), select a lower gear on a long downhill (the brakes of an old car can fade), and on P.11 slow before the hump or dip.
- **Verify:** an end sign (P.34) may close a zone of slippery road, loose stones or rockfall.
- **Don't:** overtake in the signed curve (Ley 24.449 art 48 j) or brake hard on a slippery section.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L P.4-P.24 (read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-SEN-21: Signs of possible hazards (P.25 to P.30)
- **Rule:** P.25 children or schoolchildren (black silhouette of a child with a notebook or playing with a ball); P.26 cyclists or riders; P.27 loose animals (cow, deer or other); P.28 low-flying aircraft near an airfield; P.29 unusual vehicles (tram, trolley bus, farm machinery, ambulances, fire engines); P.30 strong side winds (tree bent by wind) (Anexo L 16).
- **Why:** the hazard can appear without warning, so the precautionary speed (Ley 24.449 art 50) applies; near schools the limit drops (art 51 e.3: 20 km/h while open).
- **How:** at night on rural roads with P.27 reduce speed and use high beams when no one is coming; with P.30 hold the wheel firmly and watch for trucks and bridges.
- **Verify:** the plate with a distance or length of the zone.
- **Don't:** overtake a vehicle stopped at a school sign.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L P.25-P.30 (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 arts 50, 51 e.3.

### VEH-SEN-22: Chevrons, approach panels and directional arrows (P.2, P.31)
- **Rule:** P.2 has three variants: an approach panel (white rectangle with red stripes at 45 degrees, one stripe per 100 m left to the obstacle, used with max-danger signs), a rigid-obstacle panel (white and red stripes over the full rectangle, showing the side to pass) and the curve chevron (white square with red right-angled stripes forming an arrowhead pointing to the side where the road continues). P.31 is a yellow rectangle with an arrow (or two arrows) over a black-striped lower field, placed where the road changes direction (Anexo L P.2, P.31).
- **Why:** chevrons show the line of a bend at night.
- **How:** follow the direction in which the chevrons point.
- **Verify:** the stripes lean toward the side where traffic passes.
- **Don't:** drive on the shoulder to "cut" the bend.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L P.2, P.31 (read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-SEN-23: Advance signs: traffic light ahead (P.32), restrictive sign ahead (P.33), attention (P.6)
- **Rule:** P.32 is a black traffic-light figure with three coloured lights, possibly with a distance "a ... m": a signalised junction is coming. P.33 repeats a smaller version of the sign it announces (STOP, CEDA EL PASO or another prohibition) with a distance, placed at the stated distance or in the block before the sign. P.6 is a triangle with an exclamation mark and an explanatory plate: special alert (Anexo L P.6, P.32, P.33).
- **Why:** advance warnings are for the lights and signs you cannot see until late.
- **How:** use them to adjust speed before the junction.
- **Verify:** the distance printed on the plate.
- **Don't:** ignore a P.33 with a STOP: the STOP sign follows.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L P.6, P.32, P.33 (read 2026-11-15).

## Informative signs (I)

### VEH-SEN-24: Informative signs (green, blue, white) guide but do not give orders
- **Rule:** informative signs (*señal informativa*) are rectangles; green backgrounds for place names and directions, blue for motorways (*autopista*), dead ends and tourist or service symbols, with white lettering. "They have no legal consequences, that is, they do not transmit orders nor warn of irregularities or risks, unless they contain regulatory or warning signs" (Anexo L 19 b). Examples: I.1.2 national route (white pentagon with the number); I.1.3 provincial route (white square with black border); I.1.4 street nomenclature with name, house-number range and direction; I.8 start or end of urban area; I.10 kilometre marker; I.11-I.13 motorway start, end (red diagonal band) and name; I.14 lane-use diagram; I.15 dead end (*calle sin salida*) (blue panel, red bar across the road); I.18-I.19 route diagrams for special itineraries (for example a left turn); I.20 parking allowed; I.21 turn allowed; I.22 permitted directions; tourist and service panels (health post with a red cross, fuel pump, mechanic's wrench, hotel H, cup, telephone) (Anexo L 20-24).
- **Why:** they help you navigate; the start of urban area (I.8) is not by itself a speed order, but the legal limits change at that point (Ley 24.449 art 51 a, b).
- **How:** use I.1.2 and I.10 to identify your position for an emergency call (route and kilometre).
- **Verify:** informative panels that include an R or P sign carry that sign's force.
- **Don't:** use "no informative sign" as a defence for exceeding a legal default (VEH-SEN-45).
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L points 19-24 (read 2026-11-15).

## Roadworks

### VEH-SEN-25: Roadwork signs and devices (T.1 to T.11): orange, slow down, obey the flagger
- **Rule:** temporary signs are diamonds (square on its corner) with a black symbol or message on a reflective orange background; information signs are orange rectangles (Anexo L 39-40). The main ones: T.1 road or street under construction or closed; T.2 detour; T.3 single-lane road; T.4 narrowing; T.5 flagger (*banderillero*) ahead (a plate gives the distance, at least 100 m); T.6 men working; T.7 heavy equipment on the road; T.8 works on the shoulder (cones on the edge); T.9 explosives zone; T.10 length of construction; T.11 end of construction. The flagger waves a red flag by day and a red lamp at night to warn of danger or indicate manoeuvres (Anexo L T.5). Devices: fences (white and orange stripes at 45 degrees), cones at least 0.5 m high (orange with white bands), 200 l drums (orange and white bands), delineators, plastic New Jersey barriers (white and orange for temporary diversions, white and red for permanent ones), yellow flashing lights, red lights for forbidden zones, and pavement markings that can be removed (Anexo L 41-47). Regulatory signs (*señal reglamentaria*) in the work zone keep their normal force (Anexo L 38).
- **Why:** work zones combine narrow lanes, flaggers and plant; slowing early avoids a fine and an accident.
- **How:** reduce speed at the first T sign, merge early, obey the flagger even against a green light, keep distance from workers.
- **Verify:** CABA and the ANSV have manuals on temporary signage that show the standard layouts (see sources).
- **Don't:** drive through cones or over a closed lane edge, or ignore the flagger (Ley 24.449 art 36 puts the officer or authority first).
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L points 37-47 (read 2026-11-15); CABA Manual de Señalización Vial Transitoria (2019) and ANSV Sistema integrado de señalética de obras (2019) as illustrated references (read 2026-11-15).

## Pavement markings (H)

### VEH-SEN-26: Line colours and what continuous, dashed and double lines mean
- **Rule:** markings are white or yellow. Yellow separates opposite traffic on two-way roads with a paved carriageway, shows double centre lines on multi-lane roads and barrier lines at width transitions, obstacles passed on the right, islands and places where crossing to the opposite lane must be prevented; white is used for lane lines, edge lines, channelling lines, arrows, stop lines, crossings, parking bays, symbols and words (Anexo L 26 a). Meaning: a continuous line must not be crossed or driven on, whatever its colour; a double continuous line reinforces it and marks a minimum separation between directions; dashed lines may be crossed; a continuous line next to a dashed line allows crossing from the dashed side to the continuous one only, not the other way (Anexo L 26 c). H.1 separates opposite directions; a double yellow line must be used on busy stretches, curves, bridges, slopes, level crossings (*paso a nivel*) and wherever overtaking (*adelantamiento*) is forbidden; H.2 lane lines; H.3 edge line (Anexo L H.1-H.3). On reversible lanes the dividing line is a double dashed one with vertical or luminous signs (Anexo L H.1).
- **Why:** overtaking over a continuous line is a typical ground for fines and head-on collisions.
- **How:** if your side has the dashed line you may overtake when safe; if it has the continuous line you may not.
- **Verify:** at night the line must be reflective on motorways (*autopista*), tunnels, bridges and busy streets (Anexo L 26 b).
- **Don't:** assume a worn line "means nothing" (see VEH-SEN-42 about faded markings).
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L point 26-27 (H.1-H.3) (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 art 42.

### VEH-SEN-27: Stop line and pedestrian crossing (H.4, H.5): stop before them
- **Rule:** H.4, the stop line (*línea de detención*), is a continuous white line 0.5 m wide: stop before crossing it when ordered by an officer, a light or sign, a pedestrian or rail crossing, or when the junction is occupied (Anexo L H.4). H.5, the pedestrian crossing (*senda peatonal*), is a strip across the carriageway bounded by two white lines or marked by white stripes parallel to the traffic (zebra: rectangles 0.4-0.5 m wide and at least 3 m long), where pedestrians cross and may not stop or wait; vehicles may not park or stop on it, "not even because of traffic"; pedestrians have priority unless a light or officer says otherwise; where none is marked, the imaginary prolongation of the cross sidewalk is the crossing (Anexo L H.5; Ley 24.449 art 5 t). H.6 cycle crossing: vehicles must give way (*ceda el paso*) when crossing it.
- **Why:** blocking the crossing forces pedestrians into the carriageway.
- **How:** stop before the line; if the junction is blocked wait before the crossing.
- **Verify:** the zebra stripes and the stop line are separate markings.
- **Don't:** stop with the front wheels on the crossing at a red light.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L H.4, H.5, H.6 (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 arts 5 t, 41 e, 44 a.2.

### VEH-SEN-28: Arrows, channelling lines, inscriptions and parking bays (H.8, H.9, H.11, H.12)
- **Rule:** H.9 arrows (white): they are mandatory and show the direction to follow in the lane where they are painted; the combined arrow (straight plus curved) allows going straight or turning (Anexo L H.9). H.8 channelling lines (yellow or white diagonals or V shapes) around islands: never to be crossed or driven on; they warn of obstacles and direct traffic aside (Anexo L H.8). H.11 parking bays: white lines 0.1 m wide showing space, form and vehicle type; zigzag lines near the kerb (*cordón*) may mark restricted parking (Anexo L H.11). H.12 inscriptions: letters, numbers and symbols in white: P (bus stop), E (parking), speeds, the CEDA EL PASO triangle, the diamond (exclusive lane) and the vehicle figure; at most three words, and on motorways (*autopista*) at most one line (Anexo L H.12).
- **Why:** lane arrows are as binding as signs; many tickets are for turning from a straight-only lane.
- **How:** choose your lane before the junction according to the arrows.
- **Verify:** arrows are repeated before the junction.
- **Don't:** cross a channelling area to "save" a lane change.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L H.8, H.9, H.11, H.12 (read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-SEN-29: Painted kerbs: red = no stopping, yellow = no parking
- **Rule:** kerbs (*cordón*) may be painted to reinforce the signs; the colours are red and yellow, painted on the top face and the face towards the carriageway. A red kerb forbids parking and stopping beside the pavement; a yellow kerb forbids only parking, so you may stop to let people on or off or load or unload (Anexo L H.15). The kerb can also reinforce the general rule, for example red at a corner or garage entrance.
- **Why:** the paint is the legal sign for that kerb.
- **How:** if the kerb is red keep moving, if yellow you may stop briefly with the driver in the car.
- **Verify:** both the colour and any plate under it.
- **Don't:** park on a faded red or yellow kerb "because the paint is old" without checking the rule (VEH-SEN-42).
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L H.15 (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 art 49.

### VEH-SEN-30: Speed-reduction lines, humps, studs, delineators and fog chevrons (H.7, H.14, H.16-H.18)
- **Rule:** H.7: white transverse lines at varying spacing to induce speed reduction; H.14: traffic separators, rounded raised bands up to 10 cm high that make it hard to drive over them; H.16: studs (reflective or not; red ones mark wrong way or no entry); H.17: delineators (cones, posts, tape) with reflective elements; H.18, fog markings: a series of arrowheads (inverted V, 60-90 degrees) every 40 m in the centre of each lane: when you see only two from the one you are on, do not exceed 60 km/h; when you see only one, do not exceed 40 km/h; if you cannot see the sign or only see it at short distance, slow down and leave the road safely, stopping off the carriageway and shoulder (Anexo L H.7, H.14, H.16-H.18). The existence or absence of the fog marks "does not create responsibilities and does not exempt the driver from those he may have" (Anexo L H.18).
- **Why:** these markings tell you about hazards that signs cannot cover.
- **How:** in fog slow down, switch on low beams and fog lights (not high beams), and use the arrows to measure visibility.
- **Verify:** an explanatory sign usually announces the fog arrows.
- **Don't:** follow another car's tail-lights too closely in fog.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L H.7, H.14, H.16-H.18 (read 2026-11-15).

## Traffic lights

### VEH-SEN-31: Traffic lights: red, amber, green and the rules around them
- **Rule:** green: go on; red: stop before the stop line (*línea de detención*) or the crossing and make no movement; amber: stop if you estimate you will not clear the junction before the red, or clear it at once; amber flashing: proceed with caution (Anexo L 32 a; Ley 24.449 art 44). On a vertical head the lights from top are red, amber, green; on a horizontal head from left red, amber, green; the head and visors are yellow outside (Anexo L 31 a). The maximum speed on a signalised road is the one signed for the coordinated greens (Anexo L 32 d). You must let the vehicle that began crossing finish, and not begin yours, even on green, if there is not space on the other side (Anexo L 32 e); left turns on two-way roads are forbidden unless a sign allows (Anexo L 32 f). Pedestrians cross on a white pedestrian light, or on green when only a vehicle light exists, never on red or amber (Anexo L 32 b). Running a red is a grave fault (*falta grave*) (Ley 24.449 art 77 o).
- **Why:** it is the commonest camera ticket.
- **How:** brake at amber when you can stop safely; do not enter a junction on amber if you are far from it; wait to see free space beyond the junction.
- **Verify:** traffic lights (*semáforo*) have at least two faces per approach (Anexo L 31 c).
- **Don't:** follow the car ahead through a late amber.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L points 31-32 (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 arts 44, 77 o.

### VEH-SEN-32: Flashing amber and flashing red
- **Rule:** flashing amber warns of a risky crossing: cross with caution (Ley 24.449 art 44 a.4); flashing red warns of a dangerous crossing: stop and move off only when you see there is no risk (art 44 a.5). The controller must replace the normal cycle with flashing amber when the red light fails in any movement, when crossing greens occur, when the system jams or when the supply voltage drops below 175 V; the start-up cycle always begins with flashing amber, then all red (Anexo L 31 f). Flashing amber units are also used alone to warn of danger or to mark junctions, possibly with a speed plate (Anexo L 36 b).
- **Why:** many drivers treat flashing red like amber; the law says to stop.
- **How:** at a flashing red stop completely, look, then proceed; at a flashing amber slow down and look.
- **Verify:** if the lights are dark or flashing, drive as the law says (art 44) and keep the general priority rules in mind (traffic-law.md VEH-LEY-33).
- **Don't:** go through a flashing red without stopping.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art 44 a.4-5; Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L 31 f, 36 b (read 2026-11-15). Not verified in a primary source: priority at a junction with failed lights (the text read says only what each flashing light means).

### VEH-SEN-33: Arrow lights and lane signals
- **Rule:** arrows must point in the permitted direction; a vertical up arrow means straight on, a horizontal arrow means a turn at about a right angle to that side; when a face has a green arrow lit, vehicles may only take the directions it indicates (Anexo L 34): green vertical arrow = straight only; green left or right arrow = turn in that direction. The order of units on a vertical head is: red disc, red left arrow, red right arrow, amber disc, green disc, amber front arrow, amber left arrow, green left arrow, amber right arrow, green right arrow (Anexo L 35 a).
- **Why:** with only an arrow lit on a lane you cannot go straight if it is a turn arrow.
- **How:** read the arrow for your lane, not the main light.
- **Verify:** which arrow is lit for your lane.
- **Don't:** go straight on a green turn arrow, or turn on a green straight arrow.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L 34-35 (read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-SEN-34: Pedestrian lights and reversible-lane signals
- **Rule:** pedestrian lights (*semáforos para peatones*) have square units, orange or white, on the opposite sidewalk; white means cross (Anexo L 36 a). On reversible lanes the units are squares 0.3 m with a green arrow pointing down (you may use the lane below it) or a red X (you may not); a flashing green arrow announces the change of direction (Anexo L 36 d). At rail crossings for pedestrians a white light means cross; the orange light flashing then steady means wait behind the stop line (*línea de detención*), and a sound plays; "OTRO TREN" (another train) warns of a second train (Anexo L 36 a-1).
- **Why:** reversible lanes (some in CABA and on access roads) are indicated only by overhead lights.
- **How:** check the signal above your lane; leave a lane showing a red X as soon as it is safe to do so.
- **Verify:** the lights are mounted 5-6 m high over each lane (Anexo L 36 d).
- **Don't:** drive in a lane with a red X.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L 36 a, a-1, d (read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-SEN-35: Level crossing with barriers and lights: what each signal means and what to do
- **Rule:** the special rail traffic light (*semáforo*) has two round red lights side by side, flashing alternately, in a black screen with visors, with a bell; when working, it means a train is approaching or present, which has priority, and you may not enter; when off, the crossing is open (Anexo L 36 c). The barrier when it starts to descend or move means: you cannot begin crossing and must clear the crossing; the stop is before the line H.4 or, if none, before the barrier; when the barrier is broken the railway must replace it with a person using a red flag by day or red light at night, and a waved red flag means no advance (Anexo L R.30). Ley 24.449 treats the start of the barrier descent as amber (art 44 a.6) and prohibits crossing when a train is near, when warnings are given, when the barriers are down or moving, or when the exit is not free (art 48 k).
- **Why:** barriers and rail lights are controlled by the railway authority, not the road authority (Anexo L 2).
- **How:** steps: (1) slow at P.1; (2) stop before H.4 or the cross (P.3) if the lights flash or the barrier moves; (3) wait until the barrier is fully up and the lights are off; (4) check for a second train when two tracks; (5) cross at steady speed and do not stop on the tracks; (6) if the barrier is broken follow the flagger (*banderillero*).
- **Verify:** the light is off and the barrier is up; the number-of-tracks plate if more than one.
- **Don't:** zigzag round a lowered barrier or stop within 5 m of the rails without a barrier (art 48 k).
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.30, 36 c; Ley 24.449 arts 44 a.6, 48 k (read 2026-11-15); reg art 48 k.1-k.2 (Decreto 32/2018). The step list is a safe-practice synthesis of those texts.

### VEH-SEN-36: New level-crossing signs of the 2025 annex (P.1-1, P.11-1, P.24-1, F.1, F.2, F.3)
- **Rule:** Decreto 196/2025 added signs for level crossings (*paso a nivel*): P.1-1 "level crossing on a turn" (a "en giro" plate with an arrow under a P.1); P.11-1 irregular profile at a level crossing, a yellow diamond with a long lorry stuck at a crossing and the plate "RIESGO DE ATASCAMIENTO" (risk of getting stuck: long vehicles may ground on the ramp); P.24-1 crossroads with a level crossing on a branch; F.1 "inactive railway branch" (white rectangle, black text); F.2 pedestrian crossings over railway tracks with "CUIDADO - TRENES" and "MIRE"; F.3 "ATENCIÓN FERROCARRIL" (white high-reflectivity board 2.4 x 1.2 m, with "A ... m CRUCE SIN BARRERAS") for crossings without barriers on important roads (Anexo L P.1-1, P.11-1, P.24-1, F.1-F.3).
- **Why:** older manuals do not show them; a driver of a low-slung car or long vehicle needs P.11-1.
- **How:** on P.11-1 cross at an angle that avoids grounding, or take another route if your vehicle is long or low.
- **Verify:** the full annex is in the sources; these signs are installed progressively as signs are renewed (Anexo L point 1).
- **Don't:** assume a crossing marked as "inactive" is safe without looking: some branches are reactivated.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L P.1-1, P.11-1, P.24-1, F.1, F.2, F.3 (read 2026-11-15). The warning about reactivated branches is general caution, not in the text.

## Officers

### VEH-SEN-37: Officer signals and your own hand signals
- **Rule:** the order of priority puts the indications of the traffic authority above signs and lights (Ley 24.449 art 36), so a signal from an officer directing traffic is obeyed even against a light. The 2025 Anexo L contains no chapter of officer hand signals (checked: chapters I to IX are general principles, vertical, regulatory, warning, informative, horizontal, luminous, temporary and rail-crossing signs); the gestures are set by local rules. Drivers must reinforce the turn indicator with a hand signal when turning into a minor road or a private property (Ley 24.449 art 43 d).
- **Why:** an officer at a failed light or after a crash decides who goes.
- **How:** when an officer raises a hand toward you or points at you, stop where he indicates; if you do not understand, stop and ask. Use your arm outside the window only for the signal required by art 43 d.
- **Verify:** local traffic authority publications (for example CABA's Código de Tránsito or its education pages).
- **Don't:** accelerate through an officer's signal or try to "read" it as a light.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 arts 36 and 43 d (read 2026-11-15). Not verified in a primary source: the exact officer gestures (arm raised, extended, whistle) as they are not in the annex text read.

## Local signage: CABA and PBA

### VEH-SEN-38: Metrobus lanes and exclusive lanes in CABA
- **Rule:** the Metrobus system of the City (Ley 2992, a rapid bus system in dedicated lanes) uses exclusive lanes: driving in the Metrobus lane without being authorised is a ground for retaining the licence (*licencia de conducir*) and a citation slip (*boleta de citación del inculpado*) (Ley 2148 art 5.6.1 b.11 and 5.6.2), as is using the cycle path (art 5.6.1 b.12). The vertical sign is R.18 and the marking H.2 (double continuous line) with the inscription H.12 (diamond) (VEH-SEN-13).
- **Why:** it is one of the most photographed infringements in the City.
- **How:** never enter the bus lane, even to avoid traffic; cross it only where the markings show a crossing; where the lane is painted a colour, follow it as a barrier (the red surface is a local practice: not verified in a primary source).
- **Verify:** CABA transport pages for the map of lanes and Ley 2992.
- **Don't:** stop or park in the lane to drop passengers.
- **Source:** Ley 2148 CABA arts 5.6.1-5.6.2 (https://juristeca.jusbaires.gob.ar/compilacion-normativa-juristeca/ley-2148/h-tit-5/, read 2026-11-15); Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.18, H.2, H.12. Not verified in a primary source: Ley 2992 text and the colour of the Metrobus pavement.

### VEH-SEN-39: Speed cameras and roadside controls: how they must be signed (PBA)
- **Rule:** in PBA speed meters (*cinemómetros*) and automatic or manual systems are used under Ley 13.927 art 28: they must be approved and operate without possibility of manual alteration; officers who use them must be public officials; infractions detected on the road (except by fixed automatic meters) must be notified or made known to the driver within 10 km of the place if circumstances allow; the control operation must be at safe distances and the sector before it signalled and marked; fixed automatic cases must be notified within 60 working days of the event (art 28). Art 28 bis: where the cameras are on stretches whose permitted speed is lower than the national maximum (art 51 of Ley 24.449), their presence must be signed vertically at least 500 m before the detection zone, the sign clearly identifiable and showing the maximum speed; on motorways (*autopista*) and semi-motorways where the limit is the legal maximum a particular sign is not needed, but a sign informing of speed control on that road is; non-compliance makes the infraction records and photo-fines "null with absolute nullity" and without legal binding for payment (art 28 bis). In CABA, camera signage was not verified.
- **Why:** it is the clearest written signage defence for a photo-fine in PBA.
- **How:** drive the route again and measure the distance between the sign and the camera with GPS or the odometer; photograph the sign (or its absence) and the camera, with date.
- **Verify:** the notification arrived within 60 working days; that the sign states the maximum speed and is 500 m or more before the camera.
- **Don't:** use this defence on a motorway section where the limit is the legal maximum (the 500 m sign is not required there) or where the camera is signed 500 m ahead.
- **Source:** Ley 13.927 PBA arts 28 and 28 bis (https://normas.gba.gob.ar/documentos/0YqDnfd0.html, read 2026-11-15). Not verified in a primary source: CABA's equivalent and municipalities' rules.

### VEH-SEN-40: Tow zones and parking prohibitions: what the signs must show
- **Rule:** a car may be towed (*acarreo*) when it is badly parked and obstructs traffic or visibility, occupies spaces for emergency or public-transport vehicles, or is abandoned or broken and not repaired or removed at once; costs are paid before pickup (Ley 24.449 art 72 c.5). Signs R.8 and R.9 (with hours), R.17 reserved parking and painted kerbs (*cordón*) H.15 tell where parking is forbidden (VEH-SEN-12, VEH-SEN-29). In CABA the procedure for retained vehicles not retrieved is in art 5.6.3 of Ley 2148.
- **Why:** a tow is expensive and slow; many are avoidable by reading the plate with the hours.
- **How:** photograph the car, the signs and the kerb before leaving; if towed, ask for the record with the legal cause and take photos of the place (they are your evidence); to get the car back you need documents and to pay.
- **Verify:** the plate under the sign that gives days and hours; whether the sign is on your side of the street.
- **Don't:** assume a sign on the other side applies to you, or that the absence of a sign allows you to block a crossing or corner (the law prohibits it without a sign: Ley 24.449 art 49 b).
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 arts 49, 72 c.5; Ley 2148 CABA art 5.6.3 (juristeca h-tit-5, read 2026-11-15); Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.8, R.9, R.17, H.15. Tow fees and depots: not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-SEN-41: "No left turn" signs and left-turn rules
- **Rule:** the sign R.4 pointing left forbids the left turn; the bar runs from lower left to upper right only for left-turn bans (Anexo L R.4). Besides, on two-way roads regulated by traffic lights (*semáforo*) left turns are forbidden unless a sign allows it (Ley 24.449 art 44 f; Anexo L 32 f) and where permitted a "permitted turn" sign I.21 applies. In CABA the left turn on two-way streets without lights, or with a turn signal, is made from the lane nearest the centre line after 30 m, without crossing the axis, unless the junction is marked to do it from the right lane (Ley 2148 art 6.1.14 b).
- **Why:** many avenues in the City forbid left turns at certain hours.
- **How:** look for the plate with hours; if banned, turn at the next permitted junction, or use the "loop" shown by I.18 route diagrams.
- **Verify:** hours on the plate and the lane arrows.
- **Don't:** make the left turn from the right lane or in two steps unless marked.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L R.4, I.18, I.21, point 32 f; Ley 24.449 art 44 f; Ley 2148 CABA art 6.1.14 (juristeca h-tit-6, read 2026-11-15).

## Poor, missing or non-standard signage and fine disputes

### VEH-SEN-42: What counts as poor, missing or non-standard signage
- **Rule:** the annex sets the quality the road authority must meet; failures include: (1) a device that is not in the annex or uses an unapproved symbol (Anexo L 2: "no symbols or signs not contemplated can be used"; non-conforming advertising must be removed); (2) a sign that is damaged, faded or not retroreflective: the owner of the road must keep signs "in good condition of preservation and performance" and replace those that do not conform (Anexo L 5); the minimum reflectivity is set by IRAM 10.033/73 table II and, for high-reflectivity signs, IRAM 3.952/84 tables II and III (Anexo L 7 a); signs at curves, bridges, roundabouts (*rotonda*), crossings, accesses to paved roads, and on motorways (*autopista*) must be high-reflectivity (Anexo L 7 a); (3) a sign hidden by trees, parked vehicles, advertising or other external elements: the authorities must preserve the "integrity and visibility" of the devices and the road entity must supervise visibility by day, night and in adverse weather (Anexo L 5, 7 a); (4) markings that are not reflective where the annex requires it (motorways, tunnels, bridges, access ramps, busy streets, and always for dividing lines, stop lines, crossings, rail-crossing marks, CEDA EL PASO and PARE) (Anexo L 26 b); (5) a missing sign where one is required for the rule to exist: local exceptions and accessory provisions must be announced through the system (Anexo L 3; Ley 24.449 art 2); (6) a sign placed so that the driver cannot stop before the object (Anexo L 8 c).
- **Why:** these are the legal criteria; they turn "the sign was bad" into specific points you can prove.
- **How:** for each point note: the sign code, the defect, the date and place, and the article of Anexo L that sets the standard.
- **Verify:** compare with the annex (sources) and with the manuals for the standard dimensions.
- **Don't:** confuse a defect in an informative sign (*señal informativa*) (no legal consequences, VEH-SEN-24) with a defect in a regulatory sign (*señal reglamentaria*).
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L points 2, 3, 5, 7 a, 8 c, 19 b, 26 b (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 art 2.

### VEH-SEN-43: Using signage defects in a fine dispute (and where to go for the procedure)
- **Rule:** a fine for a rule that had to be enforced by a sign is attackable when the sign was missing, hidden or non-conforming: "it is not an infringement to fail to comply with a provision that had to be stated through the Uniform Signage System and is not" (Anexo L point 3); local exceptions must be clearly posted at the place where they apply to be valid (Ley 24.449 art 2); in PBA a speed camera (*cinemómetro, fotomulta*) not signed 500 m before (where the limit is lower than the legal maximum) makes the record null (Ley 13.927 art 28 bis, VEH-SEN-39). The procedure must ensure your right to defence and weigh the act with "reasoned sound criticism" (Ley 24.449 arts 69 a, 70 b.2). How to file the defence, the deadlines and the payment options are in [fines-and-points.md](fines-and-points.md).
- **Why:** a good dispute rests on a specific defect plus evidence, not on general complaints.
- **How:** (1) read the record: which article, which sign or rule, date, time, place; (2) check which sign was needed (VEH-SEN-45); (3) collect evidence (VEH-SEN-44); (4) file the written defence within the term in the record (see fines-and-points.md), quoting the article of Anexo L and, if PBA cameras, art 28 bis; (5) ask the authority for the installation record or inventory of the sign for that date (access to public information; the exact procedure depends on the jurisdiction, not verified here).
- **Verify:** that your defence cites the sign code and article, and attaches dated photos.
- **Don't:** ignore the notification: deadlines run from the notice, and an undefended record becomes a final fine.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L point 3 (read 2026-11-15); Ley 24.449 arts 2, 69 a, 70 b.2; Ley 13.927 PBA art 28 bis. For the procedure see fines-and-points.md. Not verified in a primary source: the information-access route for sign records.

### VEH-SEN-44: Evidence checklist for a signage dispute
- **Rule:** collect, as soon as you can: photographs of the sign (or the empty place) from the driver's viewpoint at 100 m, 50 m and at the sign, by day and, if relevant, by night with the date and time; a video of the approach with GPS or the odometer to show the distance; photographs of obstructions (tree, bus stop, parked vehicles, advertising) and of damage (faded face, bent post, sign turned away); the weather and light at the time of the event; the position of the camera or officer relative to the sign; the full notification (date received, envelope, digital notification) and the infraction record (*acta de infracción*) with its copy; and, if available, dated street-level images of the place.
- **Why:** the authority will assume the sign was in place unless you show otherwise.
- **How:** make the evidence the same day if possible: signs get repaired after a complaint, which is why the first set of photos matters; keep the original files with their metadata.
- **Verify:** the photos show the legal standard problem (for example that a no-parking sign is turned away or hidden, that the plate with hours is missing).
- **Don't:** edit the photos or measure distances loosely: the authority checks the data.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L points 3, 5, 7 (criteria to prove, read 2026-11-15); Ley 13.927 PBA art 28 bis (500 m rule). The evidence list is practical advice and not a legal requirement (not verified in a primary source).

### VEH-SEN-45: Signage defects that do not help you
- **Rule:** the missing sign is not a defence when the rule exists without a sign: the default speeds of Ley 24.449 art 51, the right-hand priority (*prioridad de paso*) of art 41, the ban on parking on crossings, corners, bus stops and sidewalks of art 49, the red light of art 44 and the duty to stop at a level crossing (*paso a nivel*) barrier (art 48 k) apply without a local sign; informative signs (*señal informativa*) (I) have no legal consequence (Anexo L 19 b); a faded marking such as the fog arrows (H.18) "does not create responsibilities nor exempt the driver" (Anexo L H.18); a worn sign that is still legible and recognisable is not a good defence; and the knowledge of the meaning of the system is presumed (Anexo L 3).
- **Why:** do not file a weak defence: it can cost time and show you did not know the rules.
- **How:** ask first: did the rule I am accused of need a sign, or does the law say it directly? If the law says it, look at other defences (wrong place, wrong plate, wrong time, camera error).
- **Verify:** compare the article of the record with VEH-SEN-3.
- **Don't:** argue that "there was no STOP sign" without checking that the junction was one where right-hand priority gives you a different duty.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 arts 41, 44, 48 k, 49, 51; Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L points 3, 19 b, H.18 (read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-SEN-46: Report a damaged, hidden or missing sign
- **Rule:** damage to a sign or device, intentional or not, must be reported to the police or judicial authority, naming the probable culprit if possible; the road entity (national, provincial, municipal or its concessionaire) must keep the devices in good condition and replace those that do not conform (Anexo L 5). Hazards that are temporary must be removed by the first authority to intervene, or signed with police intervention if needed (Anexo L 2).
- **Why:** a documented complaint helps repairs and, if a dispute follows, shows you reported the defect.
- **How:** write to the road authority of the place (Vialidad Nacional for national routes, the provincial road agency, the municipality, or the City's citizen line 147 in CABA, verify the current channel) with the place, photos and date; keep the ticket number of the complaint; in an emergency call 911.
- **Verify:** keep the complaint receipt with your evidence (VEH-SEN-44).
- **Don't:** move or straighten a damaged sign yourself on a motorway (*autopista*) or busy road.
- **Source:** Decreto 196/2025 Anexo L points 2 and 5 (read 2026-11-15). Not verified in a primary source: the current complaint channels of each authority (CABA 147 is a public line, check the City's site).
