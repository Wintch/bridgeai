# VW Gol "cuadrado" 1994: known failures and repair procedures

For the owner or mechanic of a first-generation VW Gol (BX, 1980s-1994 body, "Gol cuadrado") as sold in Argentina, mainly the GL 1.6 with a carburettor (*carburador*) and the AP engine, with notes for the 1.8. Numbers that identify the car and its service data (capacities, torque tables, the maintenance schedule) are in [vw-gol-1994-specs.md](vw-gol-1994-specs.md); this document repeats a value only when a procedure needs it and gives its source. The main reference is the Spanish workshop manual "Manual de reparación y ajustes - Gol" (called "the workshop manual" below, with printed page numbers) whose carburettor chapter covers the cars "before 1996"; the owner confirmed that the car is the AP 1.6 with a Weber DMPV two-barrel carburettor, and a 1994 car falls in the "Weber DMPV from November 1991" section (printed p. 197). It is a scanned book of the late 1990s, not an official Volkswagen document for model year 1994, and some chapters describe the later Gol (1995 onwards, "G2" era, injected). Steps that come from the G2-era text and may differ on the first generation (body, dashboard, wiring, rear suspension) are flagged in the rules. Where a figure may differ on a 1994 car it is flagged. Forum and video material is marked as secondary.

**Safety first, for the whole document.** Brakes, fuel, the battery, the hot cooling system and a car on a jack can kill or maim. Never get under a car held only by a jack: use axle stands on firm level ground, chock the wheels, and disconnect the battery negative terminal before work on wiring, the starter or the fuel system. Petrol vapour ignites: no flame, no smoking, no sparks (switching the ignition on and off) near an open fuel line, work outdoors, and keep a dry-powder extinguisher at hand. Never open the cooling system cap on a hot engine. A brake job, a steering or suspension repair and a timing belt job done wrongly are dangerous: if you doubt your skill or tools, take the car to a mechanic. This is information, not a guarantee, and a published number is not a substitute for the data of your own engine (see VEH-GRP-2).

## Safety, identification and diagnosis

### VEH-GRP-1: Work on a Gol safely: supports, fuel, battery, hot coolant
- **Rule:** Before any job, put the car on level ground with the handbrake (*freno de mano*) on (or wheels chocked if the rear is lifted), support it on axle stands (*caballetes*), disconnect the battery negative terminal, and treat petrol, brake fluid and hot coolant as hazards.
- **Why:** The Gol is light and a jack slips; the fuel line under the bonnet is near the hot exhaust manifold; brake fluid damages paint; coolant at engine temperature is pressurised and scalds.
- **How:** (1) Lift only at the jacking points shown in the owner's manual and always add stands before going under. (2) Negative terminal off before touching the starter, the alternator or the fuel pump wiring. (3) To open a petrol line, wrap a rag around the joint, release slowly, catch the fuel in a metal tray, keep the battery disconnected, work outdoors. (4) Coolant: wait until the engine has been off at least 30 minutes and the top hose is no longer hard, cover the cap with a thick cloth, open it in two stages. (5) Wear eye protection when using compressed air, brake cleaner or when working under the car.
- **Verify:** The car does not move when pushed on its stands; no petrol smell before you reconnect the battery; the engine is cold to the hand on the cap.
- **Don't:** Don't work under the car on the jack alone, don't open a hot cap, don't use a naked lamp to look into the tank or carburettor.
- **Source:** Not verified in a primary source: general workshop practice; the workshop manual (printed p. 63) only lists "disconnect the battery earth cable" and "drain the coolant" before removing the head. See also car-maintenance-basics.md for the general-car version.

### VEH-GRP-2: Identify your engine and carburettor before using any number
- **Rule:** Before applying a value from this guide, confirm which engine, carburettor, ignition and tappet type your car has, because "Gol 1994" covers several combinations.
- **Why:** The manual lists different carburettors, idle speeds and CO ranges for the CL 1.6 (Weber DMPV, two production series) and the GL 1.8 (Brosol 2E-CE), and mechanical tappets for the GL while the injected AP engines have hydraulic tappets. A wrong number leads to a wrong adjustment.
- **How:** (1) The engine number is stamped on the left of the cylinder block next to the fuel pump; the manual gives the engine codes UN (AP 1.6), UD (AP 1.8) and UQ (AP 2.0). (2) The owner-confirmed case of this guide is the AP 1.6 with the Weber DMPV made from November 1991 (part 029 129 015.37, manual p. 197); read the carburettor part number on its casting to be sure: the manual gives 029 129 015.26 for the Weber DMPV made until October 1991 and 029 129 015.37 for the one made from November 1991; a Brosol 2E-CE is used on the GL 1.8. (3) Look at the distributor: a pickup coil with a separate ignition module (the manual's layout) or a Hall sender; points are not shown in the manual. (4) Note whether the valve cover has a hydraulic-tappet inspection procedure for your engine (VEH-GRP-33). (5) Cars sold with the CHT or AE 1.6 engine (the Autolatina-era alternatives) are not covered by this manual; treat everything in this document as AP-only for them.
- **Verify:** You can write down: engine code, carburettor part number, ignition type, tappet type. Compare with vw-gol-1994-specs.md.
- **Don't:** Don't copy an idle speed, a float height or a jet number from a forum post written for a different carburettor (2E, 3E, Weber) or for a Brazilian alcohol-fuel Gol.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 11 (engine identification and codes), p. 184 and p. 197 (Weber DMPV part numbers and series), p. 198 (Brosol 2E-CE for the GL 1.8), file gol-1997-manual-taller-es-ar.pdf, read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: CHT/AE 1.6 engines were not covered by any document available to the author.

### VEH-GRP-3: Diagnose a hard cold start in this order: fuel, choke, spark
- **Rule:** When the engine is slow to start cold, check in this order: fuel in the carburettor bowl, choke (*cebador* or *starter*) closing and opening, spark at the plugs, then air leaks and compression.
- **Why:** On a carburetted AP almost every cold-start fault is one of three causes: no fuel (old petrol, weak pump, blocked filter), a choke that does not close or does not open, or weak spark (wet leads, tired coil, worn plugs). Doing them in order saves hours.
- **How:** (1) Remove the air filter lid and look at the carburettor with the engine cold: the choke plate should be nearly closed (the manual gives choke plate opening values, VEH-GRP-15). If it is wide open when cold with the choke knob pulled out (or, on an automatic-choke car, at all), the choke is not working (VEH-GRP-15). (2) Pump the accelerator with the ignition on and look into the primary barrel for a squirt of petrol (accelerator pump, VEH-GRP-16); no squirt means no fuel or a dry bowl. (3) Disconnect the fuel line at the carburettor into a jar and crank: a steady pulse of fuel should come. (4) Pull one plug lead, hold it 8 mm from the engine metal with insulated pliers (do not touch), crank, and look for a blue spark; check all four plugs for wetness (flooded) or dryness. (5) Flooded engine: ease the throttle to the floor while cranking so the choke is released, and do not keep pumping. (6) If fuel and spark are good, check for vacuum leaks and compression (VEH-GRP-13).
- **Verify:** The engine fires within 5 seconds of cranking when cold, and idles without stalling after the choke opens.
- **Don't:** Don't spray starting fluid into a carburettor that has an air-filter heat flap or a plastic choke housing without removing the air filter first, and never spray it while the engine is cranking with an open fuel leak.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 184, 189-190 and 197-198 (carburettor choke parts and gaps), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the diagnostic order is general carburettor practice; no Gol-specific hard-start procedure was found.

### VEH-GRP-4: Stalling at idle: check the idle valve fuse and solenoid first
- **Rule:** When a carburetted Gol dies at idle or when the throttle is released, check the idle-cutoff solenoid (*válvula de marcha lenta*) and its fuse before touching the mixture screw.
- **Why:** The 1.6 Weber and the 1.8 Brosol carburettors have an electromagnetic valve that cuts off the idle fuel when the ignition is off. If it loses power, the engine dies when the throttle closes. In the Gol fuse table the idle valve shares fuse 12 (10 A) with the brake lights, so a blown fuse 12 gives both "no brake lights" and "stalls at idle".
- **How:** (1) Check the brake lights: if they do not work, replace fuse 12 (10 A) after finding why it blew (a chafed brake-light wire, a damaged lamp holder). (2) With the ignition on, disconnect the solenoid connector and check for battery voltage at the plug; then touch 12 V to the solenoid and listen for a click (the solenoid in the Weber is item 19 in the manual, the carburettor figure on p. 193 shows it screwed into the body). (3) If it clicks and the engine still stalls, check vacuum leaks (VEH-GRP-13), the float level and the idle jets (VEH-GRP-17). (4) Then check the idle speed and CO (VEH-GRP-14). (5) Check also the cooling fan fuse is not confused: fuse 20 (30 A) is the thermoswitch/fan circuit, not the idle valve.
- **Verify:** With the engine warm the idle holds at the manual's speed for 5 minutes with the fan cycling and the lights on; the brake lights work.
- **Don't:** Don't solve a stall by screwing the idle speed screw until the engine runs at 1500 rpm; it hides the real fault and fails the VTV emissions test.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 275 (fuse table for the Gol 1.6, models before 1996: fuse 12 "luz de freno, valvula de marcha lenta" 10 A; fuse 20 30 A), p. 193 (electromagnetic fuel cut-off valve, item 19), p. 198 (Brosol 2E-CE with electronic idle control), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-5: Hesitation and flat spots: check pump, ignition timing and vacuum before the carburettor
- **Rule:** When the engine hesitates or stumbles when the throttle is opened, check the accelerator pump, the fuel delivery, the ignition timing and vacuum leaks, in that order, before rebuilding the carburettor.
- **Why:** A dead accelerator pump diaphragm, a clogged fuel filter, a stuck vacuum advance or a leaking manifold gasket cause the same symptom as a worn carburettor but cost much less.
- **How:** (1) Accelerator pump test: with the air filter off and the engine stopped, open the throttle by hand over 1 second and look for a clean jet of petrol in the primary barrel; the manual gives the delivery per 5 strokes (VEH-GRP-16). (2) Fuel pressure/flow: disconnect the carburettor inlet hose into a jar, run the starter 10 seconds; it should deliver a steady flow of at least half a cup; if not, change the fuel filter (item 7 in the manual's fuel system figure, p. 186-187) and test the pump. (3) Vacuum advance: with a timing light, the timing should advance as the engine speeds up (VEH-GRP-23). (4) Check the leads and plugs (VEH-GRP-20). (5) Check the intake manifold and carburettor base gaskets for air leaks (VEH-GRP-13).
- **Verify:** Smooth acceleration from idle to 3000 rpm in neutral with the engine warm, and no backfire.
- **Don't:** Don't enlarge jets or fit a different carburettor "for power"; a carburettor chosen for another displacement worsens consumption and emissions.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 184-187, 191-192 (carburettor parts, accelerator pump, delivery per 5 strokes), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the order of checks is general practice; the manual has no hesitation chapter.

### VEH-GRP-6: High consumption and black smoke: find the cause before changing parts
- **Rule:** If consumption is much higher than the manual's reference (about 12 km per litre) or the exhaust is black, check the air filter, choke, float level and needle, spark plugs and the CO reading, then tyre pressure and brakes dragging.
- **Why:** A rich mixture (a stuck choke, a leaking needle valve, a high float, a worn power valve) gives black smoke and a high CO; but a dragging brake, soft tyres or old plugs give the same consumption.
- **How:** (1) Look at the air filter: a clogged paper element makes the mixture rich; replace it. (2) With the engine hot, check that the choke plate is fully open (VEH-GRP-15). (3) With the engine off, look for fuel dripping from the carburettor or a wet manifold after the engine has been stopped: a needle valve that does not seal. (4) Measure CO with a gas analyser at a workshop; the manual gives the reference range (VEH-GRP-14). (5) Check that wheels turn freely when the car is jacked (VEH-GRP-37, VEH-GRP-38) and tyres are at the manual's pressure. (6) Look at the plug tips: black and sooty means rich, white means lean or hot. (7) Petrol in the oil (from a worn fuel pump diaphragm or from many short trips) raises consumption and CO: smell the dipstick and check the level for a rise.
- **Verify:** Consumption is within about 10-15% of the manual's figure on a tank-to-tank check, the exhaust is clear when warm, and CO is in the manual's range.
- **Don't:** Don't reduce consumption by leaning the mixture below the manual's CO: the engine will run hot, burn exhaust valves and hesitate.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 12 (consumption 12.02 km/l and CO 2% reference for the GL 1.6), p. 197-198 (CO ranges), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the list of causes is general practice.

### VEH-GRP-7: Diagnose overheating by the cooling circuit, not by adding coolant
- **Rule:** When the temperature gauge climbs, stop and diagnose in this order: coolant level, fan operation, thermostat, radiator and hoses, water pump and belt, and last the head gasket.
- **Why:** A Gol with a leaking hose, a stuck thermostat or a dead fan overheats in traffic. Driving it overheated warps the aluminium head and can cost the whole engine.
- **How:** (1) Park, switch off, open the bonnet, and wait. Check the expansion tank level cold. (2) With the engine warm, the electric fan should switch on at about 90-95 degrees C and off at 85-90 degrees C (manual values for a car without air conditioning); if it does not, see VEH-GRP-26. (3) Squeeze the top and bottom radiator hoses when warm: a cold bottom hose with a hot top one means a stuck thermostat or a blocked radiator (VEH-GRP-25, VEH-GRP-28). (4) Check the belt that drives the pump and alternator (VEH-GRP-31) and the pump for play and leaks (VEH-GRP-27). (5) If bubbles appear in the tank, the oil is milky or the coolant disappears with no leak, suspect the head gasket (VEH-GRP-29). (6) A first-generation Gol article notes clogged hoses on the AP as a typical cause: check the expansion tank hoses and the hoses between the radiator and the block.
- **Verify:** After a 20-minute drive with the heater on and the fan cycling, the gauge stays in the middle and the tank level is unchanged cold-to-cold.
- **Don't:** Don't keep driving with the needle in the red, don't pour cold water into a hot engine, and don't remove the thermostat for good (the engine will run too cool, burn more fuel and heat unevenly).
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 77 (electric fan thermoswitch: connects 90-95 degrees C, disconnects 85-90 degrees C without A/C), read 2026-11-15; AutoPapo, "Projetinho do Gol quadrado pode ser uma armadilha para o bolso" (secondary: clogged hoses as an AP overheating cause), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-8: Find an oil leak by source before replacing a gasket
- **Rule:** Clean the engine, run it, and find where the oil comes from; the usual sources on the AP are the valve cover gasket, the distributor O-ring, the crankshaft seals, the intermediate-shaft seal, the camshaft seal and the oil filter or sump gasket.
- **Why:** Oil runs down and spreads; the visible drip is rarely at the source. Replacing the wrong gasket wastes money and leaves the leak.
- **How:** (1) Degrease the engine and the front of the gearbox with a cold degreaser, wash and dry. (2) Run for 15 minutes and look with a torch: leaks at the top edge of the valve cover, around the distributor body (the rubber sealing ring where the distributor enters the engine; the manual's distributor drawing does not show it, buy it by the distributor model) and in the spark plug wells point to the cover and the distributor. (3) Oil behind the belt covers points to the crankshaft seal (pulley end), the intermediate shaft seal (item 9 on the manual's p. 53 figure) or the camshaft seal. (4) Oil at the clutch bellhousing is the rear main seal or the gearbox input seal: a mechanic's job. (5) Oil in the coolant tank or milky oil is a head gasket matter (VEH-GRP-29). (6) Oil on the plug wells means a hard valve cover gasket: replace it (VEH-GRP-32).
- **Verify:** After 100 km the area stays dry; check the dipstick level.
- **Don't:** Don't use sealant in place of a seal, and don't top up oil with additive "stop leak" to pass the inspection.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 49 (crankshaft seal, pulley end), p. 53 (timing-belt parts figure with the intermediate-shaft seal, item 9), p. 54-55 (valve cover and gasket, items 12-14), p. 60 (intermediate-shaft seal), p. 195-196 (distributor drawing), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the order of checks is general practice.

### VEH-GRP-9: Match a noise to its cause before opening anything
- **Rule:** Use the table below to link a noise to a likely cause, then check that cause with the test given.
- **Why:** Most noises of a Gol come from five places: the valve train, the belts, the front axle joints, the wheel bearings and the brakes.
- **How:**

| Noise | Likely cause | Quick check |
|---|---|---|
| Rapid ticking from the top of the engine, louder cold | Valve clearance (mechanical tappets) or a hydraulic tappet that bleeds down | VEH-GRP-33 |
| Chirp or squeal at start, worse wet | Loose or glazed accessory belt | VEH-GRP-31 |
| Rattle or whine from the timing cover | Tensioner roller or the toothed belt | VEH-GRP-30 |
| Clicking when turning the wheel at low speed | Outer CV joint | VEH-GRP-42 |
| Hum that rises with speed and changes when steering | Wheel bearing | VEH-GRP-43 |
| Clunk over bumps | Strut mount, control arm bushing, ball joint, anti-roll bar link | VEH-GRP-41 |
| Squeal when braking | Pad wear indicator or glazed pads, dust | VEH-GRP-37 |
| Grinding when braking | Pads worn to the metal, scored disc | VEH-GRP-37 |
| Clunk when changing gear or on and off throttle | Engine or gearbox mount, worn linkage bushes | VEH-GRP-36 |

- **Verify:** The noise stops after the repair and does not return on the same test route.
- **Don't:** Don't change CV joints for a hum that is a wheel bearing, or a clutch for a noise that comes from the belt guard.
- **Source:** Not verified in a primary source: the symptom-to-cause table is general practice for this car type, compiled from the manual's component descriptions (printed p. 49-62, 82-87, 93-94, 200-216, 218-236, read 2026-11-15).

### VEH-GRP-10: A petrol smell is a leak until proven otherwise
- **Rule:** If you smell petrol in or around the car, find and fix the leak before driving; the usual sources are the carburettor bowl, the fuel pump, the hose clamps, the fuel filter, the tank vent and filler hose, and the tank itself.
- **Why:** Petrol leaking near a hot exhaust is a fire risk, and a smell in the cabin can also be vapour from a leaking filler hose at the rear.
- **How:** (1) With the engine off and cold, look and feel with a dry paper towel along the hose from the tank to the pump and the carburettor; wet or stained towel is a leak. (2) Tight clamps and cracked rubber lines: replace with petrol-grade hose and new clamps. (3) A fuel smell only after a hot stop is a sign of percolation (a hot carburettor boiling the bowl): check the heat shield, the thermostat and the float. (4) A smell around the rear seat or boot: look at the filler tube hose and the tank vent, the manual's fuel-system diagram (printed p. 186) shows the filler pipe, the vent hose and the vapour separator. (5) A smell with a charcoal canister (*filtro de carbón activado*) cut or disconnected: reconnect. (6) A carburettor that drips when the engine is off has a needle valve that does not seal (VEH-GRP-17).
- **Verify:** No wet stain on a paper towel after the engine runs 10 minutes and after it stands overnight; no smell in the cabin.
- **Don't:** Don't drive with a visible fuel leak, don't patch a hose with tape, and don't test for leaks with a flame.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 186-187 (fuel-system general diagram: filler pipe, vent hose, fuel filter, pump, vapour separator, charcoal canister), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: leak-finding steps are general practice.

### VEH-GRP-11: Water in the cabin: find the path before drying the carpet
- **Rule:** When the floor is wet or the windows fog from inside, check these in order: the scuttle drain under the windscreen, the heater core and hoses, the door and windscreen seals, the tailgate seal, and rust holes in the floor and bulkhead.
- **Why:** Water in a Gol rots the floor and electrical connectors. The first-generation Gol has a known tendency to rust at the bulkhead and floor, and a damaged firewall or tunnel lets water in.
- **How:** (1) Clean the leaves from the scuttle grille at the base of the windscreen and pour a jug of water: it must drain under the car. (2) A wet passenger footwell with sweet-smelling water, with the coolant level dropping, means the heater core. (3) Hose the car while a helper looks inside; paper strips in the door seals show where it leaks. (4) Lift the carpet and look at the floor, the bulkhead and the tunnel for rust or cracks (VEH-GRP-50). (5) Tailgate: a wet boot floor means a hardened seal or rust at the rear corners. (6) Replace the rubber seals, seal cracks with seam sealer, not with silicone on rust.
- **Verify:** After a hose test and a rainy day the carpet stays dry and the windows do not fog.
- **Don't:** Don't leave a wet carpet in place, it keeps the floor rusting from above; don't run the car with water over the fuse and relay plate.
- **Source:** AutoPapo, "Projetinho do Gol quadrado pode ser uma armadilha para o bolso" (secondary, Brazil: cracks in the bulkhead and central tunnel let water in), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the checks are general practice.

## Carburettor

### VEH-GRP-12: Know which carburettor you have and how it works
- **Rule:** The Gol 1.6 uses a two-barrel Weber DMPV with automatic opening of the second throttle; the 1.8 GL uses a two-barrel Brosol 2E-CE with automatic choke and electronic idle-speed control. Understand the circuits before adjusting.
- **Why:** The carburettor mixes air and petrol with four circuits: the float bowl, the idle circuit (small jets feeding the idle mixture screw), the main circuit (main jet and emulsion tube for cruising), and the accelerator pump; the choke enriches the mixture when cold.
- **How:** (1) Fuel enters through the inlet filter and the needle valve, held by the float at a set height. (2) At idle the throttle is nearly closed and fuel comes from the idle jet through a hole below the throttle; the idle mixture screw sets how much (CO). (3) At part throttle the main jet and emulsion tube feed the primary barrel; the second barrel opens by a vacuum diaphragm (item 15 in the manual's Weber breakdown, p. 191) for power. (4) A diaphragm pump squirts petrol when the throttle opens (accelerator pump), and a power valve enriches under load. (5) The choke plate restricts air for a cold start; the vacuum "pull-down" diaphragm opens it slightly when the engine fires (item 4 in the manual's p. 190 figure). (6) The idle cut-off solenoid shuts the idle fuel when the ignition is off.
- **Verify:** You can name, on your carburettor, the inlet filter, float bowl, idle mixture screw, choke housing, pull-down diaphragm, accelerator pump cover and idle solenoid, using the manual's figures (printed p. 185 and 199).
- **Don't:** Don't remove the plastic anti-tamper cap of the mixture screw unless you will measure CO with an analyser afterwards.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 184-199 (Weber DMPV and Brosol 2E-CE specifications, parts and disassembly), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-13: Check air leaks, filters and the heat system before adjusting the carburettor
- **Rule:** Before an idle or mixture adjustment, make sure the air filter, fuel filter, ignition timing, vacuum hoses, manifold and carburettor gaskets, and the air-preheat flap are sound.
- **Why:** An air leak after the throttle leans the mixture and causes a high or hunting idle; adjusting the mixture screw to cover a leak gives a rich idle that fails the emissions test.
- **How:** (1) Replace a clogged paper air filter and the fuel filter. (2) With the engine idling, spray a little brake cleaner or carburettor cleaner (not starting fluid in quantity) around the carburettor base gasket, the manifold gaskets and the vacuum hoses; a change in idle speed shows a leak. Use a small amount and keep away from the exhaust manifold. (3) Check all vacuum hoses for splits, especially to the brake booster (*servofreno*), the vacuum advance and the choke pull-down. (4) A warped carburettor body or manifold flange (a known fault after overheating or over-tightening) leaks at the gasket even when it looks fine: check the flange on a flat surface with a feeler gauge; replace the gasket and flatten or replace the part. (5) Check the carburettor-to-manifold nuts for tightness; the gasket and the heat spacer (item 10 in the manual's p. 187 figure) must be new. (6) Check the engine's ignition timing (VEH-GRP-23) and the crankcase ventilation hose.
- **Verify:** Idle speed does not change when you spray around the joints; the manifold vacuum is steady.
- **Don't:** Don't spray flammable liquids near a hot manifold or the ignition leads, and don't tighten the carburettor nuts hard on an old gasket: you will warp the base.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 187-188 (air filter and carburettor exploded view: gasket, thermal deflector, spacer, intake and exhaust manifold), read 2026-11-15; a Brazilian forum thread on a 1.6 CHT idle loss (secondary) lists false air at the manifold and carburettor gaskets as the usual cause. Not verified in a primary source: the spray test is general practice.

### VEH-GRP-14: Adjust idle speed and mixture (CO) with a tachometer and a gas analyser
- **Rule:** Adjust idle speed and CO with the engine hot, the ignition timing correct, the choke open, the fan off and the electrical loads off, and measure CO with a gas analyser; do not adjust "by ear". The detailed step order for the Weber is VEH-GRP-62.
- **Why:** Idle speed and CO are the two settings that decide drivability, consumption and the inspection (VTV) emissions result; the mixture screw has a limited effect and the other faults must be removed first.
- **How:** (1) Conditions (from an analogous VW manual for the Golf carburettor engine): oil temperature at least 60 degrees C, radiator fan not running, all electrics off, choke valve fully open, no exhaust leaks, ignition timing correct. (2) Put the analyser probe in the tailpipe and a tachometer on the engine. (3) Reference values of the workshop manual: Weber DMPV 1.6 made until October 1991: idle 900-1000 rpm, CO 1-2%; Weber DMPV made from November 1991: idle 900-1000 rpm, CO 0.5-1.5%; Brosol 2E-CE (GL 1.8): idle 850-950 rpm, CO 0.5-1.5%. The GL data table (printed p. 12) gives idle 900 +/- 50 rpm for the 1.6 and 850 +/- 50 for the 1.8, CO 2%. For the owner-confirmed 1994 car (Weber DMPV from November 1991) the target is idle 900-1000 rpm and CO 0.5-1.5%. Use the range that matches your carburettor and compare with vw-gol-1994-specs.md. (4) Adjust the idle speed screw (throttle stop) to the speed; then turn the mixture screw (item 17 in the Brosol figure; the plastic cap, item 16, must be removed with a fine screwdriver) in small steps until CO is in the range; recheck the speed and repeat. (5) Refit a new anti-tamper cap. (6) Reconnect the crankcase breather hose (disconnected and plugged during the test in the analogous manual) and check CO again: it may rise if the oil is diluted with petrol from short trips; an oil change and a long drive bring it back.
- **Verify:** Analyser: CO in the table, idle in the table, and a steady idle for 5 minutes with the fan cycling and the headlamps on.
- **Don't:** Don't adjust the CO with a cold engine, don't set CO at 3% "for a smooth idle" and don't lean it until the engine hunts.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 184 (Weber DMPV to October 1991: idle 900-1000 rpm, CO 1-2%), p. 197 (Weber DMPV from November 1991: 900-1000 rpm, CO 0.5-1.5%), p. 198 (Brosol 2E-CE: 850-950 rpm, CO 0.5-1.5%), p. 12 (GL data: idle 900 +/- 50 and 850 +/- 50, CO 2%), p. 188 and 198 (mixture screw and plastic cap), read 2026-11-15. Conditions and method: VW Golf Mk1 workshop manual, "Adjusting idling speed" (Keihin carburettor), workshop-manuals.com, read 2026-11-15 (secondary: analogous engine family, not the Gol). The Gol manual shows the Weber's mixture screw as a tapered needle with a plastic cap (printed p. 188, Fig. V.69). Not verified in a primary source: the Gol manual pages read give the specifications but not the step-by-step adjustment text, and no Weber DMPV service guide was found on the web either (searches of 2026-11-15; Brazilian and Argentine forums describe the Weber 460 of the CHT engine and the 32/34 of the Fiat Duna, which are other carburettors); the order of steps for the DMPV is in VEH-GRP-62, from analogous Weber guides.

### VEH-GRP-15: Check the automatic choke (cold start device)
- **Rule:** Find out which choke your carburettor has, then check that the plate closes for a cold start, opens when the engine runs and warms, and is fully open when hot; check the vacuum pull-off diaphragm and its hose. The gap and positive-opening measurements are in VEH-GRP-64.
- **Why:** The Weber DMPV of the Gol GL 1.6 appears to be a manual choke: the Gacel/Senda owner's manual (same engine) tells the driver to pull the choke knob for a cold start and push it in when warm, the Gol workshop manual lists a "choke indicator" lamp (printed p. 283) and describes a choke lever with a pneumatic (vacuum) opening device (p. 189-190); the Brosol 2E-CE of the 1.8 has an automatic choke (p. 198). A choke that stays closed gives black smoke and high consumption; one that stays open gives a hard cold start and stalling.
- **How:** (1) Decide the type: a knob and cable on the dashboard with a warning lamp means the manual choke with a vacuum pull-off; no knob means automatic. (2) Cold engine, air filter lid off. Manual choke: with the knob pulled out the plate should be nearly closed and the cable should move the lever fully, with a little slack when the knob is pushed in; automatic: the plate is nearly closed when cold. (3) Start the engine: the vacuum diaphragm (item 4, p. 190) should open the plate a little. If you can, compare the gap with the manual (choke plate opening 3.7-4.3 mm for the Weber made from November 1991, 3.33-3.37 mm for the earlier series, 3-3.4 mm for the Brosol 2E-CE; VEH-GRP-64 has the method). (4) Warm up: on a manual choke push the knob in gradually as the engine warms (the warning lamp goes out); on an automatic choke the plate must be fully open within a few minutes, otherwise check its heating element, wire and fuse. (5) Check the hose between the diaphragm and the carburettor: a split hose stops the pull-off. (6) Check that the cable returns the lever fully when the knob is pushed in.
- **Verify:** Cold start without pumping the accelerator; fast idle that falls to normal as the engine warms; fully open plate when hot (knob in, lamp out).
- **Don't:** Don't wedge the choke open to "fix" a hard start, and don't drive with the choke on after the first minute (the owner's manual says to push it in as soon as the engine runs evenly).
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 184, 197, 198 (choke plate opening values), p. 189-190 (choke lever, pneumatic opening device, lever lock), p. 283 (lamp table: choke indicator, LED), read 2026-11-15; gacel-senda-1990-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, PDF p. 39-40 (cold start with the choke knob pulled out, pushed in as the engine warms; the controls index on PDF p. 14-15 lists the choke control and its warning light, manual p. 73) , read 2026-11-15; gol-1992-manual-proprietario-pt-br.pdf, PDF p. 24 (starting: cars with manual choke and cars with automatic choke), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: that every 1994 Argentine Gol GL 1.6 has the knob (look at the car); the checks of this rule are general practice, the heating element of an automatic choke is not described in the pages read.

### VEH-GRP-16: Test the accelerator pump
- **Rule:** Check the accelerator pump delivery with the air filter off, into a measuring jar, and replace the diaphragm if it is below the manual's range.
- **Why:** A weak pump gives a hesitation or stumble when the throttle is opened. The diaphragm hardens with age and with ethanol or varnish in the petrol.
- **How:** (1) Remove the air filter lid. (2) With the engine off and the bowl full, hold a jar under the pump jet (the injector tube, item 14 in the manual's Brosol figure) and open the throttle fully five times, slowly. (3) Compare with the manual's delivery per 5 strokes: Weber DMPV earlier series 3.65-3.85 cm3, Weber from November 1991 2.25-3.75 cm3, Brosol 2E-CE 2.25-4.25 cm3. (4) Look at the jet pattern: it must be one straight jet, not a dribble. (5) To repair: remove the four screws of the pump cover (item 16 in the manual's p. 192 figure), take out the diaphragm and spring, fit the new diaphragm from the rebuild kit and check the check valve.
- **Verify:** The delivery in the range and a clean jet; no hesitation on opening the throttle.
- **Don't:** Don't bend the pump linkage to change the delivery; adjust it as the manual states.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 184 (3.65-3.85 cm3 per 5 strokes), p. 197 (2.25-3.75 cm3), p. 198 (2.25-4.25 cm3), p. 191-192 (pump disassembly: four screws, diaphragm, spring), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-17: Clean and rebuild the carburettor with the right kit and a float check
- **Rule:** Clean the carburettor with a spray cleaner and compressed air, fit a rebuild kit (gaskets and diaphragms), and set the float level to the manual's value; do not poke jets with wire.
- **Why:** After a car has stood, varnish from old petrol blocks the idle and main jets. A rebuild gives back the original calibration; a wire enlarges a jet and makes the mixture rich.
- **How:** (1) Work with the carburettor removed on a clean bench; disconnect the fuel and vacuum hoses, the throttle cable and the choke connections after marking them. (2) Disassembly order in the manual (printed p. 188-193): remove the idle mixture screw and the inlet filter, the choke lever lock, the pneumatic choke device, the top cover, the float and needle valve, the jets (idle air jet, idle jet, main air and main jets, emulsion tube), the second-barrel diaphragm, the accelerator pump cover, the power valve cover, the solenoid and the secondary diffusers. (3) Wash every part in carburettor cleaner (not petrol), blow through the passages, never pass a wire through a calibrated jet. (4) Refit with the kit's gaskets and diaphragms; float height: Weber 8-9 mm, Brosol 2E-CE 26.5-28.5 mm (as the manual defines; how to measure and bend it is in VEH-GRP-63); needle valve: new. (5) Refit the carburettor with a new base gasket and spacer; refit the heat deflector (item 9 in the manual's p. 187 figure). (6) Adjust idle and CO (VEH-GRP-14). Typical kit: gaskets, accelerator pump diaphragm, power valve diaphragm, needle valve, inlet filter.
- **Verify:** Starts, idles steadily and accelerates cleanly; no petrol seeps from the cover joints.
- **Don't:** Don't mix parts of different carburettor versions: the Weber made until October 1991 and from November 1991 have different jets and diffusers.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 184 and 197 (Weber jets, float height 8-9 mm), p. 198 (Brosol 2E-CE float height 26.5-28.5 mm), p. 188-193 (disassembly sequence), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the content of rebuild kits is general practice.

### VEH-GRP-18: Warped body, worn throttle shafts and carburettor replacement
- **Rule:** If the carburettor still gives a wandering idle after cleaning and a rebuild, check for a warped base, worn throttle spindles and a cracked body; replace the carburettor with the same model rather than adapting another.
- **Why:** An aluminium or zinc carburettor warps when overheated or tightened unevenly, and worn spindles let air past the throttle; the idle then cannot be set.
- **How:** (1) Check the base flange with a straight edge and feeler gauge: any gap that a 0.05 mm blade enters is a leak. (2) Wobble the throttle spindle: a lot of side play means air leaks. (3) A forum note on carburettors ruined by alcohol fuel (secondary, Brazil) warns that a corroded body cannot be saved. (4) Fit a carburettor of the same family: the 2E (Brosol, Solex) in Brazilian catalogues are sold for AP 1.6, 1.8 and 2.0, but jets and the electronic idle control differ; match the part number to your car. (5) Reset the idle speed and CO with the analyser after the swap.
- **Verify:** No change in idle when spraying around the base, a stable idle after adjustment.
- **Don't:** Don't buy a carburettor only by "fits an AP"; the 1.6 and the 1.8 carburettors are different.
- **Source:** Not verified in a primary source: forum threads (4x4 Brasil, Brazil, 2004, secondary) and marketplace listings of carburettors for the AP; the manual (printed p. 184-199, read 2026-11-15) gives the model per engine.

## Ignition

### VEH-GRP-19: Know the ignition layout and test it in the right order
- **Rule:** The carburetted AP in the manual has an electronic ignition: battery, ignition switch, coil, a separate ignition module (six-pin) and a distributor with a pickup coil (inductive sender); test in that order: supply, module, pickup, coil, leads, plugs.
- **Why:** Most "no spark" and "misfire" faults are a loose connector, a tired coil or old leads, not the distributor itself; testing in order avoids replacing the module for nothing.
- **How:** (1) Circuit in the manual (Fig. V.80, printed p. 194): battery - ignition switch (terminal 15) - coil + terminal; coil - terminal 1 to the module; module terminals 1-6 with earth on terminal 4 (earth the module case); distributor connected to module terminals. (2) Check battery voltage and the clean earth strap first. (3) With the ignition on, measure the coil + terminal: it should read battery voltage. (4) Check the pickup coil and the air gap (VEH-GRP-22), then the coil (VEH-GRP-21), then the leads and plugs (VEH-GRP-20). (5) Hall-sender versions (fitted to other VW engines and later cars) are checked differently: with the plug disconnected, supply at least 10 V at the outer contacts and a switching signal of 0 V to at least 2 V when the engine is turned; renew the sender if there is none. (6) A car that has contact breaker points (older engines) is not described in the manual; do not use the electronic values on it.
- **Verify:** Strong blue spark on all four leads when cranking, and the engine runs smoothly at idle and 3000 rpm.
- **Don't:** Don't disconnect a plug lead with the engine running to "test", it can damage the module; don't ground the coil terminal for long.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 194-196 (ignition circuit Fig. V.80 with module, coil, distributor, switch; distributor drawing), read 2026-11-15. Hall-sender method: VW Golf Mk1 workshop manual, "Checking Hall sender" and "Checking TCI-H ignition system" (workshop-manuals.com, read 2026-11-15, secondary: analogous engine family, voltages of the Golf Motronic/TCI-H systems, not of the Gol).

### VEH-GRP-20: Spark plugs and leads: type, gap and resistance
- **Rule:** Fit plugs of the manual's types with the manual's gap and check each lead's resistance; replace the set if a lead is out of range.
- **Why:** Worn plugs and leads cause hard starts, hesitation and high consumption; a wrong heat range fouls or overheats.
- **How:** (1) Plug types in the manual for the Gol CL 1.6 carburettor engine: Autolatina VW N32 RC, Bosch W8D or NGK BCPR5ES; thread 14 mm; electrode gap 0.6-0.8 mm. (2) Remove the lead by the boot, not by the cable. (3) Lead resistance with a multimeter: the manual gives 2800-4400 ohm for the plug leads and 1200-2800 ohm for the coil lead. (4) Use a feeler gauge for the gap, bend only the outer electrode. (5) Tighten to the torque of vw-gol-1994-specs.md; a hand-tight plug plus a quarter turn on a new copper-gasket plug is a common rule if you have no torque wrench (general practice). (6) Fit leads in firing order 1-3-4-2.
- **Verify:** Plug tips are light brown; leads are within the range; the engine does not misfire in the dark with the bonnet open.
- **Don't:** Don't use a hotter or colder plug "for power"; don't use a metal-core racing lead on this ignition module.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 196 (plugs: VW N32 RC, Bosch W8D, NGK BCPR5ES, 14 mm, gap 0.6-0.8 mm; HT leads resistance), p. 11 (firing order 1-3-4-2), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the quarter-turn rule.

### VEH-GRP-21: Test the ignition coil
- **Rule:** Measure the coil primary and secondary resistance with the ignition off; replace the coil if it is outside the manual's range or leaks.
- **Why:** A coil with shorted windings gives a weak spark when hot and a stall at idle; resistance readings find most of the faults.
- **How:** (1) Disconnect the battery and the coil connectors. (2) Primary resistance (between terminals 1 and 15): 1.9-2.1 ohm (manual). (3) Secondary resistance (between the primary + terminal and the HT tower): 5500-7500 ohm. (4) Look for cracks, oil, burnt smell or carbon tracks at the tower. (5) Test with the coil warm: a coil that is within range cold but fails after 10 minutes of running is replaced. (6) Check the earth wire of the coil bracket and the coil connection to the module.
- **Verify:** Resistances in range; a 10 mm blue spark when cranking; no stall when hot.
- **Don't:** Don't connect a 12 V ignition coil with an external ballast resistor wiring the manual does not show; use the same coil type.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 196 (coil: primary 1.9-2.1 ohm, secondary 5500-7500 ohm), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-22: Test the pickup coil, air gap and module
- **Rule:** Measure the distributor pickup coil and the air gap between the rotor and the reluctor; if both are right, suspect the module.
- **Why:** The module is the most expensive part; a pickup coil with a wrong gap or a broken wire gives the same "no spark" symptom.
- **How:** (1) Disconnect the pickup connector at the distributor. (2) Pickup coil resistance: 990-1210 ohm (manual, Bosch 86AU-12100-H). (3) Air gap between the rotor and the reluctor (pickup coil to reluctor teeth): 0.2-0.6 mm; check with a non-magnetic feeler (brass or plastic). (4) Wiggle the wires while measuring: a resistance that jumps is a broken wire. (5) Check supply at the module (battery voltage on its feed) and earth; if resistances are right and there is no spark, replace the module with the same part number. (6) A test with a spare known-good module is the most practical way for an owner.
- **Verify:** Resistance 990-1210 ohm; air gap 0.2-0.6 mm; spark at the coil lead when cranking.
- **Don't:** Don't use a steel feeler gauge on a magnetic pickup; don't assume the module is bad before checking the earth.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 196 (Bosch 86AU-12100-H distributor: pickup coil 990-1210 ohm, gap between rotor and reluctor 0.2-0.6 mm, rotation clockwise), p. 194 (circuit), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-23: Set the ignition timing with a timing light
- **Rule:** Check the ignition timing with a stroboscopic lamp at idle with the engine hot, and adjust by turning the distributor; use the value for your engine.
- **Why:** Late timing gives a sluggish engine and a hot exhaust; early timing gives knock and starter kickback. The timing also changes the CO and the idle speed.
- **How:** (1) Hot engine, idle speed correct, choke open. (2) Clip the lamp to the no. 1 plug lead. (3) Find the timing mark on the crankshaft pulley or flywheel (the manual locates TDC with the pulley notch against the arrow on the lower timing cover, or the "OT" mark on the flywheel, printed p. 59-61 and 66). (4) General practice: disconnect and plug the vacuum-advance hose before the test. (5) Point the lamp at the mark and compare with the specification: the manual's table for the GL gives an initial advance of 9 +/- 0.5 degrees for the 1.6 and 1.8 (p. 12), while the distributor data of the 1.6 CL gives an initial advance of 13 degrees (p. 196); the two numbers differ, so follow the label on your engine or vw-gol-1994-specs.md. (6) Loosen the distributor clamp nut and turn the body slightly until the mark aligns, tighten and recheck. (7) Check that the advance increases as the engine speeds up and that the vacuum advance works (reconnect the hose).
- **Verify:** The mark aligns at the specified value at idle and moves forward when the engine speeds up; the engine idles steadily.
- **Don't:** Don't set the timing by ear or by "the best idle" with a worn distributor; don't forget to reconnect the vacuum hose.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 12 (initial advance 9 +/- 0.5 degrees, GL 1.6 and 1.8), p. 196 (initial advance 13 degrees, distributor of the CL 1.6), p. 59-61 and 66-67 (TDC marks), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the exact position of the degree marks and the vacuum-hose step; the two advance figures conflict within the manual.

## Cooling

### VEH-GRP-24: Pressure test the cooling system and the cap
- **Rule:** Pressure test the system at the expansion tank with a hand pump and a gauge, and test the cap.
- **Why:** A small leak that shows only under pressure is the usual reason for a coolant level that drops; a weak cap lets the coolant boil early.
- **How:** (1) Engine cold. (2) Remove the expansion tank cap and fit the tester with an adapter (the manual shows it on the tank). (3) Pump to 1 kgf/cm2 (about 1 bar): the pressure must not fall (manual). (4) If it falls, look for the leak: hose joints, radiator seams, water pump flange and weep hole, heater hoses, thermostat flange and the head gasket. (5) Test the cap on the tester: Golf manual cap opening pressures 0.9-1.5 bar by part number (analogous engine family); use the cap for your Gol. (6) A cap that fails the test is replaced.
- **Verify:** The gauge holds 1 kgf/cm2 for several minutes.
- **Don't:** Don't pump above the cap's rating; don't pressure-test a hot engine.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 76 (pressure test: 1 kgf, system sound if the pressure does not fall), read 2026-11-15. Cap pressures: VW Golf Mk1 workshop manual, "Checking cooling system and cap", workshop-manuals.com, read 2026-11-15 (secondary: Golf values, not the Gol).

### VEH-GRP-25: Check and replace the thermostat
- **Rule:** Test the thermostat in hot water and replace it if it does not open at the manual's temperature or does not open fully.
- **Why:** A thermostat that sticks closed overheats the engine; one that sticks open keeps the engine cold, so the heater is weak and the carburettor choke stays on.
- **How:** (1) Drain the coolant to below the thermostat housing (drain into a clean container to reuse good coolant). (2) Remove the water pump flange (the thermostat sits in it) and take out the thermostat and its sealing ring (manual, Fig. II.85). (3) Hang the thermostat in a pan of coolant with a thermometer and heat slowly: it should begin to open at 85-89 degrees C and be fully open by 100-104 degrees C; the lift between closed and open is measured (a = 31 mm closed, b = 37 mm open, manual). (4) Fit a new thermostat with a new ring, the spring side towards the engine. (5) Refill and bleed (VEH-GRP-28). (6) Replace the thermostat when you replace the water pump or the timing belt.
- **Verify:** The upper hose is hot after about 10 minutes of idling and the fan cycles; the gauge settles in the middle.
- **Don't:** Don't test it in an open flame, and don't leave it out.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 80 (thermostat: opening starts at 85-89 degrees C, ends at 100-104 degrees C; a = 31 mm, b = 37 mm), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the spring direction (general practice, check the part's markings).

### VEH-GRP-26: Check the fan switch, the fan and its fuse
- **Rule:** Check that the electric fan runs when the engine is hot: test the thermoswitch, the fan motor and the fuse.
- **Why:** The Gol's electric fan is the main reason for overheating in traffic when the car is stationary.
- **How:** (1) Run the engine to temperature at idle: the fan should start when the gauge is about two-thirds (thermoswitch closes at 90-95 degrees C and opens at 85-90 degrees C, manual for a car without air conditioning). (2) If the fan does not run, check fuse 20 (30 A) in the Gol 1.6 fuse table. (3) Disconnect the thermoswitch connector at the radiator and join the two terminals with a wire: the fan must start (ignition on); then the switch is bad. (4) If the fan does not run with the wire, check the fan motor with 12 V direct and the earth. (5) The switch screws into the radiator or the thermostat housing with a 30 mm hexagonal socket (manual). (6) After replacing the switch, refill the coolant and bleed.
- **Verify:** The fan starts at the right temperature, and the temperature gauge stays below the red zone in a 10-minute idle in traffic.
- **Don't:** Don't wire the fan directly to the battery permanently; it will drain the battery and the fan will not stop.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 76-77 (thermoswitch removal with a 30 mm socket; connects 90-95 degrees C, disconnects 85-90 degrees C), p. 275 (fuse 20, 30 A, thermoswitch/fan), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the jumper-wire test of the switch is general practice.

### VEH-GRP-27: Replace the water pump (with the timing belt)
- **Rule:** Replace the water pump whenever the timing belt is replaced, or at once if it leaks, is noisy or has play.
- **Why:** The pump sits behind the timing area on the AP; if it fails after a belt change you must repeat the work, and a seized pump can destroy the belt.
- **How:** (1) Drain the coolant. (2) Remove the accessory belts and the timing belt (VEH-GRP-30). (3) Remove the pump and its sealing ring from the block (a new ring each time). (4) Clean the mating faces; the article on the AP 1.6 says no liquid sealant is needed. (5) Fit the new pump with a new gasket/ring, tighten the bolts gradually and in a crossed pattern, spin the pulley by hand: it must turn freely. (6) Refit the thermostat flange with a new ring (VEH-GRP-25). (7) Refit and tension the timing belt (VEH-GRP-30), refill the coolant, run the engine and bleed the air.
- **Verify:** No leak at the pump after 10 minutes of idling and after a short drive; the pump weep hole is dry.
- **Don't:** Don't put sealant on the pump ring, and don't reuse the old ring.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 79-80 (water pump and thermostat flange; the chapter index lists "Bomba de agua" p. 79), read 2026-11-15; O Mecanico, "Saiba como trocar da bomba d'agua do motor AP 1.6 litro 8V" (secondary, Brazil, read 2026-11-15: steps and the "no liquid sealant" remark; it gives no torque or marks). Not verified in a primary source: pump torque values.

### VEH-GRP-28: Hoses, radiator, expansion tank and coolant
- **Rule:** Check the hoses, the radiator and the expansion tank every service, replace the coolant every two years, and bleed the air after any work.
- **Why:** The AP overheats when its hoses collapse inside or the radiator is clogged; old coolant corrodes the aluminium head and pump.
- **How:** (1) Cold: squeeze each hose; replace those that are soft, cracked, swollen or shiny. (2) Look for white or green crust at hose ends, pump, radiator seams and thermostat flange. (3) Radiator: a lower-than-normal temperature difference between top and bottom (more than 20 degrees C in a hot engine) with a hot top hose means a blocked core: have it cleaned or recored. (4) Coolant: drain by the bottom radiator hose; the manual gives the system capacity (6.2 litres for the GL) in the capacities table; refill with the coolant type of vw-gol-1994-specs.md, in the specified mix. (5) Bleed: with the heater on hot, run the engine with the expansion tank cap off until the fan has cycled, top up to the mark. (6) Check the cap (VEH-GRP-24).
- **Verify:** Level between marks cold, no leaks, heater hot, gauge in the middle.
- **Don't:** Don't use plain water in the long term; don't mix different coolant types.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 13 (cooling system capacity 6.2 litres, GL), p. 71-80 (cooling system, radiator, pump, thermostat), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the 20-degree C top-to-bottom rule is general practice.

### VEH-GRP-29: Recognise head gasket failure and know the outline of the job
- **Rule:** If the engine has milky oil, white smoke with a sweet smell, bubbles in the expansion tank, coolant loss with no leak or a pressurised system after the engine is off, test for a head gasket leak and, if confirmed, have the head removed, checked for flatness and re-fitted with a new gasket and new bolts.
- **Why:** The AP overheats readily and the aluminium head warps; a gasket that leaks between a cylinder and a water passage ruins bearings if ignored.
- **How:** Tests: (1) a combustion-gas (block) tester on the coolant; (2) a compression test on all four cylinders, then compare, with a wet test; (3) a cooling-system pressure test that falls with no visible leak. Job outline (manual, printed p. 63-67): (a) disconnect the battery earth, drain the coolant, disconnect electrics, hoses, the air filter and accelerator cable, the front exhaust pipe, the alternator adjuster arm and the V-belt; (b) remove the timing belt top cover, valve cover and deflector and release the toothed belt from the camshaft sprocket with the engine at TDC marks; (c) loosen the 10 head bolts in the order of Fig. II.63; (d) lift the head and gasket; (e) check head flatness (maximum 0.10 mm) and height (133 +/- 0.1 mm, do not machine below 132.6 mm); (f) fit the new gasket with the "OBEN/TOP" marking up and the guide pins; (g) tighten the new head bolts in the order of Fig. II.71 in three stages: 40 Nm, 60 Nm and then 180 degrees; (h) refit the cam sprocket, belt, timing marks, covers, refill with coolant and run. This is a job for a mechanic with a torque wrench and an angle gauge unless you have done engine work.
- **Verify:** Compression is even, the coolant level is stable, no white smoke, oil is clear after the oil change that follows.
- **Don't:** Don't re-use head bolts or the old gasket, don't skim the head below the minimum height, and don't torque the bolts in any order but the manual's.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 55 (head bolts first tightening 40 Nm, second 60 Nm, third 180 degrees), p. 63 (removal sequence Fig. II.63 and preparation list), p. 64 (flatness max 0.10 mm; head height A = 133 +/- 0.1 mm; no regrinding below 132.6 mm), p. 66 (gasket with "OBEN/TOP" up), p. 67 (tightening sequence Fig. II.71), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the gas-test and wet-test steps are general practice.

## Engine mechanical

### VEH-GRP-30: Replace the timing belt: marks, tensioner and torque
- **Rule:** Replace the toothed timing belt (*correa dentada*), the tensioner roller and the water pump together at the interval of vw-gol-1994-specs.md (and always after a year or more of standing with an unknown history), setting the marks exactly.
- **Why:** The AP belt drives the camshaft and the intermediate shaft (distributor and oil pump). If it breaks or jumps a tooth the engine stops; on a worn engine valves can also touch the pistons. A new belt with a bad mark gives a loss of power or backfire.
- **How:** (1) Disconnect the battery, remove the accessory belts (VEH-GRP-31) and the upper timing cover. (2) Turn the crankshaft by hand (bolt on the pulley, in the direction of rotation) until the pulley notch is at the arrow on the lower timing cover (or the flywheel mark at TDC) with cylinder 1 on compression: the cam lobes of cylinder 1 point up and the distributor rotor points at the mark on the distributor housing. (3) Loosen the tensioner, remove the old belt, remove the lower cover as needed. (4) Fit the new belt on the crankshaft, intermediate shaft and camshaft sprockets in that order, keeping the marks; check that the crankshaft pulley mark and the intermediate shaft sprocket mark (the manual shows the check on Fig. II.58-59) and the camshaft mark are all aligned. (5) Tension: turn the tensioner roller clockwise with its eccentric until the belt, twisted by hand midway between the camshaft and intermediate sprockets, only just turns through about 90 degrees (the Golf carburettor manual's method; the Gol manual says "se torsiona la correa"); tighten the roller nut to 45 Nm. (6) Turn the crankshaft two full turns by hand and check the marks again; (7) Refit the covers, accessory belts and check the ignition timing (VEH-GRP-23). Other torque values in the manual's p. 53 figure: camshaft sprocket 80 Nm, intermediate shaft sprocket 80 Nm.
- **Verify:** After two turns all the marks align; the engine starts and idles smoothly; recheck the belt tension after 500 km.
- **Don't:** Don't turn the camshaft with the belt off when a piston is at TDC; don't re-use an old tensioner; don't use sealant on the belt covers.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 53 (timing mechanism exploded view with torques: tensioner 45 Nm, camshaft sprocket 80 Nm, intermediate shaft sprocket 80 Nm), p. 59-61 (TDC marks, synchronism check of the crankshaft and intermediate shaft, rotor to distributor mark; tensioning by rotating the roller clockwise and twisting the belt), p. 66-67 (marks when refitting), read 2026-11-15. The 90-degree twist test and the two-turn check: VW Golf Mk1 workshop manual, "Installing toothed belt" (carburettor 1.5/1.6/1.8 engine), workshop-manuals.com, read 2026-11-15 (secondary: analogous engine family).

### VEH-GRP-31: Accessory belt: the V-belt and the Poly-V
- **Rule:** Check the alternator/pump belt for cracks, glazing and tension at every service, and replace it with a belt of the correct type.
- **Why:** A loose belt squeals, undercharges the battery and drives the pump poorly; a broken one stops the cooling and charging at once.
- **How:** (1) V-belt (trapezoidal): loosen the alternator bracket bolts, the adjuster arm bolt and the belt adjusting screw; turn the adjusting screw clockwise to tension the belt with a torque wrench: 10 Nm for a new belt, 5 Nm for a used one (manual). (2) Tighten: alternator bracket 35 Nm, adjuster arm 25 Nm, belt adjusting screw 35 Nm. (3) Run the engine 5 minutes and recheck. (4) Poly-V belt (cars with power steering or air conditioning): the tensioner pulley is held by a bolt that needs an 8 mm Allen key with a 220 mm extension (manual); the belt path differs with and without air conditioning (manual figures). (5) Replace any belt with cracks, missing ribs or oil contamination.
- **Verify:** No squeal at start or in the wet; the charging warning light is off at idle with the lights on.
- **Don't:** Don't tighten the belt by prying on the alternator by hand with a pry bar on the rear housing; don't spray belt dressing.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 49-52 (V-belt adjustment: 10 Nm new / 5 Nm used; torques 35 / 25 / 35 Nm; Poly-V tensioner; belt routing figures), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-32: Replace the valve cover gasket
- **Rule:** Replace the valve cover (*tapa de válvulas*) gasket when oil seeps at its edge or the plug wells are oily; clean the faces and tighten evenly.
- **Why:** The cork or rubber gasket hardens with heat; the leak makes the engine bay oily and the smell reaches the cabin.
- **How:** (1) Cold engine, battery disconnected. (2) Remove the air filter with the crankcase vent hose, the plug leads and their bracket, and the upper timing belt cover; note the accelerator cable and the hoses. (3) Remove the cover nuts, lift the cover (a metal cover over a plastic deflector in the manual's p. 54 figure: items 12 cover, 14 gasket, 15 plastic deflector). (4) Clean the head and cover faces with a scraper and a rag; do not let dirt fall into the engine. (5) Fit the new gasket dry or with a thin film of sealer at the half-moon joints as the part instructs; refit the cover and tighten the nuts evenly in a cross pattern, a little at a time. (6) Check the plug wells for oil and replace their seals if oily. (7) Run the engine and check.
- **Verify:** Dry cover edge after 100 km; clean plug wells.
- **Don't:** Don't overtighten the nuts (the cover warps and leaks again), and don't leave the plastic deflector out.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 54-55 (valve cover assembly: cover, gasket, plastic deflector), p. 57 (removal sequence for the camshaft job: air filter with crankcase valve, plug cable support, upper timing cover), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the tightening procedure and torque (check vw-gol-1994-specs.md).

### VEH-GRP-33: Valve clearance and tappet noise
- **Rule:** For tappet noise first find out whether your engine has mechanical tappets (needs a valve clearance check) or hydraulic tappets (needs the hydraulic check), then do the check for that type.
- **Why:** The manual lists valve clearance values for the GL (mechanical tappets: a cold clearance for each valve), while the injected AP engines use hydraulic tappets with a different test. Hydraulic tappets rattle when oil is dirty, low or aerated.
- **How:** Mechanical tappets: (1) the manual's cold values for the GL 1.6 and 1.8: intake 0.15-0.25 mm, exhaust 0.35-0.45 mm; hot: 0.20-0.30 and 0.40-0.50 mm. (2) Engine cold, cylinder at the base circle of the cam, feeler gauge between the cam and the bucket; adjust as the engine's method requires (the manual pages read do not describe the shim or adjuster method; ask a mechanic). Hydraulic tappets (manual p. 56-57): (1) run the engine until the fan starts, then at about 2500 rpm for 2 minutes; (2) remove the air filter, the plug lead bracket and the upper timing cover, the valve cover and the deflector; (3) turn the engine until the cam lobes of the cylinder to be checked point up; (4) push the tappet down with a wooden stick for no more than 5 seconds; (5) measure the gap between the tappet and the cam; if the tappet yields more than 0.1 mm, replace it; repeat for all cylinders. Before replacing tappets change the oil and filter and check the oil level and pressure.
- **Verify:** The ticking stops when warm and the clearance or yield is within the numbers above.
- **Don't:** Don't adjust clearance on a hot engine to the cold values, and don't replace hydraulic tappets before an oil change.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 11 and 12 (GL valve clearance with mechanical tappets: cold 0.15-0.25 intake and 0.35-0.45 exhaust; hot 0.20-0.30 and 0.40-0.50), p. 56-57 (hydraulic tappet check, more than 0.1 mm yield means replace), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: whether a given carburetted Gol 1994 engine has mechanical or hydraulic tappets; check with the engine data in vw-gol-1994-specs.md.

### VEH-GRP-34: Crankshaft and intermediate shaft seals (belt side)
- **Rule:** Replace a leaking crankshaft seal (pulley side) or intermediate shaft seal only with the timing belt off and with the right installer; replace the belt kit at the same time.
- **Why:** Oil that reaches the toothed belt softens it and the belt slips or breaks.
- **How:** (1) Remove the belt covers and the timing belt (VEH-GRP-30). (2) Crankshaft: remove the crankshaft sprocket bolt (12-point head) by locking the crankshaft with the two-bolt holding tool on the pulley holes, pull off the sprocket and remove the seal (manual Fig. II.42). (3) Intermediate shaft: lock the sprocket, remove its bolt, the sprocket, the flange, then prise the seal out with a screwdriver (Fig. II.57); fit a new seal with a little oil on the lip and a flange gasket. (4) Fit the seals dry-lipped, square, with a tube of the correct diameter. (5) Refit the sprockets (the manual gives 25 Nm for the intermediate flange screws and 80 Nm for the sprockets bolts in its p. 53 figure) and the belt with the marks (VEH-GRP-30).
- **Verify:** Dry area behind the belt cover after 200 km.
- **Don't:** Don't refit an oily belt; replace it.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 49 (crankshaft seal, pulley side: remove the belt, the 12-point bolt, holding tool, seal), p. 60 (intermediate shaft seal), p. 53 (torques), read 2026-11-15.

## Clutch and gearbox linkage

### VEH-GRP-35: Clutch cable failures: adjust, lubricate, replace
- **Rule:** Check the clutch pedal height and free play every service; replace the cable when it is frayed, sticks or the pedal is stiff or low.
- **Why:** The Gol clutch is cable-operated. The cable stretches and eventually breaks at the pedal end or at the fork lever, usually when you press the pedal.
- **How:** (1) Pedal height: the clutch pedal should be level with the brake pedal or up to 10 mm below it, with the brake pedal as a reference (manual); adjust with the nut and lock nut on the cable at the gearbox end. (2) Symptoms of a failing cable: hard pedal, a clutch that does not release (grinding when selecting first), a pedal that stays on the floor. (3) Replacement (manual): disconnect the battery earth, remove the access cover of the fuse box and lower the fuse box, remove the cable's safety clip at the pedal, loosen the lock nut and adjusting nut, release the cable from the fork lever and from the left engine mount bracket, pull it out toward the front; fit the new cable routed the same way with the ends greased with lithium grease; adjust the pedal height. (4) The fork lever nut tightens to 25 Nm. (5) A pedal bush (plastic, rubber) that is worn gives free play: replace it and lubricate the plastic bush with neutral soap when fitting.
- **Verify:** Smooth pedal, no grinding into first at rest, pedal returns fully, no slip in fifth under load.
- **Don't:** Don't ride the pedal; don't route the cable close to the exhaust.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 81-87 (clutch exploded view with cable and pedal; pedal bush replacement; pedal height; cable replacement; fork lever nut 25 Nm), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-36: Loose gear lever ("palanca floja"): bushes and linkage
- **Rule:** If the gear lever is loose or vague, inspect and replace the rubber bushes and stops of the lever and its control rod, and check the engine and gearbox mounts.
- **Why:** The lever is connected to the gearbox by a control rod with bushes and a ball support; they wear and give a loose, imprecise feel. A worn mount adds a clunk.
- **How:** (1) Remove the gear knob, bellows and console (manual: knob, then bellows, then console). (2) Remove the circlip and the stop and spring, then the bolts of the ball support and its base, lift the support and slide the lever back and up; the lever then comes off the control rod. (3) Replace the worn bushes (items 23 and others in the p. 93 list), the stops and the circlip (always use a new circlip). (4) Lubricate the bushes and stops with lithium grease. (5) Check the control rod coupling and the left and right rod supports for play. (6) Check the gearbox mount (the manual shows the transmission insulator mount at p. 94) and the engine mounts.
- **Verify:** Lever without slop in neutral, positive engagement of all five gears, no clunk on and off throttle.
- **Don't:** Don't shorten the rod or tighten the support to take out play; replace the bushes.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 93 (gear lever and control rod: bushes, stops, circlip, grease), p. 94 (transmission insulator mount), read 2026-11-15.

## Brakes

### VEH-GRP-37: Front pads and discs
- **Rule:** Replace the front brake pads (*pastillas*) in pairs on the same axle, check the discs for wear and run-out, and bed the pads gently.
- **Why:** The front brakes do most of the braking; worn pads score the disc and lengthen the stopping distance.
- **How:** (1) Loosen the wheel bolts, lift and support the car on stands, remove the wheel. (2) Remove the pad clamp (retaining spring) and the two caliper guide bolts; lift the caliper off the disc and hang it on a wire without bending the hose (manual). (3) Remove the pads and measure their wear: the manual gives a limit of 9.8 mm (it does not say whether the backing plate is counted; most workshops measure the whole pad, so replace at or near that). (4) Push the piston back into the caliper (a clamp or the old pad) with the reservoir cap off, watching the level. (5) Fit the new pads, the clamp and the caliper; guide bolts 40 Nm, wheel bolts 100 Nm (manual). (6) Disc: check run-out on the car with a dial gauge: maximum 0.06 mm; if you regrind the disc, the minimum thickness after grinding is 10.8 mm (manual). (7) Press the pedal several times until it is firm before moving, then bed the pads with gentle stops.
- **Verify:** Pedal firm, no pull to one side, no heat difference between the front wheels after a drive, no squeal.
- **Don't:** Don't change one side only, don't get grease on the friction face, don't let the caliper hang by the hose, and don't use a disc below minimum thickness.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 200 (pads: limit 9.8 mm, replace in pairs, guide bolts 40 Nm, wheel bolts 100 Nm), p. 201 (disc: run-out 0.06 mm, minimum thickness after regrinding 10.8 mm), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-38: Rear shoes, drums, wheel bearing and the self-adjuster
- **Rule:** Inspect the rear drums (*campanas*), shoes (*zapatas*) and wheel cylinders at every pad change; replace linings in pairs, set the wheel bearing and check the handbrake.
- **Why:** The rear drum brakes of the Gol have a self-adjusting wedge. After standing they seize and the rear wheel drags or locks; a leaking wheel cylinder contaminates the lining.
- **How:** (1) Remove the wheel, hub cap, split pin (*chaveta*), castellated nut, washer and outer bearing; pull the drum (if it will not come, back off the handbrake adjustment). (2) Measure the drum: nominal 180.00 mm, wear limit 181.00 mm, maximum out of round 0.15 mm (manual). (3) Remove the shoes: take off the retaining pins and springs, unhook the lower return spring, release the handbrake cable from the lever, take off the traction spring of the wedge and the upper return springs with pliers (manual p. 202-206). (4) Check the wheel cylinder for fluid leaks behind the dust boot: leaking = replace (the bolt is removed with a 5 mm Allen key and refitted to 10 Nm; the brake pipe is plugged to avoid losing fluid). (5) Refit in reverse: new shoes in pairs, upper internal return spring and the support rod, wedge with its lug toward the backplate (the manual's Fig. VI.7), lower spring, drum. (6) Bearing: tighten the nut to 30 Nm while turning the wheel to seat the bearing, then adjust the play (manual), fit a new split pin. (7) Wheel bolts 100 Nm. (8) Adjust the handbrake (VEH-GRP-40).
- **Verify:** The wheel turns freely with a little shoe whisper, the drum is not hot after a drive, the handbrake holds on a slope.
- **Don't:** Don't clean brake dust with a dry brush or compressed air and breathe it; don't reuse a split pin; don't leave a leaking wheel cylinder.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 201-208 (rear brake exploded view with wedge, shoe removal and installation, relining in pairs, bearing nut 30 Nm, wheel bolts 100 Nm, drum 180.00 mm and wear limit 181.00 mm, wheel cylinder with 10 Nm bolt), read 2026-11-15. Brake dust is hazardous (asbestos in some old linings): not verified in a primary source, general precaution.

### VEH-GRP-39: Bleed the brakes and change the fluid in the manual's order
- **Rule:** Bleed the brakes starting with the rear right wheel, then rear left, front right, front left, with a clear tube and a helper; change the fluid every two years with DOT 4.
- **Why:** The system has a diagonal split circuit; air gives a spongy pedal, and old fluid absorbs water and boils on a descent.
- **How:** (1) Check the reservoir level, fill to the maximum and keep it above the minimum during the job. (2) Remove the dust cap on the rear right bleed screw, fit a clear tube whose other end is in a jar of clean fluid, with the tube end above the screw. (3) Helper presses the pedal several times, then holds it down; open the screw half a turn until the pedal reaches its lowest point; close the screw before the pedal is released. (4) Repeat until no bubbles appear in the tube. (5) Do the same on the rear left, front right and front left, in this order (manual). (6) Let the car stand for half an hour and repeat the whole process (manual). (7) Fluid change (manual): siphon the reservoir, fill with new fluid, and purge each wheel: 75 ml at the rear right, 50 ml at the rear left, 30 ml at the front right and 30 ml at the front left, with the reservoir kept full; use a 250 ml measuring jar. Reservoir capacity 0.34 litres; fluid type DOT 4.
- **Verify:** The pedal is firm and does not sink under steady pressure; no leaks; the level is at the maximum.
- **Don't:** Don't let the reservoir run dry during bleeding, don't reuse fluid from the jar, don't mix DOT 5 silicone fluid with DOT 4, and don't let fluid touch paint.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 216 (bleeding order: rear right, rear left, front right, front left; wait half an hour and repeat; fluid change volumes 75, 50, 30, 30 ml), p. 12 and 14 (DOT 4, 0.34 litres), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-40: Brake master cylinder, booster, handbrake cable
- **Rule:** Check the pedal feel and the vacuum booster (*servofreno*) every service; leave the master cylinder and booster to a mechanic unless you can bleed the system afterwards; adjust the handbrake as the manual states.
- **Why:** A spongy pedal can be air or a failing master cylinder; a hard pedal is the booster or a vacuum hose; a handbrake that does not hold fails the VTV and the car rolls.
- **How:** (1) Booster test: with the engine off, press the pedal several times to exhaust the vacuum, then hold it down and start the engine: the pedal should sink slightly. If not, check the vacuum hose and the check valve at the booster. (2) Master cylinder removal (manual p. 214-215): plug the brake pipes, disconnect the level warning connector, the pipes and the vacuum hose; the booster nuts tighten to 15 Nm; bleed the system fully afterwards (VEH-GRP-39). (3) Handbrake adjustment (manual p. 215): pull the lever up to the second tooth, lift the car on stands, tighten the adjusting nut next to the equalising device until the rear wheels cannot be turned by hand, then release the lever and check that the wheels turn freely; repeat if there is drag. (4) Cable replacement: remove the drums, the shoe and spring plate, free the cable from the lever and from its guides on the body and the rear axle (manual p. 212). (5) The lever with the dashboard warning switch: the floor cover and two screws free the lever (manual p. 209-210).
- **Verify:** The handbrake holds the car on a slope on the third or fourth tooth and the warning lamp works.
- **Don't:** Don't adjust the handbrake before fitting and adjusting the shoes; don't leave the brake pipes open to air.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 209-215 (handbrake lever, cable and adjustment; master cylinder and booster with 15 Nm nuts), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the booster pedal test is general practice.

## Suspension and steering

### VEH-GRP-41: Diagnose the front end: struts, bushes, ball joints and tie rods
- **Rule:** Check the front end every 10,000 km or when a clunk, vague steering or uneven tyre wear appears: struts, strut mounts, control arm bushes, ball joints, tie rod ends and anti-roll bar.
- **Why:** The front suspension is MacPherson with triangular lower arms and an anti-roll bar. Worn bushes and joints cause clunks, wander and fail the VTV play check.
- **How:** (1) Lift the front on stands. (2) Grab each wheel at 12 and 6 o'clock and rock it: play = wheel bearing or ball joint; grab at 3 and 9 o'clock: play = tie rod end or rack. (3) Use a bar between the arm and the body to look for movement in the lower arm bush and at the ball joint. (4) Bounce the car: a shock absorber that keeps bouncing or leaks oil is replaced in pairs. (5) Look at the strut top mount and the boots. (6) Anti-roll bar bushes: the manual gives the bar diameter 18.5 mm for the injected models; replace the rubber bushes if cracked. (7) Replace worn parts: the manual shows the order (remove the wheel, the strut carrier, the joint end from the arm, the arm). A ball joint is removed with two nuts from the arm and the bolt on the strut carrier; the control arm calibration is checked with a gauge. Replace in pairs and then align the wheels (VEH-GRP-45).
- **Verify:** No play at the checks, no clunk, the car goes straight and the tyres wear evenly.
- **Don't:** Don't compress a coil spring without a proper spring compressor (the spring is dangerous), and don't weld or straighten a bent control arm.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 13 (front suspension: MacPherson, anti-roll bar, triangular arms, coil springs), p. 218-224 (strut, strut carrier, ball joint end, control arm and bushes, anti-roll bar bushes), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the rocking checks are general practice and the manual's pages are for the Gol with the revised suspension of the 1990s, which may differ in detail from a 1994 car.

### VEH-GRP-42: CV joints and boots
- **Rule:** Inspect the CV boots (*cubrejuntas*) at each service and replace a torn boot at once; a clicking noise on full lock means the outer joint is worn and needs replacement.
- **Why:** A torn boot lets the grease out and the dirt in; a joint can be saved only if the boot is replaced early.
- **How:** (1) Look for grease thrown on the inside of the wheel, the arm or the brake. (2) With the front wheels on full lock, drive in slow circles: clicking means a worn outer joint. (3) Replacement (manual): with the car on stands, remove the wheel nut, the strut carrier bolt, release the joint end and the inner joint bolts at the flange; pull the half-shaft. (4) Outer joint: remove the clamps, push the boot back and drive the joint off with a leather or plastic hammer; the manual says to grease the inner side of the new joint with 90 g of lithium grease, fit a new retaining clip, keep the contact surfaces of the boot free of grease, seat the boot between the shoulders of the shaft and the joint, and fix it with new clamps. (5) Inner joint: remove the cover with a punch and hammer, remove the circlip. (6) The self-locking hub nut is replaced. Often a complete half-shaft, new or reconditioned, is the economical choice.
- **Verify:** No clicking on lock; no grease thrown from the boots after 500 km.
- **Don't:** Don't reuse the old clamps or the hub nut; don't use general grease; don't drive on a half-shaft with a missing boot.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 225-230 (articulated half-shaft, external and internal CV joint, 90 g of lithium grease, new clamps and retaining ring), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-43: Front wheel bearing noise and replacement
- **Rule:** A hum that rises with speed and changes when steering, or play when rocking the wheel with the car on stands, is a wheel bearing; replace the bearing (a press job) and the hub nut.
- **Why:** The front bearing is pressed into the strut carrier. A bearing that fails can lock or let the wheel wobble.
- **How:** (1) With the car on stands, spin the wheel by hand and listen for a rumble; rock it for play. (2) Removal (manual p. 221): remove the strut carrier, the brake disc, the disc guard, the hub; press out the inner race of the bearing from the hub; remove the outer race with the snap ring; lubricate the housing with white lubricating paste; fit the outer snap ring, press in the new bearing, fit the inner snap ring, then the hub, the disc guard, the disc and the carrier. (3) The wheel bolts tighten to 100 Nm. (4) Rear: the rear bearings are adjusted by the hub nut (30 Nm while turning the wheel, then the play is set), see VEH-GRP-38. (5) A mechanic with a press does the front job; an owner can change the rear bearings.
- **Verify:** No rumble, no play, no heat on the hub after a drive.
- **Don't:** Don't hammer a bearing in or out; don't reuse a self-locking nut.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 220-221 (wheel bolts 100 Nm; wheel bearing removal and fitting with presses), p. 206 (rear bearing nut 30 Nm), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-44: Rear axle bushes and rear shock absorbers
- **Rule:** If the rear end clunks or the car wanders at the back, check the rear axle mount bushes, the shock absorbers and the springs.
- **Why:** The rear axle is carried by trailing tubular arms with rubber bushes in the axle support; the bushes crack and allow the axle to move, which changes toe and handling.
- **How:** (1) Lift the rear on stands under the body and look at the axle support bushes for cracks and the axle for lateral movement (a bar between the axle and the body). (2) Shock absorbers: oil on the body or no damping when pressed down hard means replace both. (3) Bush replacement (manual): remove the wheel, disconnect the brake pipe from the hose and the hose clamp, free the handbrake cable, release the exhaust hangers, support the axle with wooden blocks (manual Fig. VII.26) and replace the double split bush; refit and then adjust the wheel bearings, the handbrake and bleed the brakes (manual p. 242). (4) Rear spring and shock: unbolt the lower shock mount, remove shock and spring together, then the isolator and washer.
- **Verify:** No clunk; the rear stays level and the shocks damp.
- **Don't:** Don't compress the coil springs without a compressor; don't drive with a leaking shock.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 232-242 (rear suspension: shock absorbers and springs, axle support bushes, double split bush, bleeding afterwards), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the rear-axle layout on a 1994 BX car versus the 1990s Gol of the manual.

### VEH-GRP-45: Alignment and tyres
- **Rule:** Have the wheels aligned after any suspension or steering repair and when the tyres wear unevenly, and set the tyre pressure from the car's own sticker.
- **Why:** A worn or replaced bush, arm or tie rod changes camber and toe; the tyres then wear on one edge.
- **How:** (1) Replace the worn parts first, then align. (2) Alignment values of the manual for the Gol (printed p. 243) cover front camber, caster, toe, and rear camber and toe; they are for the Gol with the revised suspension, so ask the alignment shop for the factory data of the BX Gol or use vw-gol-1994-specs.md. (3) Tyre size 175/70 R13 (steel wheel 5J x 13) per the manual; the manual gives front 24 psi and rear 26 psi (half load) in the specification table (p. 13), but another table at p. 243 gives other values: follow the sticker on the car. (4) Check tyres for age (the four-digit date code) and cracks: a tyre older than about 8-10 years is replaced even if the tread is deep (general practice). (5) Rotate tyres front to rear at service.
- **Verify:** The car tracks straight and the steering wheel is centred; tyres wear evenly.
- **Don't:** Don't mix tyre sizes on one axle, don't leave an old tyre on the rear "because it has tread", and don't align a car with worn bushes.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 13 (tyres 175/70 SR13, pressures 24 and 26 psi), p. 242-243 (wheels and tyres; tyre pressures 26/26 and 26/30 for the 1.6; alignment values), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the tyre age guide (general practice) and the pressure to use; the manual's two tables differ.

## Electrical

### VEH-GRP-46: Battery and charging: test before replacing the alternator
- **Rule:** Check the battery, terminals, belt and charging voltage in that order before replacing the alternator or the regulator.
- **Why:** Most charging faults are a loose or corroded terminal, a loose belt or a battery that no longer takes a charge; the alternator regulator (*regulador*) is a cheap part on the Bosch alternators.
- **How:** (1) Clean and tighten the battery terminals and the earth strap to the body and engine. (2) Voltage with the engine off: about 12.6 V is full; below 12.2 V recharge. (3) Voltage with the engine at 2500-3000 rpm and the lights on: the manual gives a regulated voltage of 13.7-14.6 V (electronic regulator with thermal compensation). (4) Below 13.5 V: check the belt (VEH-GRP-31), the connectors on the alternator and the fuse; then the regulator and brushes, which on the Bosch 65 A and 90 A alternators are removed first from the rear (manual p. 262-264). (5) Above 14.8 V: regulator. (6) The manual lists a 12 V maintenance-free battery of 36 Ah (without air conditioning) and 54 Ah (with air conditioning) and a 55 A alternator; see vw-gol-1994-specs.md. (7) A battery that discharges overnight: pull fuses one at a time with a milliammeter on the negative lead to find the drain.
- **Verify:** 13.7-14.6 V at 2500 rpm with loads on; the car starts after standing a week.
- **Don't:** Don't disconnect the battery with the engine running to "test" the alternator; don't charge a battery with the cells open near a flame.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 14 (electrical system: controlled voltage 13.7-14.6 V; alternator 55 A; battery 12 V maintenance-free 36 and 54 Ah), p. 262-264 (Bosch 65 A and 90 A alternators, removal of the regulator first), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the 12.6 V and 12.2 V figures and the drain test are general practice.

### VEH-GRP-47: Starter and ignition switch faults
- **Rule:** If the engine does not crank, check the battery and terminals first, then the starter solenoid feed, the ignition switch and the earths; if it cranks slowly, suspect the battery or the starter.
- **Why:** A click with no crank is a weak battery or a poor connection at the starter; silence with the ignition on points to the switch, the neutral/clutch wiring or a blown fuse.
- **How:** (1) Headlamps on: if they dim to nothing when cranking, the battery or a terminal is bad. (2) Check the large cable between the battery and the starter and the earth cable to the gearbox. (3) Click only: tap the starter body (a worn commutator may then work for one start) and replace or recondition the starter. (4) Nothing, but lights on: test the ignition switch output (terminal 50) with a test lamp while turning the key to start; if the lamp lights but the starter does not, the starter or its solenoid is bad; if not, replace the ignition switch contact. (5) The Gol's key switch has an anti-theft steering lock (the 1993 owner's manual shows the key positions); a worn key gives intermittent contact: cut a new key and replace the switch if the contact is worn. (6) The starter is bolted to the gearbox bell: disconnect the battery first, remove the starter cable and the mounting bolts (manual Fig. IX.8, p. 266); its bench disassembly (solenoid, end cap, brush holder, field frame) is shown step by step in Fig. IX.9-IX.11 (p. 267-269); the starter bush sits in the bell housing and is reached only with the starter removed (p. 270). (7) After reassembling, check the clutch cable and linkage did not move.
- **Verify:** Starts at the first turn of the key in cold and hot conditions.
- **Don't:** Don't keep cranking for more than 10 seconds at a time; don't hammer the starter if it is bolted on loosely.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 266-270 (starter removal, disassembly figures, starter bush), read 2026-11-15; owner's manual (file gol-1993-manual-propietario-es-ar.pdf, ignition switch positions page, read 2026-11-15, low-quality scan, only the layout was legible). Not verified in a primary source: the test steps are general practice.

### VEH-GRP-48: Fuse box and relays: use the manual's table and look for the short
- **Rule:** When a circuit is dead, check its fuse from the manual's table, but if the fuse blows again find the short instead of fitting a bigger fuse.
- **Why:** The Gol's fuse box is old; terminals oxidise and the plate becomes unreliable. Shared fuses explain odd symptoms (stall plus no brake lights).
- **How:** (1) The manual says the electrical distribution box is on the left side under the panel (G2-era text: 7 relays, the window circuit breaker, 34 fuses of which 6 spare, 9 harness connectors, p. 270; the first-generation box is smaller and the 21-fuse table below applies to it); its clips have double contacts, so a loose fuse or relay usually means a bent clip. (2) The manual's table for the Gol 1.6 before 1996 (21 fuses, blade type): 2 sidelights 10 A; 3 horn, radio, clock, dash 10 A; 4 main/dipped switch and boot light 15 A; 6 right sidelight and glovebox light 10 A; 7 left sidelight 10 A; 8 and 9 left and right high beam 10 A; 10 and 11 right and left dipped beam 10 A; 12 brake lights and idle valve 10 A; 14 turn signal switch, reversing light and rear lamp 5 A; 15 wiper motor and switch 10 A; 16 ventilation switch 25 A; 17 rear heated window 30 A; 20 fan thermoswitch 30 A. The 1.8 table is similar and adds 18 rear wiper 15 A. (3) A fuse that blows at once: find the short (a chafed wire under the dash or in the boot). (4) Relays and the plate: clean the terminals with contact cleaner; replace a relay by a same-type one. (5) The manual's relay carrier shown at p. 273 belongs to the injected Gol; do not look for it on a carburetted car.
- **Verify:** The circuit works and the fuse does not blow after 30 minutes of use.
- **Don't:** Don't replace a fuse with a larger one, a coin or foil; don't use a test lamp on electronic modules.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 275-276 (fuse tables for the Gol 1.6 and 1.8, models before 1996), p. 270 (box location and capacity, G2-era), p. 273 (relay carrier, injection), p. 84 (fuse-box access cover in the clutch cable job), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-49: Wiper motor and the dashboard
- **Rule:** If the wipers stop, check fuse 15, the wiper switch and the motor earth before replacing the motor; for dead gauges, check the fuse 3 circuit, the connectors behind the cluster and the speedometer cable.
- **Why:** The wiper motor is cheap to test; many dashboard faults are a loose connector or the speedometer cable.
- **How:** (1) Wipers: fuse 15 (10 A, wiper motor and switch). (2) Disconnect the motor and apply 12 V directly: if it runs, the fault is the switch or wiring; if not, the motor or its earth (clean the earth point). (3) A motor that runs but does not move the blades: the linkage has jumped or the spline is stripped; the manual removes the arms and the lower windscreen trim to reach the wiper support and linkage (Fig. IX.26-IX.27, p. 280, G2-era dashboard, may differ); refit the linkage in the park position. (4) A motor that runs slowly: worn brushes or dry linkage; grease the pivots. (5) Dashboard: fuse 3 (horn, radio, clock, dash 10 A) and the clusters connectors; a speedometer needle that jumps or stops is a dry or broken cable (manual p. 279). (6) Warning lamps all lit with the engine running: the alternator (VEH-GRP-46). (7) The fuel and temperature gauges depend on the sender units and the voltage stabiliser; a wrong reading with all the connections good points to the sender.
- **Verify:** Wipers on all speeds and park correctly; gauges and lamps work.
- **Don't:** Don't force a jammed wiper arm by hand, which strips the motor spline; don't clean the cluster with wet cloths.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 275 (fuses 3 and 15), p. 279-280 (speedometer cable; wiper removal figures, G2-era), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: test steps are general practice.

## Body

### VEH-GRP-50: Rust spots typical of the Gol cuadrado: where to look
- **Rule:** Inspect the known rust areas each year: the bulkhead (*torpedo*) and the central tunnel, the floor and sills, the rear wheel arches, the door bottoms, the tailgate and the windscreen scuttle; treat rust early.
- **Why:** The paint of the period and panel gaps that hold water make the first-generation Gol rust from the inside; rust in the structure fails the VTV and the car's safety.
- **How:** (1) Lift the carpets and mats and look and poke with a screwdriver at the floor, the sill (*zócalo*) and the rear axle mounts. (2) Look at the bulkhead and the tunnel for cracks and rust: a secondary Brazilian article describes cracks in the firewall and central tunnel that let water in. (3) Check the lower door edges, the wheel arch lips, the bottom of the tailgate, and the drain holes (clean them). (4) Surface rust: sand to bright metal, treat with a rust converter, prime, paint and seal seams. (5) Holes or flaking in the sills, floor pan or axle mount areas: have a body shop cut and weld new metal; do not fill with filler. (6) Wax or underbody coating after repair.
- **Verify:** No holes, no flaking in the structure; the VTV inspector does not flag corrosion of the chassis.
- **Don't:** Don't cover rust with filler and underseal; don't ignore rust around the suspension and seat belt mounts.
- **Source:** AutoPapo, "Projetinho do Gol quadrado pode ser uma armadilha para o bolso" (secondary, Brazil, read 2026-11-15: poor paint of the period, panel gaps that trap water, cracks in the firewall and central tunnel). Not verified in a primary source: the full list of rust points is general practice for this type of car; no Argentine owners' club list was found.

### VEH-GRP-51: Door locks, window regulators and seals
- **Rule:** Lubricate the locks and regulators once a year, replace hardened seals, and replace a regulator only after checking cable, guide and motor.
- **Why:** Dry locks and regulators break; hardened door seals let in water and wind noise.
- **How:** (1) Locks: spray graphite or a dry lock lubricant into the cylinder, not oil; lubricate the latch with a white lithium grease. (2) A door that will not open from outside: the rod clip at the lock has come off; remove the door panel and refit the clip. (3) Window regulators: remove the door trim, check the cable or scissor mechanism and the guide channels; clean and grease the channels with silicone; replace a frayed cable or a regulator with broken teeth. (4) Seals: clean with silicone spray; replace seals that are flat, cracked or shrunk. (5) Central locking is available on some versions: the manual lists central locking and electric windows in the electrical chapter (p. 281-282).
- **Verify:** Doors close with a soft thud; windows run smoothly; no water or wind noise.
- **Don't:** Don't force a window with a stuck regulator; don't use oil on the lock cylinder.
- **Source:** Not verified in a primary source: general practice; the manual's electrical chapter index lists electric windows and central locking at printed p. 281-282 (read 2026-11-15) but those pages were not read in detail.

## Bringing a Gol back after a year parked

### VEH-GRP-52: Before reviving a parked Gol: paperwork, safety walk-around and the order of work
- **Rule:** Do not start a Gol that has stood about a year and drive it at once; first check the legal papers, then do a safety walk-around, then follow the order: rodents and leaks, fluids, fuel, battery, brakes and tyres, first start, short test drive, workshop checks, VTV.
- **Why:** After a year the petrol has turned to varnish, the battery is flat or dead, brake fluid has absorbed water, calipers and drums can be seized, tyres and belts are aged, and mice may have chewed the wiring. Each step has a risk if done out of order (for example, cranking with old fuel clogs the carburettor jets).
- **How:** (1) Papers: confirm that the insurance (*seguro*), the vehicle registration card (*cédula verde*), the annual road tax (*patente*) and the VTV are in order before the car goes on the road; see ownership-and-paperwork.md and fines-and-points.md. (2) Walk-around: look under the car for fluid stains, look at the tyres, look for rodent nests in the engine bay, air filter box, heater intake and under the seats. (3) Work in this order, each described in its own rule: rodents and wiring (VEH-GRP-56), engine oil and coolant (VEH-GRP-56), fuel and carburettor (VEH-GRP-53), battery (VEH-GRP-54), brakes, clutch and tyres (VEH-GRP-55), first start (VEH-GRP-57). (4) After the first drive, do the mechanical checks that apply (timing belt, belts, pump, hoses) and then the pre-VTV checklist (VEH-GRP-58, VEH-GRP-59).
- **Verify:** A written checklist with each step ticked; the car has insurance and a valid VTV, or a way to reach the workshop that the law allows (check with the insurer).
- **Don't:** Don't go on the road with old fuel, a weak battery, unchecked brakes or old tyres, and don't take the car to the VTV before the first week of normal use.
- **Source:** AutoPapo, "Carro muito tempo parado: um guia para evitar problemas" (secondary, Brazil, read 2026-11-15: after about a year belts and hoses may dry out, oil change if more than 6 months, tyres, battery, fuel age 3 months); O Mecanico, "Fusca parado ha mais de um ano" (secondary, Brazil, read 2026-11-15: do a complete diagnosis before use, clean fuel system, brakes, ignition, tyres). Not verified in a primary source: the legal status of a parked car and the documents required (see ownership-and-paperwork.md).

### VEH-GRP-53: Old fuel, the tank and the carburettor after a year
- **Rule:** Drain the old petrol, check the tank for rust and water, replace the fuel filter, clean or rebuild the carburettor, and only then try to start.
- **Why:** Petrol older than about three months oxidises to varnish and gum, clogs the carburettor idle jets, and water collects in the tank; the accelerator pump diaphragm is dry. Starting on old fuel blocks the jets again.
- **How:** (1) Disconnect the battery. (2) Drain the tank through the fuel line (disconnect the hose at the pump, put a hose into a metal can) or by removing the tank: smell the fuel, look for sediment and water. Old petrol is hazardous waste: store it in an approved can and take it to a collection point, do not pour it down a drain or burn it. (3) If there is rust flakes or dirt, remove the tank and have it cleaned or replaced. (4) Replace the fuel filter (item 7 of the manual's fuel-system figure) and the fuel hose if it is hard or cracked. (5) Remove, clean and rebuild the carburettor (VEH-GRP-17) with a kit; clean the float bowl and jets; replace the pump diaphragm. (6) Fill with a few litres of fresh petrol first; prime the carburettor bowl using the accelerator pump or the fuel pump lever, not by pouring petrol into the throat. (7) Charcoal canister and vapour separator: check the hoses.
- **Verify:** Clean fuel in a glass jar from the carburettor inlet; no leaks at the clamps; the bowl fills.
- **Don't:** Don't start the engine on the old petrol "just to see", don't pour petrol into the air filter, and don't smoke near the tank.
- **Source:** AutoPapo, "Carro muito tempo parado" (secondary, Brazil: regular petrol about 3 months), O Mecanico, "Fusca parado ha mais de um ano" (secondary, Brazil: remove and wash the tank so residue does not clog filters and carburettor jets, restore the carburettor by cleaning and recalibration), read 2026-11-15; workshop manual, printed p. 186-187 (fuel system diagram), p. 188-193 (carburettor disassembly), read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-GRP-54: Battery and electrical connections after a year
- **Rule:** Assume the battery is dead; recharge it slowly or replace it, clean all terminals and earths, and check the charging voltage after the first start.
- **Why:** A battery left a year sulphates and may never hold a charge; a deep-discharged one can also swell and leak acid.
- **How:** (1) Wear eye protection. Inspect the battery for swelling, cracks and leaks; if so, replace it. (2) Charge it with a bench charger at a low rate (about one tenth of its Ah capacity) for several hours; the manual lists a 36 Ah or 54 Ah maintenance-free battery. (3) Test: 12.6 V rested after a charge is good; if it drops below 12.2 V within a day, replace it. (4) Clean the terminals with hot water and a wire brush, grease them, tighten, and clean the earth points on the body and engine. (5) Check the wiring for rodent damage (VEH-GRP-56) and the fuses with a test lamp. (6) Check the alternator output at first start (VEH-GRP-46). (7) Lights, horn, wipers, indicators and brake lights are checked now and not at the inspection (VEH-GRP-58).
- **Verify:** The battery holds 12.4 V or more after 24 hours rested; the engine cranks briskly.
- **Don't:** Don't jump-start a frozen or swollen battery; don't charge sealed batteries at a high rate; don't connect the charger to a car that is cranking.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 14 (battery 12 V maintenance-free, 36 and 54 Ah), read 2026-11-15; AutoPapo, "Carro muito tempo parado" (secondary, Brazil: the battery is usually the first to suffer, may need recharging or replacement), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the charge rate and voltage thresholds (general practice).

### VEH-GRP-55: Brakes, clutch and tyres after a year
- **Rule:** Before the first drive, check that all four wheels turn freely, check the brake fluid and pedal, and the clutch pedal; replace the brake fluid and old tyres.
- **Why:** Linings stick to the drums and discs rust; calipers and wheel cylinders seize after a year; brake fluid has absorbed water; tyres crack and flat-spot.
- **How:** (1) Chocks, gear in neutral, handbrake off: push the car a few metres; if a wheel drags, lift it and find the cause: a seized rear shoe (hit the drum lightly, back off the handbrake adjustment (VEH-GRP-40) and free the shoe), a seized caliper piston or guide pin (VEH-GRP-37), a rusted handbrake cable. (2) Check the reservoir level, the colour of the fluid and look for wet spots at the wheel cylinders, calipers, hoses and master cylinder. (3) Replace the brake fluid using the manual's procedure (VEH-GRP-39) and replace any rubber hose that is cracked or swollen; if calipers or wheel cylinders leak, replace or recondition them in pairs. (4) Clutch: press the pedal; if the clutch is stuck to the flywheel, with the engine off select first and press the pedal firmly several times; free the cable with grease (VEH-GRP-35). (5) Tyres: check the four-digit date code and cracks in the sidewall and between the tread blocks; replace tyres more than about 8-10 years old; inflate to the sticker pressure and check the spare. (6) Wipers: replace hardened blades.
- **Verify:** All four wheels spin freely, the pedal is firm, no drips; no cracks in the tyres.
- **Don't:** Don't drive with a pedal that sinks; don't sand rusted discs until they are below minimum thickness (10.8 mm after regrinding, manual); don't reuse old fluid.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 200-216 (brakes), p. 82-87 (clutch), read 2026-11-15; AutoPapo, "Carro muito tempo parado" (secondary: brake linings can stick to the drums; tyres), O Mecanico, "Fusca parado ha mais de um ano" (secondary: wheel cylinders rusted, replaced as pairs; tyres from 2007 cracked), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the 8-10 year tyre age guide is general practice.

### VEH-GRP-56: Rodents, oil, coolant, belts and lights after a year
- **Rule:** Look for rodent damage; change the engine oil and filter, the coolant if it is old or dirty, check the belts and hoses, and test all lights.
- **Why:** Mice nest in the air filter box and heater intake and chew wire insulation, hoses and the fuel line; old oil carries moisture and acid, belts and hoses age even without use.
- **How:** (1) Rodents: remove the air filter, look in the air box and ducts for nests; check wire looms for chewed insulation and repair with proper connectors (tape over a bare wire is a fire risk). (2) Oil: after more than 6 months change the oil and filter (grade and quantity of vw-gol-1994-specs.md; the manual gives a 15W-40 API SG or better multigrade and a sump capacity of 3 litres without filter, 3.5 litres with). (3) Coolant: if it is cloudy, rusty or more than two years old, drain and refill (VEH-GRP-28). (4) Belts: the timing belt is replaced if its age exceeds the interval or the history is unknown (VEH-GRP-30); the accessory belt if cracked (VEH-GRP-31). (5) Hoses: squeeze for softness (VEH-GRP-28). (6) Lights: switch on in turn the sidelights, dipped and main beam, indicators, hazard lights, brake lights, reversing lights, number plate lamp, and the horn; replace blown bulbs and clean corroded sockets (fuses in VEH-GRP-48).
- **Verify:** No chewed wiring, fresh oil, a sound belt, all lights working.
- **Don't:** Don't run the engine on year-old oil with a worn timing belt of unknown age.
- **Source:** AutoPapo, "Carro muito tempo parado" (secondary, Brazil: oil change after more than 6 months; belts and hoses dry out after about a year), read 2026-11-15; workshop manual, printed p. 14 (lubricants 15W-40 API SG) and p. 13 (sump 3 and 3.5 litres), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: rodent damage steps are general practice; none of the sources mentions rodents.

### VEH-GRP-57: First start and the first drive after the revival
- **Rule:** Prime the oil, check for water in the cylinders, start the engine with all the checks done, let it run, then drive gently and re-check for leaks.
- **Why:** The first start after a year dry-runs the bearings and cylinder walls; a flooded or seized engine can be damaged if forced.
- **How:** (1) With the battery connected, the plugs out and the coil lead earthed (or the coil connector off), turn the engine by hand on the crankshaft pulley bolt one or two turns to feel that it is free (a stiff engine or water from the plug holes means stop and call a mechanic). (2) Crank on the starter for 5-10 seconds with the plugs out until the oil pressure lamp goes out. (3) Refit the plugs and leads (VEH-GRP-20), reconnect the coil. (4) Check that the bowl is full (VEH-GRP-53); do not pump the accelerator repeatedly. (5) Start; let the engine idle: the oil pressure lamp must go out within a few seconds; the charging lamp must go out. (6) Check for leaks of fuel, oil and coolant while idling for 10 minutes; watch the temperature, the fan cycling and the idle. (7) Short drive at low speed (a few kilometres), test the brakes at walking pace first, then re-check the fluids and leaks. (8) After 100 km re-check the belt tension, the coolant level and the oil level.
- **Verify:** Oil lamp out in seconds, no leaks, stable idle after warm-up, brakes straight and firm.
- **Don't:** Don't rev the cold engine, don't drive on the motorway on the first day, and don't ignore a lamp on the dashboard.
- **Source:** AutoPapo, "Carro muito tempo parado" (secondary, Brazil, read 2026-11-15: wait for the oil lamp to go out and idle a few minutes before pressing the accelerator). Not verified in a primary source: the priming steps are general practice.

## Technical inspection (VTV) and emissions

### VEH-GRP-58: Pre-VTV checklist for a Gol cuadrado
- **Rule:** Before the technical inspection (*VTV*), check these items in order and fix what fails; confirm the official list with ownership-and-paperwork.md.
- **Why:** A failed VTV costs a second visit; the Gol's age makes lights, brakes, suspension play, exhaust, emissions, tyres and corrosion the usual causes.
- **How:** (1) Documents and plates: plate lamps, plates and the registration card, as listed in ownership-and-paperwork.md. (2) Lights: sidelights, dipped and main beam (aim checked on a wall), indicators, hazard lights, brake lights, reversing lights. (3) Horn and wipers, washers and windscreen state. (4) Brakes: pedal firm, equal braking left to right on a brake tester, handbrake holds on the third or fourth tooth (VEH-GRP-40), no leaks. (5) Steering and suspension play: no play in tie rods, ball joints, wheel bearings (VEH-GRP-41, VEH-GRP-43), CV boots intact (VEH-GRP-42), shocks not leaking. (6) Tyres: legal tread (the wear marks), no cracks, correct size; spare tyre and tools. (7) Exhaust: no leaks, silencer fixed, noise level. (8) Seat belts, mirrors, door locks, windscreen glass. (9) Body: no structural rust at the sills, floor and mounts (VEH-GRP-50). (10) Emissions: the engine tuned and the CO checked (VEH-GRP-59). (11) Fluids: no leaks of oil, fuel or coolant (VEH-GRP-8).
- **Verify:** You have ticked each item; the car does not show warning lamps; leaks are dry.
- **Don't:** Don't take the car to the VTV for a pre-test with a cold, untuned engine, and don't hide leaks or rust with filler or underseal.
- **Source:** Not verified in a primary source: the VTV check list of the plant is not reproduced here; it was not available to the author as an official document. Generic items; confirm with the official VTV list of the jurisdiction (the Province of Buenos Aires runs its own VTV regime, CABA and other provinces differ) in ownership-and-paperwork.md.

### VEH-GRP-59: Pass the VTV emissions test with a carburetted engine
- **Rule:** Warm the engine, make sure it is tuned, adjust the CO to the manual's range, and arrive at the test with the engine hot and the exhaust leak-free.
- **Why:** A carburetted engine without catalytic converter is tested by its exhaust gas at idle (CO, HC) and in some jurisdictions also at about 2500 rpm; a rich mixture, worn plugs, bad timing or an air leak fail it.
- **How:** (1) Do the tune-up first: new plugs and air filter, ignition timing (VEH-GRP-23), air leaks (VEH-GRP-13), valve clearance (VEH-GRP-33), compression. (2) Adjust the mixture with an analyser to the manual's CO (VEH-GRP-14): 0.5-1.5% for the later Weber DMPV and the Brosol 2E-CE, 1-2% for the early Weber DMPV. (3) Check the exhaust for leaks (a leak before the sampling point dilutes the reading). (4) The limit: according to a press note (secondary) citing Decreto 779/1995, Annex I item 4.1.3, petrol cars made from 1 January 1995 may not exceed 2.5% CO at idle; the 1994 Gol is earlier and the limit applied to it by the plant is not confirmed here: ask the plant for the printed limit, and aim at the manual's range, which is below 2.5%. (5) The test procedure example (CABA Decreto 198/2006, Annex VIII, the air-quality regulation of the City): measurement at idle (600-1200 rpm) and at 2500 rpm; one retest after 180 seconds at 2500 rpm; a reading is void if CO plus CO2 is below 6% (a leak or a dilution). (6) Before the test drive 15-20 minutes so the engine and oil are hot; do not use the choke. (7) A smoky or oil-burning engine (worn valve stem seals, rings) will not pass by adjustment: seals are replaced (manual p. 62) or the engine overhauled.
- **Verify:** The analyser reading before the test: CO in the manual's range, HC low and steady, CO + CO2 above 6%.
- **Don't:** Don't lean the mixture beyond what is stable just to pass; do not add additives instead of fixing; do not tamper with the emissions equipment.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 184, 197, 198 (CO ranges), p. 62 (valve stem seals), read 2026-11-15; El Cronista, "Por orden de la Ley 24.449, multaran a los vehiculos que superen el 2,5% de emisiones de monoxido de carbono por el cano de escape" (secondary, read 2026-11-15, citing Decreto 779/1995 Anexo 1 item 4.1.3); CABA Decreto 198/2006, Anexo IV and Anexo VIII, Boletin Oficial CABA norm 83624 (read 2026-11-15; the table of limits by model year is an image that could not be read). Not verified in a primary source: the CO and HC limits applied to a 1994 carburetted car in the Province of Buenos Aires VTV (the annexes of Decreto 779/1995 and the provincial rules were not reachable).

### VEH-GRP-61: Typical parts, and how to buy them without paying twice
- **Rule:** Buy parts by the engine code, the carburettor part number and the part number of the old part; replace related parts together; do not give prices without a date.
- **Why:** The Gol exists with several engines and carburettors, and many parts look the same but differ; buying a kit that includes the related parts saves labour.
- **How:** Typical parts by job: (1) Tune-up: spark plugs (the types of VEH-GRP-20), leads, air filter, fuel filter, distributor cap and rotor. (2) Carburettor: rebuild kit (gaskets, accelerator pump and power valve diaphragms, needle valve), base gasket and spacer. (3) Cooling: thermostat with ring, fan thermoswitch, hoses, coolant, cap, water pump with ring. (4) Timing: toothed belt, tensioner roller, water pump, front seals, accessory belt. (5) Valve cover gasket and plug-well seals. (6) Brakes: front pads, rear shoes, wheel cylinders, DOT 4 fluid, hoses, caliper repair kit. (7) Clutch: cable, pedal bushes. (8) Front end: struts, mounts, control arm bushes, ball joints, tie rod ends, CV joint kits or complete half-shafts, anti-roll bar bushes. (9) Electrical: battery, alternator regulator and brushes, ignition coil, pickup coil, module, fuses and relays. Cost-aware tips: ask for a price with the date and the brand; compare an original, a first-fit brand and a generic; a "kit" (belt, tensioner, pump) is cheaper than the parts separately; old stock of genuine parts is available in Argentina through dealers and independent importers, and the Gol was widely sold in Brazil and Argentina so many generic parts exist (secondary: marketplace listings). Keep the old part to show the seller.
- **Verify:** The new part matches the old one (connectors, number of teeth, size) before fitting.
- **Don't:** Don't buy by "fits Gol" alone, and don't fit a part that needs force.

## Weber DMPV adjustment procedures (second source pass, 2026-11-15)

Nothing local or on the web gave a written Weber DMPV service procedure (the Gol workshop manual prints the data tables and the disassembly figures only). The three rules below therefore combine the manual's figures for the DMPV with the method of the Brosol 2E7 chapter in the AP engine workshop manual (compilation) and with Weber guides for other carburettors, and each says which part is which. Use a gas analyser and a tachometer, work with the air filter in place unless the step says otherwise, and have a mechanic do the calibration if you doubt the result.

### VEH-GRP-62: Set the idle speed and the mixture of the Weber DMPV in the right order
- **Rule:** On the warm engine set the idle speed with the throttle stop screw and the mixture with the tapered mixture screw under its plastic cap, then read the CO: **idle 900-1000 rpm and CO 0.5-1.5 %** for the Weber DMPV made from November 1991 (part 029 129 015.37); **900-1000 rpm and CO 1-2 %** for the earlier one (015.26).
- **Why:** idle speed and CO act on each other, so changing one without resetting the other leaves both wrong; the CO decides drivability, consumption and the inspection (VEH-GRP-59).
- **How:** (1) Make the engine ready: oil above 60 degrees C and the fan not running, all electrics off, choke knob pushed in with the warning lamp out (VEH-GRP-15), ignition timing correct (VEH-GRP-23), no air leaks (VEH-GRP-13), tachometer connected and gas analyser probe in the tailpipe. (2) Find the mixture screw at the base of the carburettor: the manual's Fig. V.69 shows a tapered screw with a small plastic cap (remove the cap with a fine screwdriver and fit a new one at the end). If the setting is lost, seat the screw lightly and open it about 1 to 2 turns (a starting point used for other Weber carburettors, not a DMPV figure), never force a tapered screw into its seat. (3) With the engine at temperature set the speed with the throttle stop screw to about 950 rpm. (4) Turn the mixture screw slowly in small steps to the position of the highest steady speed, then lean it slightly until the speed just starts to drop; reset the speed to 950 rpm after each change. (5) Read the CO and trim the mixture screw until it is inside the range of the table; recheck the speed. (6) Reconnect the crankcase breather hose if it was off for the test and read the CO again (VEH-GRP-14). (7) Switch the fan and the headlamps on: the idle must stay steady (the idle solenoid, VEH-GRP-4, must cut the idle fuel when the ignition is switched off). (8) Fit a new plastic cap.
- **Verify:** analyser CO inside the range and tachometer 900-1000 rpm with the engine warm, steady for 5 minutes with the fan cycling.
- **Don't:** adjust by ear or on a cold engine; do not lean a tapered screw until the engine hunts; do not use the figure of the 1.8 Brosol (850-950 rpm) on the Weber.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 184 (Weber DMPV 015.26: idle 900-1000 rpm, CO 1-2 %), p. 197 (015.37: 900-1000 rpm, CO 0.5-1.5 %, table checked on the page image), p. 188 (plastic cap 1 and screw 2, Fig. V.69), read 2026-11-15; conditions and the reset-the-speed step: VW Golf 2 workshop text for the Weber TLA (vwmanual.ru, secondary, other carburettor) and the Weber 32/34 thread at fiatduna.com.ar (secondary, forum, other carburettor), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: the order of steps (peak speed, then lean, then analyser) and the 1 to 2 turn starting point, which are general Weber practice and not from a Gol or Weber DMPV document.

### VEH-GRP-63: Check the float level and the float travel of the Weber DMPV
- **Rule:** With the top cover off, check that the float sits **8 to 9 mm** (level) and moves **41 to 43 mm** (travel) as in the manual's table, and correct by bending the float tab only if the carburettor starves or floods and the needle valve is good.
- **Why:** a low float starves the engine under acceleration at speed; a high one floods the bowl, gives black smoke and petrol in the oil; the level is the base of every other adjustment.
- **How:** (1) Cold engine, battery earth off, no flames; remove the air filter and the carburettor top cover, catch the petrol. (2) Check the needle valve and its seat for wear and the float for petrol inside (shake it). (3) Measure the level on the cover: the Brosol 2E7 chapter of the AP engine workshop manual (compilation) holds the cover about 30 degrees from vertical so that the float just rests on the needle without pressing it and measures with a gauge cut from 2 mm sheet; the Gol manual does not say from which face the 8-9 mm is taken, so measure from the cover face with its gasket fitted (the usual Weber practice for other models) and confirm with a mechanic or the part's data sheet if the car misbehaves. (4) To correct, bend the tongue of the float arm that touches the needle (a tongue set so that the float shuts the needle earlier gives a lower fuel level, a later shut-off a higher one); do it in small bends and recheck the dimension each time. (5) Float travel: check the distance the float moves between the needle closed and the float hanging; bend the second (stop) tab for 41-43 mm. (6) Refit the cover with a new gasket and run the engine to check for leaks.
- **Verify:** both dimensions inside the table; no petrol dripping from the cover or the bowl vent after a drive; the idle does not drift rich with the engine warm.
- **Don't:** bend the float arm by hand without a gauge; do not reuse the old cover gasket; do not use the 26.5-28.5 mm of the Brosol 2E-CE for the Weber.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 184 and 197 (Weber DMPV: float height 8-9 mm, float travel 41-43 mm, both series; needle valve body 175), p. 188-191 (cover and float removal, float pin 2.5 mm), p. 198 (Brosol 2E-CE float height 26.5-28.5 mm), read 2026-11-15; AP engine workshop manual (compilation), PDF p. 263-268 (Brosol 2E7: float checked with a home-made gauge, cover tilted about 30 degrees, 26.5-28.5 mm), read 2026-11-15; fiatduna.com.ar Weber 32/34 thread (secondary, forum: gasket in place, bend the tab). Not verified in a primary source: where the 8-9 mm is measured from on the DMPV, how the 41-43 mm travel is measured and which tab sets it (not described in the pages read, general Weber practice).

### VEH-GRP-64: Check the choke pull-off gap and the positive throttle opening of the Weber DMPV
- **Rule:** Check the choke plate gap with the vacuum device and the opening of the primary throttle with the choke closed: **choke plate opening 3.7 to 4.3 mm and positive throttle opening 0.55 to 0.75 mm** for the Weber DMPV made from November 1991 (3.33 to 3.37 mm and 0.65 to 0.85 mm for the earlier 015.26).
- **Why:** the gap is how much the vacuum diaphragm opens the choke once the engine fires; too small floods the engine, too large makes it stall when cold; the throttle opening sets the fast idle of a cold start.
- **How:** (1) Carburettor on the car or on the bench, air filter off. (2) Disconnect the vacuum hose of the pneumatic choke device. (3) Close the choke plate fully with its lever. (4) Apply about 350 mmHg of vacuum to the device with a hand pump (the Brosol 2E7 chapter uses this value): the plate opens; measure the gap between the plate edge and the barrel wall with drill bits of the diameters of the gap, and compare with the table. (5) If the gap is wrong, turn the adjusting screw of the device with a screwdriver while the vacuum is held; check the device for leaks (the vacuum must hold). (6) Positive throttle opening: with the carburettor mounted and the choke closed, the primary throttle must open a small angle; measure the gap with a drill bit and bend the linkage (or turn the screw the carburettor has) to the table. (7) The second-barrel pneumatic positioner (item 15 on p. 191) can be tested with the same pump: the second throttle must start to open when the primary has opened to a fixed angle and be fully open when the primary is. (8) Refit and test the cold start (VEH-GRP-15).
- **Verify:** gap and opening inside the table, vacuum holds on the pump, the choke opens on a cold start without stalling.
- **Don't:** use a bent drill or a gauge of the wrong size; do not bend the choke lever linkage to hide a split diaphragm; do not apply more vacuum than a hand pump gives.
- **Source:** Workshop manual, printed p. 184 (choke plate opening 3.33-3.37 mm, positive throttle opening 0.65-0.85 mm), p. 197 (3.7-4.3 mm and 0.55-0.75 mm, table checked on the page image), p. 189-191 (choke lever, pneumatic opening device, second-barrel pneumatic device), read 2026-11-15; AP engine workshop manual (compilation), PDF p. 266-270 (Brosol 2E7: pneumatic choke device checked with a vacuum pump at about 350 mmHg, gap measured with drills, positive throttle opening, second-barrel positioner test), read 2026-11-15. Not verified in a primary source: that the method of the 2E7 chapter applies step for step to the Weber DMPV (the Gol manual gives only the values and the parts), and the vacuum value for the DMPV.
