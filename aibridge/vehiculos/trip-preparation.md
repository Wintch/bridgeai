# Trip preparation

For drivers who will cover hundreds or thousands of kilometres in Argentina, many of them in old cars. Each rule has an ID (`VEH-VIA-n`)
so that a message or a checklist can cite it. The legal list of what you must carry, speed limits and fines live in
[traffic-law.md](traffic-law.md) and [fines-and-points.md](fines-and-points.md); here is the practical side: what to check, what to
pack, how to drive on difficult roads, what to do when it goes wrong, and how to cross a border.

**Safety and legal caveat.** This is information, not legal advice. Border and document rules change often (digital documents,
Chilean customs, minors' permits): confirm on the official page named in each rule in the week before you travel. A pre-trip check
does not replace a workshop: if the brakes, steering, tyres or fuel system show any doubt, a professional looks at them before you
leave (see [car-maintenance-basics.md](car-maintenance-basics.md)). Never work under a car on a jack alone, and never open a hot
cooling system. Emergency numbers: 911 (national emergency line; coverage varies by province), 107 (ambulance, SAME; CABA and part of
PBA), 100 (firefighters).

## Before leaving: the car

### VEH-VIA-1: Do the pre-trip check a week before, not the night before
- **Rule:** the driver must check, before entering the road, that the driver and the vehicle are in adequate safety condition; the responsibility is the driver's. Schedule the check and any service one week before departure so that parts and workshop slots are available.
- **Why:** Ley 24.449 art. 39 a) puts the verification on the driver. A problem found the night before becomes a cancelled trip or a risky departure; found a week before, it is a normal repair.
- **How:** (1) Book the service or check-up 7-10 days ahead if the car is due for oil, filters, belts or brakes, or has more than 10,000 km since the last service. (2) Do rules VEH-VIA-2 to VEH-VIA-10 yourself at home on a flat surface with the engine cold. (3) Drive 30-50 km at highway speed with the final luggage load, then re-check for leaks, noises, smells and temperature. (4) Fix what you found, then print the checklist at the end of this document.
- **Verify:** every line of the checklist ticked at least two days before leaving; the 30-50 km test drive produced no new leak, noise, warning light or vibration.
- **Don't:** leave on a long trip with a "small" symptom (a vibration, a soft brake pedal, a coolant level that drops) hoping it will go away under load.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 39 a) (consolidated text, ISEV edition updated to Feb 2019, read 2026-11-15). The 7-10 day timing is practical advice: Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-VIA-2: Check engine oil and all fluids cold, on level ground
- **Rule:** before a long trip check engine oil, coolant, brake fluid, power steering fluid (if fitted), clutch fluid (if hydraulic) and washer fluid; top up to the marks and look for leaks under the car.
- **Why:** a long trip at constant high load and high ambient temperature consumes oil and exposes small leaks and weak hoses; low brake or coolant level is a safety failure, not a nuisance.
- **How:** (1) Park on level ground, engine off at least 5 minutes (oil) and fully cold (coolant). (2) Oil: wipe the dipstick, insert fully, read between MIN and MAX; use the grade in the owner's manual. (3) Coolant: look at the expansion tank marks only; never open the cap hot (see VEH-VIA-9). (4) Brake fluid: level between MIN and MAX; a level that is falling means worn pads or a leak: workshop. (5) Put a clean cardboard under the engine overnight and look for drops in the morning. (6) Carry 1 litre of the correct oil and 2 litres of coolant mix.
- **Verify:** levels read at MAX or just below; the cardboard is dry; the oil on the dipstick is not milky (coolant contamination) or gritty.
- **Don't:** overfill oil above MAX; top up brake fluid repeatedly without finding why it drops; mix different coolant types.
- **Source:** general practice from vehicle owner manuals (any maker); Not verified in a primary source: no single Argentine regulation sets this procedure. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-3: Inspect belts and hoses, and replace the doubtful ones
- **Rule:** inspect the accessory belt(s) (alternator, water pump, fan, power steering) and all coolant and fuel hoses; replace any that is cracked, glazed, frayed, swollen, soft or leaking, and confirm the timing belt is within its interval.
- **Why:** a broken accessory belt stops the alternator and water pump within minutes (overheating); a split coolant hose empties the system; a snapped timing belt can wreck some engines. Rubber ages with time, not only with kilometres.
- **How:** (1) With the engine off, look along the whole length of each belt for cracks across the ribs, missing chunks, oily or shiny surfaces; press the belt midway: roughly 10 mm of deflection is typical, the manual gives the exact value. (2) Squeeze the radiator hoses cold: they must be firm but not rock hard or spongy; check the ends at the clamps for white or green crust (leak). (3) Check fuel hoses and clamps for seepage and cracks (carburetted cars have rubber hoses on the carburettor and pump). (4) Look up the timing belt interval in the manual (see car-maintenance-basics.md); if the age or the km is unknown, change it before the trip.
- **Verify:** belts without cracks; hoses firm with dry clamps; timing belt date and km written in the service book.
- **Don't:** tighten a squealing belt to the limit (it ruins the pump bearings); leave a hose "to be done on the way back".
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source (no regulation). Read 2026-11-15. Timing belt interval: see the vehicle manual and [vw-gol-1994-specs.md](vw-gol-1994-specs.md) for that model.

### VEH-VIA-4: Check all five tyres: pressure loaded, tread, age, damage
- **Rule:** before the trip check tread depth (legal minimum 1.6 mm in the central zone), sidewalls, age, and set the pressure for the loaded car; include the spare wheel.
- **Why:** tyres are the only contact with the road; a tyre with no legal tread depth is both a fine and an accident risk; heat, load and speed on a long trip are what burst weak tyres.
- **How:** (1) Tread: the wear indicators (small bars in the grooves) must not be flush with the tread; measure with a depth gauge (see car-maintenance-basics.md). (2) Look for cuts, bulges, cracks, nails, uneven wear on inner edges. (3) Pressure, cold: use the sticker on the door pillar or fuel flap or the manual, with the value for full load/highway if two values are given; check again at every second fuel stop (hot tyres read about 0.2-0.4 bar higher: do not release air). (4) Check the spare: pressure, tread, age, valve cap. (5) Check wheel nuts are tight with the wheel brace.
- **Verify:** depth gauge reads 3 mm or more for a long trip (1.6 mm is only the legal floor); all five pressures noted on the checklist.
- **Don't:** run the tyre pressure "soft for comfort" on a loaded car; ignore a cracked sidewall; use a tyre older than the manufacturer's advice (see car-maintenance-basics.md on tyre age).
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 29 of the regulation, item 4.3 (minimum tread 1.6 mm) and art. 48 l) (prohibition to drive with faulty tyres or without legal tread), consolidated text; Decreto 196/2025 art. 10 repeats the 1.6 mm figure (read 2026-11-15). The 3 mm recommendation is practical advice: Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-VIA-5: Make sure the spare wheel, jack and wheel brace work
- **Rule:** the car must carry a usable spare wheel (full size or the one the maker supplied), a jack, a wheel brace that fits the wheel nuts, and the locking-nut key if the wheels have anti-theft nuts.
- **Why:** on a remote road a puncture is routine; with no working spare, a tow can cost a day and hundreds of kilometres. Seized wheel nuts and missing keys are the common reason a puncture becomes a breakdown.
- **How:** (1) Take the spare out of its place and check pressure; a temporary "space-saver" spare has a speed limit printed on it (usually 80 km/h). (2) Loosen one wheel nut with the car's own brace to prove it fits and turns. (3) Learn where the jacking points are (owner's manual). (4) Carry a wheel chock or a piece of wood, gloves, a torch and a mat. (5) For gravel or Patagonia consider two spares or a tyre repair kit plus a 12 V compressor.
- **Verify:** you can change a wheel at home in 15-20 minutes without help.
- **Don't:** use the jack alone to work under the car (use jack stands, see car-maintenance-basics.md); keep the locking-nut key "somewhere at home".
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 29 reg. item 4.4 (a spare wheel in emergency use may be asymmetric; observe the pressure and speed marked on it), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; rest is general practice: Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-VIA-6: Test all lights and replace wipers and washer
- **Rule:** check low beam, high beam, side lights, brake lights, indicators, hazard lights, reversing light, plate light and fog lights; replace worn wiper blades; fill the washer reservoir.
- **Why:** low beams must be on at all times on national routes, and long trips include dusk and night; a burnt bulb is both a fine and a collision risk, and the technical inspection (*VTV*) rejects missing lights.
- **How:** (1) With a helper or against a wall or shop window, operate each light in turn; press the brake pedal and look at the reflection. (2) Clean the lenses; hazy plastic lenses can be polished. (3) Carry a spare bulb for each type (see VEH-VIA-33). (4) Wiper blades: replace if they smear or chatter; they cost little. (5) Check the aim of the headlights if you carry a heavy load.
- **Verify:** every light works; the washer jets spray on the glass; no streaks in a rain test (use the washer).
- **Don't:** drive at night with one headlight; rely on only the daytime running lights at dusk or in rain if they do not light the rear.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 47 a) and its regulation (low beams or daytime running lights on at all times on national routes), consolidated text with Ley 27.425 and Decreto 32/2018, read 2026-11-15; Ley 24.449 art. 31 (lighting system).

### VEH-VIA-7: Have the brakes inspected before a long trip
- **Rule:** check the brake pedal feel, pad or shoe thickness, disc and drum condition, fluid level and its age, and the handbrake; if anything is doubtful, have a workshop inspect and repair it before leaving.
- **Why:** mountain descents and heavy loads brake much harder and for longer than city use; worn brakes or old fluid (which absorbs water and boils) can fade or fail.
- **How:** (1) The pedal must be firm, not spongy, and must not sink slowly under steady pressure. (2) Listen for metal-on-metal grinding or squeal when braking; look for a car that pulls to one side. (3) Look through the wheel spokes: pad friction material below about 3 mm means replacement. (4) Check the date of the last brake fluid change; if more than 2 years or unknown, change it (see car-maintenance-basics.md). (5) Hold the car on a gentle slope with the handbrake only to test it. (6) Any brake repair is a workshop job unless you are experienced.
- **Verify:** workshop report or your own record: pads/shoes thickness in mm, fluid date, no leaks at the wheels.
- **Don't:** ride the brakes down a long descent (use a low gear, see VEH-VIA-28); bleed or open brake lines without knowing how.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 29 a) 1 of the regulation (the braking system must be safe and effective); thresholds are general practice from owner manuals: Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-8: Test the battery and the charging system
- **Rule:** before a long trip test the battery (voltage at rest and while starting) and the alternator output, clean the terminals, and replace a battery that is old or weak.
- **Why:** a dead battery stops the car far from help; batteries fail more in extreme heat and cold, and a failing alternator drains the battery silently.
- **How:** (1) Engine off, 30 minutes after driving: a healthy 12 V battery reads about 12.6 V; below 12.4 V is a charge or health problem. (2) Engine running at 2,000 rpm, lights on: 13.8-14.7 V at the terminals is normal; less means a belt or alternator fault. (3) Look at the date code on the battery: over 4-5 years is on borrowed time. (4) Clean white or blue corrosion with hot water and a brush and tighten clamps by hand with a spanner (not with pliers). (5) Carry jumper leads (see VEH-VIA-13). Details in car-maintenance-basics.md.
- **Verify:** the readings above written on the checklist; the engine starts at the first try cold and hot.
- **Don't:** disconnect the battery with the engine running; trust a battery that cranks slowly "because it still starts".
- **Source:** general practice from battery makers and owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-9: Prepare the cooling system for summer heat
- **Rule:** before a summer trip confirm the coolant mixture and level, the radiator and its hoses, the fan operation, the thermostat and the radiator cap, and that the radiator fins are clean.
- **Why:** high ambient temperature, load, air conditioning and long climbs are what reveal a weak cooling system; overheating can crack a cylinder head within minutes.
- **How:** (1) Coolant: the correct concentrated coolant mixed about 50/50 with distilled water unless the manual says otherwise; renew it if it is older than the interval or brown. (2) Level in the expansion tank between the marks when cold. (3) Start the engine and let it warm up: the electric fan (if fitted) must start around the temperature in the manual; the lower hose must get hot only after the thermostat opens. (4) Look through the front of the radiator for mud, insects, leaves and bent fins; blow with water or air from the back to the front. (5) Replace a radiator cap whose seal is cracked. (6) Know what to do if the gauge rises (see VEH-VIA-32).
- **Verify:** the temperature needle sits at the middle in a 30 km test drive including a hill; no coolant on the ground after parking.
- **Don't:** open the cap or expansion tank cap on a hot engine (scalding steam); top up with plain water as a permanent fix (it corrodes and boils sooner).
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-10: Load the car safely and respect the maximum weight
- **Rule:** keep the load inside the vehicle capacity and seating, keep heavy items low and forward of the rear axle, secure them, and do not let the load hide lights, plate or the driver's view.
- **Why:** an overloaded or badly loaded car brakes badly, overheats, wears tyres and can swing; an unsecured object becomes a projectile in a crash.
- **How:** (1) The car's plate or manual gives the maximum permitted weight and the rear axle limit; weigh if you are close. (2) Put heavy items on the floor of the boot against the rear seat; use straps or a net. (3) Roof box or rack: respect the roof load limit, which is low (typically 50-75 kg including the rack); the car's handling changes and the fuel use rises. (4) Raise tyre pressure to the loaded value (VEH-VIA-4). (5) Keep the number of occupants within the number of belts and head restraints.
- **Verify:** all seats have a belt in use; rear view and lights are not blocked; the car does not scrape on bumps.
- **Don't:** load the boot to the roof against the rear window; leave loose objects on the rear shelf.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 40 g) and k) (number of occupants related to the capacity; use of seat belts) and art. 48 q) (it is prohibited to carry a load that disturbs visibility, affects aerodynamics, hides lights or protrudes beyond the permitted limits), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; roof load limits: Not verified in a primary source (see the vehicle manual).

## Documents and equipment

### VEH-VIA-11: Carry the documents the law requires, plus the extras
- **Rule:** to drive on the road you need a valid driving licence for that type of vehicle, the vehicle title card (*cédula verde*) or the driver's authorisation card, and the current mandatory insurance certificate; carry the national ID (*DNI*), and make copies. The full legal list and the digital options are in traffic-law.md.
- **Why:** driving without them stops the trip at the next checkpoint; the regulation allows the documents to be shown on paper or in digital format, but a phone with no battery or no signal is not a document.
- **How:** (1) Licence, vehicle card and insurance certificate on paper in the glove box, in addition to the digital copies in the Mi Argentina app. (2) Take photographs of each and keep them offline on the phone. (3) Check the expiry dates of the licence, the insurance and the technical inspection (*VTV*) sticker or certificate. (4) For a car you do not own, see VEH-VIA-42. (5) Add the insurer's assistance number and your policy number written on paper (do not post it online).
- **Verify:** each document in date; you can show all three in under 30 seconds.
- **Don't:** assume an expired technical inspection is tolerated on a trip; leave documents in the boot with the luggage.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 40 a), b) and c) (licence, vehicle card, insurance certificate), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; Decreto 196/2025 art. 14 (new art. 37 of the regulation: documents may be shown in digital or physical form) and art. 16 (new art. 40), read through argentina.gob.ar on 2026-11-15 (partial read). See [traffic-law.md](traffic-law.md).

### VEH-VIA-12: Carry the mandatory safety equipment: extinguisher and warning triangles
- **Rule:** carry a standardised fire extinguisher (*matafuego*) fixed in the place the maker indicates, and portable warning triangles (*balizas*) in an accessible place, not buried under the luggage.
- **Why:** both are legal requirements to circulate (except for motorcycles), the technical inspection checks them, and you need them at the worst moment.
- **How:** (1) Extinguisher for a car or light van (categories M1 and N1) up to 3,500 kg: the regulation text up to 2019 required one extinguisher of 1 kg nominal capacity, 3 B extinguishing potential, with a pressure gauge; the technical inspection checks an ABC 1 kg unit with a charge or manufacturing date not older than one year (secondary source). (2) Triangles: at least two (the earlier text required two), retro-reflective, stable in winds up to 70 km/h; Decreto 196/2025 rewrote this article and the text read says only that the triangles must be carried in an accessible place, so keep two. (3) Fix the extinguisher with its bracket in the cabin (not loose in the boot). (4) Check the gauge needle in the green zone and the date on the label. (5) Add a high-visibility vest for every occupant who may leave the car.
- **Verify:** extinguisher in date with gauge in the green; two triangles in their box within reach of the driver; the rule in traffic-law.md still gives the same numbers.
- **Don't:** use a rechargeable-type extinguisher you have not had inspected; carry the extinguisher loose where it can hit the occupants.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 40 f) and art. 29 b) of the regulation (extinguisher and triangles), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; Decreto 196/2025 art. 10 (new art. 29 of the regulation; partial read: number of triangles and extinguisher mass were not found in the text read); the one-year charge age and ABC 1 kg at the inspection: Not verified in a primary source (iProfesional article, 2023).

### VEH-VIA-13: Pack the practical equipment list
- **Rule:** pack a basic kit of tools, supplies and personal items suited to the length and remoteness of the trip.
- **Why:** most roadside problems (puncture, flat battery, loose hose, empty tank, minor electrical fault) are fixable in minutes with the right small item, and assistance on a remote route can take hours.
- **How:** use the table below and add to it for Patagonia or the mountains.

| Group | Items |
|---|---|
| Tyres and wheels | spare wheel checked, jack, wheel brace, locking-nut key, 12 V compressor, puncture repair kit, wheel chock, gloves |
| Starting | jumper leads (at least 16 mm² for cars), towing strap or rope, a portable jump-starter pack |
| Tools | spanner set, screwdrivers, pliers, adjustable spanner, cable ties, insulating tape, duct tape, fuse puller, torch with spare batteries or a head torch, multimeter if you know how to use it |
| Fluids | 1 L engine oil, 2 L coolant mix, 1 L brake fluid (sealed), washer fluid, drinking water 5 L |
| Spares | see VEH-VIA-33 for the old-car list |
| Safety | extinguisher, triangles, vests, first-aid kit, blanket, whistle |
| People | water, food, sun protection, medicines, charger and power bank, paper map of the route, a warm coat even in summer in Patagonia |
| Documents | VEH-VIA-11, a pen, paper copies of the phone numbers |

- **Verify:** the kit fits in the boot without blocking the rear view; you know where each item is.
- **Don't:** carry fuel in the cabin or unapproved containers; overload the car with the kit.
- **Source:** general practice (ACA, manufacturers and travel guides); Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-14: Seat everyone correctly: belts and child restraints
- **Rule:** every occupant uses a seat belt, and children under 10 travel in the rear seat in an approved child restraint system sized to their weight.
- **Why:** the law requires it, and on long trips at 100 km/h or more the belt decides the outcome of a crash.
- **How:** (1) Count belts and head restraints before assigning seats. (2) Use a child seat or booster of the correct group for weight and height, installed with the car's belt or ISOFIX if available. (3) Never carry a child on the lap or between the arms of the driver. (4) Check belts are not frayed or stuck. (5) Pack pets in a carrier or with a pet harness, not loose.
- **Verify:** all belts click; child seat is firmly held (less than 2-3 cm movement at the belt path).
- **Don't:** transport people in the boot or more people than belts.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 40 g) (children under 10 in the rear seat) and g.1 and k) of the regulation (restraint system; belts for all occupants), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15.

## Planning

### VEH-VIA-15: Plan daily stages and rest stops, not just the total distance
- **Rule:** divide the journey into daily stages that you can drive in daylight with a rest every 2 hours, and book lodging for the end of each stage.
- **Why:** fatigue and night driving cause many long-trip crashes; hurrying to make a distance leads to driving tired and at night, when animals and unlit vehicles are the greatest danger.
- **How:** (1) A comfortable stage for one driver is 6-8 hours of driving (roughly 500-700 km on good highway, much less on gravel or in mountains). (2) Include a stop at least every 2 hours (see VEH-VIA-22) and a proper meal. (3) Use the map of the route and the road status (VEH-VIA-18) to estimate the hours realistically; add 30% for roadworks and trucks. (4) Plan to arrive before dark. (5) Share the plan with someone at home.
- **Verify:** each day ends in a named town with a booked bed; each stage has a fuel stop planned.
- **Don't:** plan more than one driver-day of distance per day because "the road is straight".
- **Source:** the 2-hour rest rule is common road-safety advice: Not verified in a primary source; the daily distances are practical rules. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-16: Plan fuel stops on long empty routes (Ruta 40, Patagonia)
- **Rule:** on routes where service stations are far apart or unreliable, refill at every opportunity, never let the tank drop below half, and know your car's real range with margin.
- **Why:** in Santa Cruz and other parts of Patagonia there are stretches of about 200-350 km with no or unreliable fuel, stations sometimes run out, and a wind or gravel surface increases consumption.
- **How:** (1) Compute the real range: tank capacity (litres) divided by consumption on the worst surface (gravel and headwind can add 30% or more). (2) Mark every station on the route and the distance between them; ask at the last station whether the next one has fuel. (3) Refill when half-empty, even if it means a short detour. (4) For stretches near or over your range carry an approved fuel container fixed outside the cabin (not inside). (5) Carry cash (some stations accept only cash or have no connection). (6) Check at the previous town whether the pump has your grade of fuel (see VEH-VIA-46).
- **Verify:** on the route sheet every leg between stations is shorter than 60% of the real range.
- **Don't:** trust the dashboard "range" figure on gravel; assume the next station has fuel; carry the can in the passenger area.
- **Source:** secondary sources (a Ruta 40 tourism site and travel guides, read 2026-11-15) describe a stretch of about 350 km without stations on the Bariloche-El Calafate route, and that some stations at Bajo Caracoles, Tres Lagos and Gobernador Gregores are sometimes out of fuel: Not verified in a primary source. Check the current list with YPF or the provincial tourism office.

### VEH-VIA-17: Use the service-station networks and know their limits
- **Rule:** plan stops at stations of the main networks (YPF, Axion, Shell, Puma) and at the Automóvil Club Argentino (*ACA*) stations and offices where they exist; assume remote stations have limited stock and hours.
- **Why:** big-network stations have more reliable fuel quality, card payment, toilets and repair help; ACA offices provide membership assistance and maps.
- **How:** (1) Download the network apps or the maps (offline) before leaving. (2) If you are an ACA member (or covered by a motor-club agreement through your insurer), note the assistance number and the coverage area and kilometres of free tow. (3) At night, stop at lit stations on the highway, not at isolated pumps. (4) Keep the fuel receipt for the day if you suspect bad fuel.
- **Verify:** you have the assistance number saved and written on paper, and know your cover.
- **Don't:** assume your insurer's assistance covers remote gravel roads; ask before leaving.
- **Source:** general practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15. See [insurance.md](insurance.md) for what assistance policies cover.

### VEH-VIA-18: Check the state of the roads with Vialidad Nacional and the provinces
- **Rule:** the day before and the morning of each stage, check the road status for the national routes you will use, and for provincial routes check the provincial road authority.
- **Why:** snow, ice, floods, roadworks, fires and wind can close or damage routes; Ruta 40 and mountain roads are often closed in winter.
- **How:** (1) Open the "Estado de Rutas" page of Vialidad Nacional (argentina.gob.ar/transporte/vialidad-nacional/estado-de-rutas). (2) The status codes used in reports are normal passable (TN), passable with caution (TP), passable with extreme caution (TEP) and impassable (INT). (3) For concession routes check the concessionaire's web or phone on the toll plaza signs. (4) Check provincial authorities (for example the road directorates of each province) and local news and social media. (5) At a stop, ask truckers and station staff: they know the local state of the road.
- **Verify:** you have a status for every route of the next stage, with the time you read it.
- **Don't:** depend on one app; rely on a status read two days ago.
- **Source:** argentina.gob.ar/transporte/vialidad-nacional page and its link to Estado de Rutas (read 2026-11-15); the status codes come from a news report about Chubut roads: Not verified in a primary source. The institutional situation of Vialidad Nacional was disputed in the courts after Decreto 461/2025 (July 2025); the road status page was online when read.

### VEH-VIA-19: Check the weather and the National Weather Service (SMN) alerts
- **Rule:** read the SMN alerts and forecast for the whole route before leaving and again during the trip; change or delay the stage when there is a warning for wind, snow, ice, storms, floods or fog.
- **Why:** strong winds, snowfall and floods are the main causes of route closures and of vehicles being stranded in Patagonia and the mountains.
- **How:** (1) Open smn.gob.ar/alertas and look at the colour level for each province and department of the route: yellow, orange and red alerts increase in severity. (2) For mountain passes also check the pass operator (VEH-VIA-45). (3) In summer afternoons in the central and northern regions storms form quickly: plan to be indoors by late afternoon on the alert days. (4) Never drive through water over a road ("vado"): flowing water can carry a car away.
- **Verify:** alert level noted for each stage on the checklist; a decision made (go, delay, reroute).
- **Don't:** ignore an alert because the sky looks clear at departure.
- **Source:** Servicio Meteorológico Nacional, smn.gob.ar/alertas: the page could not be read when checked (HTTP error), so the alert level meanings are Not verified in a primary source; confirm on the site on the day. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-20: Share your itinerary and prepare for no signal
- **Rule:** before leaving, tell a person at home your route, stops and expected arrival times, download offline maps, and carry a paper map and a power bank.
- **Why:** large parts of Ruta 40 and the Patagonian steppe have no mobile coverage; if you break down, someone must know when to start worrying and where to look.
- **How:** (1) Send the plan (route, lodging, daily check-in times) to a trusted person. (2) Download offline maps of the whole route; save the phone numbers on paper. (3) Charge the phone and a power bank; carry a 12 V charger. (4) When there is no signal, tell the last station or town where you are heading. (5) In truly remote areas consider a satellite messenger.
- **Verify:** the contact person can name your next stop and the time you will check in.
- **Don't:** leave a remote route without telling anyone.
- **Source:** general practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

## Driving technique

### VEH-VIA-21: Keep the low beams on, day and night, on national routes
- **Rule:** on national routes keep the low beams (or daytime running lights) on at all times, day and night, whatever the natural light; use high beams only where the road is dark and there is no oncoming traffic, and dip them before crossing a vehicle.
- **Why:** it is mandatory on national routes, and it makes you visible at distance; dipping avoids dazzling the oncoming driver.
- **How:** (1) Turn on the low beams when you leave the town and leave them on until the end of the route; a car with automatic lights does it for you. (2) High beams: only in rural zones and motorways when the natural light is insufficient or visibility requires, and dip in time before an oncoming vehicle. (3) Use the flash of the headlights to warn of an overtake. (4) Keep headlights clean and aligned.
- **Verify:** the lights indicator is on; the rear lights come on with the low beams (check in a shop window).
- **Don't:** assume that the daytime running lights light the rear; they often do not (relevant in rain and dusk).
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 47 a) and b) with its regulation art. 47 (low beams or daytime running lights on national routes at all times; dipping before crossing), consolidated text with Ley 27.425 and Decreto 32/2018, read 2026-11-15. Check [traffic-law.md](traffic-law.md) for provincial rules.

### VEH-VIA-22: Rest every two hours and never drive tired
- **Rule:** stop at least every two hours (or 200 km), walk for 10-15 minutes, drink water, change drivers when you can, and stop at once if you feel drowsy.
- **Why:** fatigue slows reactions like alcohol; the highest-risk times are the early morning, the post-lunch dip and the late night.
- **How:** (1) Fix the stops on the plan (see VEH-VIA-15). (2) If you yawn, drift in the lane or miss a turn, stop at the next safe place and sleep 20 minutes; coffee helps only a little and for a short time. (3) Eat light meals while driving; avoid heavy lunches. (4) Take no alcohol, and ask about the effect of any medicine on your driving. (5) Share the driving where there are two drivers.
- **Verify:** driving time per stage on the checklist is in blocks of two hours or less.
- **Don't:** "push on to arrive"; use the rumble strips as an alarm clock.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 48 a) (prohibition to drive with physical impairment, drugs or medicines that reduce aptitude or with alcohol above the limit; consolidated text, limits may have been lowered later, see traffic-law.md) read 2026-11-15; the 2-hour rest is common road-safety advice: Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-VIA-23: Overtake with care on two-lane routes, especially trucks
- **Rule:** overtake only on the left, when the road ahead is clear for a safe distance, never near a curve, a crest, a bridge or a junction, with signals, and return to the right quickly.
- **Why:** head-on collisions on two-lane routes during overtaking are among the worst crashes; a loaded truck takes a long time to pass and hides the road ahead.
- **How:** (1) Hold back at a safe distance (at least 2 seconds) to see beyond the truck. (2) Check the mirror: nobody should be overtaking you. (3) Warn with the headlight flash (or the horn in rural zones) and the left indicator. (4) Accelerate and pass quickly, then return with the right indicator once you can see the truck in the mirror. (5) A left indicator on the vehicle ahead means it may be about to overtake or turn: do not start the manoeuvre. (6) If in doubt, wait for a long straight with a clear road, or an overtaking lane. (7) With a low-power car (as in many 1990s cars) take twice the distance you think you need.
- **Verify:** you started the manoeuvre with a clear view of at least the distance you will need at the speed of the oncoming traffic.
- **Don't:** overtake in a column "to catch up"; overtake with the oncoming traffic near; overtake on a solid centre line.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 42 (overtaking rules a-h; trucks must facilitate overtaking on narrow roads by moving to the shoulder), art. 48 j) (no lane changes or overtaking in curves, junctions and dangerous zones), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; the 2-second gap is in art. 48 g) of the regulation.

### VEH-VIA-24: Handle strong wind, as in Patagonia
- **Rule:** in strong crosswinds reduce speed, hold the wheel with both hands, expect gusts at gaps, bridges and when passing trucks, and keep a safe lateral margin.
- **Why:** crosswinds can push a light car out of its lane, especially a tall, loaded or roof-rack car; the effect is sudden when you pass a truck or leave a sheltered stretch.
- **How:** (1) Slow down earlier than you think; 80-90 km/h in gusty sections. (2) Anticipate: a row of trees or a hill ending means a gust. (3) Pass trucks with extra margin: the truck shields you and then the wind returns. (4) Do not use a roof box or high load in windy sections; remove it if the SMN alerts for wind. (5) Open doors with care: the wind can tear a door from your hand and bend the hinges. (6) Park facing the wind.
- **Verify:** the car stays in its lane without large steering corrections.
- **Don't:** drive fast in the wind to "get out of it"; get out of the car at the roadside in a strong gust without holding the door.
- **Source:** general practice from the Patagonian road guides and the SMN wind alerts; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-25: Drive gravel roads (*ripio*) slowly and smoothly
- **Rule:** on gravel reduce speed, keep a larger distance, avoid sudden braking or steering, and slow down for oncoming traffic and for stones thrown by other vehicles.
- **Why:** traction is much lower; flying stones break windscreens and tyres; washboard (*calamina*) and soft shoulders can throw the car off the road; dust hides oncoming traffic.
- **How:** (1) Typical safe speed is 50-70 km/h, lower in washboard or loose gravel. (2) Keep to the more compacted part of the road (usually the two wheel tracks); avoid soft piles on the edge. (3) Brake early and gently; steer smoothly. (4) Slow down and keep right when a vehicle approaches, to avoid thrown stones; keep a 100 m margin behind another car. (5) Use the lights in dust. (6) Check tyre pressure: slightly lower improves grip and comfort but weakens the sidewalls; do not lower much on a car with a low-profile tyre. (7) Check the underbody after rough sections. (8) Carry two spares or a repair kit in remote areas.
- **Verify:** no cracks in the windscreen, tyres intact after the section.
- **Don't:** drive at asphalt speed; follow a truck closely; brake hard in a corner.
- **Source:** secondary sources: travel forum and Ruta 40 tourism site advise 50-60 km/h on gravel and describe washboard stretches; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-26: Drive carefully in fog, rain and at night
- **Rule:** in fog, heavy rain or at night, reduce speed to match visibility, increase the following distance, use low beams (and fog lights only in fog), and stop at a safe place when you cannot see the road.
- **Why:** many multi-vehicle crashes on routes happen in fog; stopping distance on wet roads is double that of dry roads; at night unlit vehicles, cyclists, animals and pedestrians appear late.
- **How:** (1) Use low beams, not high beams (high beams reflect off the fog). (2) Use the front and rear fog lights only while visibility is poor, and turn them off after. (3) Keep at least 2 seconds behind the car ahead, 4 seconds or more in rain. (4) Use the road edge line as a guide, and slow when approaching junctions and tolls. (5) If you must stop, leave the carriageway completely, switch on the hazard lights and have everyone wait behind the guardrail. (6) Avoid night driving on rural two-lane roads. (7) Clean the windscreen inside and outside; use the demister.
- **Verify:** you can stop within the distance you can see to be clear.
- **Don't:** follow the tail lights of the car ahead at high speed; stop on the carriageway.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 47 f) (fog lights only for their own purpose) and art. 50 (precautionary speed: the driver must always drive at a speed such that he can control the vehicle in the circumstances), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; the 2-second gap in art. 48 g) of the regulation; other figures are general practice: Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-VIA-27: Expect animals on the road
- **Rule:** on rural routes at dawn, dusk and night, slow down and expect cattle, horses, sheep, guanacos, rheas (*ñandúes*), foxes and dogs on the carriageway and the shoulder.
- **Why:** hitting a large animal at highway speed can kill the occupants; in Patagonia guanacos and sheep cross at all hours, and loose livestock is a common cause of crashes at night.
- **How:** (1) Reduce speed at dusk and night and at signs of animals. (2) Use the high beams when there is no traffic; eyes reflect the light. (3) If an animal appears, brake firmly in a straight line; do not swerve into the opposite lane. (4) If you see one animal, expect more. (5) After a crash with an animal, put out the triangles and call 911 or the road concessionaire (VEH-VIA-35). (6) Leaving animals loose on a road is prohibited: the road authority or owner is responsible, which matters for the insurance claim.
- **Verify:** no night driving in unfenced cattle regions unless unavoidable; speed reduced at dusk.
- **Don't:** swerve at speed; stop at the roadside next to cattle in the dark.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 48 s) (prohibition to leave animals loose on the road; the road authority or company must file reports for removal) and its regulation, consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; the driving advice is general practice: Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-VIA-28: Cross mountain passes with the car prepared for altitude and cold
- **Rule:** before a mountain pass check the status and the hours, fill the tank, keep the engine and brakes cool using low gears, and prepare for cold, altitude and sudden weather changes.
- **Why:** long descents overheat the brakes, a carburetted or low-power engine loses power at altitude, passes are closed by snow, and people can feel the effects of altitude (headache, nausea).
- **How:** (1) Check the pass status (VEH-VIA-45) and the weather (VEH-VIA-19) before leaving. (2) Fill the tank before the climb. (3) Climb in a lower gear to keep the engine cool; on a descent use the same gear as for climbing it, so that the engine brakes the car, and brake in short pulses, not continuously. (4) At altitude expect about 3% less power for each 300 m of height (rule of thumb); a carburetted car may run rich and stall at idle: keep revs a little higher. (5) Carry warm clothes, water and a blanket; chains if there is a risk of snow (VEH-VIA-29). (6) Do not stop on the carriageway in a curve. (7) People: drink water, avoid alcohol and heavy food, and descend if someone gets very sick.
- **Verify:** temperature gauge steady; brakes firm at the bottom of the descent.
- **Don't:** descend in neutral or with the engine off; ride the brake.
- **Source:** the power-loss rule of thumb and the driving advice are general practice from manufacturers' manuals and mountain-driving guides: Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-29: Carry and use snow chains when the conditions require them
- **Rule:** when snow, ice or frost change the normal driving conditions, fit appropriate chains on the driven wheels; where a sign or the authority requires chains, you cannot proceed without them.
- **Why:** the regulation says that when climate phenomena such as snow or ice change the normal conditions, the driver must fit suitable chains to the tyres; without them the car blocks the road and risks a crash.
- **How:** (1) Buy chains of the right size for your tyre and test-fit them at home (the instructions are on the package) before the trip. (2) Fit them on the driving wheels (front for front-wheel-drive cars), at a safe parking place before the chain control point. (3) Drive at 30-40 km/h; remove them as soon as the road is clear (chains damage the asphalt and the chains). (4) Carry gloves, a mat to kneel on and a torch. (5) For a car with little clearance, consider textile "snow socks" if allowed by the pass authority: check.
- **Verify:** you can fit the chains in under 10 minutes in the dry.
- **Don't:** drive on bare asphalt with chains; wait to fit them until stuck.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 48 u) regulation (obligation to fit chains when snow, ice or similar conditions change the circulation conditions; chains are allowed on mud, snow and ice), consolidated text with Decreto 32/2018, read 2026-11-15; local chain-control rules (Mendoza, Neuquén, Río Negro and others) change by province: Not verified in a primary source.

## Old cars on long trips

### VEH-VIA-30: Prepare an old car for a long trip with a full service and a test run
- **Rule:** before a long trip with a car of the 1990s do a full service (oil, filters, plugs, belts, hoses, coolant, brake fluid, tyres) and a test run of at least 300-500 km on the same type of road, with the load, at least a week before departure.
- **Why:** old cars have components that have aged (rubber, wiring, cooling system) and fail suddenly under sustained load; the test run finds what the workshop did not.
- **How:** (1) Service per car-maintenance-basics.md and the model documents ([vw-gol-1994-specs.md](vw-gol-1994-specs.md), [vw-gol-1994-repairs.md](vw-gol-1994-repairs.md)). (2) Inspect the fuel system (hoses, tank, filter, pump) and the cooling system (radiator, hoses, water pump, thermostat, fan). (3) Check the electrical system: battery, terminals, alternator, earth straps. (4) Check suspension bushes, shock absorbers and wheel bearings. (5) After the test run, re-check levels and leaks. (6) Choose the route with towns and workshops along the way, and check that your insurer's assistance covers the full route.
- **Verify:** a 300-500 km run with no overheating, no stalling, no new noises; levels unchanged.
- **Don't:** start a thousand-kilometre trip with a car that has not been driven far in months (see car-maintenance-basics.md on a car parked for months).
- **Source:** general practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-31: Keep an old carburetted car from overheating and from vapour lock
- **Rule:** keep the cooling system in perfect condition, avoid long idling in heat, and know the signs of vapour lock (hot stall after a stop in heat) and how to handle it.
- **Why:** carburetted engines with mechanical fuel pumps in the engine bay heat the fuel; in hot weather and on hills the fuel boils in the lines or the pump and the engine loses power or stalls and does not restart until it cools. Alcohol-blended fuel boils at lower temperature.
- **How:** (1) Keep the coolant, thermostat, radiator and fan perfect (VEH-VIA-9). (2) Do not idle for long in traffic in heat; if you must stop for long, turn off the engine. (3) On a hot stall, stop in the shade, open the bonnet, wait 10-20 minutes; do not pour cold water on the carburettor or the engine. (4) Check that the heat shield, insulation and the hot-air ducts are in place; keep the fuel hoses away from the exhaust. (5) Keep the fuel tank above one quarter in hot weather and buy fresh fuel. (6) Start in a hot restart by pressing the accelerator pedal slowly to the floor once and holding it while cranking (flood-clear), without pumping. (7) If it repeats, see a workshop.
- **Verify:** the engine restarts after 15 minutes on a hot day; the temperature gauge stays at the middle.
- **Don't:** keep cranking a stalled hot engine (it floods the cylinders and drains the battery); open the radiator cap while it is hot.
- **Source:** general mechanical practice for carburetted cars (workshop knowledge and forum reports); Not verified in a primary source. The model-specific details are in [vw-gol-1994-repairs.md](vw-gol-1994-repairs.md). Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-32: React correctly when the temperature gauge rises
- **Rule:** if the temperature gauge goes to the red or the warning light comes on, turn the heater on at full, ease off the accelerator, stop as soon as you can in a safe place and switch the engine off; do not open the cooling system until it is cold.
- **Why:** driving on an overheated engine can warp the head and blow the head gasket in minutes; a hot cooling system can scald you.
- **How:** (1) Turn off the air conditioning and turn on the heater to the maximum (it removes heat from the engine); (2) look for a safe place to stop off the carriageway; (3) engine off, bonnet open from the inside release, stand clear of steam; (4) wait at least 30-45 minutes; (5) when cold, check the level in the expansion tank and top up with coolant or, in an emergency, clean water; (6) look for leaks, a broken belt or a fan not turning; (7) if the level drops again quickly, do not continue: call assistance. (8) If the gauge went into the red for more than a minute, have the engine checked afterwards (oil on the cap, white smoke, bubbles in the tank are signs of a head gasket failure).
- **Verify:** the temperature returns to the middle after refilling and a short run; the level does not drop.
- **Don't:** open the radiator cap hot; pour cold water into a hot engine; keep driving "to the next town".
- **Source:** general practice from owner manuals; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15. See also [car-maintenance-basics.md](car-maintenance-basics.md) on overheating diagnosis.

### VEH-VIA-33: Carry spares for a 1990s carburetted car
- **Rule:** for a long trip in a car of the 1990s, carry the spare parts that most often fail and are cheap and small, and know how to fit them.
- **Why:** for such a car many parts are hard to find on the road; replacing a belt or a fuse yourself saves a tow.
- **How:** use the list below; adapt it to the model (for the Gol see vw-gol-1994-specs.md).

| Group | Spares |
|---|---|
| Belts and cooling | accessory (alternator) belt of the correct size, 2 radiator hoses (upper and lower) or a universal hose and 4 clamps, 5 L of coolant mix, a spare radiator cap |
| Electrical | assorted fuses of every rating in the box (and a fuse puller), a set of bulbs (headlamp, brake/tail, indicator), a spare relay if the car uses one, 2 m of wire, connectors, tape |
| Ignition | a set of spark plugs (gapped), a distributor cap and rotor, ignition leads, coil if affordable, points and condenser if the ignition has them |
| Fuel | a spare fuel filter, 1 m of fuel hose and clamps, carburettor gasket set if you can fit it |
| Cables and controls | spare throttle or choke cable, clutch cable (cable-operated clutch), speedometer cable if known weak |
| Fluids | 1 L engine oil, 1 L brake fluid, 1 L distilled water |
| Wheels | see VEH-VIA-5 |
| Tools | the toolbox of VEH-VIA-13, including a spark-plug spanner and a feeler gauge |

- **Verify:** the list is in the boot and you have fitted each item at least once.
- **Don't:** carry parts you do not know how to fit; carry brake fluid that has been opened for a year.
- **Source:** general mechanical practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15. Model details: [vw-gol-1994-specs.md](vw-gol-1994-specs.md).

## Breakdowns and accidents on the road

### VEH-VIA-34: When the car stops, get it and the people off the carriageway
- **Rule:** if the car fails, pull onto the shoulder as far as possible, switch on the hazard lights, have everyone leave by the side away from traffic and wait behind the guardrail or far from the road, and place the warning triangles.
- **Why:** stopping on the carriageway or beside it with people nearby is how many secondary crashes happen; a stopped vehicle must be warned to the users of the road.
- **How:** (1) Signal and steer to the shoulder; do not stop on a bend or a crest if you can reach a safe place. (2) Hazard lights on; put on the high-visibility vests before leaving the car. (3) Passengers out through the right side, children first, and wait well behind the guardrail or on the embankment, never between the car and the traffic. (4) Place the triangles: one behind the car, and on a two-way road one in front too; the regulation text read does not give the distance; the usual guidance is about 50 m behind (more before a bend or crest, up to 100 m on a fast road): Not verified in a primary source. (5) Walk on the side facing the traffic. (6) Call for help (VEH-VIA-35).
- **Verify:** everyone is behind the barrier, the triangles are visible from at least 100-150 m in each direction.
- **Don't:** stay in the car on the shoulder of a fast road at night; repair the car on the carriageway side; use the triangles as the only warning on a curve.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 59 (a vehicle stopped on the carriageway or shoulder by force majeure must be warned to the users of the road at least with the immediate placement of regulation triangles), art. 48 i) (stopping on the shoulder is prohibited without an emergency), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15. Distances: see the note above.

### VEH-VIA-35: Know the emergency numbers and call the right one
- **Rule:** call 911 for an emergency with injuries, danger or crime; use 107 (SAME) for an ambulance where it exists, 100 for fire, and the numbers of your insurer, your assistance club and the route concessionaire for a breakdown.
- **Why:** 911 coverage and the availability of 107 differ by province, and the right call saves minutes.
- **How:** (1) 911: national emergency line (ambulance or police), centralising all emergencies in some provinces. (2) 107: ambulances of the SAME, covering the City of Buenos Aires and parts of the Province of Buenos Aires; elsewhere use 911 or the local hospital. (3) 100: firefighters. (4) 103: civil defence (floods, disasters). (5) Vialidad Nacional and the route concessionaires have assistance numbers on the signs along the route and the toll plaza; for a general contact Vialidad Nacional publishes a switchboard on its site. (6) Write your insurer's and the assistance number on paper. (7) Say where you are: the route and the kilometre post (*km*).
- **Verify:** the numbers are in the phone and on paper; you know the km post.
- **Don't:** rely on a single number; give up when there is no signal: walk to higher ground or the next vehicle.
- **Source:** argentina.gob.ar emergency numbers page (argentina.gob.ar/tema/emergencias, read 2026-11-15; as reported by search: 911, 100, 103, 105, 106, 107); Vialidad Nacional page argentina.gob.ar/transporte/vialidad-nacional (institutional switchboard (011) 3985-5700, read 2026-11-15). Numbers by province: Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-VIA-36: Call your insurer and the assistance club, and know what they cover
- **Rule:** call the assistance service of the insurer (or of the automobile club) before accepting any tow, say your exact location, and keep every receipt.
- **Why:** assistance cover usually includes a tow up to a number of kilometres and a destination; a tow arranged privately may not be refunded.
- **How:** (1) Have the policy number and the assistance phone ready (on paper; do not publish them). (2) Give the route, km post, direction, the car make and plate, and the nature of the problem. (3) Ask for a time estimate and for the name of the tow company; keep the call reference. (4) Do not leave the car unattended in a remote place if you can avoid it. (5) If you arrange a tow by yourself, ask the insurer first, and keep the invoice. (6) Towing a car with another private car is allowed only in cases of force majeure, with a rigid link and care.
- **Verify:** you know before leaving the km limit and the exclusions of your assistance (gravel roads, other countries, rented cars).
- **Don't:** accept a tow without a price and a destination; pay in cash without a receipt.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 48 ñ) (towing of vehicles only by specialised vehicles; other vehicles in cases of force majeure with rigid couplings), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; assistance clauses depend on each policy: see [insurance.md](insurance.md).

### VEH-VIA-37: After a collision, do what the law requires and protect the scene
- **Rule:** stop at once in a safe place, protect the scene with the triangles, help the injured and call 911, then exchange licence and insurance data with the other parties and report the accident to the authority.
- **Why:** the participants in a traffic accident are obliged to stop, give licence and insurance data, report the fact to any authority and appear if summoned; leaving is a crime.
- **How:** (1) Stop in a safe place without creating new risks; switch off the engine and put the hazard lights on. (2) Check people; do not move an injured person unless there is a fire risk; call 911. (3) Triangles and vests. (4) Take photographs: the cars, the plates, the road, signs, skid marks and the documents. (5) Exchange the data of the licence and the insurance with the other party; if the other party is not present, attach the data to the damaged vehicle. (6) Report to the police or the authority on site (the "acta de choque"). (7) Notify your insurer within the period in the policy (see insurance.md). (8) Do not admit fault and do not sign what you do not understand. (9) After a serious accident the car may need a new technical inspection.
- **Verify:** you have the other party's licence, vehicle and insurance data, the report number and photographs.
- **Don't:** leave the scene; accept money in cash in exchange for no report without advice; drive away a car with doubtful brakes or steering.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 65 (obligations of participants in an accident: stop, provide licence and insurance data, report the fact, appear), art. 59 (obstacles), art. 34 of the regulation item 5 (after an accident the technical inspection certificate may lose validity if safety elements are damaged), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15. See [insurance.md](insurance.md).

### VEH-VIA-38: Act fast on an engine fire
- **Rule:** if you see smoke or flames from the engine bay, stop, switch off the engine, get everyone out and well away, call 100 or 911, and use the extinguisher only if the fire is small and you are safe.
- **Why:** fuel and plastics burn quickly; a fire in a car can spread within minutes, and the fuel tank and tyres can burst.
- **How:** (1) Stop away from dry grass. (2) Engine off; everyone out and at least 30 m away, upwind. (3) Do not open the bonnet fully: open it slightly and aim the extinguisher through the gap at the base of the flames; opening wide feeds the fire. (4) If the fire is large, let it burn and wait for the firefighters. (5) Never go back for luggage.
- **Verify:** the extinguisher in the cabin is within reach and in date (VEH-VIA-12).
- **Don't:** use water on a fuel fire; open the bonnet wide.
- **Source:** general fire-safety practice (extinguisher manufacturers and firefighters); Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15. Emergency numbers: argentina.gob.ar emergency page, read 2026-11-15.

## Crossing borders by car

### VEH-VIA-39: Gather the vehicle and driver documents for crossing a border
- **Rule:** to leave Argentina by a border crossing with a vehicle, carry the vehicle title card (*cédula verde*) or the driver's authorisation, the insurance with international cover, valid ID (DNI or passport), the licence, and have the plate number engraved on the windows.
- **Why:** the official page says these are required, and the border authorities of Argentina and the neighbouring country check them.
- **How:** (1) Check each document is in date and in the name of the person who travels (or authorised, VEH-VIA-42). (2) The plate characters engraved on the car's glass: the official page lists it as a requirement; if you have no engraving, ask a workshop (cheap and quick). (3) Bring the paper documents (do not depend on the phone). (4) Check that the passport or DNI is valid for the country (for Chile, DNI with at least 6 months validity, as reported). (5) Check that the vehicle is in order: technical inspection, no unpaid fines that could stop the exit, no pending liens. (6) A car on credit or leased may need the lender's authorisation: ask the lender (see ownership-and-paperwork.md).
- **Verify:** compare the documents with the official page the week before leaving, because the rules change.
- **Don't:** assume digital documents will be accepted on the other side (VEH-VIA-42).
- **Source:** argentina.gob.ar page "Información Trámites en Frontera" (argentina.gob.ar/node/62311: cédula verde or azul, insurance with international cover, plate engraved on the glass), read 2026-11-15; Chile DNI six months: secondary report (Chile ChileAtiende/press), Not verified in a primary source. See [ownership-and-paperwork.md](ownership-and-paperwork.md).

### VEH-VIA-40: Get the Mercosur insurance certificate (*Carta Verde*) or the insurance required by the destination
- **Rule:** before leaving, ask the insurer for the insurance certificate that covers third-party liability in the country or countries you will visit, and carry it on paper.
- **Why:** a normal domestic policy may not cover damage in another country; the neighbouring countries require third-party liability insurance valid there.
- **How:** (1) Ask your insurer for the "Carta Verde" (Mercosur green card) or the cover extension for the countries on the route; it is often issued per trip and may cost extra. (2) Check the countries: for Brazil, Uruguay and Paraguay the Carta Verde (Mercosur certificate) is the standard instrument; Chile asks for a valid mandatory third-party liability insurance (Chilean customs ficha) and some sources state that a specific Chilean policy may be needed: confirm with the insurer. (3) Print the certificate; officers may ask for paper. (4) Note the claims phone abroad. (5) Check what is covered (third-party damage only; not your car or passengers unless the policy says so).
- **Verify:** the certificate names the countries and the dates, and the plate and make match.
- **Don't:** assume that "full coverage" in Argentina is valid abroad; cross with an expired certificate.
- **Source:** argentina.gob.ar border page (insurance with international cover, read 2026-11-15); Chile Aduanas ficha (valid mandatory third-party insurance, via ChileAtiende ficha 106, read 2026-11-15); the Carta Verde and Chile's SOAPEX are described in secondary sources (Brazilian insurers and press): Not verified in a primary source. See [insurance.md](insurance.md).

### VEH-VIA-41: Understand how the minimum documents differ by country
- **Rule:** for Uruguay, Brazil and Paraguay take the vehicle card, the licence, the insurance certificate and your ID, and check the country page for extras; for Chile follow the customs permit (VEH-VIA-44).
- **Why:** Mercosur countries accept the national ID card or passport and the vehicle documents with fewer formalities than other countries, but the vehicle rules are enforced at the border.
- **How:** (1) Uruguay: vehicle card; if other owners appear on it and are not travelling, a notarised authorisation or the authorised-driver card. (2) Brazil: ID valid for Mercosur, driver's licence, vehicle card and the Carta Verde; a notarised authorisation if the car is not yours; a tourist entry record. (3) Paraguay: similar; check with the insurer. (4) Opening hours, queues and fees at borders change: check the border page for the crossing. (5) Fuel prices and payment methods differ across the border; carry some cash in the local currency.
- **Verify:** consult the official migration and customs pages of the destination country in the month before leaving.
- **Don't:** plan a crossing without checking the hours and the pass status (Cristo Redentor and Patagonian crossings close in bad weather).
- **Source:** Colegio de Escribanos de Córdoba summary published by a local newspaper (La Voz de San Justo, 2018): Not verified in a primary source and possibly out of date; confirm with each country's customs and migration authority. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-42: Cover the case of a driver who is not the owner
- **Rule:** if the driver is not the registered owner, the driver must carry an authorisation that the border accepts: the authorised driver's digital or blue card where valid, or a notarised authorisation from the owner.
- **Why:** the owner's vehicle is not allowed to leave the country with another driver without the owner's authorisation; the Chilean customs page asks for a notarised authorisation.
- **How:** (1) The blue card (*cédula azul*) stopped being issued in 2024; existing ones remain valid until a change of ownership; from late 2024 the registered owner can authorise one or more people in the Mi Argentina app (Disposición DNRPA 343/2024; Res. Ministerio de Justicia 566/2025 as reported). (2) For leaving the country, reports in 2024 to 2026 disagree about whether the digital authorisation is accepted at the borders; the safe path is the owner's authorisation certified by a notary (*escribano público*), as the argentina.gob.ar border page says, plus the printed digital card. (3) Ask for the notarised authorisation 1-2 weeks ahead; it states the destination, the dates and the driver. (4) Chile specifically asks for a notarised authorisation when the driver is not the owner. (5) Bring the owner's copy of the ID if possible.
- **Verify:** ask DNRPA, your notary or the border authority about the current acceptance of the digital authorisation in the week before travelling.
- **Don't:** lend the car for a border crossing on a message; rely on a phone screenshot.
- **Source:** argentina.gob.ar/node/62311 (authorisation of the owner certified before a notary, read 2026-11-15); Chile Aduanas (via ChileAtiende ficha 106: notarised authorisation, read 2026-11-15); the digital authorisation: press reports on Disposición 343/2024 (La Voz de San Justo, 2024) and later notes, Not verified in a primary source. See [ownership-and-paperwork.md](ownership-and-paperwork.md).

### VEH-VIA-43: Handle minors travelling by car across a border
- **Rule:** a minor who travels with both parents needs documents proving the relationship; a minor who travels alone, with only one parent or with a third party needs a travel authorisation processed through the migration authority; plan it before the trip.
- **Why:** without the required authorisation the minor is not allowed to leave the country, even if the adults are legitimate guardians.
- **How:** (1) With both parents: DNI or passport of the minor and the birth certificate (*partida de nacimiento*) or the family record book. (2) Alone, with one parent or with a third party: authorisation by the Dirección Nacional de Migraciones: normal (about 10 working days), express (48 hours) or instant (up to 2 hours; offered at the airports, at the head office and at the border crossings). (3) It can be for a single trip (to be used within 90 days of the issue), for a period, or until the age of majority. (4) If one parent is abroad, the authorisation is processed through the consulate. (5) Since 2025 the system was digitalised (electronic registry and QR validation; a notarised power is no longer required according to press reports): check the current procedure. (6) Bring the originals and a copy.
- **Verify:** the authorisation is valid for the dates of the trip and the destination.
- **Don't:** assume that a letter signed by a parent is enough; start the procedure the week before travelling.
- **Source:** argentina.gob.ar/node/488933 (Dirección Nacional de Migraciones: when authorisation is needed, normal, express and instant modes, validity of 90 days, read 2026-11-15); change of 2025: iProfesional article (2025-05-16), Not verified in a primary source. Seat rules for minors in the car: VEH-VIA-14.

### VEH-VIA-44: Enter Chile with a vehicle: customs permit and SAG declaration
- **Rule:** to enter Chile by car obtain the temporary vehicle entry permit from Chilean customs (online or at the border), carry the required insurance and the owner's authorisation if you are not the owner, and declare to the SAG any animal or plant product you carry.
- **Why:** the permit lets the vehicle stay without paying duties; the SAG controls prevent pests and diseases and fines apply to undeclared items.
- **How:** (1) Permit for a private vehicle with residents of Argentina travelling for tourism: free; apply online on the customs site (ChileAtiende ficha 106) and print the form, or at the border; valid 90 consecutive days or less, according to the customs page (some sources mention up to 180 days: check). (2) Documents: ID of the driver, proof of ownership (vehicle card), valid mandatory third-party insurance, notarised authorisation if you are not the owner. (3) Complete the migration (PDI) and phytosanitary (SAG) controls. (4) SAG: declare under oath any plant, animal, their products and by-products, handicrafts, agricultural chemicals, veterinary medicines and animal feed that you carry in clothes, luggage or in the vehicle, regardless of amount; the declaration is free (digital at ingresoachile.cl or paper). (5) Eat or leave at the border: fruit, vegetables, meat, dairy, honey, seeds, nuts and similar products are usually restricted (the SAG page does not give the detail: ask or check). (6) Undeclared items can mean sanctions and fines, and the product is confiscated.
- **Verify:** the permit form and the SAG declaration are with the passports before the queue; the car has no hidden food (check under the seats and the cooler).
- **Don't:** carry fresh fruit, meat, cheese or honey "for the trip"; hide products.
- **Source:** Chile Aduanas via ChileAtiende ficha 106 (temporary vehicle entry from and to Argentina: free, 90 days, documents, notarised authorisation), read 2026-11-15; SAG, sag.gob.cl/ambitos-de-accion/declaracion-jurada-sag-de-ingreso-chile (what must be declared; sanctions), read 2026-11-15; the detailed list of restricted foods: Not verified in a primary source.

### VEH-VIA-45: Check the Cristo Redentor pass before every crossing to Chile
- **Rule:** before leaving for the Cristo Redentor pass (Ruta 7, Mendoza-Los Andes) check the official status of the pass, the hours and the chain requirements, and plan to be flexible about the date.
- **Why:** the pass is often closed for snow, wind and avalanches, sometimes for days, and queues of hundreds of vehicles form after reopening; closures can strand travellers.
- **How:** (1) Check the Sistema Integrado Cristo Redentor official channels and the Gendarmería Nacional (Argentina) and Chilean border authority on the day, and again at Uspallata. (2) The pass has opening hours that can change in winter; arrive early. (3) In snow, carry chains (VEH-VIA-29) and be ready for a long wait; fill the tank, carry warm clothes, food and water. (4) Book lodging in Uspallata or Mendoza with free cancellation. (5) Alternative crossings exist (other passes) but may have their own closures. (6) Observe the Chilean speed and lane rules in the tunnel. (7) Complete the pre-registration forms if the integrated control offers an online one.
- **Verify:** a status check on the day with time and source recorded.
- **Don't:** leave Mendoza without confirming that the pass is open; trust a three-day-old news item.
- **Source:** press reports (Perfil, Los Andes, MDZ; one of them dated 2026-06-18 reports a preventive closure) and the recommendation to use the official Sistema Integrado Cristo Redentor channels; Not verified in a primary source: the official page was not read. Read 2026-11-15.

## Fuel and money

### VEH-VIA-46: Choose the right fuel and grade for the trip, especially for an old car
- **Rule:** use at least the octane rating (RON) the manual specifies; in Argentina the regular grade (*Súper*, grade 2) is about 95 RON and the premium grade (grade 3; for example YPF Infinia) about 98 RON; an old car designed for lower-octane fuel does not need premium, and the premium does not improve its performance.
- **Why:** too low an octane causes engine knock (*pistoneo*) and damage; too high brings no benefit if the engine is not designed for it; gasoline in Argentina contains bioethanol, which attacks old rubber parts and increases vapour lock.
- **How:** (1) Look at the fuel cap flap or the manual for the octane required; when it is a 1990s car designed for lower octane (see vw-gol-1994-specs.md) the Súper grade of today is adequate. (2) Fill up at busy stations of known networks where the fuel turns over fast. (3) Do not mix a tank with fuel of dubious origin; if the car stumbles after fuelling, stop and tell the station. (4) Keep the tank above a quarter to avoid sediment in the filter and carburettor. (5) Gasoline with bioethanol up to 12% is normal in Argentina (Ley 27.640, from memory): old rubber fuel hoses may deteriorate faster, so replace them with modern ethanol-resistant hoses. (6) For a car converted to compressed natural gas (*GNC*) the cylinder and the regulator have mandatory periodic checks: check the expiry before a trip.
- **Verify:** the octane on the pump matches the manual; the car runs smoothly and does not knock under load.
- **Don't:** use diesel in a petrol car; add "fuel boosters" of unknown origin; run on the lowest grade in a car that requires premium.
- **Source:** secondary sources (La Nación, Perfil, Cronista: Súper about 95 RON, Premium about 98 RON; read 2026-11-15; one 2012 report quotes 93 octanes for Súper): Not verified in a primary source. The exact current minimum is in the Secretaría de Energía fuel specification resolution (not located). Ethanol content: Not verified in a primary source (memory of Ley 27.640). GNC: Ley 24.449 art. 29 of the regulation item 6 (ENARGAS norms), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-47: Budget the fuel and plan the payment methods
- **Rule:** estimate the fuel cost with a 20% margin for the route (consumption on the worst surface), and plan how you will pay at stations and tolls, with cash and cards.
- **Why:** prices of fuel change often and differ by region; some remote stations have no card terminal or no connection.
- **How:** (1) Compute litres from distance and consumption at highway speed with load (usually 10-20% more than the "official" figure). (2) Prices vary by province and by station: example as of 2026-11-15, check the station or the YPF app. (3) Carry cash in small notes; keep one card as a backup. (4) Keep every receipt for the trip budget. (5) At the border use the destination currency or cards for fuel.
- **Verify:** the trip budget has a fuel line with the date of the price assumption.
- **Don't:** assume a card works everywhere in Patagonia.
- **Source:** general practice; Not verified in a primary source. Read 2026-11-15.

### VEH-VIA-48: Plan tolls and the toll tag (*TelePASE*) before leaving
- **Rule:** before the trip check the tolls on the route, make sure the toll tag is active and has balance, and carry cash for plazas without the tag lane.
- **Why:** tolls on national and provincial routes add up on a long trip, and an unpaid toll becomes a fine and a debt linked to the plate.
- **How:** (1) Use the tolls guide (tolls-and-telepase.md) to list the toll plazas and the amounts (example only as of the date shown in that document). (2) Check the toll tag account and balance in the app of the provider. (3) Carry cash in small notes for plazas that do not accept the tag or cards. (4) Keep the receipts. (5) Use the hazard lights when stopping at the toll, as the law says for toll stations.
- **Verify:** the toll tag shows balance for the number of plazas on the route.
- **Don't:** go through a toll lane reserved for the tag without one; leave tolls unpaid.
- **Source:** Ley 24.449 art. 47 e) (hazard lights to indicate the stop at toll stations), consolidated text, read 2026-11-15; for the toll amounts and rules see [tolls-and-telepase.md](tolls-and-telepase.md).
